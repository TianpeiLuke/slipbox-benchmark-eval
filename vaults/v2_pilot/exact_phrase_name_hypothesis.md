# Hypothesis: a query-literal business name raises its ranking and walk-ins

Hypothesis tested implicitly by two businesses in The Verge's "The restaurant nearest Google" (2023-10-26): a local business named with the exact phrase its customers type — Thai Food Near Me (Murray Hill, Manhattan) and Barber Shop Near Me (Coral Springs, Florida, chosen in 2019 by Felix Silva) — appears measurably higher in that query's results and draws measurably more walk-ins than a comparable nearby business with an arbitrary name.

Current evidence is testimony, not measurement. Teddy Jirapraphanan, one of four owners, says a random name leaves you "in the bottom" among "a million restaurants close by". Confounds: a photo went viral on Twitter in March, ahead of the opening, and the goofy name itself earned news coverage and "an influx of customers in the first crucial months". Imitation is selection, not proof: more than 20 "Notary Near Me", seven "Plumber Near Me", half a dozen "Affordable Dentist Near Me" in Texas.

Falsifier: an otherwise comparable nearby business with a random name that ranks and fills equally well.
