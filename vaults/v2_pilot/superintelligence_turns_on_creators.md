# Hypothesis: superintelligent machines could turn on their creators — Rationalists' AI risk argument

The hypothesis: if machines become more intelligent than humans, they could turn on their creators by doing things their creators had not intended. It is attributed to the Rationalists, a community led by Eliezer Yudkowsky, an internet philosopher and self-taught AI researcher, who in later years called themselves effective altruists. They believed AI could find a cure for cancer or solve climate change, yet worried about misbehaving AI bots. The proposition is falsified if systems that exceed human capability are shown to pursue only their designers' stated aims. As reported by The Age on 2023-12-09, Demis Hassabis and two British colleagues, connected to Yudkowsky, were seeking money in 2010 to build "artificial general intelligence," or AGI, a machine that could do anything the brain could do.
