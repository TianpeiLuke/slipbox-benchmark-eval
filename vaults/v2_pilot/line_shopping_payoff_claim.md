# Why Line Shopping Pays for the Time It Costs

The argument for line shopping is a mismatch between what it gains and what it costs. Sporting News's guide "Line Shopping in Sports Betting – How to Find the Best Odds" (2023-11-06) puts the cost at "just seconds" when a bettor uses a live odds feed showing differences between sportsbooks, or longer when working manually by installing more than one sports betting app on a phone and tapping among them to compare.

The gain is larger. Betting volume is rarely the same at every sportsbook, so one may be forced to move a line or change odds while another is not; prices may also differ from the opening. Caesars Sportsbook's point spreads can be "a half-point to two points better than the competition's," and its moneyline odds may be more favorable. A half-point at that size changes which side of a posted number a spread wager settles on. As the guide puts it, shopping around lets a bettor "lay less, win more, and improve your chances."
