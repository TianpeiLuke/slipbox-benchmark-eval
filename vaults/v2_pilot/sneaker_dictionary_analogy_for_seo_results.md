# The Nike-written dictionary entry: a model for reading SEO-shaped search results

A model offered in The Verge's "The people who ruined the internet" (2023-11-01): after SEO techniques spread, "googling anything can now feel like looking up 'sneaker' in the dictionary and finding a definition that sounds both incorrect and suspiciously as though it were written by someone promoting Nike ('footwear that allows you to just do it!')." The analogy names the exact defect. A dictionary entry carries authority because no vendor wrote it; SEO, by contrast, means telling the algorithm whatever it needs to hear so a site ranks high, then showing visitors advertising. So a top-ranked result can be right in form — neutral, encyclopedic, apparently disinterested — and vendor-authored in substance. What breaks is the illusion that the internet was ever about anything other than selling stuff.
