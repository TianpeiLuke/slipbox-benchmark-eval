# Google's opening argument: Play Store dominance is the fruit of competing with the iPhone

In his opening statement for Google in the Epic v. Google trial, lead attorney Glenn Pomerantz told the jury that Google's dominance over the Android app market "isn't anything nefarious but simply the natural outcome of Google fiercely competing with the iPhone and its iOS App Store, where Google would like the court to believe that competition truly lies." His stated warrant, reported by The Verge on 2023-11-07, was that "You cannot separate the quality of a phone from the quality of the apps in its app store, and that means Google and Apple compete against each other." The argument's leverage is definitional: on that framing the relevant market is phones and ecosystems, where Google plainly lacks a monopoly, rather than Android app stores alone. The reporter judged it potentially "a winning argument in the case" if the jury accepts it, while noting Google spent much of its 45 minutes explaining away apparently bad behavior as normal business practice and "didn't always succeed out of the gate."
