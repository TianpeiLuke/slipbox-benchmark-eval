# Default search engine: the placement a device maker sells, not a choice a user makes

A default search engine is the query service preselected by a browser or device maker, so a user searches through it without setting anything up. That passivity is the whole commercial point: the preselection, not the ranking, is the scarce asset distributors sell. Google paid $26.3 billion in 2021 to multiple browsers, phones and platforms, from companies including Apple, Samsung and Mozilla, to hold that slot; about $18 billion went to Apple, a partner since 2003. The yield was measurable early. A 2007 Google email showed users who moved their browser homepage to Google did 15% more Google searches, and those who switched away dropped 27%.
