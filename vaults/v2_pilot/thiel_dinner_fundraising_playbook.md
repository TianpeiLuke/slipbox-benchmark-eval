# Hassabis's One-Year Reconnaissance to Land a Private Meeting with Peter Thiel

In 2010, Demis Hassabis, a 34-year-old British neuroscientist, needed money from a hard-to-reach investor, Peter Thiel, to start building artificial general intelligence with two colleagues; at the time, few people were interested in AI. He treated the introduction as an operation with a measurable objective: a private second meeting.

The procedure, recorded in The Age on 2023-12-09: first, a year of pre-research on the target. "I was preparing for that meeting for a year," Hassabis said. "I thought that would be my unique hook in: I knew that he loved chess." Second, entry through an existing referral chain — the community of Eliezer Yudkowsky, the internet philosopher and self-taught AI researcher whose Rationalist (later effective altruist) circle included Hassabis and, separately, Thiel, who had developed a fascination with the singularity. Third, at the cocktail party in Thiel's San Francisco town house overlooking the Palace of Fine Arts and a swan pond, scan the room for the obsession: there, in the living room, was a chessboard. Fourth, open with the tension only that host would chase — the bishop against the knight, two pieces of equal value with vastly different strengths.
