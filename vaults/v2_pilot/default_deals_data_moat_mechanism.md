# DOJ's theory of harm in US v. Google: platform deals starve rivals of search data

In the Justice Department's antitrust case against Google, roughly two months old as of 2023-11-13, the government's core theory of harm is that Google uses its platforms and its deals with partners to block out any competition in search or advertising, hindering competitors from accessing the data they would need to improve their products. The DOJ has said Google gets 16 times more data than Bing does everyday. The remedies on the table track that logic: if Judge Amit Mehta rules against Google, the company could be banned from making anticompetitive and exclusive deals with smartphone and computer manufacturers and wireless carriers, forced to share its APIs with third-party developers, or required to turn over all or most of the data it has collected to other search engines so they can improve their products and attract more users.
