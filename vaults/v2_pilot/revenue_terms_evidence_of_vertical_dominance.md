# Revenue-ranked queries as evidence of Google's vertical search dominance

In TechCrunch's 2023-11-13 roundup of the US v Google trial, the reporter interprets a publicly released list of the 20 search terms that made Google the most money during the week of September 22, 2018 — a list Judge Amit Mehta ruled to make public, with per-term revenue and query counts redacted — as proof of Google's dominance of vertical search, meaning search in very specific market categories. The warrant is saturation: the terms are concentrated in the categories where advertisers pay most — "Auto Insurance," "Car Insurance," "Car Insurance Quotes," "Cheap Car Insurance," "Insurance Quotes," "Cheap Flights," "DirecTV," "AT&T," "Spectrum," "Comcast," "Xfinity," "Hulu," "Free Credit Report," "LifeLock." The iPhone 8 aside concedes the list is unsurprising, since "September 22, 2017 was the official release date of the iPhone 8" and Google and Apple maintain a mutually beneficial relationship even while competing. The reporter also attributes "LifeLock" to the 2017 Equifax breach. The whole reading rests on one week in 2018.
