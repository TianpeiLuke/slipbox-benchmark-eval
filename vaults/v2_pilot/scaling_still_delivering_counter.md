# Counter-argument: the "monolithic LLMs hit a wall" prediction is undercut by LLMs' own record

The TechCrunch article "8 predictions for AI in 2024" (2023-12-19) predicts that 2024 "will be the year something gives" for large language models, yet its own framing undercuts that: LLMs "have indeed proved more capable than anyone expected, and have grown correspondingly more so as more compute is added." That record is the strongest evidence against the predicted wall. If capability keeps scaling with compute, the anticipated diminishing returns on parameter counts — that a 500-billion-parameter model "may technically produce better results" while its compute "could provably be deployed more effectively" — describes an engineering preference, not a limit the models reach. A mixture of experts would then be a cheaper option, not a sign that monolithic LLMs have failed.
