# Artificial general intelligence (AGI): a machine that could do anything the brain could do

Artificial general intelligence, or AGI, is defined as a machine that could do anything the brain could do — general capability matching human cognition, rather than a tool built for one task. In 2010, Demis Hassabis, then a 34-year-old neuroscientist, and two colleagues who all lived in Britain were looking for money to start building it, pitching Peter Thiel at Thiel's San Francisco town house. The definition matters as a benchmark: at the time, after a half-century of research, the AI field had failed to deliver anything remotely close to the human brain, and few people were interested in funding AI at all.

(Source: "How ego and fear fuelled the rise of artificial intelligence," The Age, 2023-12-09.)
