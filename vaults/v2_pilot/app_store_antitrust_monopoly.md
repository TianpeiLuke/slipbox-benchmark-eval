# "Unlawful Monopoly" in Epic v. Google: Legal Theory vs. Market-Leader Meaning

In Epic v. Google, tried before a jury with opening arguments reported by The Verge on 2023-11-07, the phrase "unlawful monopoly" names a legal theory, not a popularity ranking. Epic sued Google in 2020, alleging Google Play constitutes an unlawful monopoly. Epic lead attorney Gary Bornstein's task was proving Android *functions* as an unlawful monopoly — conduct-based: Google allegedly "bribes" or "blocks" attempts to compete with Google Play. The plain-language sense, "the most-used store," is what Google attacked. Google lead attorney Glenn Pomerantz countered that "Google doesn't have a monopoly on app stores or phones in general," noting "every single Samsung phone comes with two app stores right on the homescreen." Confusing the two meanings lets each side answer the other's different question.
