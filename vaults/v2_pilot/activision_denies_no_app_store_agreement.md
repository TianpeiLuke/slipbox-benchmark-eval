# Activision's denial undercuts Epic's $360 million "payment not to compete" narrative

Epic's account of Google buying off rivals rests heavily on a $360 million figure. At the opening day of the Epic v. Google trial on Monday, 2023-11-06, Epic attorney Gary Bornstein told jurors Google "pays actual and potential competitors not to compete," citing $360 million as "an actual number you'll hear about" — the alleged payment that kept Activision from opening an app store that could compete with Google Play. The Verge's 2023-11-07 report contests that characterization at its foundation: Activision told The Verge in 2022 that it "never entered into an agreement that Activision would not open its own app store," and Google now says it is armed with the evidence to prove it. That is not a quibble over margin size; it removes the named recipient and the documented quid pro quo from Epic's most concrete-sounding example. Bornstein conceded on Monday that Google "was too clever" to draw up contracts specifically forcing developers not to compete with the Play Store, which compounds the gap between the narrative and the paper trail.
