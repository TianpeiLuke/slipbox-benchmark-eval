# AI's reach into classrooms and battlefields set the stakes of Silicon Valley's quiet race for control

By late 2023 artificial intelligence was already quickly reshaping the world along a broad front, from how children are taught to how wars are fought. That breadth is what gave the Silicon Valley contest for control of the technology its consequences: "The Age" (9 Dec 2023) describes it, years before OpenAI's near meltdown, as a "little-publicised but ferocious competition", fought among the world's richest men — Musk, Larry Page, Mark Zuckerberg of Meta, investor Peter Thiel, Satya Nadella of Microsoft and Sam Altman of OpenAI — each angling for a piece of a business that one day could be worth trillions of dollars.

The readers' first real sight of those feuds came in November 2023, when rebel OpenAI board members tried to force out Altman because they believed they could no longer trust him to build AI that would benefit humanity; over five chaotic days the lab looked as if it would fall apart, until giant investors and employees threatening to follow Altman out the door made the board back down. OpenAI, whose ChatGPT chatbot fundamentally changed the technology industry, was then valued at more than US$80 billion (A$123 billion).
