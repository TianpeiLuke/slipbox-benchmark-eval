# The DOJ's implied case that Apple's "best product" defence is pretextual

In US v. Google, on 26 September 2023 in Washington, DC, Justice Department lawyer Meagan Bellshaw pressed Eddy Cue on a question she did not have to state: whether Apple placed Google at the centre of its platform for the money rather than the product. The source names this directly — "whether Apple picked Google because it's the most lucrative choice or the best product" was "a key part of the DOJ's entire case."

The warrant is Apple's own paper and its own ledger. The Information Services Agreement, in place since 2002 and renegotiated by Cue with Sundar Pichai in 2016, "pays Apple billions of dollars a year" and makes Google the default search engine on all Apple devices; in 2016 Apple sought a higher percentage of Google's revenue from Apple-directed users, and Cue conceded he never considered walking away: "I always felt like it was in Google's best interest, and our best interest, to get a deal done." Then Bellshaw showed emails and slides in which Cue and Apple "railed against Google's privacy policies" — after Cue had testified that user privacy is "Absolutely" important to Apple, and that the ISA bars Apple from offering a choice of search engines during setup.
