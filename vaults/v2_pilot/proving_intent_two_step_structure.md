# The two-step path to proving intent in the FTX trial against Sam Bankman-Fried

At the criminal trial of Sam Bankman-Fried, founder of the failed cryptocurrency exchange FTX, the government faces a two-step burden in the element Christopher LaVigne, a former federal prosecutor, identifies as the crucial one: intent. The procedure splits cleanly in difficulty.

Step one is the mechanical part. Prosecutors must show that certain transactions actually took place. This is "pretty simple and a little boring," LaVigne says — whatever records the Southern District of New York holds for those transactions will simply be entered and shown. It establishes movement of money, nothing more.

Step two, where LaVigne notes "all the drama is likely to co"me, is the one that decides the case: connecting those recorded transactions to Bankman-Fried personally by proving he knew what he was doing was wrong and lied about it anyway. The transactions prove themselves; the state of mind behind them must be demonstrated separately.
