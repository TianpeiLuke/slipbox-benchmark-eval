# Google's Antitrust Defense: Paying $26.3 Billion for Defaults Is "Just Doing Business"

In the U.S. government's antitrust trial over search, reported by TechCrunch on 2023-10-31, Google's defense rests on a single claim: it is merely doing business. Partners choose Google as the default search engine because it is the best engine, consumers benefit from that quality, and therefore paying for placement does not make its actions illegal. That position has to carry the figure Google search head Prabhakar Raghavan testified to in court on October 28, 2023: Google paid $26.3 billion in 2021 to multiple browsers, phones and platforms, from companies including Apple, Samsung and Mozilla, to be the default. About $18 billion of that went to Apple, whose default deal with Google dates to 2003. CEO Sundar Pichai, who testified at the same trial, said the spending was decided with the consumer in mind, so Google could take in all the data and be the best search engine across different companies' devices.
