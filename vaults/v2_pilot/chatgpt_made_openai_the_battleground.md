# OpenAI as the pivot of AI's control-versus-freedom argument

In *The Age*'s report of 2023-12-09, "How ego and fear fuelled the rise of artificial intelligence," the argument over whether AI "should be controlled or set free" is shown to run through OpenAI rather than through the abstract idea of the technology. The reason is ChatGPT: thanks to that chatbot, OpenAI "has fundamentally changed the technology industry and has introduced the world to the risks and potential of artificial intelligence." A lab valued at more than $US80 billion ($A123 billion) thereby became the shared reference point for a debate conducted among Silicon Valley founders, chatbot users, academics, legislators and regulators, and one that pits Elon Musk, Larry Page, Mark Zuckerberg of Meta, Peter Thiel, Satya Nadella of Microsoft and Sam Altman of OpenAI against one another.

The claim is tested by what happened inside that same company months later, when rebel board members tried to force out Altman on the belief they could no longer trust him to build AI that would benefit humanity. Over five chaotic days, the control-versus-freedom argument stopped being a policy abstractions and turned into a single boardroom confrontation, one whose outcome giant investors and employees threatening to follow Altman out the door ultimately reversed when the board backed down. That episode "gave the world its first glimpse of the bitter feuds among those who will determine the future of AI" — and confirms the argument's logic: because OpenAI made both the promise and the hazard legible at once, every contest over AI's direction is now conducted by reference to it.
