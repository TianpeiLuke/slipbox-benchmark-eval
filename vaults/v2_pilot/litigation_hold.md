# Litigation Hold: The Duty to Preserve Evidence Once Litigation Is Reasonably Anticipated

A litigation hold is an obligation that attaches once litigation is reasonably anticipated: a party must preserve documents and communications that could be relevant to the case, overriding its ordinary deletion practices. That is why automatic-deletion policies become legally exposed once a hold applies. In the U.S. government's antitrust trial against Google, the DOJ touched on Google's policy of deleting internal chat messages despite the company being subject to a litigation hold. In February 2023, the DOJ had accused Google of systematically destroying chats through its history-off option, which deletes messages every 24 hours unless a user manually changed the setting.
