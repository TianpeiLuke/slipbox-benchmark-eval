# AI Funding Scarcity in 2010 After Half a Century of Failed Research

In 2010, the neuroscientist Demis Hassabis and two British colleagues were seeking money to build "artificial general intelligence," or AGI — a machine that could do anything the human brain could do. At that moment, few people were interested in AI. After a half-century of research, the AI field had failed to deliver anything remotely close to the human brain. That scarcity of enthusiasm and capital was the environment into which DeepMind was born.
