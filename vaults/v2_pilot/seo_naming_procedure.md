# Procedure for naming a business so that search finds it

Procedure as practiced by Teddy Jirapraphanan and Felix Silva (The Verge, 2023-10-26).

First, establish the literal query phrase customers already type. Jirapraphanan, co-owner of the New York restaurant Thai Food Near Me, based the name on his own habit: "Everywhere I go, I'm craving Thai food. I have to search 'Thai food near me' all the time."

Second, reject arbitrary or brand-only candidates. Jirapraphanan's reasoning: "When you have a million restaurants close by, you will be in the bottom [of rankings] if it's a random name." In 2019 Silva set aside "The Barber Shop" and more than 20 other options for his Coral Springs, Florida store, choosing Barber Shop Near Me because the phrase is neutral and memorable.

Third, pair the name with search-visible assets: a Google Maps profile carrying high-quality photos, videos and updates (some experts say active profiles improve rankings), plus keyword-bearing reviews — Silva's "light bulb moment" was Google returning a non-vegan restaurant because a customer review highlighted the keyword "vegan".

Postcondition: a search for the phrase returns the business. The name only gets customers in the door; service and reviews sustain the ranking.
