# "Risk-neutral" as euphemism: the author's objection to Bankman-Fried's self-description

In her Verge trial column "Sam Bankman-Fried was a terrible boyfriend" (2023-10-10), the author pushes back on Bankman-Fried's own label for his conduct: he "called this being 'risk-neutral,' which seems like a fancy way of saying he was a gambling addict." The objection is aimed at the vocabulary itself — a financial-sounding term that launders compulsion into a neutral analytical stance. She reads the rest of his self-presentation the same way. Her opening paragraph says that none of her ex-boyfriends had "made me the CEO of their sin-eater hedge fund while refusing to give me equity and bragging about how there was a 5 percent chance they'd become the president of the United States." Under her interpretation, that 5 percent figure is the addict's odds, not the analyst's.
