# Epic's price-harm claim rests on monopoly-set app store and payment fees

Epic's case against Google depends on one relation: that Google's app-store and payment-processing terms are set by monopoly power rather than by competition, and that the consequence is consumers pay more for apps than they would in a contested market. Epic's earlier legal filings suggested this upshot for consumers — higher prices for apps than there would be "if there were more competition and / or lower app store and payment processing fees." Epic lead attorney Gary Bornstein argued the same structure at the opening described by The Verge on 2023-11-07, showing jurors 70 percent app profit margins on $12 billion in revenue a year, per Epic, and Google "pay[ing] actual and potential competitors not to compete." Epic sued Google in 2020 over in-app purchase fees. The claim fails if the fee levels would persist under competition.
