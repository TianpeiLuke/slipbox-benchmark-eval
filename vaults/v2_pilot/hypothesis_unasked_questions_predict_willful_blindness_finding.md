# Told to "stop asking questions," a principal facing factfinder inference of willful blindness

Hypothesis, drawn from The Verge's 2023-10-31 trial coverage: where a principal leaves a multi-billion-dollar related-party liability unexamined after subordinates told him "they were busy and I should stop asking questions because it was distracting," factfinders infer knowledge or willful blindness rather than ignorance. Sam Bankman-Fried testified he discovered FTX's fiat@ftx account in June 2022 but its purpose only in October, while directing Caroline Ellison to repay third-party loans and continuing to invest. He conceded no Alameda policy barred spending FTX customer deposits and that he fired nobody: "I wasn't trying to build out blame for it." The reporter's framing — "simple questions nailed Bankman-Fried to the wall," and "$8 billion has a way of focusing the mind" for the jurors — supplies the inference mechanism. Falsified if contemporaneous documents, such as the Alameda balance sheet from June 13th, 2022 listing "ftx borrows," evidence substantive inquiry, escalation to counsel or law enforcement, or personnel action tied to the $8 billion discrepancy.
