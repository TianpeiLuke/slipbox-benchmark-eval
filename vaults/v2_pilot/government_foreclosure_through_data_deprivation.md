# DOJ's Theory of Harm in the Google Antitrust Case: Defaults Cut Rivals Off From Data

In the U.S. government's antitrust trial against Google, as reported by TechCrunch on 2023-10-31, the government's core allegation is that Google uses its platforms and its deals with partners to block out any competition in search or advertising, and that this same lock-up hinders competitors from accessing the data they would need to improve their products. The theory therefore runs in two linked steps: owned distribution plus paid defaults deny rivals visibility, and denied visibility denies them the query data that would let their products reach a quality high enough to challenge Google. The claim over Google's dominance in the online search market was filed in 2020. It is separate from a claim filed in January by the Department of Justice along with eight states including New York, California and Colorado, which aims to halt Google's anticompetitive scheme, unwind its monopolistic grip on the market and restore competition to digital advertising. Google's counter is that it is simply doing business, since partners want the best default engine.
