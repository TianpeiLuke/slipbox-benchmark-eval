# The Article's Argument: The Burden of Proof Makes the Google Case an Uphill Battle

In "Is Google Search better than the rest? And is that fair?", published by The Age on 22 October 2023, the author argues that the US antitrust suit against Google "could be an uphill battle" because the government "has to prove that Google somehow broke the law to achieve the status quo" — that is, show that Google's dominance as the default search provider was unlawfully won rather than earned by being, as Google contends, simply the best product. The same paragraph grants the case a countervailing value: for everyone outside the courtroom it "offers a rare opportunity for some insight into what the tech giant does with its enormous market share," specifically whether Google is still focused on being the best, or uses its ubiquity to squeeze users for money even at the expense of product quality, and how "organic" its results are.
