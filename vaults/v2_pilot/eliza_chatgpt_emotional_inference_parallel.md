# Hyland: ChatGPT's "feel" difference from ELIZA lies in the model, not the psychology

Adam Hyland, a PhD student studying AI at the University of Washington, draws out the emotional component of ChatGPT: conversations with it have a palpably different "feel" than with more rudimentary chatbots. He points back to ELIZA, the chatbot created by MIT computer scientist Joseph Weizenbaum in 1966. "In the 1960s, ELIZA offered a chatbot, the response to which was very similar to how people reacted to ChatGPT," Hyland said. "Humans interacting with the system inferred emotional content and a narrative through line in chat messages." The parallel locates the difference between ELIZA and ChatGPT in the sophistication of the underlying model, not in the psychology of the interaction: users in 1966 were already supplying the emotion and the story themselves.
