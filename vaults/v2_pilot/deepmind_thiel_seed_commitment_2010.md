# Thiel's firm backs Hassabis's AGI venture with £1.4 million in 2010

In 2010, Demis Hassabis, then a 34-year-old neuroscientist, and two colleagues who all lived in Britain were looking for money to start building "artificial general intelligence," or AGI — a machine that could do anything the brain could do — at a time when few people were interested in AI. The day after a cocktail party at Peter Thiel's San Francisco town house, a meeting Hassabis said he had been "preparing for [...] for a year" because he knew Thiel loved chess, Thiel and his venture capital firm agreed to put £1.4 million (A$2.7 million) behind Hassabis's AGI venture. Thiel had made his fortune on an early Facebook investment and with Musk in PayPal's early days. (The Age, 9 December 2023)
