# Bellshaw's Internal Google Documents Turn Google's Own Default-Setting Criticism Against Its Consumer-Preference Defence

In the U.S. government's antitrust trial against Google on 2023-10-31 (TechCrunch), Google's defence to paying for default search placement is consumer preference: everybody wants Google as the default engine because it is the best, so the practice is lawful business. DOJ lawyer Meagan Bellshaw's exhibits undercut that position from inside Google. She showed CEO Sundar Pichai a 2007 email from a Google product strategy meeting recording that people who changed their browser homepage to Google did 15% more Google searches, and those who switched away saw Google searches drop 27% — the meeting summary told Pichai and other Google leaders that "focusing on homepage market share is one of the most effective things we can do to make gains in search market share." The accompanying $26.3 billion Google spent on defaults in 2021, about $18 billion of it to Apple, is the budget version of that logic.
