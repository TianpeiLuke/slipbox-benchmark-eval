# Complaint's core allegation: Google is "starving the free press" at a cost of billions

The central harm asserted in the class action antitrust complaint filed this week in the U.S. District Court in D.C. is that Google is "starving the free press" by sharing publishers' content on Google, losing them "billions of dollars." The plaintiff, Helena World Chronicle, an Arkansas-based publisher that owns and publishes two weekly newspapers in Arkansas, brings the claim against Google and parent company Alphabet under the Sherman Act and other U.S. antitrust law, framing Google's redistribution of news content inside its own products as anticompetitive conduct that diverts publishers' content, readers and ad revenue.
