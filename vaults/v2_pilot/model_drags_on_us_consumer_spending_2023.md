# Jacobsen's three drags on US consumer spending, October 2023

Brian Jacobsen, chief economist at Annex Wealth Management, identified three factors that in combination depressed the growth of US consumer spending in late 2023: higher energy prices, student loan debt repayments, and "real disposable incomes that have been on a declining trajectory since June". His verdict on the trio was blunt — it "doesn't bode well". He described the slowdown of US consumer spending growth as having "come to a boil during a hot summer, and the temperature is really starting to come down". The written opinion was reported by The Sydney Morning Herald on 1 October 2023. Supporting the first factor, US crude settled at $US90.79 a barrel on the Friday before, down 92¢ that day but far above the $US70 of June, at the highest level in more than a year. The article dated this assessment to the 29 September 2023 close, when August data showed US consumer spending growth cooler than expected alongside slightly cooler inflation — a reading positive for inflation but a risk to the spending that had kept the US economy out of recession, since resumed US student-loan repayments diverted dollars away from purchases.
