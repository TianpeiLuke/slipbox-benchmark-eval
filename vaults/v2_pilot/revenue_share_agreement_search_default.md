# Revenue Share Agreement (RSA): Google's Payment Deal for Default Search and Chrome

A revenue share agreement (RSA) is the payment arrangement Google strikes with Android device makers and wireless carriers, conditioning Google's money on those partners setting Google Search and the Chrome browser as the defaults on the devices they ship. Jamie Rosenberg, a Google employee who focuses on Android and Google Play, described the structure while testifying in Google's defense on November 8, 2023, in the Justice Department's antitrust trial against Google (US v Google). He contrasted RSAs, held with makers and with carriers such as Verizon, with the mobile app distribution agreement (MADA): that requires Android smartphone makers like Samsung or Oppo to pre-load a bundle of 11 Google apps, including Search, Chrome and Play, which he said need not be defaults. TechCrunch reported this on November 13, 2023.
