# "Higher-for-longer": the market's assumption that central banks hold rates elevated rather than cut quickly

"Higher-for-longer" names the market's assumption that a central bank keeps policy rates elevated well past the near term instead of cutting quickly — as Sydney Morning Herald reporting on 2023-10-01 put it, "Wall Street accepts a new normal where the Federal Reserve is likely to keep interest rates high for longer."

The concept describes expectations, not announced policy. Anchors cited then: the US Federal Reserve's main rate at its highest level since 2001, with the central bank signalling in the week before that it may cut in 2024 by less than earlier expected, and its next rate meeting ending November 1. In Australia, the RBA was expected to hold at its Tuesday meeting, with National Australia Bank forecasting another hike to 4.35 per cent in November.

Consequences observed: the 10-year US Treasury yield back at 4.58 per cent, near its highest since 2007; interest-rate-sensitive healthcare and consumer stocks weakest; the S&P 500 down 4.9 per cent in September.
