# Single-prompt anecdotes cannot settle Gemini Pro's quality

The TechCrunch piece "Early impressions of Google's Gemini aren't great" (2023-12-07) draws its case almost entirely from isolated, uncontrolled observations: individual X posts and the author's own one-off prompts to Bard, where Gemini Pro began rolling out that week. A handful of wrong 2023 Oscar answers, one failed request for a six-letter French word, and a few buggy Python snippets are diagnostic of specific prompts, not of the model's general capability, and no benchmark, sample size, or comparison against GPT-4 on the same tasks underlies them. The article concedes the point directly: after Google emphasized Gemini's enhanced coding skills in a briefing earlier that week, it allows that "perhaps it's genuinely improved in some areas — posts on X suggest as much." Those same posts also report coding failures, so the evidence cuts both ways and remains too thin to establish whether Gemini Pro is better or worse than its competitors overall.
