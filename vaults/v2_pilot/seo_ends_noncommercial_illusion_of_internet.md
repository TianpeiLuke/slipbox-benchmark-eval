# SEO's Own Success Broke the Internet's Illusion of Being About Something Other Than Selling

The argument in Alice Raybould's The Verge piece "The people who ruined the internet" (2023-11-01) is that search engine optimization is disliked on principle, not merely on results. SEO works by telling the algorithm whatever it needs to hear for a site to rank as high as possible, borrowing Google's presumption of objectivity — the company holds a 90 percent market share for online search — to draw readers in, and then showing them advertising. By 2023, users were calling search results "absolutely unusable", "garbage", and "a nightmare", and googling anything could feel like looking up "sneaker" in a dictionary and finding an entry written by someone promoting Nike. The author's explanation for why nearly everyone hates SEO and the people who do it for a living: the practice seems to have successfully destroyed the illusion that the internet was ever about anything other than selling stuff. The hatred, on this reading, is aimed at the messenger — SEO made visible a commercial logic that had always been operating underneath.
