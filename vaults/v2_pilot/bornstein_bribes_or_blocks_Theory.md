# Bornstein's Frame: Google Pays Rivals Not to Compete, or Blocks Them

In Epic's opening arguments in Epic v. Google (Epic sued Google in 2020, per The Verge on 2023-11-07), lead attorney Gary Bornstein made the case that Android functions as an unlawful monopoly by "basically calling Google a bully and a cheat that 'bribes' or 'blocks' any attempt to compete with Android's Google Play store." His words: "Google pays actual and potential competitors not to compete. Literally gives them money and other things of value." He offered a figure jurors would hear often — "$360 million" — described as an alleged payment that kept Activision from opening an app store that could compete with Google Play, alongside payments to game developers not to build their own app stores or standalone app launchers like Epic did with Fortnite. The result, in his framing: a status quo where the vast, vast majority of Android app installs are from Google Play, with only a tiny sliver attributable to the Galaxy Store preinstalled on every Samsung phone.
