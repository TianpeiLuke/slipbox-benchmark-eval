# Treasury yield as the anchor for what investors pay for stocks

Treasury bonds are regarded as some of the safest investments available, so the yield they pay is the return investors can obtain without taking equity risk. That makes the Treasury yield the reference price for risk assets: when that yield rises, investors are less likely to pay high prices for stocks and other riskier investments, and equity prices fall. The Sydney Morning Herald reported on 1 October 2023 that this mechanism was a big reason the S&P 500 dropped 4.9 per cent in September 2023, dragging what had been a big gain for the year down to 11.7 per cent. In the session described, the 10-year Treasury yield returned to 4.58 per cent after dipping to 4.52 per cent, again near its highest level since 2007.
