# The reporter's gator fantasy implicates journalism too

In "The people who ruined the internet" (The Verge, 2023-11-01), the reporter's self-description of what he hoped to witness at a South Florida rooftop bar functions as the piece's central argument: the drive to dress up reality for engagement is not a defect peculiar to SEO professionals. He booked a plane ticket after hearing that a 10-foot alligator would be released at a party for the digital marketers accused of ruining the internet, conceding that a link reading "WATCH: 10-foot Gator Prepares to Maul Digital Marketers" would have gotten his click. Then he names the fuller fantasy — an SEO professional attacked by a "gigantic, prehistoric-looking reptile" in front of him, which he could "repackage such a tragedy into a sensationalized anecdote for a viral article," implying nature punished the bad guy while "assuming the ethical high ground." His stated vulnerabilities: he uses Google, wants reliable results, and enjoys "the sweet taste of revenge." The alligator measured five and a half feet, not 10 — "Classic clickbait!"
