# DOJ's Antitrust Case Against Google Centers on Default Search Engine Agreements

The Verge reported on 2023-09-26 that the U.S. Department of Justice's antitrust case against Google focuses heavily on default search engine agreements with device manufacturers. During Apple executive Eddy Cue's testimony, he confirmed that Apple receives billions annually from Google for making Google Search the default on iOS devices. The DOJ argues these agreements create de facto dominance across platforms, preventing meaningful competition. Cue stated, "Certainly there wasn't a valid alternative to Google at the time," and emphasized that Apple chose Google based on product quality rather than financial incentives. However, internal documents revealed Apple's concerns about Google's privacy policies, contradicting claims that the partnership prioritizes user privacy.
