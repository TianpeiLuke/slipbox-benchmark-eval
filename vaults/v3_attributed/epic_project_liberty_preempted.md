# Epic's Project Liberty Mention Undercuts Google's Bad Faith Argument

The Verge reported on 2023-11-07 that Epic's own mention of Project Liberty in its opening statement blunts Google's attempt to use the plan as evidence of bad faith, since the fact was disclosed rather than revealed. During the Epic v. Google trial opening arguments, Epic attorney Gary Bornstein accused Google of anti-competitive behavior including paying developers not to compete, such as an alleged $360 million payment to prevent Activision from opening its own app store. However, Epic had previously mentioned Project Liberty in its own opening, which undermines Google's strategy of portraying Epic's disclosures as selective revelations designed to paint Google as a monopolist.
