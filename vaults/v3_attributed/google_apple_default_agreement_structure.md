# Google-Apple Search Deal Mutual Obligations

TechCrunch reported on 2023-10-31 that under the Google-Apple search agreement running since 2003, Google pays Apple approximately $18 billion annually to be the default search engine on Apple devices. "Google agreed not to promote Chrome to Safari users" as part of this arrangement, which prevents Apple from switching to competing browser engines or offering users browser choice during device setup. The agreement also blocked Google from using banners, pop-ups, or other promotional methods within its apps to encourage Safari users to switch to Chrome. This mutual obligation has kept Apple dependent on Google's search payments while preventing Google from promoting its own browser on iOS devices, maintaining the status quo that benefits both companies financially.
