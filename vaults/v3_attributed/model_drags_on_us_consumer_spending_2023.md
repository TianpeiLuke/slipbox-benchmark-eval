# US Consumer Spending Growth Declines Due to Energy Prices, Student Loans, and Falling Incomes

The Sydney Morning Herald reported on 2023-10-01 that US consumer spending growth is being depressed by three concurrent factors: rising energy prices, resumed student-loan repayments, and declining real disposable incomes since June. Brian Jacobsen, chief economist at Annex Wealth Management, stated: "Higher energy prices, student loan debt repayments and real disposable incomes that have been on a declining trajectory since June doesn't bode well." Oil prices reached their highest level in over a year, with US crude settling at $90.79 per barrel despite a slight decline from recent peaks. The resumption of student loan repayments is diverting consumer funds away from discretionary spending, while real disposable incomes have been falling since June, creating additional pressure on consumer purchasing power.
