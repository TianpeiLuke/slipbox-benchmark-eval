# Google's 2007 Internal Research Showed Default Search Placement Directly Drives Query Volume

TechCrunch reported on 2023-10-31 that Google's internal research from 2007 demonstrated a direct causal relationship between default search placement and query volume. According to testimony in the Google antitrust case, a 2007 email from a Google product strategy meeting stated: "when people changed their browser homepage to Google, they did 15% more Google searches. When they switched away, their Google searches dropped 27%." This internal data shows that homepage market share directly influences search market share, with Google's VP Nitin arguing that focusing on homepage placement was "one of the most effective things we can do to make gains in search market share."
