# Epic's Monopoly Argument Against Google Centers on Higher Consumer App Prices

The Verge reported on 2023-11-07 that Epic Games' antitrust case against Google hinges on proving Google's Android app store constitutes an unlawful monopoly that leads to higher consumer prices. Epic's lead attorney Gary Bornstein argued that Google maintains its dominance by "bribing" or "blocking" competitors, resulting in most Android app installs coming from Google Play. Epic claims Google's 70 percent app store margins on $12 billion annual revenue demonstrate monopolistic behavior. The company alleges Google pays developers not to build competing app stores, citing a $360 million payment to prevent Activision from launching its own store. Epic's earlier legal filings suggested consumers pay more for apps due to Google's monopoly power rather than competitive market forces, leading to higher app store and payment processing fees than would exist with more competition.
