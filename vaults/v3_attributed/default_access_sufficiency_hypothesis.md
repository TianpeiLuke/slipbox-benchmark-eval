# Google Antitrust Trial Hypothesis on Default Placement vs. Product Quality

TechCrunch reported on 2023-10-31 that a central hypothesis in the Google antitrust case is that default search engine placement, rather than product quality, determines market dominance. The government's counterfactual proposition is that "a search engine with comparable product quality and equal access to paid default placement would reach competitive scale," which would be falsified if a rival given such placement failed to grow market share. This hypothesis challenges Google's defense that its $26.3 billion annual payments to be default search engine across platforms including Apple, Samsung and Mozilla are justified because consumers prefer Google's superior product quality.
