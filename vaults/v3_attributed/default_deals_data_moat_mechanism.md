# DOJ's Antitrust Theory Against Google Centers on Platform Defaults and Exclusive Deals

TechCrunch reported on 2023-11-13 that the Justice Department's antitrust case against Google centers on how the company's platform ownership and partner deals create monopoly power. The government argues that Google's default placements and exclusive distribution agreements suppress rival search engines, which prevents competitors from accessing the query data needed to improve their services and attract users. According to the DOJ, "Google uses its platforms and deals with partners to block out any competition in search or advertising, thus hindering competitors from accessing the data they'd need to improve their products." The case could force Google to share APIs with third-party developers and end anticompetitive exclusive deals with device manufacturers.
