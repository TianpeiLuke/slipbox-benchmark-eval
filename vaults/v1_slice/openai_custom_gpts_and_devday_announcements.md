# GPTs: OpenAI's Custom Chatbots And The Dev Day Announcements

GPTs are OpenAI's tool for building custom conversational, action-taking AI systems powered by OpenAI's models, including the models underpinning ChatGPT. They were announced at OpenAI's first-ever developer conference, held Monday, November 6, 2023, where the Microsoft-backed AI startup announced a host of new products — the more notable being tools to create custom "GPTs" (that is, domain-specific chatbots), new text-to-speech models, an API for the text-to-image model DALL-E 3, and an improved version of OpenAI's flagship model GPT-4, called GPT-4 Turbo.

What distinguishes a GPT from a prompt is that the knowledge is built in. With GPTs, a user can train a model on a cookbook collection, for example, so that it can answer questions about ingredients for a specific recipe; or they can give a model their company's proprietary codebases so that developers can check their style or generate code in line with best practices. Some of the initial GPTs, all created by OpenAI, include a Gen Z meme translator, a coloring book and sticker creator, a data visualizer, a board game explainer and a creative writing coach. ChatGPT can accomplish these tasks given carefully engineered prompts and foreknowledge, but purpose-built GPTs drastically simplify things — and might just kill the cottage industry that emerged around creating and editing prompts to feed to ChatGPT.

The consequence TechCrunch draws for ChatGPT itself is a change of role: with GPTs, ChatGPT could become more a gateway to a broader ecosystem of AI-powered chatbots than the end-all-be-all. GPTs introduce a level of personalization far beyond that ChatGPT offers today, and — once OpenAI sorts out its capacity issues — TechCrunch expects an explosion of creativity there. Will ChatGPT be as visible as it once was after GPTs flood the marketplace? Perhaps not; but it will not go away, it will simply adapt and evolve, no doubt in ways not even its creators can anticipate.
