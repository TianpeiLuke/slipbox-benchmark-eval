# Venture Capital

## Definition

In the corpus, venture capital (VC) names the money that specialist VC firms — and the individual "venture capitalists" and partners who run them — put into privately held startups, tracked through a sequence of named funding rounds (seed, pre-Series A, Series A through E and beyond) from Thiel's early backing of DeepMind through FTX's $400 million Series C.

## Context

The corpus shows venture capital operating at both ends of the startup lifecycle. Early-stage examples include Thiel's venture capital firm putting £1.4 million into the not-yet-named DeepMind, and Motier Ventures backing Mistral AI's €105 million seed round, Poolside's separate $126 million seed round, and Index Ventures leading (with Iconiq and Motier Ventures also participating in) Adaptive's seed round at a $100 million valuation — all part of "VC firms fighting for the most competitive funding rounds" in the French AI boom. Later-stage venture capital appears in FTX's $400 million Series C round that pushed its valuation to $32 billion, and in Rainforest's Accel-led $8.5 million seed round alongside Bolt's earlier $355 million Series E. The corpus also treats venture capital as an industry with its own geography and macro trends: Sand Hill Road as the physical center of Silicon Valley VC, Sequoia Capital as a "high-powered venture capital firm" whose former partner was among the witnesses lined up to testify in the FTX trial, Israeli startups drawing $95 billion in venture capital over the prior five years even as funding had already fallen 73% year-over-year in the first half of 2023 — a decline reported in July, before the October Hamas attacks further disrupted the sector — and US-headquartered VC investment into China collapsing from $45.4 billion to $14.5 billion between 2021 and 2022 amid Beijing's tech crackdowns and Washington's new restrictions on outbound U.S. investment in Chinese tech.

## Key Characteristics

- **Equity-for-capital exchange with startups** — VCs and VC firms fund privately held companies in return for an ownership stake, as with Thiel's firm taking a position in DeepMind and Accel leading Rainforest's seed round.
- **Staged by named rounds** — investment is tranched into seed, pre-Series A, and Series A/B/C/D rounds that track a company's maturity, illustrated by FTX's Series C, StarNews Mobile's pre-Series A, and Clearco's Series D.
- **Concentrated in identifiable firms and hubs** — a small set of named firms (Sequoia Capital, Accel, Index Ventures, Motier Ventures, Janngo Capital) and physical hubs (Sand Hill Road) recur as the deal-making core of the industry.
- **Sensitive to macro and geopolitical conditions** — VC deployment can swing sharply with regulatory crackdowns and geopolitics, as shown by the drop in US VC activity in China and the 73% year-over-year decline in Israeli startup funding reported in mid-2023, before the October Hamas attacks further disrupted the sector.
- **Distinct from the startups it funds** — the corpus consistently separates the VC firm/investor role (Sequoia, Accel, Janngo Capital, UpWest) from the founder/startup role it capitalizes, even when the same article covers both.

## Background (external)

More broadly, and outside what this corpus documents, venture capital is generally defined as a form of private-equity financing supplied by specialized firms or funds to startup, early-stage, and emerging companies judged to have high growth potential, in exchange for equity or an ownership stake (Wikipedia, "Venture capital," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Venture_capital).
