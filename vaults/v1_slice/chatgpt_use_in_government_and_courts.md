# ChatGPT Use In Government And Courts

Governments and courts have both adopted ChatGPT and moved to police it: the U.K. Judicial Office issued guidance permitting judges to use ChatGPT and other AI tools to write legal rulings and perform court duties, laying out ways to use AI responsibly in the courts including being aware of potential bias and upholding privacy.

Other cases show the same tension. City lawmakers in Brazil enacted a piece of legislation written entirely by ChatGPT without knowing it; weeks after the bill passed, Porto Alegre councilman Ramiro Rosário admitted he had used ChatGPT to write the proposal and had not told fellow council members until after the fact. In South Africa, following speculation, social media users fed portions of President Cyril Ramaphosa's November 21 speech in Johannesburg through AI detectors, alleging parts of it may have been written with ChatGPT; South African presidency spokesperson Vincent Magwenya refuted the claims and local officials are investigating. Running the other way, a Texas federal judge added a requirement that any attorney appearing in his court must attest that "no portion of the filing was drafted by generative artificial intelligence," or, if it was, that it was checked "by a human being."
