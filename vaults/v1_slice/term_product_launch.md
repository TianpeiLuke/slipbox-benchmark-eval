# Product Launch

## Definition

Across this corpus, "launch" (and "launched") is used to describe a company unveiling, announcing, or first shipping a new product, feature, or device — the corpus does not itself define the term, but consistently applies it to this announce-and/or-ship moment (see Context).

## Background (external)

ProductPlan's product-management glossary defines a product launch as "a business's planned and coordinated effort to debut a new product to the market and make that product generally available for purchase," noting that launches also build anticipation, gather early-adopter feedback, and generate momentum beyond simply enabling sales (ProductPlan, accessed 2026-09-02, https://www.productplan.com/glossary/product-launch/).

## Context

Across the corpus, launches recur as the mechanism by which companies move a product from development into the market, and the term covers a spectrum from a single press event to a multi-year rollout. Google's annual hardware event unveiled the Pixel 8 and Pixel 8 Pro alongside the Pixel Fold, Android 14, Pixel Buds and Pixel Watch 2, showing that a single launch event can bundle several products at once. Apple's "Scary Fast" event was anticipated as the venue for unveiling M3-powered MacBook Pros, a refreshed iMac, and new USB-C accessories, illustrating that launches are often previewed and rumored before the event itself. Amazon's "Explore with Alexa" was "first announced in September" and only became available afterward, alongside the Echo Pop Kids speakers being "available for purchase" at $49.99 — showing the announce-then-ship pattern explicitly. OpenAI's ChatGPT similarly moved through staged launches: an initial November 2022 release, then a paid ChatGPT Plus tier and plug-in/API support unveiled in March 2023, then further features (Enterprise, browsing, voice, GPTs) rolled out through the rest of the year. Valve's Steam Deck OLED was announced at a press event ahead of its device launch, with press being told units would be "available to order" on a specific date and begin shipping shortly after. Keep Labs' medication-storage device took years between its 2019 preorder campaign and "this week started shipping its first product to consumers," showing a launch can be delayed substantially between announcement and actual availability.

## Key Characteristics

- **Two-stage structure** — an "unveiling" or announcement stage (often at a press or hardware event) is followed by a separate "available for purchase" or shipping stage, and the gap between them can range from days to years, as with Keep Labs' multi-year gap between its 2019 preorder campaign and 2023 shipping date.
- **Bundling** — a single launch event frequently introduces multiple products or features together, as Google did with the Pixel 8 line, Android 14, and several accessories in one hardware event.
- **Pre-launch anticipation** — launches are frequently preceded by rumors and analyst predictions about what will be unveiled, as with the expected Apple "Scary Fast" event coverage of M3 chips and MacBook Pro refreshes before the event occurred.
- **Staged/iterative rollout** — for software products like ChatGPT, "launch" is not a single moment but a sequence of feature releases (Plus tier, plugins, Enterprise, voice, GPTs) unveiled over many months following the initial debut.
- **Concrete availability signals** — corpus reporting marks a launch's completion with specific, checkable facts: a price, an order date, or a shipping date, e.g. the Steam Deck OLED being "available to order on November 16th" with shipping "as orders start to roll in," or the Echo Pop Kids being "available for purchase, starting at $49.99."
