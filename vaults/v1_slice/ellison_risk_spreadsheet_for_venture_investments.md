# Ellison's Risk Spreadsheet For Venture Investments

Caroline Ellison testified that she prepared a spreadsheet at Bankman-Fried's request in the fall of 2021 and shared it with him, analysing the risk to Alameda of repaying its loans if they were abruptly recalled by its lender Genesis. Bankman-Fried wanted to use $3 billion for venture investments, so Ellison ballparked what that would do to Alameda's risk: in the as-is scenario she put the chance they would not be able to meet the loan recalls at 30 percent, and if Bankman-Fried spent $3 billion on investments, the chance rose to 100 percent — even with FTX customer funds.

What made this the worst of her testimony, in The Verge's account of the October 10th, 2023 proceedings, was not the math, which seemed pretty arbitrary, but the assumption underneath it: Ellison's calculations assumed Alameda could borrow $1.8 billion in normal dollars and $1.5 billion in crypto from FTX, and the spreadsheet made this explicit with a row labeled "FTX borrows," which Ellison said were customer funds. The background condition was that Alameda was also getting loans from outside lenders such as Genesis, because when FTX started there were not a lot of customer funds to borrow.

The day ended with a document shared between Ellison and Bankman-Fried, his comments appearing in bubbles along the main text. Ellison had written that she was worried about "both actual leverage and presenting on our balance sheet." Bankman-Fried responded with a note: "Yup, and could also get worse."
