# The Hypocrisy Argument: Google Became The Monopoly It Denounced

The DOJ's argument, pressed in cross-examination by Bellshaw, is that Google has become the monopoly it denounced years ago — that the company objected to a rival's search default when it was the one being shut out, and now defends the same arrangement when it is the beneficiary.

The grounds are Google's own 2005 letter. Cast your memories back to 2005, when Microsoft's Internet Explorer became the default search engine: Google's legal chief David Drummond sent Microsoft an angry letter saying that making Internet Explorer the search default was anticompetitive. After establishing that Google understands the inherent value of defaults, Bellshaw brought up Drummond's letter precisely to establish the hypocrisy of Google today. The letter declared that problems with a default setting are made worse by how changes to defaults are handled, and that most end users "do not change defaults."

The warrant is that the letter's reasoning has not changed hands with the market position. These are exactly the arguments that other search engine companies, like DuckDuckGo, Brave or Microsoft's Bing, make when they accuse Google of being anticompetitive by making deals with Apple and others — so if Drummond's reasoning was sound in 2005, it convicts Google now. The DOJ doubled down on this in the trial.

The same cross-examination pressed a second consistency question, on the litigation hold. Pichai said that he took action against the history-off default for chat in February to comply with the DOJ's litigation hold, according to CNBC. Bellshaw then pulled up a message exchange in 2021 where Pichai asked for history to be turned off in a group chat. Pichai responded that he wanted to discuss a personnel matter and that the subject had nothing to do with the litigation hold, which he said he takes great care to comply with.
