# Shadowbanning

## Definition

Shadowbanning, as TechCrunch describes it, is when a platform "shadowbans, or limits the visibility of" a user's content without the platform notifying the user that anything has been restricted, so the user only discovers it by noticing a drop in views or reach.

## Context

In the corpus the term names the central accusation in the October 2023 controversy over Meta's handling of Palestine-related content: Instagram users accused Meta of shadowbanning their Stories about Palestine, and TechCrunch frames the belief that Meta "shadowbans, or limits the visibility of," such content as longstanding rather than new to that month.
The same allegation is cited beyond Meta's own platforms as a driver of platform choice: users turned to Snapchat's Snap Map for firsthand Gaza content specifically because accusations of shadowbanning Palestinian content on Instagram and Facebook had eroded their trust in those platforms.
Meta's official response did not deny that reach had dropped; it denied the mechanism, attributing the reduced Story reach specifically to a global, content-neutral bug in Stories that re-shared Reels or Feed posts, and separately attributed a different global bug — one that had prevented some users from going live — to technical error rather than deliberate suppression tied to subject matter.

## Key Characteristics

- **Covert, not disclosed** — the defining feature reporters and users pointed to is that visibility drops silently, with no notification to the affected account; Bella Hadid described being "immediately shadowbanned" and her Story views dropping by "almost 1 million" with no explanation from the platform.
- **Detected by comparison, not confirmation** — users inferred shadowbanning by comparing engagement across their own posts (e.g., a user whose Stories fell from "100s of views to zero or a handful" and rebounded only after posting unrelated content like a cat photo), since the platform provided no direct evidence of restriction.
- **Contested mechanism** — Meta disputed that the effect was shadowbanning-by-design, attributing the reduced Story reach to a bug that "affected accounts equally around the globe" and "had nothing to do with the subject matter of the content," rather than confirming intentional visibility limits.
- **Trust-eroding beyond the platform itself** — the accusation pushed users toward alternative platforms (Snap Map) even though the accused conduct occurred on Instagram and Facebook, showing shadowbanning claims can shift audience behavior across platforms, not just within one.
