# Epic Games v. Google Goes To Trial On November 6, 2023

Epic Games, the maker of Fortnite, went to trial with Google on November 6, 2023, hoping to convince a jury that Google engages in anticompetitive behavior with regard to its Android app store, Google Play, and its commission structure. Match had settled its own antitrust lawsuit with Google the week before, but Epic's case proceeded.

This case differs slightly from Epic's battle with Apple on the same topic, because this time Epic cannot allege there is no other way to load apps onto Android devices, as it could with Apple: Android does allow for sideloading apps. Epic will suggest Google makes that process cumbersome, but the bulk of its arguments will center on what it believes are anticompetitive agreements between Google and device manufacturers and developers.

The precedent Epic's case relies on is a Microsoft case in which courts found Microsoft to have a monopoly over the operating system and to be abusing it, making it hard for users to download alternative browsers, like Netscape, on Windows devices. To make its case, Epic plans to bring claims under Sections 1 and 2 of U.S. antitrust law, the Sherman Act, as well as California's anticompetitive laws, the Cartwright Act and the Unfair Competition Law. It will attempt to argue that Google restrains competition within two separate markets: the distribution of apps to Android users, and the market for payment processing solutions for content inside Android apps.
