# Class Action Settlement

## Definition

A class action settlement is an agreement that resolves a class-action lawsuit before (or instead of) a full trial verdict, typically trading a payment and/or changed business practices from the defendant for an end to the collective claim, in place of the class-wide monetary and injunctive relief a plaintiff would otherwise seek at trial.

## Context

The corpus shows the pattern most clearly in the Epic v. Google app store litigation: Google settled separately with all 50 U.S. state attorneys general and a number of consumers who had brought parallel antitrust claims, and reached a last-minute settlement with Match Group as well, leaving Epic Games as the only original plaintiff still pursuing trial.
Match's settlement let it take advantage of Google's "User Choice Billing" program — a reduced-commission alternative payment option — rather than continuing to litigate; Epic's CEO Tim Sweeney called Match "an awesome partner" and said he hoped Match "got out of their settlement what they needed to get for their business," contrasting Match's choice to settle with Epic's decision to keep fighting because it viewed cash settlements as insufficient while Google's "Google tax" remained in place.
The Helena World Chronicle class action against Google and Alphabet illustrates what a plaintiff seeks when a settlement has not (yet) been reached: the suit asks for damages plus an injunction requiring Google to obtain publisher consent before using their data to train AI products, showing the class-wide monetary-and-injunctive relief that a settlement would otherwise substitute for.
The corpus also shows settlements shaping corporate behavior after the fact: Apple's earlier keyboard class-action settlement is cited as a factor that "tipped the scales" toward redesigning the MacBook Pro keyboard and dropping the Touch Bar.

## Key Characteristics

- **Substitutes for trial** — a settlement ends the case without a jury verdict; in the Google app-store litigation, settling with the state AGs, consumers, and Match Group left only Epic to actually go to trial.
- **Terms can stay undisclosed** — the corpus notes it was "not clear" what the AG/consumer settlement included, and Google would not say whether it had offered Epic a settlement.
- **Can substitute non-cash remedies for damages** — Match's settlement gave it access to a reduced-commission billing option rather than (or alongside) a cash payment, illustrating that settlements can trade behavioral/business-practice changes for dropped claims.
- **Not universally accepted by co-plaintiffs** — Epic explicitly declined to settle on cash terms alone, showing settlement is a choice each plaintiff or plaintiff group makes independently even within the same litigation.
- **Downstream business impact** — Apple's keyboard settlement is presented as a cost that influenced a later product redesign decision, showing settlements can have effects beyond the parties strictly bound by them.
