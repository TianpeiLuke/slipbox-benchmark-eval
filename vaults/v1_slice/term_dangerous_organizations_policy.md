# Dangerous Organizations and Individuals Policy

The Dangerous Organizations and Individuals policy is the named enforcement category Meta cites when it removes content tied to groups that governments designate as terrorist organizations, most directly Hamas.
In the corpus its practical trigger is Hamas: content praising or supporting Hamas, which governs Gaza and is designated a terrorist organization by the United States and the European Union, is expressly forbidden on Meta's platforms.
Meta tracks enforcement of this policy as its own category, separate from its Violence and Incitement policy, reporting that it removed "seven times as many pieces of content on a daily basis for violating our Dangerous Organizations and Individuals policy in Hebrew and Arabic alone" in the three days after October 7, 2023, compared with the two months prior.

## Context

In the corpus the policy is the mechanism through which Meta enforces against Hamas-related content during the Israel-Hamas war, and it is the recurring flashpoint in disputes over whether that enforcement is applied evenly.
Meta invokes the policy to justify removing content that praises or supports Hamas — Hamas being "designated as a terrorist organization by the United States and the European Union" — and Instagram separately cited a related "Community Guidelines against violence or dangerous organizations" when it denied that a specific violent, threatening comment violated its rules, illustrating that the same policy family both blocks and, in users' eyes, under-enforces depending on the content.
The designation logic recurs beyond Meta's own platform actions: the corpus notes that Hamas is "considered a terrorist organization by the U.K., U.S. and other countries" in the context of the Web Summit/Cosgrave controversy, and that UNRWA operates "alongside Hamas, which Israel, the United States and other countries designate as a terrorist organization" in Gaza — showing the same underlying designation of Hamas driving both platform moderation policy and geopolitical characterization of the region's governing party.

## Key Characteristics

- **Named, tracked enforcement category** — Meta reports removal volumes specifically "for violating our Dangerous Organizations and Individuals policy," distinct from its Violence and Incitement policy, letting the company cite a sevenfold daily increase in Hebrew/Arabic enforcement after October 7.
- **Anchored to external terrorist designations** — the policy's practical trigger in the corpus is content supporting Hamas, an organization the U.S., EU and U.K. designate as a terrorist group; the platform rule imports government designations rather than defining "dangerous" independently.
- **Contested in application** — Palestinian users and digital-rights advocates argue the policy (and its Community Guidelines "violence or dangerous organizations" language) is enforced inconsistently, citing a case where a violent, threatening comment was ruled not to violate it, and past instances where the Al-Aqsa Mosque hashtag was blocked after being "mistaken for a terrorist organization."
- **Localized to Arabic and Hebrew content** — the volume Meta cites for this policy is scoped specifically to Hebrew and Arabic content, not platform-wide, reflecting the languages most implicated in Israel-Hamas war content moderation.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, Meta's own Community Standards describe the Dangerous Organizations and Individuals policy more broadly than the Hamas-specific enforcement described above: it generally bars praise, substantive support, and representation of any individual or organization that Meta designates as dangerous, a designation list that spans terrorist organizations, hate organizations, criminal organizations, and other violence-inducing entities, not just Hamas.
