# Google's Knowledge Graph And Featured Snippets

As described in the Helena World Chronicle complaint, Google's Knowledge Graph is a database, launched in May 2012, from which Google draws a summary of content that it displays as a "Knowledge Panel" to the right of search results when a user searches for information on a topic. The complaint characterises it as one of Google's older question-and-answer technologies and treats it as part of the alleged harm to publishers, alongside newer AI technologies.

The complaint states that "Google compiled this massive database by extracting information from Publishers' websites — what Google calls 'materials shared across the web' — and from 'open source and licensed databases.'" By 2020, the Knowledge Graph had grown to 500 billion facts about 5 billion entities, but the complaint alleges that much of the "collective intelligence" Google tapped into was content "misappropriated from Publishers."

Featured Snippets are a distinct Google technology in which Google algorithmically extracts answers from webpages; the complaint cites them too, on the grounds that they shift traffic away from publishers' websites.
