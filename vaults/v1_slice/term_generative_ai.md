# Generative AI

## Definition

In the corpus, "generative AI" is the umbrella label applied to the 2023 AI boom's content-producing systems — OpenAI's ChatGPT (built on GPT-3.5/GPT-4) and Adobe's Firefly image and vector-graphics models are the flagship examples named — rather than a term the corpus itself formally defines.

## Context

The corpus treats "generative AI" as the umbrella term for the mainstream AI boom of 2023, distinct from the EU AI Act's regulatory term "general purpose AI" (GPAI), which lawmakers deliberately chose as a more technology-neutral label for "the most powerful models behind the current boom in generative AI tools" so the law would not be pinned to one architecture like transformers (doc_0525).
OpenAI built ChatGPT specifically "to gather more data on how people use and interact with generative AI," and its free, chat-based interface is credited with "taking generative AI mainstream" as a consumer product rather than a research artifact (doc_0047).
Enterprise adoption is depicted as cautious relative to vendor hype: a Morgan Stanley survey found 56% of CIOs said generative AI was affecting investment priorities, but only 4% had launched significant projects, most still in evaluation or proof-of-concept (doc_0160).
The technology spans well beyond chatbots — Adobe's Firefly generative AI models generate images and vector graphics for creative software (doc_0043), and Amazon executive Tye Brady discussed generative AI alongside humanoid robots and mobile manipulation as an input to robotics work (doc_0528).
The same capability that drives adoption also drives the corpus's recurring regulatory and creative-industry concerns: the EU warned that "widely accessible generative AI tools" pose disinformation risks to elections (doc_0248), and the National Music Publishers Association called generative AI "the greatest risk to the human creative class that has ever existed" in its copyright submission to the US Copyright Office (doc_0271).

## Key Characteristics

- **Illustrated by content-producing examples rather than defined outright** — the corpus shows the term through instances of new text, images, and vector graphics being produced from a prompt, illustrated by ChatGPT's text generation (doc_0048) and Firefly's image/vector generation (doc_0043).
- **Consumer breakout via chat interfaces** — ChatGPT's free, web-based chat interface is what took generative AI "mainstream," reaching the fastest adoption of any consumer app (doc_0047, doc_0048).
- **Enterprise adoption lags vendor hype** — large-company CIOs report slow, cautious rollout despite heavy vendor promotion, weighing API/SaaS costs against ROI (doc_0160).
- **Distinct from but overlapping "general purpose AI"** — EU lawmakers deliberately avoided calling the underlying models "generative AI" in law, choosing "general purpose AI" instead to stay technology-neutral rather than tied to transformer-based architectures (doc_0525).
- **Applies across domains beyond chatbots** — spans creative tools (Adobe Firefly) and robotics (Amazon's mobile manipulation work), per the corpus (doc_0043, doc_0528).
- **Focus of copyright and disinformation controversy** — named by the NMPA as a threat to the "human creative class" via unlicensed training data (doc_0271), and flagged by the EU as an election-disinformation risk requiring safeguards (doc_0248).

## Background (external)

The corpus uses "generative AI" as a familiar shorthand without formally defining it. Per IBM, generative AI "is artificial intelligence (AI) that can create original content — such as text, images, video, audio or software code — in response to a user's prompt or request," distinguishing it from AI systems built only to classify or act on existing data (IBM, *What is generative AI?*, accessed 2026-09-02).
