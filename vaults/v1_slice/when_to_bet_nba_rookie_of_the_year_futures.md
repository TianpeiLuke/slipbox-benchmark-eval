# The Timing Trade-Off In Rookie Of The Year Futures Betting

There is a fixed trade-off in when to bet NBA Rookie of the Year futures: the earlier you bet, the better the odds, but you bet without much information that could be key to an educated decision; conversely, if you wait for the data you need for an informed bet, the odds tighten and you see less of a return.

The mechanism is symmetric between bettor and book. When the lines are first released, the season has not started, so there are no statistics, trends or player news — the pre-season odds are so favorable precisely because even the oddsmakers lack the data to craft more specific lines. Once the season starts and you can see how the rookies are performing, you can gather the relevant data; but sportsbooks are gathering the same information and tighten the Rookie of the Year lines in response. So while you can better guess who will hoist the Wilt Chamberlain Trophy, the odds are tighter and your potential return reduced.

Stated as pros and cons: betting early gives an opportunity to find better value and betting odds that are fresh and unadjusted, but carries much higher risk because a player's performance, health and team dynamic can change through the season, and offers limited information. Waiting gives a lot more information for an informed bet and avoids betting on a player not remotely in contention, but reduces the opportunity to find value and the odds may not be as favorable. The range over which this holds is bounded by the individual: it is always important to take your risk tolerance into consideration while weighing the potential rewards, and no matter when you decide to wager, always gather as much information as possible before risking your money.
