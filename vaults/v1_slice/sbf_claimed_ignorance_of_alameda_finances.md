# Bankman-Fried's Claimed Ignorance Of Alameda's Finances

Testifying on October 28th, 2023, Bankman-Fried attacked the prosecution's premise that he knew about the hole in FTX by claiming he did not learn of the $8 billion liability associated with Alameda until October 2022, that he found it all by himself by looking at a computer database, and that when he found it he was "very surprised." The Verge's reporter recorded the claim with heavy scepticism.

The counter-argument extended to reinterpreting each earlier warning as something innocuous. On why Alameda served as FTX's "payment provider" for bank transfers, he said FTX could not get bank accounts right away and he anticipated it would take a year or two, so rather than wait he used Alameda: "My understanding at the time was that there were teams managing the process. At the time, I wasn't entirely sure what was happening." On Adam Yedidia's testimony about an August 2022 conversation regarding the enormous amount Alameda owed FTX, Bankman-Fried remembered it differently — Yedidia was just asking about Alameda's risk profile and he was not talking about insolvency at all. On the dramatic balcony conversation with Nishad Singh at their penthouse, he said Singh thought Alameda's liabilities had gotten too high and that FTX was spending too much on marketing, while Bankman-Fried still thought Alameda had more assets than liabilities so it was all fine, and besides, if Singh thought he would be better at marketing he could take it over — it did not have anything to do with the money Alameda owed FTX at all.
