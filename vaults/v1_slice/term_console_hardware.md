# Console Hardware

## Definition

In the corpus, "console hardware" refers to physical gaming devices — home consoles like the PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X/S, and portable or hybrid handhelds like the Nintendo Switch and Valve's Steam Deck — treated as fixed platforms that specific games are tied to, that get revised with mid-cycle refreshes, and that ship with matched first-party controllers.

## Context

In the corpus, console hardware surfaces in two recurring forms: home consoles that plug into a TV (PS5, Xbox Series X/S) and portable or hybrid handhelds (Nintendo Switch, Steam Deck) that can also dock to a screen.
Marvel's Spider-Man 2 is described as a "PS5 exclusive," tying a specific game to specific console hardware, while the "Console Wars" framing pits Xbox and PlayStation hardware against each other directly.
A macOS gaming review notes Lies of P shipping the same day on "PS5, Xbox Series X/S and Windows," using console hardware names as shorthand for distinct target platforms alongside PC.
The Steam Deck OLED coverage treats the device explicitly as console-adjacent hardware: Valve engineer Pierre-Loup Griffais describes enabling the Steam Deck to work as a "hybrid living room console" as a "side goal" of the team, and reviewers compare it functionally to the Wii U as "a portable console to use around the house."
Fan reaction to the Microsoft-Activision deal shows console hardware driving business concerns: fans worried Activision titles like Crash Bandicoot and Spyro, once PlayStation exclusives, could again become tied to a single console's hardware ecosystem.
Controllers are treated as accessories to specific console hardware families — the Xbox Wireless Controller for Xbox Series X/S, the DualSense for PS5, the Switch Pro Controller for Switch — each controller's design and compatibility defined by the console hardware it ships with.

## Key Characteristics

- **Bundled, factory-fixed components** — a console's chip, screen, and battery are packaged together at manufacture, released as a new unit rather than sold as separate upgrades; the Steam Deck OLED combines a die-shrunk AMD APU (same CPU and GPU, just more power-efficient) with a new 7.4-inch HDR OLED screen and a 25 percent larger battery.
- **Mid-cycle hardware refreshes** — manufacturers revise console hardware within a generation without launching a full sequel; the Steam Deck OLED is explicitly "not the Steam Deck 2," described as a die-shrunk chip plus display refresh similar to the Switch OLED update, and paralleled by Sony's PS4 Slim/PS5 Slim revisions.
- **Home vs. portable/hybrid form factors** — PS5 and Xbox Series X/S are TV-connected home consoles, while Switch and Steam Deck are handhelds that can also dock to a television, blurring the line between "portable" and "living room console."
- **Platform-exclusive software ties** — console hardware can gate which games are playable on it, as with Spider-Man 2's PS5 exclusivity and the fan concern that Activision titles could become exclusive to one console family again.
- **Dedicated first-party controllers** — each console hardware line ships with a matched first-party controller (Xbox Wireless Controller, DualSense, Switch Pro Controller) that defines the baseline input experience for that hardware, with third parties building compatible alternatives around it.

## Background (external)

General definition, not corpus-sourced: a video game console is an electronic device that outputs a video signal to display a game, typically played with a controller.
It is distinguished from a PC by shipping with a fixed, manufacturer-preselected set of hardware components rather than user-configurable parts, which lets console makers optimize the design around that fixed hardware and gives developers a stable, unchanging performance target.
Handhelds are consoles that build the display and controls into a single portable unit with a battery, and hybrids (like the Nintendo Switch) combine handheld and home-console use.
(Source: Wikipedia, "Video game console," Wikimedia Foundation, accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Video_game_console)
