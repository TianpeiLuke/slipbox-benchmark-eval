# Meta's Moderation During An Earlier Hamas-Israel Conflict

During an earlier Hamas-Israel conflict, the digital-rights organisation 7amleh documented more than 500 reports of Palestinian "digital rights violations" over the 13 days leading up to the ceasefire — including the removal and restriction of content, hashtags and accounts related to the conflict. The figure was recounted to TechCrunch for its 19 October 2023 report on Meta's moderation of Palestinian content.

Meta attributed some of the instances of perceived censorship to technical issues, such as one that prevented users in Palestine and Colombia from posting Instagram Stories. It attributed others to human error, such as blocking the hashtag for Al-Aqsa Mosque — the holy site where Israeli police clashed with Ramadan worshippers — because the name was mistaken for a terrorist organization. The company also blocked journalists in Gaza from WhatsApp without explanation.

Meta committed to implementing policy changes based on the report's recommendations, including updating its keywords associated with dangerous organizations, disclosing government requests to remove content, and launching a hostile speech classifier for Hebrew content. 7amleh advocacy manager Jalal Abukhater added that Meta has improved its response to harassment, at least in comparison to other social media platforms such as X (formerly Twitter): although harassment and abuse remain rampant on Instagram and Facebook, he said the company has been responsive to suspending accounts with patterns of targeting other users.

Abukhater's scope condition on all of this is that the improvements do not survive an escalation. "The balance of power is very fixed, in reality, between Israelis and Palestinians," he said. "And this is something that today is reflected heavily on platforms like Meta, even though they have human rights teams releasing reports and trying to improve upon their policies. Whenever an escalation like the one we're experiencing now happens, things just go back to zero."
