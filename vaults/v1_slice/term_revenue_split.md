# Revenue Split

## Definition

A revenue split, in the corpus, is the percentage pair — such as 70/30, 50/50, or 95/5 — by which a platform and a counterparty divide proceeds: Google's revenue share agreements (RSAs) with Android device makers and carriers, Google's 70/30 app-store split with developers, and Twitch's and Kick's subscription-revenue splits with streamers.

## Context

In the corpus the term surfaces across three unrelated disputes that share the same underlying mechanic: dividing proceeds between a platform and a counterparty. In the US v. Google antitrust trial, Google's revenue share agreements (RSAs) pay Android device makers and wireless carriers like Verizon a cut in exchange for setting Google Search and Chrome as defaults. In the Epic v. Google trial, Epic argued that Google's 70/30 app-store revenue split with developers is likely descended from an arbitrary decision Valve made with Steam two decades earlier, rather than from any principled calculation of the value Google's store provides. On the creator-platform side, Twitch's default subscription revenue split for Partners is 50/50, with a premium 70/30 tier once offered to select streamers before Twitch discontinued it in favor of ad revenue; rival platform Kick instead lures streamers away with a 95/5 split.

## Key Characteristics

- **Expressed as a ratio** — revenue splits are stated as two percentages that sum to 100, such as 70/30, 50/50, or 95/5, denoting each party's share.
- **Applies across different platform relationships** — the corpus shows the same mechanic governing device-maker default agreements (Google RSAs), app-store developer terms (Google/Epic 70/30), and creator subscription payouts (Twitch, Kick).
- **A lever platforms adjust unilaterally** — Twitch changed its subscription split from a 70/30 deal for select streamers to a 50/50 default, then introduced the Partner Plus program to restore a 70/30 rate for qualifying creators, showing the split is a platform-controlled term rather than a fixed market rate.
- **A point of leverage in competitive disputes** — Epic used the 70/30 app-store split's arbitrary Valve/Steam origin to challenge Google's justification for its rate, and Kick's more generous 95/5 split is a stated draw for streamers dissatisfied with Twitch's terms.

## Background (external)

The corpus uses "revenue split" and "revenue share" only in the specific device-maker, app-store, and creator-payout deals described above, without defining the general concept. Outside the corpus, revenue sharing is defined as the distribution of the total revenue generated by the sale of goods and services among stakeholders or contributors, distinct from profit sharing (which splits only what remains after costs) (Wikipedia, "Revenue sharing," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Revenue_sharing).
