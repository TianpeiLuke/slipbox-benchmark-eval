# Adam Yedidia's Testimony And The $8 Billion Bug

Adam Yedidia, the prosecution's second witness, testified that in June 2022 he submitted a report to Bankman-Fried on Signal showing that $8 billion in customer money held in an internal database tracking cash wired to an Alameda account called "fiat at ftx.com" was missing.

Yedidia met Bankman-Fried in college at MIT and the pair remained close friends; assuming a robotic posture on the stand, he said he worked out of FTX's Hong Kong office from January to October of 2021 and then in the Bahamas until the collapse. He referred to a group Signal thread called "People of the House," referring to Bankman-Fried's $35 million penthouse where many employees lived; on who was paying the rent, Yedidia recalled Bankman-Fried saying he "assumed it's just Alameda paying for it in the end." Yedidia said Bankman-Fried had told him, before he began working in the Bahamas in 2019, that he and Ellison had had sex, and asked whether it was a good idea for them to date — Yedidia said no, and Bankman-Fried responded that he was expecting that answer. One of Yedidia's responsibilities was fixing the bug in the code that gave Alameda preferential treatment. He said he and Bankman-Fried spoke about the missing $8 billion at the pickleball court at the resort in Nassau, Bahamas, where he asked his boss if things were OK because it "seemed like a lot of money" from FTX customers was at risk. "Sam said, we were bulletproof last year. We aren't bulletproof this year," Yedidia testified; asked when they would be bulletproof again, Bankman-Fried said he was not sure, but it might be six months to three years. Yedidia said Bankman-Fried appeared "worried or nervous," which was atypical, but that he still trusted Bankman-Fried and Ellison to "handle the situation." On cross-examination, Christian Everdell, Bankman-Fried's attorney, focused on how Yedidia was the one responsible for developing and reviewing the code.
