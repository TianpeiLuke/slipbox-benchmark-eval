# Project Liberty: Epic's Deliberate Payment Bypass

Epic is the studio behind Fortnite, the extraordinarily popular free-to-play game, which makes money by selling in-game items with its virtual currency V-Bucks; until August 13th, 2020, if a player used an Android or iOS device and installed the game through an official app store, buying V-Bucks triggered an in-app payment fee to Google or Apple — fees critics call the "Google tax" or the "Apple tax," and which Epic definitely was not a fan of.

August 13th, 2020 was a very busy day. Epic announced it was bypassing Apple and Google's app store fees, deploying a hotfix update to Fortnite without either company's knowledge that let players buy V-Bucks directly through its own payment processing option at a discount. Apple and Google almost immediately reacted by kicking Fortnite off their app stores for breaking the rules. Then Epic was ready and waiting with two lawsuits and an attack ad depicting a Fortnite hero throwing a unicorn-llama hammer into a giant screen reminiscent of Apple's famous "1984" Macintosh ad. It was a striking publicity blitz followed by a lot of slow-moving court proceedings: the Apple lawsuit went to court in 2021 and a ruling came down that September, mostly decided in Apple's favor, though both parties are waiting for the Supreme Court to potentially weigh in, while the Google one was delayed again and again.

The premeditation is documented, and Google is countersuing over it. At trial Google pointed out how Epic hatched a secret plan called "Project Liberty" to quietly update Fortnite with code to bypass app store fees, get its app kicked off Apple's and Google's app stores, and sue. It showed out-of-context quotes from internal Epic communications — phrases like "How do we not look like the bad guys?", "Just planting the nefarious seed now" and "I mean everything we're attempting is technically a violation of Google's policy, right?" — to suggest Epic knew it was breaking bad at the time. An email from Epic CEO Tim Sweeney reads: "Here is our official plan for communicating with Google about bypassing the Google Play Store: SAY NOTHING TILL IT SHIPS."

Epic did not try to hide it in court. Epic mentioned Project Liberty in its own opening statement, so by the time Google raised it, it had already been an hour since Epic admitted it intentionally broke Google's rules. "Epic decided to stand up because that's what you do to a bully," Bornstein told the jury.
