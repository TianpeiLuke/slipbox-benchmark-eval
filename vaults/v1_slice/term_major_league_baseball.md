# Major League Baseball (MLB)

## Definition

Major League Baseball (MLB) is the professional baseball league that the corpus treats as both a competitive structure of clubs and a governing body, exercising Commissioner-led authority to investigate and discipline rule violations while also running the season, postseason, and free-agent market that anchor the corpus's baseball coverage.

## Context

The corpus treats MLB as both the competitive league — teams, ballparks, postseason races, trades and free agency — and the governing body that polices it. The Houston Astros sign-stealing scandal shows MLB acting as regulator: the league investigated "68 people, including 23 Astros players," suspended general manager Jeff Luhnow and manager A.J. Hinch, fined the Astros $5 million (the maximum allowed), and stripped draft picks, all under Commissioner Robert Manfred, while declining to vacate the team's 2017 World Series title. The 2023 postseason coverage frames MLB as the league umbrella under which the annual Wild Card Series and Division Series are played across named ballparks (e.g., Minute Maid Park, Dodger Stadium, Citizens Bank Park). The 2023–24 offseason coverage — the Winter Meetings, the blockbuster Shohei Ohtani $700 million deal with the Dodgers and Juan Soto's trade to the Yankees — shows MLB's free-agency and trade system, where players move between the league's clubs. MLB also appears as a media and sponsorship platform: the collapsed FTX had its name "branded on Major League Baseball umpires' polos," and MLB Network figures (e.g., Alanna Rizzo, Tony Petitti) surface in coverage of on- and off-field controversies.

## Key Characteristics

- **League-and-regulator duality** — MLB is simultaneously the competitive structure of clubs and games and the disciplinary authority that investigates and punishes rule violations, as in the Astros case.
- **Commissioner-led governance** — Robert Manfred, as Commissioner, held final say on sanctions and publicly defended not stripping the Astros' World Series title, calling that step "difficult and impractical."
- **Structured season format** — clubs progress from a regular season into Wild Card and Division Series rounds before the World Series, the Fall Classic that decides the league champion.
- **Active free-agency and trade market** — MLB's offseason, including Winter Meetings, is where marquee free agents (Ohtani) and trade chips (Soto) change teams, shaping competitive balance for the following season.
- **Commercial visibility** — MLB's brand and personnel (umpire uniforms, MLB Network) are used by sponsors and broadcasters as evidence of the league's national reach, as seen with FTX's now-collapsed sponsorship deal.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, Major League Baseball is documented as the highest level of professional baseball in North America: a league of 30 clubs (29 in the United States, 1 in Canada) split between the American League (formed 1901) and National League (formed 1876), which operated as separate legal entities until they merged into a single organization led by the Commissioner of Baseball in 2000.

Each club plays a 162-game regular season, and the champions of the two leagues meet in the World Series, a best-of-seven series first played in 1903.
