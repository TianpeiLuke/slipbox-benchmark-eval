# Steam Deck OLED Display Upgrade

The new Steam Deck's main upgrade is its OLED screen, which replaces the original LCD and, crucially, has native support for HDR and faster frame rates. The OLED also has smaller bezels, making the display larger at 7.4 inches compared with the original 7-inch screen: the original Steam Deck had a 7-inch 800p LCD running at 60Hz, and the new model squeezes a 7.4-inch OLED screen into the same space at the same resolution but with a refresh rate of up to 90Hz.

The HDR OLED display looks fantastic, featuring a wider P3 color gamut — covering 110 percent of the P3 color space — pure blacks and a peak brightness of 1,000 nits, compared with the original max of around 400 nits. In action, this means the screen's bezels meld with the black of any game, creating a more polished frame for retro aspect ratios and providing room for higher-contrast colors. The screen on the new model doesn't support variable refresh rates (VRR), but it now tops out at 90Hz where the original could only hit 60Hz, and like all OLED screens it has near-instantaneous response times. As before, anti-glare etched glass is available, but only on the premium model. Engadget's verdict on the panel: the updated display is smooth, crisp and bright, like a perfect lemon tart.
