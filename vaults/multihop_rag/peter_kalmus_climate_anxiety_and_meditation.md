# Peter Kalmus, Climate Anxiety and Meditation

Climate scientist Peter Kalmus strives for two hours of meditation a day to keep his climate anxiety at bay, telling NBC News that "When I'm meditating, I don't feel anxious," and that otherwise the anxiety is "completely overwhelming."

The reporter set the scene in Belfast, Maine, on a picture-perfect August evening during the seaside town's "Summer Nights" concert series: hundreds of picnickers sprawled across a lawn speckled with colourful blankets, yachts bobbing in the bay, guitars blaring, children wrestling and dancers twirling across a concert lawn in the golden glow of a dying summer night. Off to the side, Kalmus sat alone, eyes closed, toes tucked beneath his body, catching an occasional side-eye from onlookers. Where many people avoid thinking too deeply about the worst scenarios of climate change, he is fixated on them. "It's kind of weird and lonely," he said. "People don't want to talk about this stuff at parties."
