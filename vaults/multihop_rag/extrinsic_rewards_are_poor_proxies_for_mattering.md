# Titles, Money And Awards Are Poor Proxies For Mattering

Against the common answer to "how do you know if you've achieved your potential" — that we look to extrinsic rewards such as titles, money and awards as the metrics of success — Adam Grant attacks the premise that those markers measure what they claim to. "They are status symbols that we use to broadcast to others and to ourselves that we matter and I think they are really poor proxies for mattering," he says. His counter-evidence is ordinary experience: "I think we have all been in situations where we can admire people not for what they've achieved but for who they've become or what they've contributed to others."

What he substitutes for those metrics is the process he credits in his own development. In both writing and diving he focused on his own growth and progress and relied on the guidance of credible mentors — in his case his diving coach, who was ultimately his greatest champion, and teachers who knew him well and believed in his capacity to be a great writer.
