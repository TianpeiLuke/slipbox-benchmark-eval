# Heat Stress Correlation with Census Income and Race

Correlating the mapped heat indices with census data revealed that low-income neighbourhoods and residents of colour experienced higher temperatures and greater humidity, which together amplify heat stress, while census tracts with higher incomes suffered less heat stress.

Heat stress was also generally higher in areas with a greater percentage of Black and African American residents. A figure credited to TC Chakraborty of PNNL shows Baltimore census areas coloured by heat index and by the percentage of Black residents, and the comparison indicates that where a greater proportion of residents are Black, heat stress is higher.
