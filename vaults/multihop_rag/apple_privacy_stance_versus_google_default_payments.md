# Apple's Privacy Stance Versus Google's Default Payments

Meagan Bellshaw's privacy line of questioning attacked the premise that Apple chose Google because Google had the best product: if Apple genuinely regards Google as bad on privacy, then something other than product quality must explain why Apple gives Google a central place on its platform.

Bellshaw asked Cue whether Apple believes user privacy is important, to which he said, "Absolutely." She then showed a series of emails and slides in which Cue and Apple railed against Google's privacy policies, and Cue readily agreed with them: "We've always thought we had better privacy than Google." He said one provision of the ISA was that Google had to allow people to search without logging in, and that Apple has done things in Safari and around its platforms to make it harder for Google or anyone else to track users. Bellshaw never quite said it, but The Verge read the DOJ's implication as this: Google is a privacy menace anathema to everything Apple believes is important to its users, yet Apple gives it that central place because Google pays so handsomely. She then asked Cue to review some of Apple's financial filings and whether it is true that the ISA represents a significant portion of Apple's profits. Cue's rebuttal was that this is not how Apple looks at it, because the framing doesn't account for all the work Apple did to make its platform so appealing that an agreement like this could work as well as it does.
