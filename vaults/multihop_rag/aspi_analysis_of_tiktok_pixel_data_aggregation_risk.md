# ASPI's Analysis Of TikTok Pixel Data Aggregation Risk

Researchers at the Australian Strategic Policy Institute argue that the danger of TikTok's tracking pixel lies less in the type of data collected — which resembles what US tech giants gather — than in how that data can be aggregated and in who can ultimately reach it.

Jocelinn Kang, a technical specialist at the institute, said data from a tracking pixel could be aggregated across websites, apps and social media platforms, and that pixel tracking could identify users through their "browser fingerprint," a combination of their IP address, browser and system details. The linking gets sharper as identifiers accumulate: "However, when more identifying data such as email and phone number is associated with a user, their web activity can be better linked," Kang said.

Strategic Policy Institute researcher Samantha Hoffman said the data collected by TikTok's pixel was similar to that of US-based tech giants Google and Meta, but that the difference was "the intent." Advertising data had "incredible propaganda value," she said, and the warrant joining that to the risk is state access: "If you think about that, plus the access that TikTok is required to give the Chinese government, that's the problem." She notes TikTok itself made that access explicit — in November 2022 the company changed its privacy policy to state clearly that user data can be accessed by some employees from across the world, including China. "They talk about how even data collected overseas can be used by the company and its partners, and would be kept private unless security organisations make demands of it," Hoffman said. China's national intelligence laws allow the government to pull data upon request from companies based there.
