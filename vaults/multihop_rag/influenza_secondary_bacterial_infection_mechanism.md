# How Influenza Sets Up A Secondary Bacterial Infection

Influenza can lead to a more serious illness later because the virus triggers inflammation of the respiratory tract, which causes the tract to become irritated and can allow secondary or bacteria to enter -- the mechanism described by Dr. Gregg Sylvester, chief health officer at CSL Seqirus, a New Jersey biopharmaceutical company.

The range over which this matters is narrow but not negligible. For most of the millions of people who get the flu each year in the U.S., the virus brings a few days of fever, body aches and weakness before it goes away. But for some, the influenza virus can lead to severe illness, hospitalization -- even death. Sylvester noted that while Miller's experience is not typical of most women in her age range, "it probably occurs more often than we know."

Sylvester draws the practical consequence directly from the mechanism: "A viral illness like influenza can set you up for something more serious further on," he warned. "That's why it's so important to get a vaccine."
