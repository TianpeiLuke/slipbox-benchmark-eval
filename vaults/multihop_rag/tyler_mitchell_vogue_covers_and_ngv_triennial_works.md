# Tyler Mitchell's Vogue Covers And NGV Triennial Works

Tyler Mitchell was just 23 when he became the first Black photographer to shoot the cover of American Vogue, photographing Beyoncé, and in November 2023 three of his works were on show in the NGV's Triennial exhibition, The Age reported on 27 November 2023.

His dreamy, delicately hued Beyoncé portraits spoke of a precocious visual talent, and in the five years since he has exhibited around the world, become an Instagram darling with more than 500,000 followers, and shot what became the most controversial vice-presidential portrait in living memory. When his portrait of Kamala Harris dressed in sneakers and casual clothing was chosen for the cover of Vogue for February 2021, the social media backlash was swift: critics questioned why his alternative image — in which Harris looked more vice-presidential — had not been used instead, and the magazine, bowing to outrage over the perceived lack of respect shown to the first woman to reach such high office, later issued a limited edition using the more formal shot. The controversy did little to dent the New York-based photographer's reputation as a rising star of the medium.

Of the three Triennial works, Picnic depicts a family gathering — young men throwing a football, a child lifted on shoulders, wicker baskets and a thermos, and in the background a darkened wood that perhaps hints at the fragility of a moment so peaceful and pure. In the others his figures are arranged across a sandy expanse in idealised depictions of Black American leisure: in Albany, Georgia a football flies through the air, a father in dress shirt and tails twirls a child, and a young boy rides a toy truck; Vastness focuses on a teenage boy standing patiently as his younger siblings stretch his wildly elongated sleeves out beside him, with a dog nearby sitting listening to a violinist. These carefully staged set-ups are evidence of Mitchell's desire to establish a new visual narrative for Black Americans.
