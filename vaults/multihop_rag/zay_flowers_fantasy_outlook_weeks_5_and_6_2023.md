# Zay Flowers' Fantasy Outlook In Weeks 5 And 6 Of 2023

CBS Sports listed Zay Flowers as a "Flex Starter in PPR" in both Week 5 and Week 6 of the 2023 season, and in both weeks the argument turns on the same conditional: Flowers is worth starting in catch-counting formats only if Baltimore's run game fails, because his target volume rises when the Ravens cannot run.

The Week 5 case, published 4 October 2023 ahead of a game with Pittsburgh, opens by noting that Ravens-Steelers games have come to be loved or hated for tough defense and low-scoring battles, which might hurt Flowers' chances of scoring — it will probably take a little luck, though that isn't too big an ask given how the Steelers secondary has played recently. Starting Flowers means counting on the Steelers run defense staying strong and the Ravens using him to create solid plays as a run-game extension, and hoping for a splash play, which makes him a risky flex with a much safer floor in PPR than in half- or non-PPR. The usage evidence: his target share increases in competitive Ravens games, unlike Week 4's blowout win, and especially when the run game isn't overly efficient, as in Weeks 1 and 3; of his 29 targets, 18 have come on throws of three or fewer air yards and half of those have been receiver screens. Pittsburgh's run defense had improved considerably over its past two games, giving up just 3.5 yards per carry and 2.22 yards after contact per run against the Raiders and Texans, which could open up more targets for Flowers. The Steelers haven't seen many receiver screens, but the ones they've defended have yielded a pathetic 13.75 yards after catch per reception, making screens an exploitable play for Flowers; the deep ball is exploitable too, with Pittsburgh allowing 16 of 33 targets of 15-plus air yards to be completed for 22.5 yards per catch and three touchdowns, against Flowers' own 4-of-5 for a sizzling 136 yards on deep passes. The historical caution is that in five career games against Pittsburgh, Lamar Jackson has thrown four touchdowns — all to wideouts, all in the red zone, none on deep throws and none on receiver screens. In catch-counting formats the column would start Flowers over Tank Dell, the Broncos receivers, Jordan Addison and George Pickens.

The Week 6 case, published 12 October 2023 ahead of a game at Tennessee, weakens the recommendation on the same logic. If the author had supreme confidence in Baltimore not having much success on the ground he would start Flowers without hesitation, but Tennessee's run defense has weakened a bit and the Ravens could be apt to take advantage; the upside for a couple of long targets is what keeps Flowers in the flex conversation. The breakdown lists Baltimore at Tennessee, the Ravens favoured by 4, an over/under of 41, an opponent ranked 28th versus wide receivers, 12.3 projected points, a 28th receiver ranking, and year-to-date totals of 29 receptions on 40 targets for 317 yards, no touchdowns and 12.5 fantasy points per game. The Week 5 prediction held: the Ravens run game couldn't get going, Flowers saw a season-high 11 targets at Pittsburgh, but he underwhelmed with five catches for 73 yards and two drops that would have pushed him past 100 yards. His deployment changed — a 15.27 average depth of target, his most downfield game of the season and the first time he had both a double-digit ADOT and more than six targets, lining up wide on 66.7% of snaps with hitches still his route of choice but more diversity than before, all while sharing the field with Odell Beckham and Rashod Bateman. Tennessee ranks bottom-five in catch rate allowed to outside receivers at 75.8% but handles them well otherwise, with top-10 marks in yards per catch (12.5) and yards after catch per reception (3.34), and is equally adept against hitch routes and receiver screens, Flowers' two most-run routes; the Titans have allowed exactly 50% of 15-plus air-yard attempts to receivers to be completed for a bottom-five yards per catch allowed, while being league average in yards after catch and touchdowns on those throws, with slightly better numbers if four Week 1 hiccups are discounted. The verdict prefers Christian Kirk, Marquise Brown, Michael Pittman and Jakobi Meyers as WR2 options that week.
