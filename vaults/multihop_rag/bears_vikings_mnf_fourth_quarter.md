# The Fourth Quarter Of Bears 12, Vikings 10

The fourth quarter on Nov. 27, 2023 produced the game's only touchdown, two Justin Fields fumbles and a Cairo Santos field goal that won it 12-10 for Chicago. Late in the third-to-fourth quarter transition Byron Murphy broke up a Fields third-down pass and Santos — who had already missed from 48 that night — drilled a 55-yarder to make it Bears 9, Vikings 3.

Minnesota's offense kept turning the ball over. At 10:25 p.m. Dobbs threw his fourth interception of the night, Justin Jones nearly getting it before Kyler Gordon came away with it, everything on the Vikings' offensive side looking like a chore; Ty Chandler had been ineffective with 11 yards on five touches, and Jordan Addison's third-down catch that extended one drive left him with only 25 yards. Chicago then squandered its chance: Fields found Darnell Mooney for a big third-down conversion to get firmly into field goal range, but at 10:29 p.m. Josh Metellus forced a fumble that Sheldon Day recovered, wasting the Bears' opportunity to make it a two-score game.

Minnesota turned that into the lead. Brandon Powell, the Vikings' biggest playmaker at two key points, went 18 yards to near midfield; Dobbs then overthrew a wide-open Jordan Addison out of bounds on what would have been a walk-in touchdown; Matt Eberflus challenged a later Addison grab that stood, and an Alexander Mattison run brought Minnesota to the edge of field goal range. Dobbs hit Hockenson to reach the red zone and then, at 10:43 p.m., hit Hockenson again for the first touchdown of the night to make it Vikings 10, Bears 9.

Chicago's response was ugly before it was decisive. Fields threw behind the line of scrimmage to Mooney, who was dropped for a four-yard loss on a call that had not worked all half, and at 10:49 p.m. Metellus knocked the ball out of Fields' hands for the second time, with Anthony Barr recovering. Minnesota then threw behind the line of scrimmage itself, lost yardage, passed on a long field goal attempt and punted with 2:36 remaining and a one-point lead.

The winning drive took four plays. Fields hit D.J. Moore for 16 on the first play ahead of the two-minute warning, picked up a first down with his legs to cross midfield with 85 seconds left and no timeouts, then after a pair of incompletions faced 3rd & 10 at the Vikings' 49, out of field goal range — and hit Moore for 36 yards, exactly what Chicago acquired him to do. The Vikings burned their two timeouts, the Bears knelt on third down, and at 11:04 p.m. Santos drilled the field goal to put Chicago back in front with 10 seconds for Minnesota to work a miracle. At 11:07 p.m. it was over: the Bears never scored a touchdown and barely took advantage of four Vikings turnovers, but walked out of Minnesota winners as the Vikings fell back to .500.
