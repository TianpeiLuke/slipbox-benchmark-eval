# The M3 MacBook Pro Lineup And Its Configurations

The M3 MacBook Pro line covers all three new chips across two sizes: the 14-inch MacBook Pro can be configured with any of the M3, M3 Pro or M3 Max, while the 16-inch is limited to just the Pro and Max.

The new MacBook Pro, which went on sale that week, was announced at the Scary Fast event alongside a new iMac and several members of the M3 line — a big departure for Apple following rumors it had planned the initial M3 launch for WWDC, before the supply chain thought differently. Announcing the M3, M3 Pro and M3 Max at once affords the company options, though less so for the M3-only iMac. An M3 Ultra is presumably also on the way in the not-too-distant future, but will almost certainly be confined to desktops like the Mac Studio and Mac Pro owing to the limitations of system architectures; Apple also recently shot down rumors surrounding a 27-inch iMac.

On configurations and price, the M3 Max comes in both 14-core CPU/30-core GPU and 16-core CPU/40-core GPU flavors; the review unit was the latter, which also means 48GB of memory and 1TB of storage by default, and it is a $4,000 computer as configured. Bumping memory and storage up to 128GB and 8TB respectively comes to $7,200 — a hefty increase from the base level 14- and 16-inch models, which run $1,600 and $2,500 to start. Apple lowered the barrier of entry for the lowest end model, but upgrades add up quickly; this is a real investment, and a system likely to last a while. Apple is currently on a one-year upgrade cycle for its first-party M-series chips, but it is genuinely tough to imagine an advance as pronounced as the one from Intel to in-house silicon happening any time soon; there is no such thing as a future-proofed laptop, so the best you can hope for is good mileage, which certainly applies here.

Alongside building new chips, Apple has spent the last few years listening to consumers in a way it had not for decades: finally updating the camera, building a better keyboard — the class action settlement probably tipped the scales as well — and ditching the well-meaning but ultimately ineffectual Touch Bar altogether. The arrival of the new 14-inch signaled the merciful end to that technology, as the last Touch Bar Mac, the 13-inch Pro, was replaced by the new 14-inch model.
