# The Epic v. Apple Ruling And Its Limited Weight Here

In the Apple case both sides lost, but Epic arguably lost more: even though Apple has incredible power over the iPhone, Judge Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers ruled the company did not have an unfair monopoly in that case, partly because she decided the relevant market for Fortnite was "digital mobile gaming transactions" rather than, say, iPhone apps. She also decided that Epic violated its developer agreement with Apple and would have to pay.

Epic did win something. Rogers barred Apple from keeping iPhone users in the dark about alternate ways they can pay for apps, and may have even allowed developers to add their own payment mechanisms.

The precedent's reach into the Google trial is limited in two specific ways. First, that was a different case — and it is not over until the Supreme Court weighs in or declines to take a look. Second, Google cannot tell jurors that Apple won its case, or that other plaintiffs settled; the judge in this case says so. Epic declined to answer on the record when asked about the Apple outcome.
