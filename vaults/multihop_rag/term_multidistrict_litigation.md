# Multidistrict Litigation

## Definition

In the corpus, "the multidistrict litigation" names the consolidated federal proceeding combining the Uber sexual-assault lawsuits: a panel of judges ruled that more than 80 of the cases filed against Uber can be consolidated into federal court, and attorney Rachel Abrams — whose firm, Peiffer Wolf Carr Kane Conway & Wise, is one of the law firms working on it — filed the petition to consolidate the actions.

## Context

The term surfaces in coverage of the Uber sexual-assault lawsuits, where hundreds of women sued Uber claiming the company hasn't done enough to prevent driver-perpetrated assaults. A panel of judges ruled that more than 80 of these cases can be consolidated into federal court, and attorney Rachel Abrams — one of the lawyers who filed the petition to consolidate the actions — is identified as working on "the multidistrict litigation." The consolidation is procedural, not substantive: it changes where and how the cases are heard, not whether Uber is liable.

## Key Characteristics

- **Consolidation across jurisdictions** — more than 80 separately filed cases against Uber were combined into one federal court rather than being litigated individually in their home jurisdictions.
- **Petition-driven** — the consolidation followed a petition filed by plaintiffs' counsel (Rachel Abrams) asking a judicial panel to combine the actions.
- **Single judge for pretrial matters** — Judge Charles Breyer, in the Northern District of California, was assigned to preside over pretrial matters such as witness and expert depositions and document discovery for the consolidated cases.
- **Pretrial coordination, not necessarily joint trial** — Breyer may preside over trials only if the parties agree; otherwise cases return to their home jurisdiction for trial, and expected proceedings run one to two years.
- **Distinct from class action** — Uber's terms of use bar class actions in sexual-assault cases, so each case must still be heard individually even after consolidation; a separate, California-only consolidated lawsuit against Uber exists apart from the federal MDL.
- **Contested by the defendant** — Uber has filed several motions to dismiss aimed at stopping consolidation, arguing it did not owe plaintiffs a duty to protect against criminal conduct by drivers.

## Background (external)

Outside this corpus, multidistrict litigation is a formal federal procedure created by 28 U.S.C. §1407: the U.S. Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation determines whether civil actions pending in different federal districts share common questions of fact, and if so transfers ("centralizes") them into a single district for coordinated pretrial proceedings before one judge, to avoid duplicative discovery and conflicting pretrial rulings; cases not resolved during that process are then remanded to their original districts for trial (U.S. Judicial Panel on Multidistrict Litigation, "About the Panel," accessed 2026-09-02, https://www.jpml.uscourts.gov/about-panel).
