# The 2023 Chemistry Nobel for Quantum Dots

The 2023 Nobel Prize in chemistry honours three scientists who discovered and created quantum dots: Moungi Bawendi, a chemist at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) in Cambridge; Louis Brus, a chemist based at Columbia University in New York City; and Alexei Ekimov, a physicist now working at Nanocrystals Technology, Inc., in Briarcliff Manor, N.Y.

The Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences announced the honour on October 4, and the trio will split a prize of 11 million Swedish kronor, or about $1 million. The award recognises a peculiar class of matter: typically, matter made of the same kinds of atoms arranged in the same way has the same properties, but tiny specks called quantum dots can contain the same molecules yet have different colours and other qualities depending on their size. The ability to make a whole rainbow of quantum dots simply by tweaking their size makes them highly useful — they light up TV screens and help doctors see inside the body.
