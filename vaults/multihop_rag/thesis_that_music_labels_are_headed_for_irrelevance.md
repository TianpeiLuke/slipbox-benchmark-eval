# Thesis That Music Labels Are Headed for Irrelevance

The argument, common among music business observers from the world of tech, is that record labels could in the long term be headed for irrelevance. Its grounds are the arrival of digital platforms, including open ones like YouTube and SoundCloud, together with the proliferation of indie music distribution platforms such as DistroKid and TikTok's SoundOn.

The warrant joining grounds to claim is a claim about what labels are for: in a world where anyone can record music using a desktop audio workstation (DAW) and upload it directly to YouTube, or to Spotify via a distribution platform, labels have little left to do. Recording and distribution — the functions the label historically supplied — are available to any artist directly, so on this reasoning the label's remaining role shrinks toward nothing. Music Business Worldwide presented this as the speculation that tech-world observers "like to" make, and as the position Warner Music Group CEO Robert Kyncl was answering at the Code Conference in September 2023.
