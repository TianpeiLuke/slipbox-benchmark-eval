# Alameda Research's Origins And Naming

Alameda Research was the cryptocurrency trading firm Bankman-Fried started in 2017 during a crypto bull run, and it was named for Alameda County in California, where its first office was set up. Testifying on October 28th, 2023, Bankman-Fried said he knew "basically nothing" about cryptocurrency at the time but wanted to do arbitrage on it anyway, and that he is "somewhat introverted, naturally."

The name was chosen for cover. In his own words on the stand: "Effectively, we wanted to be under the radar at that point in time. I didn't want to call it Sam's Crypto Trading Firm or anything like that. We — there are a lot of competitors and people who we didn't particularly want to know what we were building out because they would race to do it. 'Research' was a sort of generic word, which filled out the company name. And that was — it was far better than the internal name that we had at that point, which was Wireless Mouse." The Verge's reporter noted she would find this more believable had she not already watched a video, played during Gary Wang's first day of testimony with Bankman-Fried and the jury present, of Bankman-Fried explaining on a podcast that the name made it easier to get a bank account.

The founding assembled the people later charged alongside him: Bankman-Fried went on a hiring spree, taking on Wang to program the computers, then Nishad Singh about a month after founding Alameda Research, and finally Caroline Ellison. Though Bankman-Fried was CEO and also majority owner, he wanted to be clear that he did not supervise Wang's direct work. After a run of wildly successful arbitrage — 50 percent to 100 percent annualized returns, per his testimony — he decided to found a cryptocurrency exchange, FTX, figuring he would fail and putting the chance of success at 20 percent, without defining the time period he had in mind.
