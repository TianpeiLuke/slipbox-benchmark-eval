# Millennials As The New Boomer Homebuying Cohort

One of the key reasons for the resilience of the housing market in both the early 1980s and today is demographics: "Noticeably, demographics were favorable back then, with baby boomers having entered the prime homebuying age," Jeseo Park and her Bank of America team wrote, arguing that millennials are in a similar position today. The structure of the argument is that a large generation reaching prime homebuying age supplies a floor of demand that offsets rate-driven weakness — millennials are a giant generation all collectively coming of homebuying age nearly simultaneously, just like their boomer parents in the 1980s, and as Redfin puts it they are the biggest share of the "homebuying pie," purchasing about 60% of homes bought with mortgages during the past few years.

The relation Bank of America draws from this holds over a specific range. "Some sales activity should be supported by millennials reaching the prime homebuying age, and single-family building permits have steadily held up. This can help the housing market retain some of its momentum without falling apart," Park's team wrote. They believe low inventory of existing homes for sale combined with solid home sales to millennials could help put a "floor beneath home prices" — but that does not mean there won't be some near-term pain due to higher mortgage rates.
