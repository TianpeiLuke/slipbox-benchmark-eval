# How Ketamine Caused A Drowning Rather Than Direct Toxicity

On the toxicological account of Matthew Perry's death, ketamine did not kill directly; it produced unconsciousness, and the unconsciousness produced a fatal injury.

"The concentration of ketamine found in Matthew Perry's blood was sufficient to cause loss of consciousness and lack of responsiveness to external stimulation," Lewis Nelson, chair of the Department of Emergency Medicine and chief of medical toxicology at Rutgers New Jersey Medical School, said in a statement provided to Fox News Digital.

That chain -- dose, then unresponsiveness, then drowning -- is what the mechanism explains: "This explains why he slipped under the water and did not awaken. Ketamine does not generally cause death from cardiac or respiratory effects, but rather from associated injury."
