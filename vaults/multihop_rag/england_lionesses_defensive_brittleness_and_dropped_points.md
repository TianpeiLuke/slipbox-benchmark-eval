# England's Defensive Brittleness And Dropped Points

Against the reading that England's Olympic exit turned on the final night in Glasgow, The Guardian argues the damage had been done in the preceding games — and that the underlying problem is defensive brittleness rather than attacking quality.

On the goal-difference framing, the counter-grounds are the earlier results: two poor goals conceded against the Dutch at Wembley, a 3-2 defeat in Belgium in October and a 2-1 loss to the Netherlands in September would all be full of regrets, so an extraordinary mountain remained that even a Lauren James masterclass and a gut-busting display from Lucy Bronze could not lift them over. The piece concedes there will be what-ifs either way, with the first goal conceded in Utrecht appearing offside in the buildup. On the attacking framing, the counter is that for large parts of the Wembley game England were not very good, and that for all their creativity and verve going forward perhaps the biggest flaw in the side is a lack of presence in the two penalty areas — the physicality and alpha energy to do the grunt work, to convert the crosses and to head them away. Individually, Jess Carter had a poor game and was withdrawn for Esme Morgan after half-time, Alex Greenwood over-committed for the first Dutch goal, and even the all-conquering Mary Earps plummeted to Earth with a poor first-half error. But beyond personnel, a basic brittleness seems to have crept into the team, a sense of melting surfaces and cracks in the ice: Sarina Wiegman talks a lot about defending against counterattacks because this is basically the key to the whole enterprise — cut off the counter and England's technical quality lets them easily dominate — and it is also the essential measure of how the team functions as a collective, in the efficiency of the press, the closing of angles and the command of space; Lineth Beerensteyn's two goals were the sort England simply were not conceding four months earlier. So England passed the ball around a bit and gave it away a bit and essentially ran around trying to feel normal again, and on countless occasions Kelly or Hemp would win a first or second ball, look up and see a penalty area empty of white shirts — issues not so much of tactics but of cohesion and energy: the timing of runs, and the sharpness required not just to anticipate where the ball is going to be but actually get there. You can still chase when running on fumes, but to create needs an idea and a vision, the clarity of mind to see the picture before it has materialised.
