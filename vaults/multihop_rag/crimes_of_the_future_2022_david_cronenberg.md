# Crimes Of The Future: Cronenberg's World Where Surgery Is The New Sex

Crimes of the Future, David Cronenberg's 2022 body horror drama starring Viggo Mortensen, Léa Seydoux and Kristen Stewart, is available on Hulu. It imagines a world where humans have lost the ability to feel pain, and where several people have developed a disturbing disorder that causes their bodies to spontaneously spawn new organs. Polygon frames it against a present-day anxiety — microplastics and pollutants are in our lungs, blood, food, drinking water and the air we breathe, and what that is doing to our bodies is unknown.

The new reality has spawned a trend: live surgery, wherein performance artists plagued with this condition tear into their own bodies in an effort to shape meaning out of this strange new biological fact. Mortensen stars as Saul Tenser, a world-renowned performance artist who, alongside his partner Caprice (Seydoux), stands on the cutting edge — literally and figuratively — of the phenomenon. When Saul's activities catch the attention of a mysterious group of evolutionary activists, as well as the lascivious eye of a government employee named Timlin (Stewart), he is forced to confront what he and everyone around him is changing into, and whether that can even be considered "human" any more.

As macabre as it is moving and grotesque as it is sensuous, it is an exquisite work of science fiction horror where surgery is the new sex and our very bodies have rebelled against us for the incalculable destruction we have inflicted on the planet — a film in intimate conversation with the anxieties of the present and a stunning return to form for one of cinema's most forward-thinking directors. Howard Shore's growling, guttural score is engrossing, and the leading trio of performances are a virtual match made in heaven. The reviewer's one caveat: don't see it on a full stomach.
