# DMA And DSA Oversight Of Meta's Advertising Consent

Beyond consumer and data protection law, the European Commission itself has an additional oversight role on Meta directly, because the company is also subject to the EU's Digital Markets Act (DMA) and Digital Services Act (DSA). Under the DSA, Meta's social networks Facebook and Instagram were designated as very large online platforms (VLOPs) earlier in 2023, and since late August 2023 they have been expected to be compliant with that digital rulebook.

The operative feature of both pan-EU laws is a symmetry requirement on consent. Both put restrictions on the use of personal data for advertising, explicitly requiring that consent is obtained from users for such a purpose, and that consent must be as easy to withhold as to affirm. So one issue the Commission — which is the sole enforcer of the DSA on VLOPs — might weigh in on in the coming months is whether clicking accept versus digging out a credit card to pay a monthly charge are equivalently easy.

The DSA also contains provisions intended to combat unfair or deceptive design, such as targeting choice interfaces that make it "more difficult or time-consuming" to pick one option over another. The boundary on those provisions is that the DSA's rules against dark patterns are only intended to be applied where consumer protection and privacy laws, which also take aim against unfair choices, do not.
