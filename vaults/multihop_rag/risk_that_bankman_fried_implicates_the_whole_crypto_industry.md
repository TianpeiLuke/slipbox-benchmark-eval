# The Risk That Bankman-Fried Implicates The Whole Crypto Industry

The Verge argued that Bankman-Fried's own defense could damage crypto's public perception far beyond his case, because his post-collapse behavior suggests he's something of a wild card.

The grounds are the defenses available to him: he may suggest he was acting on the advice of his lawyers, but he may also introduce other evidence that could be troublesome — implying that he was engaged in standard industry behavior, or that everything that happened was Binance's fault. That may be risky, but "we already know that Bankman-Fried loves risk." Hermine Wong frames the danger as a question: "Is he going to throw the entire industry under the bus? An idea like, 'Everyone was doing this, it's not fair I'm the only one who was charged?'" That may not fly in a court of law, but it could absolutely damage public perception of crypto at large. The precedent is his own conduct: just before he was arrested, Bankman-Fried brought forward messages from a crypto group chat in testimony he planned to give before Congress, in which he primarily blames Binance and his lawyers at the firm Sullivan & Cromwell. Other group chat messages may be brought into evidence during the trial, which Wong says could be embarrassing for the whole industry: "You can imagine some of these founders, CEOs, people of that echelon talk to each other somewhat informally about what's going on. If it seems like his peer group supported him, or worse, fawned over him, that's a real problem for the industry's reputation."
