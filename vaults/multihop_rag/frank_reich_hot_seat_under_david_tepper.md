# Frank Reich's Hot Seat Under David Tepper

Sporting News argued on Nov. 6, 2023 that Frank Reich's job as Panthers head coach was genuinely at risk after one season, because Carolina's offense had been poor and owner David Tepper has a demonstrated history of impatience — with the "Thursday Night Football" game against the Bears among the most important remaining on the schedule.

The grounds begin with performance. Carolina's offense had been less-than-impressive in 2023, and that was the main reason Reich found himself under fire; he had already given up play-calling to offensive coordinator Thomas Brown after the team's early-season struggles, and the lackluster offense averaged the fifth-fewest yards (283.4) and sixth-fewest points (17.5) per game. The second ground is testimony: ESPN's Adam Schefter, speaking on "The Pat McAfee Show," implied the retread coach's seat was growing hotter with each passing week, saying "I don't think anybody's got a long rope there just because of the type of season that they're having." Schefter stopped short of saying Reich was on the hot seat, but detailed that Tepper would not be afraid to move on from Reich if he deemed it necessary: "One thing about David Tepper: he's not very patient. He's as successful as he is because of the way he's operated his business. And it's worked for him, basically at every level. He does what he wants. And I don't think that people are thinking that there's trouble right now with those people. But would anybody be surprised with anything that happens in Carolina this year? No."

The warrant joining the grounds to the claim is the precedent of Matt Rhule: Schefter pointed to Carolina's firing of Rhule as an example of Tepper's impatience, noting rumors that the owner was going to wait things out with Rhule in the weeks before his in-season departure, only for Tepper to pull the trigger and part with him. The argument is carefully hedged — the Panthers are not guaranteed to fire Reich even amid a poor season, and he might not be fired at all if he can help Carolina earn wins in the second half — but it concludes that Reich will need to deliver some wins to earn a second season, and that if Tepper loses patience he could go back to the drawing board and bring in another new head coach. Schefter also noted the added stake in the Bears game: because Chicago holds Carolina's No. 1 draft pick, a Bears win would be a huge victory in a bigger way than just the game itself, as the team grapples with justifying the trade-up to select Young.
