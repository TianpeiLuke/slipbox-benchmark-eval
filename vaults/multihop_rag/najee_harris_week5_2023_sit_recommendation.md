# Najee Harris As A Week 5 2023 Sit Against Baltimore

CBS Sports listed Najee Harris under "Sit Him" for Week 5 of the 2023 season against Baltimore: the claim is that Harris is at best a low-end starting option, and the warrant is that a volume-based back loses his ceiling when his offense generates almost no scoring opportunities and the opponent has not conceded a rushing touchdown all year.

The grounds are unusually specific about the Steelers' red-zone drought. Harris has zero touchdowns this season and just one carry inside the 15-yard line; Pittsburgh has run the second-fewest red-zone plays in the league, 18, one more than New England, and the second-fewest plays inside the five-yard line, two, again one more than New England — across four games, which the column calls nuts. Baltimore has yet to allow a rushing score to a running back this year and has otherwise been solid, holding backs to 4.0 yards per carry and roughly league average in other metrics including 15 missed tackles; only three players have averaged over 4.0 yards per carry against them — Joe Mixon at 4.5 without 100 total yards or a score, Zack Moss at 4.1 with 122 rushing yards on 30 carries plus a receiving touchdown, and Pierre Strong at 9.8, most of it in garbage time the previous week. The breakdown lists Pittsburgh at home versus Baltimore, the Ravens favoured by 4, an over/under of 38, an opponent ranked 12th versus running backs, 9.3 projected points, a 30th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 210 rushing yards, four receptions, 34 receiving yards, no touchdowns and 7.1 fantasy points per game.

The counter-evidence the column weighs is that Harris himself has improved and could be on his way to being a reliable RB2. He is averaging 4.3 yards per run with a 12.2% explosive run rate — the frequency of his 12-plus-yard runs — which is fifth-best among 44 qualifying running backs; he is 13th in avoided tackle rate and top-10 in yards after contact per carry at 3.53. In Week 4 he had his first game over 100 total yards, against the Texans, averaging 5.1 yards per rush, just shy of the season high he technically held on six carries against the 49ers in Week 1, with a 32-yard reception carrying him past triple digits. History is also on his side: he had over 120 total yards and a touchdown in a Week 17 win in Baltimore last year, scored on the Ravens with exactly 50 total yards at home in Week 14, and has posted at least 10 non-PPR points in three of four career games against them. The Ravens, the column concedes, have been pretty good against the run but can give up some numbers. The verdict still ranks Breece Hall, Rhamondre Stevenson, Dameon Pierce and Jaleel McLaughlin — the last conditional on Javonte Williams sitting out — ahead of Harris.
