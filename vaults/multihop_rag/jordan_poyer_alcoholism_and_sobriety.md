# Jordan Poyer's Alcoholism And Sobriety

Jordan Poyer stopped drinking in March 2020, after downing a shot of tequila on March 13 in the Puerto Vallarta airport bar during a 12-hour flight delay, and his first significant temptation came that spring at a Bills defenders' gathering in Washington, D.C., where he put a shot of tequila down instead of drinking it, The New York Times reported on 15 December 2023.

The circumstances of that moment were public. With NFL team facilities closed because of COVID-19, Bills defenders met in Washington for a few days to train, study and bond; on the first evening cornerback Josh Norman welcomed the players to his home and out came the shots of tequila. When Poyer was handed one, he looked at it, then at his teammates, and put it down. Norman and the others, who had never seen him turn down a drink, were taken aback — "What?" one said. "Come on, Deejay Poyo!" Deejay Poyo was Poyer's alter ego, inspired by tequila and a turntable; for Poyer there was value in being someone else. His teammates did not know he had not put alcohol to his lips in about three months. "When I put that drink down for the first time, it was almost like I overcame a demon," Poyer says. The more time that passed, the less he wanted to drink; he has no desire for it now, and the smell of alcohol almost makes him gag.

The drinking began at Oregon State. Poyer spent his childhood in Astoria, Ore., a picturesque town on the mouth of the Columbia River not far from the Pacific Ocean, where his mother and stepfather worked at a juvenile detention center and raised him with a strict hand; when he was no longer under their watch, unsupervised as a freshman at Oregon State, he drank, and drank and drank. It never seemed to affect his play in college, where he was a consensus All-American, and despite continued drinking he made steady progress in his early NFL years. By then his drinking was out of control: he would down a six-pack of IPAs in 20 to 30 minutes for a quick buzz, and blacking out was a regular occurrence. His behaviour tested his marriage, though there was a draw between him and Rachel like a north pole to south. After the Bills lost to the Houston Texans in the wild-card round of the 2019 playoffs, Poyer was crestfallen and says he drank heavily every day for five weeks; at one point Rachel rid their home of all alcohol, but he would still sneak what he could, hiding it from her beneath bathroom cabinets and other places "like a child," she says. He was close to losing her. The drinking, he realised, was an attempt to relieve stress — stress from football expectations and stress from family life; he was a worrier, except with a drink in his hand. He went to an Alcoholics Anonymous meeting, then another, and another.
