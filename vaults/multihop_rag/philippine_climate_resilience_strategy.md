# The Philippine Climate Resilience Strategy

While price stability and fiscal sustainability are important macroeconomic issues, environmental sustainability is increasingly gaining importance: climate change uncertainties and challenges need to be managed, and both the Philippine government and the private sector are hard-pressed to deliver on their commitments. Despite climate change risks, the Philippines has the opportunity to position itself as a prime destination for foreign investments against climate change, or environmental, social and governance investments. The government calls for embedding resilience, sustainability and nature-based solutions in infrastructure planning and investment to enhance climate resilience, and investments in renewable energy are expected not only to enhance energy security but also to reduce greenhouse gas emissions. From an economic vantage point, GHG emissions are economic "bads" and a cause of inefficiency because they get over-produced; their effects go beyond national borders, and while the Philippines contributes minimally to global GHG emissions, the World Risk Index 2022 report identifies the Philippines as the most disaster-prone country in the world.

The government's stated 2028 outcomes are three: that climate and disaster risk resilience of communities and institutions will increase; that ecosystem resilience will be enhanced; and that a low-carbon economy transition is enabled. To ensure these are realized, the government is set to safeguard cross-sectoral convergence and implement a comprehensive risk management approach to address the adverse consequences posed by climate change, and it will also promote a green and blue economy coupled with improved governance to guarantee long-term climate and disaster resilience.
