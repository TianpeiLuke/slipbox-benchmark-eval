# The Case That Michigan's Sign-Stealing Was Systematic Cheating

Big Ten coaches argued that Michigan's conduct was not advanced scouting but sustained cheating: the claim, from one Big Ten coach, is that "They (Michigan) have been manipulating the game and cheating the game for two-and-a-half years," and the warrant is that knowing exactly what the other team is doing confers an on-field advantage so large that "Michigan might as well have been playing with 15 guys on the field," as reported by The New York Times on 3 November 2023.

The coach extended the argument to the consequences of inaction and to player safety. "What's the message the Big Ten is sending now by doing nothing? Win now, pay later? We might as well just send people to (scout) their practices and their games. It doesn't encourage anybody to follow the rules. It's just telling them to do the opposite and say, f— it." And: "When a running back gets hurt against Michigan because they knew exactly what play was coming, will that kid and his family have the ability to sue the Big Ten?" The same coach set the disparity in enforcement alongside it: "This guy's being investigated for three different things now between the (alleged) illegal signal stealing, the (alleged) illegal recruiting during COVID and that investigation into the offensive coordinator and alleged computer hacking. There are guys (on that call) who could lose jobs, and then there's this guy over here (Harbaugh) who is gonna get a new, bigger contract now, and they won't do anything about him."

Purdue coach Ryan Walters put the evidential grounds on the record publicly, on his radio show on the Thursday night ahead of Saturday's game against the Wolverines: "They aren't allegations. It happened. There's video evidence. There's ticket purchases and sales you can track back. We know for a fact they were at a number of our games. We've had to teach our guys a new language."
