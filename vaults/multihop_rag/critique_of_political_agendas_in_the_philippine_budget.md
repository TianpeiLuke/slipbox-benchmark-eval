# Critique of Political Agendas in the Philippine Budget

Business World's closing objection targets the assumption that a national budget is a purely technical instrument aimed at socio-economic equilibrium. National budgets, it argues, might not be innocent of political agendas — call them plans and programmes — that can divert money from the proper socio-economic goals of equilibrium that a balanced budget might work towards.

The column concedes the opposing possibility rather than asserting bad faith outright: traditional politicians, called "trapo" in the Philippines, might have it in their hearts to think about the country and the welfare of future generations. It then leaves the question open as a challenge about intergenerational cost: "How many generations will pay for the monstrous debt incurred for the dubious objectives and vanities of some today?"
