# National Football League (NFL)

## Definition

In the corpus, the National Football League (NFL) is the professional football league covered as both a sports competition and a media business — its 2023 season tracked through AFC/NFC conference standings and playoff seeding, its "Thursday Night Football" package sold to Amazon Prime Video, and its revenue growth under Commissioner Roger Goodell, credited by Essentially Sports with a $27 billion revenue target for 2027.

## Context

The corpus treats the NFL as both a sports league and a media business: Essentially Sports traces its "humble beginnings in 1920" to its status as an "$18 billion juggernaut" today, with Commissioner Roger Goodell targeting $27 billion in revenue by 2027.
The same piece cites an estimate from Joe Pompliano that the NFL "will bring in $20 billion in revenue this year, more than the Premier League, La Liga, Bundesliga, Serie A, and Ligue 1 combined," and separately states outright that "the NFL is already the world's richest league."
Its season structure surfaces repeatedly in the corpus: standings are tracked by conference and division (AFC East/North/South/West, NFC East/North/South/West), with regular-season results determining division leaders and wild-card seeding heading into the playoffs.
Weekly games are also branded by broadcast window — most notably "Thursday Night Football" — which airs exclusively on Amazon Prime Video under an 11-year rights deal, with only the two competing teams' local markets exempted from needing a Prime subscription.
The league also anchors seasonal traditions distinct from other major U.S. leagues, most notably Thanksgiving Day games, which in 2023 drew an average of 34.1 million viewers across three games — a tradition the corpus contrasts with the NBA's Christmas Day slate — alongside a growing Christmas Day broadcast tradition of its own.

## Key Characteristics

- **Two-conference structure** — teams are split into the AFC and NFC, each further divided into divisions, with standings and tiebreakers (head-to-head record, conference record) determining playoff seeding.
- **Regular season into playoffs** — the corpus's Week 13 standings coverage shows an 18-week regular season narrowing toward division leaders and wild-card contenders as the playoff picture takes shape.
- **Commissioner-led revenue strategy** — under Commissioner Roger Goodell, the league has set an explicit financial target ($27 billion by 2027) and, per the corpus, is already "the world's richest league."
- **Thursday Night Football broadcast rights** — the "Thursday Night Football" window airs exclusively on Amazon Prime Video under an 11-year deal, with local-market exemptions for the two competing teams.
- **Recurring scandal and safety scrutiny** — the corpus notes a history of controversies (Kaepernick's protest, domestic-violence cases, Deflategate, Bountygate, CTE concerns) running alongside active initiatives such as international games and the "Play Smart, Play Safe" concussion-safety program.
- **Holiday broadcast traditions** — Thanksgiving Day games are a fixture of the NFL calendar with viewership the corpus contrasts directly against the NBA's Christmas Day tradition.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, the NFL is the highest level of professional American football: 32 teams split evenly between the American Football Conference (AFC) and National Football Conference (NFC), each with four four-team divisions.
Each season runs an 18-week regular season in which every team plays 17 games and takes one bye week, after which seven teams per conference enter a single-elimination playoff bracket whose conference-championship winners meet in the Super Bowl (Wikipedia, "National Football League," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/National_Football_League, accessed 2026-09-02).
