# IMPALA's Call For An EU Legislative Fix

The artists' groups argue that the EU must legislate now to restore material reciprocity: "Unless this error is fixed, EU member states will not be able to continue applying the longstanding principle of material reciprocity in the payment of broadcast and public performance royalties on sound recordings from non-EU countries," they said in a statement issued on Tuesday November 28, 2023.

The grounds are the scale of the transfer and the Commission's inaction. "A devastating transfer of over €125 million [USD $137 million] every year out of Europe is on the horizon," IMPALA Executive Chair Helen Smith said in March. "We have been calling on the European Commission to address this since the ruling came out in September 2020, but despite some initial positive signs last year the silence in recent months has been deafening." The groups noted in their Tuesday statement that the European Commission "has on several occasions acknowledged concerns about the impact of the ruling and made clear its intention to find a balanced solution. But a solution has yet to be proposed." The warrant joining loss to obligation is a claim about responsibility: "It is the EU's responsibility to prevent European artists and producers losing millions every year to the USA, which has chosen not to protect these rights. A proposal is needed now to restore legal certainty, safeguard cultural diversity and European sovereignty," Smith said, adding: "We call on the EU to act and also exert increased trade pressure on the USA to raise their level of [copyright] protection. Their stance is costing the world music economy hundreds of millions a year." Along with IMPALA, Adami and Ntb/Kunstenbond, other signatories include the Swedish musicians' union Musikerförbundet, Belgian collective management society PlayRight, the Swedish Artists' and Musicians' Interest Organisation (SAMI), the Swedish Union of Professional Musicians (SYMF) and unisono, the German Federation of Musicians.
