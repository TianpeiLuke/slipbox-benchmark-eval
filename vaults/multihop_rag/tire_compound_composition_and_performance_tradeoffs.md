# Tire Compound Composition And Its Performance Tradeoffs

A tire's composition breaks down, in general terms, into four parts. "Natural rubber and synthetic rubber each represent about 25 percent of materials used in tire manufacturing. Fillers, primarily carbon black and silica, represent an additional 25 percent," Bridgestone's Niaura says. "The remaining percentage consists of steel, textiles, oils, resins, and other ingredients used to cure the tire or serve as anti-degradants." How all of this factors into each manufacturer's specialty blends is complicated.

The relation between blend and behavior runs across three properties: by blending different components, manufacturers can adjust a tire's grip (how sticky it is), tread life (how long it lasts), and rolling resistance (how efficient it will make your car).

Those three have traditionally traded off against one another — high-grip tires are traditionally short-lived and inefficient — but thanks to sticky science and renewable materials, that is changing.
