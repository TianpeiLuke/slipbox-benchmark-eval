# Home

Home, by Doug Levandowski and Yeonsoo Julian Kim, is a haunted house exploration simulator that can be played with a full table or alone as a solo journaling experience — and it is one of the most recommended tabletop RPGs of Wesley "Nox" Crowe, creative director for Hunters Entertainment, who notes that its being a horror game will surprise nobody who knows his work.

It uses custom tarot cards and a whiteboard to track your character's progress as they dare to shine a light on the chilling danger of a variety of potential scenarios full of ghosts and other dark entities. Action is propelled by those custom cards — the Night Deck — which are filled with prompts requiring creative interaction from players. Each round, players take turns as the director of the story, guiding the narrative forward and using the cards' guidance to describe and draw each room of the haunted house they are exploring. Crowe singles out the structural achievement: the game encourages collaboration in determining the story's direction and steers the action forward without a single game master having to do the often large amount of preparation role-playing games usually require. As play progresses, characters collect wounds, push past the terrors awaiting them, and work together to resolve the mysteries at the root of each horror; the solo mode plays just as smoothly as the group experience, leaning on the prompts and the player's imagination to create a journal documenting the character's harrowing encounter.
