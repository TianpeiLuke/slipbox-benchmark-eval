# Meta's Open Source AI Strategy And Motives

TechCrunch reads the AI Alliance release's closing jab — its contrast with a vision that would relegate AI innovation to a small number of closed, proprietary companies — as saying a lot about Meta's ulterior motives.

The grounds are the identity of the critics. Google, OpenAI and Microsoft, a close OpenAI partner and investor, have been among the chief critics of Meta's open source AI approach, arguing that it is potentially dangerous and disinformation-encouraging; unsurprisingly, none are members of the AI Alliance despite being longtime members of the Partnership on AI. Those companies now have a clear horse in the race and perhaps regulatory capture on the mind, but TechCrunch judges that they are not wrong entirely: Meta continues to take calculated open sourcing risks, within the bounds of regulators' tolerances, releasing text-generating models like Llama that bad actors have gone on to abuse but which plenty of developers have built useful apps upon.

The counterweight is a claim about which platform wins. "The platform that will win will be the open one," Yann LeCun, Meta's chief AI scientist, was quoted as saying in an interview with The New York Times; he is among the more than 70 influential signers of a letter calling for more openness in AI development. TechCrunch grants that LeCun has a point, citing an estimate that Stability AI's open source AI-powered image generator Stable Diffusion, released in August 2022, is now responsible for 80% of all AI-generated imagery.
