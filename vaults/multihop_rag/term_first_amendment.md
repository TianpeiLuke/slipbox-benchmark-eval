# First Amendment

## Definition

The First Amendment is the provision of the US Constitution invoked in litigation over whether social media platforms have a constitutionally protected right to make their own editorial and content-moderation decisions, as distinct from any speech rights of the platforms' users. In the corpus's framing, the operative question is "Do social media companies have a First Amendment protected right to exercise what you might call editorial judgment — or what you also might call content moderation — in sorting out what expression does and does not appear on the platforms that they own?"

## Context

The corpus discusses the First Amendment as the constitutional basis on which NetChoice and the Computer and Communications Industry Association (CCIA) sued Texas and Florida over HB 20 and SB 7072, the two states' laws restricting how platforms moderate content. Federal trial judges entered injunctions blocking both laws on constitutional grounds, and the Fifth and Eleventh Circuits then split on the resulting appeals, a conflict the corpus describes as one of the bases the Supreme Court uses in deciding to take up the pair of cases. NYU law professor Paul Barrett frames the dispute as being about the platforms' own First Amendment rights, "not the rights of their users" — i.e., whether content moderation is itself a form of protected editorial expression. Oregon Senator Ron Wyden is quoted arguing that "thanks to the First Amendment, Florida and Texas cannot force websites or social media apps to host hateful content, misinformation and spam, as their deeply misguided laws would require."

## Key Characteristics

- **Platform-rights framing** — in this litigation the First Amendment question concerns the speech rights of the social media companies themselves, not the speech rights of individual users.
- **Editorial judgment as the contested category** — the core legal question is whether content moderation qualifies as constitutionally protected "editorial judgment" over what appears on a platform.
- **Source of the circuit split** — First Amendment challenges to HB 20 (Texas) and SB 7072 (Florida) produced conflicting rulings from the Fifth and Eleventh Circuits, the conflict that brought the cases to the Supreme Court.
- **Invoked defensively against compelled hosting** — Wyden argues the First Amendment is what currently prevents Florida and Texas from forcing platforms to host content (hate speech, misinformation, spam) they would otherwise remove; NetChoice's Steve DelBianco separately warns, without invoking the First Amendment by name, that letting the laws take effect would force platforms to host a "tidal wave of offensive content and hate speech."

## Background (external)

The corpus treats the First Amendment as settled background law without quoting its text. Per the National Constitution Center, the First Amendment to the US Constitution reads: "Congress shall make no law respecting an establishment of religion, or prohibiting the free exercise thereof; or abridging the freedom of speech, or of the press; or the right of the people peaceably to assemble, and to petition the Government for a redress of grievances." It was passed by Congress on September 25, 1789, and ratified December 15, 1791, as the first of the ten amendments in the Bill of Rights (National Constitution Center, *The Constitution: Amendment I*, accessed 2026-09-02).
