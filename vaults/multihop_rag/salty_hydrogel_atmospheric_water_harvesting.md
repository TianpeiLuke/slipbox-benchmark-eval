# Salty Hydrogel Atmospheric Water Harvesting

A hydrogel is a mesh of large molecules called polymers; the new hydrogel captures humidity — water vapor — from the air and expands like a sponge in water as it does so, then releases the water it has absorbed when heated by the sun.

Carlos Díaz-Marín, a mechanical engineer at the Massachusetts Institute of Technology (MIT) in Cambridge, leads a team developing a device to address water shortages that would affordably pull water from air without a need for electricity, and the key ingredient is a new, super-salty gel. The idea is not new: many types of hydrogels have been developed to suck water from air, and previous work showed that adding salts to hydrogels upped how much water they could absorb, because salts attract moisture, including from the air, on account of their chemical properties. What was unknown was how much salt a hydrogel could stash, and how pushing that salt content to the maximum might boost the hydrogel's water-slurping power — the question Díaz-Marín and his team set out to answer.
