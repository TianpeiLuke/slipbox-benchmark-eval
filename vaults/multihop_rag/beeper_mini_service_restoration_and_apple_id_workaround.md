# Beeper Mini's Service Restoration And Apple ID Workaround

On Monday 11 December 2023, Beeper announced on its blog that it had updated the Beeper Mini app to fix the issue that "caused messages not to be sent or received." It did not say exactly how, but noted that the security of Beeper Mini remains unchanged, promising it still includes local, end-to-end encryption as previously described; CEO Eric Migicovsky told TechCrunch he could not share anything on that topic beyond what was already in the blog post.

There was a caveat to the restoration: phone number registration was not yet working, so users had to sign in with an Apple ID, and the startup said it was working to address this. Beeper also decided to make Beeper Mini free for the time being given how "chaotic" things had been over the past few days, writing that "as soon as things stabilize (we hope they will), we'll look at turning on subscriptions again."

Distribution was initially outside the store: users could get the updated version from beeper.com/update on their phone because the company had not yet submitted the new version to the Play Store, claiming it needed to do final testing — which meant users were asked to sideload the update, riskier than downloading from the Play Store where apps undergo reviews and basic security checks. The app update went live on Google Play at 1:28 pm ET.

The update, which now prompts users to sign in with their Apple ID, also includes new features such as chats that open at the last unread message and an improved video player. In TechCrunch's tests, signing in with an Apple ID generated an Apple prompt noting the ID was being used to sign in with a device "near Los Angeles, CA," where the tester was not located; a "2FA submission error" appeared after updating the app but an uninstall/reinstall fixed it, a known bug. After setting up the new Beeper Mini, a message appeared on the tester's iPhone reading "Device Added to Your Account. A Mac now has access to iMessage," which offers a hint as to Beeper's fix.
