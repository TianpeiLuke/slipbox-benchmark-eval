# AI Developers Should Be Required To License Training Material

The NMPA's second demand is that AI developers be required to license the materials they use for training and keep records of them — not that music never be used for AI training, but that developers license it, not unlike how streaming services, bars and restaurants, or movie producers license music.

Many key players in the music industry and beyond have been adamant that using copyrighted works to train AI algorithms without authorization is copyright infringement, and a number of lawsuits have been launched against AI companies on these grounds. The NMPA's grounds for feasibility are practical: "Licensing musical works before training use is both required and practicable," the submission states, adding that "there are well developed processes in place for technology ventures to obtain free market licenses on a large scale." Scale is the operative condition, given that AI algorithms such as large language models are trained on countless millions or even billions of pieces of digital data. The NMPA points to existing activity as evidence that work on licensing "is already underway," citing news reports earlier in 2023 that UMG, along with Warner Music Group, were in talks with Google to secure licenses for artists' vocals and musical melodies to create AI-generated songs.
