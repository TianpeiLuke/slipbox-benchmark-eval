# Danny Elfman's Coachella Performances

The last person in the world who thought Danny Elfman would play Coachella was Danny Elfman — and then, in 2022, most movie buffs did a double take, Polygon wrote on 2023-10-13. As Elfman put it in a video for the music festival, "Coming out and doing something as myself, not as Jack [Skellington], was terrifying and exciting."

Coachella 2020 was ultimately canceled due to the COVID-19 pandemic, but Elfman was purely activated and kept creating, releasing his new music as a studio album, Big Mess, before eventually taking the Coachella stage in his now-notorious 2022 performance. As envisioned, the Elfman revue bounced from known tunes to weirdo grooves, all backed by animated graphics that paired well with extracurricular substances — though it was not a total upending of expectations, since there was still room for Mr. Skellington at Coachella.

In a flurry of post-show interviews Elfman comes off as rejuvenated. The show has not realigned the trajectory of his career — he went on to score White Noise for director Noah Baumbach and continues to tour with his symphonic works — but Polygon finds it hard to imagine him shaking the moment in the spotlight. The outlet flags that earlier that year a Rolling Stone report uncovered a 2018 sexual misconduct lawsuit filed against the composer, which he settled out of court; Elfman has vehemently denied the accusations as "vicious and wholly false," no additional accusations or investigations have come out in the wake of the story, and Disney and his longtime collaborators continue to work with him.

During the Coachella set Elfman proclaimed it was "my first time onstage as 'myself' in 27 years," likely referring to his time acting as Jack Skellington — though Polygon suggests that, looking back, Elfman as the frontman of Oingo Boingo does not seem to have been his true "self" either: the act launched at Coachella is not a return to form, it is the final form. Elfman said at the time there were no plans to tour the laser-lit Coachella act, although he busted it out earlier in 2023 at the North Island Credit Union Amphitheatre in California, and Polygon judges it entirely possible his rock star persona will explode out of nowhere with the rhythm of his percussive scores — the best-case scenario for a musician who never wants to be defined and is often trapped in the conventions of his business.
