# The FTT Token And The "Sam Coins"

FTT was a token created by Bankman-Fried and Wang and issued by FTX, and it was one of several "Sam coins" — a nickname for tokens Bankman-Fried was heavily involved in and owned a lot of, either personally or through Alameda. Wang described FTT as being "sort of equity" in FTX, a characterisation The Verge's reporter suspected some crypto lawyers would dispute but that would probably please the SEC.

The distribution was lopsided from the start. Alameda got its war chest — 60 percent to 70 percent of the initial supply — for free, while seed investors got FTT at 10 cents a coin and FTT first listed at $1 a coin. Ellison testified that Bankman-Fried felt $1 per coin was psychologically important and directed her to buy up FTT using Alameda if the price fell below a dollar.

The discriminating feature of the Sam coins was that their book value was not realisable: they were almost certainly worth less than the value displayed on the balance sheet, because trying to sell them all at once would crater the prices. Bankman-Fried nonetheless directed Ellison to put those coins on the balance sheets Alameda showed to lenders, even though she felt doing so was "somewhat misleading." FTT also figures in the origin story of Alameda's negative-balance privilege: Wang said Bankman-Fried at first told him the code change letting an account go negative was to pay for expenses for the FTT token, but once implemented the ability to go negative was used for other things, including trading and unlimited withdrawals of FTX's trading fees and customer money.
