# Alexa's Limitations As An On-The-Go Assistant On Echo Frames

The feature meant to set the Echo Frames apart is that Alexa is built in — and that is exactly the premise The Verge's reviewer attacked: Alexa has never been and still isn't a particularly good voice assistant when you're on the go, being fine at answering odd queries, setting timers and telling you the weather, but much better at controlling your smart home than at replacing Siri or Assistant.

It isn't that the glasses can't do things: you can now make non-Alexa calls and, on an Android phone, reply to text messages, and directions are possible but clunky. Alexa told the reviewer they'd get a notification on their phone to start, which meant tweaking notification settings to a less preferred option; once working, it suggested going to Bowling Green in Kentucky instead of the Manhattan train station five minutes away. Most things remain easier and faster on the phone, so the reviewer had little reason to use Alexa on the glasses, a problem exacerbated by having sunglasses instead of regular glasses. The dependency on the phone cuts both ways: with a stable Bluetooth connection and phone signal, Alexa works reliably on the go, but there's little offline usability, and the Alexa app must always be open and running in the background. A further, cultural objection: it's just weird to say "Alexa" in public — the wake word can be changed to Echo, which didn't help the reviewer — and this applies to nearly every pair of smart glasses with a voice assistant, because earbuds are a visual and cultural signal we all implicitly understand. Talking to midair with AirPods in reads as being on a call; talking with no phone or AirPods in sight got odd looks from strangers and confused looks from friends.
