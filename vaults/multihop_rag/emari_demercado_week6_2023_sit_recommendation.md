# Emari Demercado As A Week 6 2023 Sit At The Rams

CBS Sports listed Emari Demercado under "Sit Him" for Week 6 of the 2023 season at the Los Angeles Rams: the claim is that Demercado is a candidate for about 10 PPR points and perhaps four non-PPR points, and the warrant is that a likely split with Keaontay Ingram costs him carries while Ingram's size puts him at the goal line, leaving Demercado short of the touch volume the matchup would require.

The role grounds are the core. After James Conner left the previous game with an injury, Demercado played 100% of Arizona's snaps — the Cardinals could have used veteran Corey Clement to help but kept him on special-teams duty — and Demercado played fairly well, flashing solid speed and physicality on his runs with some patience blended in; he is not as big as Conner but played kind of like him. This week Arizona released a depth chart with second-year back Keaontay Ingram as the first stringer, and Ingram is expected to work with Demercado in the backfield. Ingram's record is poor: through 15 regular-season games over two years he has averaged 1.9 rushing yards per carry with a career-long run of 13 yards, and while he managed 4.4 yards per run in the past preseason no carry went more than 11 yards, with his 2022 preseason no better than his 2023 one. Demercado's own secure role is on passing downs — he has been Arizona's third-down player since Week 2, playing 74% of those snaps, with some reps in the two-minute offense — and that should remain part of his role going forward, though the column says that unless Ingram gets a hot hand early, Demercado should also be given a chance on running downs. Ingram is listed at 6-0 and 220 pounds, which is why the goal-line work may go his way if the Cardinals can somehow get there.

The matchup grounds close it. The Rams have taken a step forward as a decent run defense: over their past three games they are holding running backs to 3.6 yards per carry and are better than league average in yards before and after contact as well as in missed tackles. A rival running back has posted 13-plus PPR points in three of their past four games, but each of those backs had at least 20 touches — and no one looks like even a 12-touch back for the Cardinals. The breakdown lists Arizona at the Rams, Los Angeles favoured by 7, an over/under of 48.5, an opponent ranked 10th versus running backs, 11.6 projected points, a 20th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 48 rushing yards, six receptions, 40 receiving yards, one touchdown and 4.2 fantasy points per game. The author would rather start both Colts running backs, Javonte Williams, and Chuba Hubbard if Miles Sanders is out.
