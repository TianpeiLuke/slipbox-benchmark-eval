# West Indies' Powerplay In The T20 Decider Chase

West Indies reached 39-2 at the end of the powerplay chasing 133 in the deciding T20I, after a flying start from Johnson Charles was checked by two England wickets.

Reece Topley found some early inswing to the right-handers in the first over: Brandon King survived an LBW appeal off the first ball, which was missing leg, before another inswinger to Charles ran away for four leg-byes, leaving 7-0 — every boundary hurting with such a low target. In the second over Charles, who had been player of the match when West Indies beat England at the T20 World Cup 11 years earlier with 84 off 56 balls, pulled Woakes for a big six with a hint of top edge and glided a boundary past short third man, taking it to 20-0. Topley then had King caught for 3 by Buttler at mid-on off a slog, 20-1. In the third over Nicholas Pooran launched his second ball over midwicket for six and England thought he edged the next to the keeper but ran out of time to review, leaving 30-1 with West Indies needing 103 from 17 overs. Woakes bowled Pooran for 10 at 33-2 — UltraEdge showed Pooran had edged an earlier Topley ball, but it did not matter as he tried to ride the bounce of an awkward delivery that cramped him for room, took the inside of an angled bat and ricocheted onto the stumps. The live blog noted England needed early wickets to have a realistic chance and that one more in the powerplay, before bringing on Adil Rashid, would put them right back in the game. Topley continued in the fifth over for just three runs — sensible, since the spinners would do most of the work after the powerplay, and he had quietly had a good series as comfortably the most economical seamer on either side — and in the sixth Charles mistimed a short ball from Woakes just short of Moeen in the covers, with Moeen doing well to save the boundary, capping a good England fightback in which only nine runs came in three overs.
