# Michigan's Counterclaim That Rivals Shared Its Signs

Michigan's counter to the sign-stealing case is that it is hardly alone in trying to decipher opponents' signals, and it has shared evidence with the Big Ten and the NCAA showing that staff members at Ohio State, Purdue and Rutgers exchanged intelligence on the Wolverines' signals before the previous year's Michigan-Purdue Big Ten Championship Game. The Athletic reported that documents breaking down Michigan's signals for numerous plays were compiled by Purdue staffers ahead of that game based on information provided by Ohio State and Rutgers football personnel. The person who supplied the documents told The Athletic that he spent 10 to 12 hours per week trying to decode signals from game video, and that sharing decoded signals with other schools is a common practice.

The counterclaim does not dispose of the charge, because it attacks the wrong premise. The primary difference, per the same reporting, is that Michigan is accused of using in-person scouting — prohibited under NCAA rules for nearly 30 years — to acquire its information, rather than decoding from video.

Ohio State denied the allegation outright. Asked about the claim that Ohio State had given information about Michigan's signs to Purdue before the 2022 Big Ten championship game, coach Ryan Day said, per the Columbus Dispatch: "I can tell you this right now that nobody here did any of that. We went through and made sure we asked all the questions and got our compliance people involved. None of that came back at all. I can answer very strongly that that did not happen." Separately, Yahoo Sports reported that Day and his family have no connection to the Big Ten and NCAA investigations into Michigan; a weekend report had suggested Day may have had some involvement in the probe into the Buckeyes' arch-rivals, but the NCAA told the Big Ten there are no ties that it knows of between the Ohio State coach and the investigation.
