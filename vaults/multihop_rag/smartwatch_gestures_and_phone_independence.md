# Smartwatch Gestures And Phone Independence

The Verge's reviewer argued that double tap points toward a smartwatch future independent of phones — devices that, instead of triaging notifications and calls, handle them entirely. Her grounds are her own changed behaviour over a few weeks: the combination of glanceable apps, widgets, Siri, and double tap has started to change how she uses her Apple Watch, and even with double tap's existing limitations she can do more while on the go without her phone, reaching for it much less, and when she does, for much more intentional purposes like reading, shopping, or watching a video.

A concrete example supplies the warrant. She never used to use the more analog, aesthetically pleasing watchfaces because it meant sacrificing her complications; with double tap she can use those watchfaces now, because all she has to do is flick her wrist and double tap to view her widgets, and if she wants to launch an app she can ask Siri to open it. The gesture, in other words, removes the reason a display choice used to cost her functionality.

She keeps the claim hedged. It does not always work that seamlessly: sometimes the primary action is not what she wants, and eventually she reaches a point where she has to use her other hand — in the watchface example, once Siri opens the app she may still have to scroll with the digital crown to reach the information she needs. But it is not hard to imagine future iterations of double tap that both scroll and select: something not quite as comprehensive as Assistive Touch, but striking a finer balance between simplicity and customizability without relying too much on Apple trying to figure out what you want to do. She also notes you can already use multiple fingers for the gesture, so other iterations are easy to imagine.
