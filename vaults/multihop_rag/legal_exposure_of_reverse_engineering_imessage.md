# The Legal Exposure Of Reverse-Engineering iMessage

Beeper's co-founder argues that reverse-engineering iMessage is legally protected, pointing to a provision in copyright law — the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, DMCA 1201 F — which says that reverse-engineering for the purposes of interoperability is protected.

The technical grounds are that Apple may not be able to simply block Beeper Mini texts automatically if it wanted to take action against the company, though the co-founder admits how Apple will react is still uncertain. The legal protection is not decisive either: the DMCA provision will not necessarily prevent Apple from sending Beeper a Cease and Desist, and Apple previously sued spyware maker NSO Group to block it from using Apple's services, so it could likely make a legal case here as well if it chose to.

What may hold Apple at bay is regulatory timing rather than copyright law. The Digital Markets Act, a law in Europe, says big tech companies will have to have an interoperable interface for their chat networks; and there are also stirrings of antitrust efforts in the U.S., where Apple is under federal scrutiny, which could make for bad timing to target Beeper.
