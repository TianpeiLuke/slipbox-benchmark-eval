# Patrick Kane's Fit With The Detroit Red Wings

Detroit's case for Kane rests on reuniting him with a former linemate. When Kane was traded from the Blackhawks to the Rangers in the spring it was not just to join a playoff contender but to reunite him with former Chicago teammate Artemi Panarin, with whom he created magic during Panarin's first two NHL years, leading to a Hart Trophy and Art Ross Trophy for Kane in 2015-16. The Red Wings do not have Panarin, but that summer they acquired another of Kane's Blackhawks wing partners in Alex DeBrincat — Kane was DeBrincat's main set-up man when the current Red Wings star was potting 40 goals a season, and Kane had called DeBrincat's trade from Chicago to Ottawa "disappointing" and "crushing," so Detroit would give him another shot at playing with the talented sniper.

The circumstances also fit. Elliotte Friedman said: "I'm really curious about the Detroit one. They have the cap room, they have the space on the roster, they're doing very nicely so far this season. As we look on Sunday night, they're sitting there very much in contention in the Atlantic Division and the Eastern Conference. So, I definitely think there's a lot of people wondering about Detroit and whether or not he ends up there."

The financial and competitive picture completes the argument. The Red Wings have the fifth-most cap space in the league, so money is not an issue; it had been assumed one of Buffalo, Ottawa or Detroit would take the last step out of its rebuild and become a contender, and through the early going it looked like the Wings were that team — so adding Kane could be just the move to solidify their spot as a playoff contender.
