# Beyond Antibiotics: The Emerging Therapeutic Arsenal

Because bacteria's rising resistance is making antibiotics obsolete, some scientists argue the response must be to look beyond traditional antibiotics for new weapons that will not themselves fuel the rise of superbugs — an emerging arsenal of viruses that kill bacteria, CRISPR, designer molecules and cell-slicing enzymes.

The warrant joining the claim to that ground is the hope that these experimental treatments, some of which have been tested in patients, will kill superbugs without promoting resistance in the way that antibiotics do. "The vision, for me, is that we move beyond antibiotics and really just see a much broader palate of options," Chase Beisel, leader of the RNA synthetic biology research group at the Helmholtz Institute for RNA-based Infection Research in Germany, told Live Science. The argument carries a scope condition: until these new therapeutics are ready for prime time, the world still needs to curtail its overuse and misuse of antibiotics, which experts say is speeding up the rate at which these lifesaving drugs become obsolete.
