# Reliance O2C and Exploration Business Economics

RIL's traditional businesses — oil-to-chemicals (O2C) and exploration and production (E&P) — still account for 45-50 per cent of RIL's consolidated EBITDA, but they behave differently from the consumer businesses and attract a different valuation.

The O2C business spans expansive refining and petrochemicals, and its performance can be volatile depending on trends in crude oil prices and refining margins. In times of high crude oil prices, higher refining and petchem margins have helped overall profitability; conversely, when crude prices fall, refining and petchem cracker margins have been on a falling spree, adding pressure to overall profit performance. Business Line notes that Reliance's O2C business has best-in-class margins and may not be comparable to Indian refiners, given its scale of operations, efficiencies and export focus.

The valuation consequence is that, while O2C remains a significant part of the company, it will not attract the same high valuation as RIL's consumer-facing businesses. Based on the value assigned to the segment by analysts, its overall contribution to RIL's valuation has not changed much from the roughly $75 billion at which it was estimated when stake sale talks with Saudi Aramco were ongoing in 2019; scope for an increase in the medium term can arise from expansion in the petrochemicals business. The E&P business is small relative to Reliance's overall size, with analyst estimates for its value accounting for less than 10 per cent of overall RIL value.
