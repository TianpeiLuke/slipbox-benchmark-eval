# Snap Map As A Firsthand Record Of Gaza

As of November 3, 2023, the world was watching the humanitarian crisis in Gaza unfold in real time through firsthand accounts documented on, of all places, Snapchat. Amid calls for a ceasefire, TikTok and X (formerly Twitter) users were urging followers to view the devastation of Gaza for themselves on Snap Maps, which has displayed hot spots throughout northern Gaza since the Israeli airstrikes started in October 2023. You do not need an account to view Snap Map — the desktop version of the map is publicly accessible. "Snap Maps is a WEALTH of real-time information and is an important source for all journalists who aren't on the ground right now," X user inejmydarling posted.

Snap did not provide TechCrunch with hard data, but confirmed that since October 7 the company has seen a "moderate" increase in submissions to public Stories from Gaza, and said that more people from around the world are viewing content from the region. In the weeks since Israel's blockade of the territory began, screen recordings of the map, which displays bright red hotspots throughout northern Gaza, have been shared online.

The content itself is ordinary-hours documentation: in recent videos posted from Gaza City on Thursday night, users recorded bombings from their windows, and videos posted from the previous morning showed smoke rising from remaining buildings, demolished neighborhoods and people crowding around medical vehicles for treatment. As one X user wrote on October 29, 2023: "I'm begging you guys to please PLEASE check snap maps. Look at the difference between the living conditions just a few miles away. The people of Gaza are posting these publicly for us to see and share. Please listen to them. These are all from today. You can check yourselves."
