# Dodger Stadium: Los Angeles Dodgers Postseason Atmosphere

Dodger Stadium ranked No. 2 among the eight remaining 2023 playoff teams' home-field environments in The New York Times' 8 October 2023 ranking, on the strength of its late-inning intensity and sheer size. It opened in 1962, has a capacity of 56,000 and drew an average attendance of 47,371 in 2023.

Its memorable postseason moment is Kirk Gibson's walk-off homer in Game 1 of the 1988 World Series: with two outs and a runner on in the ninth, the Dodgers down one and down to their last out, Gibson hobbled toward home plate against Hall of Fame A's closer Dennis Eckersley with an injured left hamstring and a balky right knee. He fouled off Eckersley's first offering and almost fell over, fouled off a couple more, worked the count full, then pulled an outside pitch into the right-field seats and somehow made his way around the bases — one of the most badass moments in World Series history.

The caveats and the case both hold. All the jokes about traffic still making its way into the parking lot in the second inning are justified, and the "intensity" of baseball in L.A. isn't the same as in St. Louis or Boston or any other city where athletes are the biggest celebrities in town. But by the middle innings the stadium is packed, and by the late innings of playoff games the place has the over-the-top intensity of a Michael Bay car chase, with Mookie Betts as Bumblebee; more than one opposing pitcher that season had to tweak his PitchCom device to deal with the late-inning noise from a ballpark that once again held the largest total attendance in baseball. "You've got a four-deck stadium," Freddie Freeman said that summer. "It's the only one in baseball. You've got 50,000 people, every single night here. For them to go out of their way to make you feel good when you're doing your job, it means a lot. I do appreciate it. It does make you feel good inside and also just means you're doing your job pretty good." The stadium DJ is a maniac, with the volume set at a level that can't possibly meet OSHA standards and some twisted desire to have noise blaring at all times, and there is the intimidation factor of an iconic ballpark that feels like no other in baseball — Dodger Stadium knows what it is and uses every bit of it to its advantage.
