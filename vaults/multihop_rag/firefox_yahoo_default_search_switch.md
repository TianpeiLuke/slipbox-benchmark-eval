# Firefox's Yahoo Default Search Switch, As Recounted By Mitchell Baker

On November 1, 2023, Mozilla CEO Mitchell Baker provided a defense of Google's quality as a search engine, even in "competitive" environments, by recounting how Mozilla "failed" when it switched Firefox's default search engine from Google to Yahoo. Baker was providing a recorded deposition for Google's defense, and she noted that Mozilla's users apparently wanted and expected Google.

The backstory: Yahoo inked a deal with Mozilla back in 2014 to pay the browser maker $375 million annually to be the default search engine on Firefox, at a time when Google was offering $276 million, said Baker. TechCrunch's verdict was that it was a bad deal.

Baker's account of why, according to Bloomberg: "I felt strongly that Yahoo was not delivering the search experience we needed and had contracted for." Yahoo had promised to reduce the number of ads and offer less user tracking, she said, but ended up gradually serving users more ads anyway. "The number of users who stayed with Firefox declined noticeably during the years when Yahoo was the default," said Baker.
