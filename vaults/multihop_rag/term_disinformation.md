# Disinformation

## Definition

Disinformation is false or misleading content spread online — distinct from the illegal-content category (like terrorism or hate speech) in that, per the corpus's account of EU regulation, disinformation "is not illegal in the EU per se," yet still counts as a *systemic risk* that large platforms must assess and mitigate.

## Context

The corpus's central storyline is the EU's escalating regulatory pressure on X (formerly Twitter) over disinformation tied to the Israel-Hamas war: the Commission's October 2023 warning and formal information request to Musk accused X of being used "to disseminate illegal content and disinformation" following the Hamas attacks, and a EU-commissioned study later found X had "the largest ratio of mis/disinformation posts" among major platforms.
Because X is a Digital Services Act (DSA) "very large online platform" (VLOP), the obligation to mitigate disinformation risk is legally binding on it even though the underlying falsehoods are not themselves illegal; the EU's voluntary Code of Practice on Disinformation (which Musk pulled X out of earlier in 2023) is treated by the Commission as a signal of DSA compliance on this exact risk.
Disinformation also surfaces as a generative-AI worry: Suleyman warned DeepMind's ethics board that AI "could lead to an explosion in disinformation," EU commissioner Vera Jourova flagged AI-generated disinformation as an election threat, and early fears that ChatGPT would be used "to generate disinformation on a massive scale" pushed European policymakers toward mandating security assessments of generative AI products.
Real-world examples in the corpus include a video-game clip falsely presented as Hamas missile footage circulating on X, and misinformation described as "rampant on social media" during the Gaza crisis, prompting some users to turn to Snap Map for unmediated, firsthand footage instead.

## Key Characteristics

- **Not illegal per se in the EU** — disinformation is treated as a systemic risk to be mitigated, not content that platforms are legally required to remove the way they must remove illegal content.
- **Distinct from "misinformation" but often paired with it** — the corpus uses both terms (e.g., "mis/disinformation posts," Mozilla's content-policy list of "misinformation" as a harm category), generally without a sharp technical distinction, though disinformation carries a stronger connotation of deliberate falsehood or manipulation (e.g., "Kremlin propaganda").
- **A VLOP systemic-risk obligation** — under the DSA, platforms designated VLOPs (like X) must proactively assess and mitigate disinformation risk, which is what makes an EU formal-information request or investigation possible even absent an illegal-content finding.
- **Measurable and comparable across platforms** — the EU's Code of Practice enabled a pilot study producing "structural indicators" (e.g., how easily disinformation content is found, how much engagement it gets) that let regulators rank X as worst among mainstream platforms.
- **A named risk of generative AI** — beyond social-media falsehoods, the corpus repeatedly frames disinformation as a downstream risk of AI text/image generation (ChatGPT, deepfakes), motivating both EU AI-Act-era safeguards and industry-side labeling commitments.
