# A Presidential Veto Of An ITC Import Ban Is Extraordinarily Rare

Experts told The Verge that Apple getting a presidential veto of the ITC ban would be like lightning striking the same place twice. "It is extraordinarily rare for the President to overturn a decision from the International Trade Commission and in my view, [it's] unlikely in this particular case," said Andrei Iancu, co-chair of the Council for Innovation Promotion and partner at Sullivan & Cromwell LLP, who was director of the US Patent and Trademark Office from 2018 to 2021.

The grounds are the standard for a veto: a company usually has to show a public interest or health policy basis, and that isn't the case here. "It's unlikely that the import exclusion order will be disapproved by the USTR and the president," said Smith Brittingham, partner at Finnegan, Henderson, Farabow, Garrett & Dunner, LLP. "The only real answer would be, 'Well, it's Apple.'" The apparent counterexample is handled rather than ignored: in 2013 then-President Barack Obama did veto an import ban on the iPhone after Samsung accused Apple of infringing its cellular data patents. But according to Ben Levi, Apple then had arguments it lacks now — the patents were considered standard and essential, so Apple could raise fair, reasonable, and nondiscriminatory licensing (FRAND) issues, and the Obama administration was concerned the ban would give Samsung "undue leverage"; the ban also only affected the iPhone 4 and older iPad models, since newer iPhones didn't use an infringing chipset. In the Apple Watch case Apple has none of those arguments. "In that case 10 years ago, Apple really rolled the dice and pulled the rabbit out of the hat. It's very unlikely for them to do that again," Levi said.
