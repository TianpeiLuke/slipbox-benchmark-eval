# Theory: A Financial Mismatch At OpenAI

The proposition is that some major mismatch in the financial department was the cause for dismissal. Could OpenAI be losing even more money than Altman admitted or projected? It seems impossible, TechCrunch writes, but the costs of running this operation have no precedent, nor really does the operation itself.

A variant, offered as purely speculative: what if Altman had been secretly pursuing an internal project, perhaps at significant cost, against the advice of the board and without the necessary safety measures that probably should accompany such research? It sounds a little wild, but firing your CEO like this is also a little wild.

What weighs against the family of financial theories is the difficulty of concealment: it is hard to imagine what Altman could have kept from the board and CTO that would be so damning. A separate possibility is that Altman was making personal investments in a way the board disagreed with — with OpenAI poised to be a kingmaker in the field, he would certainly be in a position of power. One would think that, as an ideologically driven person already rich beyond belief and at the head of the world's leading AI company, Altman would have risen beyond having to do this kind of side deal, or at the very least that scrutiny on him and those close to him would prevent them. But one can never be sure.
