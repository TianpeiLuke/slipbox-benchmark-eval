# Movie Theaters' Pivot To Alternative Content

The mechanism that turned the once declared-for-dead multiplex into the go-to place for stars previously at home on Netflix is a supply problem meeting a format opportunity. When studios began diverting some of their titles to streaming platforms, movie theaters began thinking harder about how they could fill their screens — a question exacerbated in autumn 2023 by an actors strike that led to the postponement of big releases like "Dune: Part Two." Movie theaters are increasingly not just a marquee of movie showtimes but a big-screen stage for a variety of visual media: BTS released a concert film earlier in 2023 with higher ticket prices and limited showtimes, and the Metropolitan Opera has for years done popular live broadcasts in theaters.

Concert films are not themselves new — just the month before, the Talking Heads classic "Stop Making Sense" returned to theaters for a decades-later encore — but "The Eras Tour" heralds something new and potentially game-changing in the movie industry. The scope condition is important: few acts can do what Swift and Beyoncé can, and their expected success is unlikely to be replicated. Still, "The Eras Tour" could be the start of an expansion of what a movie theater can be — think the Sphere, only much cheaper and in most towns.

Greg Marcus states the underlying business redefinition: "You could say we're in the movie business, but really we're in the getting-together-with-other-people business. The more we do of it, the more the customers will think about it and the more talent will go: This is something I could do."
