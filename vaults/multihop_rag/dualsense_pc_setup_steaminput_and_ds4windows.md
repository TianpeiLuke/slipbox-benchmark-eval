# Setting Up A DualSense On PC

Using the DualSense on a PC is possible but a little complicated: it connects over Bluetooth for a wireless connection, yet games that do not support the DirectInput controller API likely will not recognize it as a controller. Plugging the controller into your PC might solve that, and there are other ways around the barrier if playing wirelessly matters to you.

The first route is Steam, which supports the DualSense through its SteamInput API — that makes Sony's controller a first-class citizen in Steam's eyes, right next to the Xbox controller, and plenty of games should offer no-fuss DualSense support. For PC games on other services such as PC Game Pass and the Epic Games Store, where you might experience middling compatibility, the fix is to add them as a "non-Steam game" within Steam: click the "Add a Game" option in the bottom left corner of the Steam client, then find the executable (.exe) file in your storage. The second route avoids Steam entirely: download an app called DS4Windows along with some drivers, which ensures game compatibility on PC by emulating the DualSense as an Xbox controller, and which also gives you a host of settings to customize button mapping. For working out which PC games support the DualSense's haptics and adaptive trigger features, and the steps to get them working, Polygon points readers to the site PCGamingWiki.
