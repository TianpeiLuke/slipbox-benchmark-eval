# Alexa Kids Data Handling And The Echo Pop Kids Launch

Amazon says it is not training its LLM on kids' answers, and that "Explore with Alexa" and any future LLM-backed features will continue to follow the same data handling policies as "classic Alexa" — the non-AI Alexa. In practice that means the Alexa app will include a list of the questions asked by kids in the household, for those with a kids' profile, along with the response Alexa provided; that history can be stored or deleted either manually or automatically, depending on the account's settings. These statements were made by the company to TechCrunch and reported on 25 October 2023, alongside the launch of the feature.

The launch also brought hardware. The new Echo Pop Kids speakers became available for purchase at the same time, starting at $49.99 in the U.S., in two new designs — Marvel's Avengers and Disney Princess — that feature corresponding character themes, letting kids hear a greeting, fun fact or joke about an Avenger or Disney Princess in keeping with the theme. Both include six months of access to the Amazon Kids+ subscription service, which, beyond "Explore with Alexa," also offers a range of kid-friendly games, apps, books, videos and more, including custom Alexa themes. Buying a Kids device is not a precondition for the feature, however: "Explore with Alexa" works on any device set to kids mode, or any communal family device, if parents have set up their kids' voice ID.

Two scope limits attach to the launch as described. The experience is available in English only at first, with internationalization further down the road. And the offline arrangement is framed as a stage rather than an endpoint — Amazon eventually wants the generative AI experience integrated at runtime for both kids and adults, while knowing it needs to proceed carefully, especially with kids: "We do want to integrate an LLM in runtime in a more protected way than we would integrate it for adults…However, this approach lets us iterate and figure out the right ways to get both safe and delightful content outputted from the LLM for kids," says Venkataswamy. On the adult side, he says Amazon was preparing to launch an AI-powered "Let's Chat" Alexa experience later that same year.
