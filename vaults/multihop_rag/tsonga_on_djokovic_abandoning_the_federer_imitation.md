# Tsonga On Djokovic Abandoning The Federer Imitation

Jo-Wilfried Tsonga argued in December 2023 that Novak Djokovic started out trying too hard to be like Roger Federer and Rafael Nadal, and sent his career into overdrive only when he embraced his true nature: the claim is that authenticity, not imitation, is what made Djokovic shine, and the warrant is that a divisive but honest persona earns appreciation where a borrowed one does not.

The former world No. 5 told the Generation Do It Yourself podcast that Djokovic longed to be like his great rivals: "I consider that he had a period where he didn't want to be himself. He wanted to be Roger Federer or Rafael Nadal, when perhaps he should have remained himself all along." Tsonga's grounds for saying the change worked are about reception rather than results: "I think today he is himself. And even if it is divisive, I think that's why it is appreciated by many people. Because now he's completely honest."

Tsonga identified the specific trait Djokovic had been suppressing, and argued the suppression was the mistake: "He wanted to get away from this warrior image that he has, because he is a Serb, who experienced difficult things in his childhood. So he's a warrior, he should have assumed it from the start. I think he would have been loved for that."
