# MADA And RSA: Google's Two Android Distribution Agreements

Google binds Android device makers through two distinct contract types, described at trial on November 8, 2023 by Jamie Rosenberg, a Google employee who focuses on Android and Google Play, testifying in Google's defense. A mobile app distribution agreement (MADA) requires Android smartphone makers — like Samsung or Oppo — to pre-load a bundle of 11 Google apps on the device, including Search, Chrome and Play. Rosenberg's discriminating point about MADA is that the pre-loaded apps do not have to be the default choices.

A revenue share agreement (RSA) is the second and separate instrument. RSAs run with smartphone makers and also with wireless carriers such as Verizon, and unlike MADAs they do require the counterparty to set Google search and the Chrome web browser as defaults. So the boundary between the two is pre-installation versus default status: MADA buys presence on the device, RSA buys the default slot.

Rosenberg defended the arrangement on two grounds — that Google apps like Search are "best in class," and that the RSAs motivate other companies to make or sell more Android devices. He framed the broader competitive setting by saying the competition between Google and Apple is "as intense as it gets," per Bloomberg.
