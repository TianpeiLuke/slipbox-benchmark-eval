# Disabling The SpO2 Sensor As An Import Ban Workaround

According to both Smith Brittingham and Ben Levi, Apple could redesign the Apple Watch to avoid the infringed patents until the appeals process is done — theoretically, all Apple has to do is push out a firmware update that disables the SpO2 sensor, and then it could go right back to importing Apple Watches for sale.

The entry-level SE points the way, since it is unaffected by the ban because it doesn't have blood oxygen sensing capabilities; the import ban wouldn't affect every Apple Watch. The Verge also noted the limits of the damage: while the Apple Watch is a popular product making up roughly 30 percent of the smartwatch market, Apple won't go under if the watch is out of commission in just the US — it will hurt to lose such an important market, but Apple can still sell all three of its Apple Watch models abroad. There are indications Apple is actively pursuing the workaround: Bloomberg reports that Apple engineers are scrambling to alter the blood oxygen algorithms on the watches, and quotes an unnamed Apple spokesperson saying the company plans to submit a workaround to the US customs agency to get the product back on shelves. Apple says it is exploring both legal and technical options to get around the ban; the accompanying photo is credited to Amelia Holowaty Krales / The Verge.
