# Project Hug: Google's Developer Incentive Program, As Characterized By Both Sides

Project Hug is an initiative that forms part of the Google Games Velocity Program, and it is a second set of agreements Epic will highlight — those between Google and Android app developers themselves, as distinct from Google's agreements with device makers. Its defining feature is contested, and the two characterizations mark the boundary of the concept.

Epic will cite it as an example of where Google paid competitors not to distribute their apps on Android outside of the Play Store, by targeting those developers that were most at risk of attrition from Play and then approaching them with a monetary offer. As an example, Epic will point to Activision Blizzard — the owner of King.com and maker of Candy Crush and Call of Duty — as one developer that wanted to go the route of opening its own app store. Google and Activision Blizzard then entered into a three-year agreement in January 2020, worth approximately $360 million, to keep the developer on Google Play, Epic will argue.

Google, however, will characterize this program as a way to incentivize developers to launch their apps on Android at the same time as they launch on iOS, and plans to dispute Epic's claims that it prevented developers from opening their own app stores.
