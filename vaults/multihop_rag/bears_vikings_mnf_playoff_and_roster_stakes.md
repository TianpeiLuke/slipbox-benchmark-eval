# Playoff And Roster Stakes In Bears-Vikings On Monday Night

Heading into Week 13 on Nov. 27, 2023, the game was the difference between the No. 6 and No. 7 seed for the Vikings, with larger ramifications: a loss would leave the Packers, Rams and Saints trailing Minnesota by only a half-game in the wild-card race, while a win would keep the Vikings well within striking distance in the NFC North. After the 12-10 loss Minnesota (6-6) dropped to the No. 7 seed in the NFC with those three teams a half-game back; a win would have put the Vikings in position to compete for the division crown down the stretch, but merely holding a playoff spot would now be a battle for Kevin O'Connell's squad. Chicago's own hopes remained extraordinarily distant at 4-8.

The individual stakes centred on Justin Fields. Sporting News called the final six games critically important for a quarterback fighting to prove he is the Bears' franchise passer, and noted Chicago had a strong chance of landing the No. 1 overall pick in April's draft thanks to the Panthers' ugly season. On the night Fields flashed the good and the bad — excellent on Chicago's opening drive, though it ended without points, then undone by missed throws and costly turnovers that let the Vikings hang around — but when it mattered most he was poised and sealed the Bears' first win over an NFC North opponent since 2021. The Bears entered the night holding the No. 1 and No. 4 picks in the 2024 NFL Draft and would remain in those slots with or without a loss, thanks to the strength-of-schedule tiebreaker.

Availability shaped both sides. The Vikings were officially without Justin Jefferson, holding out their star receiver until they returned from their bye in Week 14, as Adam Schefter reported that day; D'Onta Foreman remained out for the Bears along with cornerback Tyrique Stevenson. Dobbs came in having shown good command of the Vikings' offense in three games since a trade out of Arizona, with an especially strong connection with T.J. Hockenson. Conditions were 15 degrees in Minnesota with a feels-like of 2, making the roof at U.S. Bank Stadium useful, and Vikings legend Adrian Peterson was in the building to watch his former team.
