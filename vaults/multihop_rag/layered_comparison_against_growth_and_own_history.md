# Layering Comparisons Against Growth And A Stock's Own History

To avoid pitfalls while using relative valuation, add layers of comparison after the initial peer screen. The first step is the ordinary one, comparing stocks based on relative valuation. The second step is to assess how their valuation fares relative to growth prospects, since many other factors can justify different valuations. The worked illustration is that TCS has always commanded a valuation premium to peer stocks like Infosys, Wipro and HCLTech because of its superior margins and much more consistent growth, so just because the other stocks are cheaper on a PE basis does not mean they are cheap compared to TCS.

The third layer is to assess each company's valuation against its own long-term, that is 5-10 year, mean and median valuations, and to judge how the stocks fare relative to their own history. The precondition for the procedure is that a peer comparison has already been made; its purpose is to stop a cheaper headline multiple being read as a cheaper stock when growth quality or a company's own valuation history explains the gap.
