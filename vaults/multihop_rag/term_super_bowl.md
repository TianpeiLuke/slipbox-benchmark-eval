# Super Bowl

## Definition

In the corpus, the Super Bowl is the season-long reference point every team, player, and betting market is measured against — the game whose winners are still identified as "defending Super Bowl champions" months later (doc_0059) and which is called "the pinnacle for prop betting" and "the most prominent sports betting day of the year" (doc_0201).

## Context

The corpus treats the Super Bowl as the horizon every team's season is measured against: the New York Times' Pick Six column frames one-third of the regular season through "the biggest questions facing Super Bowl contenders," ranking the Chiefs, Eagles, 49ers, Bills, Bengals, Cowboys, Ravens, Lions, Dolphins, and Jaguars by their Super Bowl odds (doc_0316).
Franchise identity is tied to it retroactively — the Cincinnati Bengals are described as having reached "a Super Bowl appearance in 2021" as the marker of the team's turnaround (doc_0071), and Travis Kelce is introduced as having "helped bring his team to Super Bowl victory in both 2020 and 2023" (doc_0044), with the Kansas City Chiefs subsequently referred to simply as "the defending Super Bowl champions" (doc_0059).
A Christmas Day matchup between the 49ers and Ravens is previewed explicitly as a "potential Super Bowl sneak peek" (doc_0003), showing how regular-season games get framed as previews of the eventual championship.

## Key Characteristics

- **Season-defining stakes** — teams and players are described relative to it long after the fact ("defending Super Bowl champions," "two-time Super Bowl MVP") (doc_0059, doc_0003).
- **Odds and futures market** — sportsbooks release "next Super Bowl champion" odds shortly after the previous Super Bowl concludes, and those odds shift through the season as teams change (doc_0061).
- **Special-event betting draw** — sportsbooks single out "Super Bowl betting lines" alongside World Series odds and March Madness as flagship special-event markets carrying their own promotional bonuses (doc_0072).
- **Peak prop-betting event** — the Super Bowl is called "the pinnacle for prop betting" and "the most prominent sports betting day of the year," drawing "hundreds of prop bets" including exotic novelty props on the halftime show, the coin toss, and the color of the sports drink dumped on the winning coach (doc_0201).

## Background (external)

The corpus never defines "Super Bowl" itself — it only reports how teams, players, and betting markets relate to it.
Per Wikipedia, the Super Bowl is "the annual championship game of the National Football League (NFL), serving as the final game of each NFL season" (Wikipedia, *Super Bowl*, accessed 2026-09-02).
