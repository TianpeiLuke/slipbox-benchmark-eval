# The Road To El Dorado (2000): The Animated Buddy Comedy

The Road to El Dorado is a 2000 animated buddy comedy directed by Eric "Bibo" Bergeron, Don Paul and Jeffrey Katzenberg, running 1h 29m, with voice performances by Kevin Kline, Kenneth Branagh and Rosie Perez. Polygon's framing is that the film came out at the wrong time — during the transition point between the Disney Renaissance and the wave of crass CG movies ushered in by Shrek, a period the reviewer dubs the Beloved Failures era. It failed spectacularly in theatres, but home video turned it into a cult classic and a meme powerhouse, with many frames repurposed as reaction GIFs and meme templates; the reviewer argues the vivid facial expressions and body movement lend themselves to those formats while the movie itself is genuinely hilarious.

The setup: two runaway con men from Spain somehow end up in South America, where the locals of El Dorado believe them to be gods. The banter between pragmatic Tulio and idealistic Miguel — Kline and Branagh in easy comedic repartee, with married-couple-like shipping potential — is the film's engine, joined by snarky Chel (Perez), a local who wants out of the city. What distinguishes the trio from their era's animation is that they are morally grey lying characters, a rarity when heroes and princesses saved the day. Trying to leave with buckets of gold, they must first keep up the charade, which lands them in increasingly ridiculous situations: local festivities, a 2-vs-15 sports game, and finally defending El Dorado from the conquistador Hernán Cortés — each impossible feat pulled off again, with excellent banter and what Polygon calls a banging Elton John soundtrack. It is available to stream on Netflix.
