# Disney's Remake And Franchise Oversaturation

Polygon argues that even with much higher budgets and flashier visuals, the live-action remake fad Disney has ridden for the past decade contributes to a feeling of nostalgia oversaturation, and that the studio's many spinoffs and reboots do not help — some legacy projects have been good, but the sheer volume of them does not seem built to last.

The sharpest case is timing. One of the most recent Disney live-action projects announced is a rendition of 2016's animated hit Moana, a great movie that has not even really had a chance to build its own legacy: the kids who watched it when it first came out are mostly still kids. Disney, the argument goes, is assuming anything it makes will have an instant legacy without letting the nostalgia naturally brew. The second case is breadth. Disney's brand management of Star Wars, Marvel, Fox, the Muppets, and a host of other properties emphasizes the oversaturation, with characters like Baby Yoda and Tony Stark sitting alongside Mickey Mouse and Cinderella in theme parks, on Disney Plus promotional material, and on Bob Iger's X header. Disney plays up "Disney Magic" as part of its brand, but the article's closing question is what is left of that when every major franchise in America is lumped together as Disney magic.
