# Dracula 2000 (Dimension Films)

Dracula 2000 is Patrick Lussier's turn-of-the-millennium vampire film starring Gerard Butler, and Polygon argues that with the scars of nu-metal healed and Olivia Rodrigo's Paramore-infused pop tracks having gone full "vampire," now is the time to give Butler's half-bare-chest take on Dracula the respect it is due. It was the Oct. 6 pick in the 2023-10-10 Halloween Countdown, listed on Max.

Called maybe the most Dimension Films movie to ever bear the Dimension Films studio logo — with The Faculty a close second — the film follows a group of thieves, including of-the-moment faces like Omar Epps, Jennifer Esposito, and Shane West, transporting a stolen silver coffin from London to New Orleans in the belief there is treasure inside. When they find Dracula instead, they unleash overacted, canted-angled, seven-string-guitar-plucking hell. As Dracula hunts down local college student Mary Van Helsing, played by Justine Waddell, with whom he already shares a psychic connection, Mary's dad's new vampire-hunter assistant Simon, played by a young Jonny Lee Miller, is on his tail.

Critic Matt Patches writes that Butler, never more a heartthrob, plays Dracula in a mesmerizing, baroque manner — think Nicolas Cage doing Dracula if this year's Renfield was emo turn-of-the-millennium trash — and constantly bites unsuspecting B-list actors to add to his army of hot people, who fight the heroes in multiple obligatory scenes of wire-fu. Lussier cuts it all up like it is the video for "Freak on a Leash," which makes it objectively quite unlike most horror movies available today; enjoyment may vary depending on tolerance for Hot Topic goofs, but without it you cannot enjoy Lussier's sequel Dracula II: Ascension, starring Jason Scott Lee as a martial-arts-fighting priest.
