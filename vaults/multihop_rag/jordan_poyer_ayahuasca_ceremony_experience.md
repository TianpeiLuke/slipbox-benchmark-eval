# Jordan Poyer's Ayahuasca Ceremony Experience

Jordan Poyer set two intentions before his first ayahuasca ceremony — to understand himself better and to love better — and describes the experience as the highest version of himself lecturing him out loud for about an hour, The New York Times reported on 15 December 2023.

For the first hour after swigging it down he lay in a bean bag chair in silence, setting those intentions; eventually the shaman provided a second cup, and then, enchantment. "At first what it felt like was my soul left my body for a good two minutes," Poyer says. Initially it was unsettling. "People try to control it, but you can't. It took me about five or 10 minutes to figure it out. The ego has to die so the medicine can work. In order to let go and let the medicine do what it's supposed to do, we have to just breathe."

The content of the experience followed the intentions. First, highest-version Poyer addressed his desire to understand himself better: "He was basically saying, 'Jordan, look at your life, bro — what are you mad at?'" Poyer says. "It was what I needed to hear because I wasn't appreciating anything — not my wife, my daughter, my family, my house. It was all about me and what I wanted." Then it turned to loving better, and it was, he says, as if a Pandora's box opened on how to love. The remainder of the ceremonies, which lasted nine hours each, were about curiosity, energy and connectedness. "It's about how to be a good human," he says. "And it grounds you. Really grounds you."
