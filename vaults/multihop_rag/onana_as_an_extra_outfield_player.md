# Onana As An Extra Outfield Player

Erik ten Hag's model of André Onana treats the goalkeeper as a potential extra outfield player who can push progressively higher up the pitch — but subordinates that role to shot-stopping and makes it conditional on the opposition. "First the job of a keeper is to save, is to [guard] his goal, that's his main job and don't forget this," Ten Hag said on 28 October 2023. "But, yeah, he is a keeper who we can use as an extra player and if we are developing [more] he can be more progressive and even higher up the pitch — an extra player, so that's an advantage."

The idea comes from Uefa's technical report on Onana's display for Internazionale in May's Champions League final, which described him as a "holding midfielder" — a formulation that intrigues because it suggests an evolution of what Guardiola introduced to the domestic game, a No 1 in Ederson performing as a de facto extra defender to maintain fluidity along the backline. Onana was so advanced as to be a No 6 rather than, as the technical report posited, a "sweeper-keeper", able to take up residence in the middle third of his half as his team pushed deep into enemy territory.

The range over which the model holds is explicitly limited. Asked whether facing the champions had come too soon for Onana to function as "the extra man", Ten Hag said: "It also depends on the opposition, how strong is the press, for instance, and how good is the team around him. So if it's possible — in my philosophy he has to do it every game [but] it is not always possible, so you have to adapt and this what I make clear. Even if you're not playing well, if the team is not playing well, or you're not playing to the standards you expect, you still have to win — so find a way to win, be pragmatic."
