# Urban Heat Stress Disparity Across 481 US Cities

A new study published in One Earth showed that across 481 US cities, heat stress falls disproportionately on poor people and people of colour: "We found that the current disparity was quite consistent across cities: in more than 90% of the cities we considered, we found inequalities in heat exposure related to both income and race," said TC Chakraborty, an Earth scientist at Pacific Northwest National Laboratory and the study's lead author.

The context is that when record temperatures and heat domes envelop wide areas of the United States each summer, people experience these extreme heat events differently. Those who live in historically marginalised neighbourhoods — where discriminatory land use and housing policies allowed segregation and racism to flourish — still face today a greater risk from high temperatures and from the health effects of heat stress. This kind of information can help city leaders use better measures of heat disparities and protect the populations at greatest risk of heat exposure.
