# Manipulation Difficulty And Job Change

Tye Brady argues that automating manipulation is worth pursuing because it removes menial work rather than removing jobs — while conceding the change is sweeping. The grounds are first the difficulty: it's a very, very hard problem when you're talking about millions and millions of different objects, of all different sizes, scales and weights in dimensionality, where the ability to not only grasp the item but also identify it and look for damage on it is pretty incredible.

The claim follows from a stated goal: "I want to eliminate every menial, mundane, repetitive job out there. So, if I can automate that, and allow our employees to focus more on what matters, on higher level tasking, that's a total win." Brady then supplies the warrant himself and marks its limit, tying it back to the MIT partnership: "The way it's played out is, you replace a certain thing. So the jobs changed. The jobs exist, but it's a big sweeping change."
