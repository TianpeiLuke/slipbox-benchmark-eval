# Sony Music's "Artist-Led" Approach To AI

Sources close to Sony Music's HQ in New York have suggested to MBW that the company is taking an "artist-led" approach to its experiments with any AI platform that can manipulate the work or voice of its roster — which is offered as the explanation for why Sony did not join Universal and Warner in "Dream Track."

The grounds are that Sony did not decline on principle. MBW understands that Sony Music, the recorded music arm of Sony Music Group, had like Universal and Warner been in discussions for months with YouTube over potentially licensing the experiment, and the company had shown its own willingness to experiment with generative AI music platforms that same week via an announcement about a project with David Gilmour, The Orb, and AI company Vermillio. In the case of Dream Track, MBW understands, Sony first offered the opportunity to participate to a number of its artists, but this group of acts did not show enthusiasm about participating. MBW notes that if one of these artists was Bad Bunny, signed to Sony's The Orchard, the reluctance is easy to see: the Puerto Rican superstar had expressed fury the other week about a popular track on TikTok that had cloned his vocal stylings via AI.
