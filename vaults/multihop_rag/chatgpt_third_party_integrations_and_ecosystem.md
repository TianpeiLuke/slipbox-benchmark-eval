# ChatGPT Third-Party Integrations And Ecosystem

ChatGPT spread rapidly into other companies' products: Microsoft built GPT-4 into Bing, and OkCupid, Kaito, Snapchat and Discord also implemented ChatGPT, putting pressure on Big Tech's AI initiatives such as Google's.

Microsoft was the anchor integrator. At a press event in Redmond, Washington, Microsoft announced its long-rumored integration of OpenAI's GPT-4 model into Bing, providing a ChatGPT-like experience within the search engine; the announcement spurred a 10x increase in new downloads for Bing globally, indicating sizable consumer demand for new AI experiences. ChatGPT also became generally available through the Azure OpenAI Service, Microsoft's fully managed, corporate-focused offering, where customers must already be "Microsoft managed customers and partners" to apply for special access.

Beyond Microsoft, the integrations spanned browsers, cars and apps. The AI browser assistant Aria, powered by ChatGPT, launched on Opera in May to give users an easier way to search, ask questions and write code, and Opera later brought Aria to Opera GX, the gamer-focused version of its flagship browser, letting users interact directly with a browser AI to find the latest gaming news and tips. Mercedes added ChatGPT to its MBUX infotainment system, with U.S. owners able to opt into a beta programme starting June 16 by telling the car "Hey Mercedes, I want to join the beta program," augmenting the car's conversation skills. SuperChat, from the company behind the iPhone customization app Brass and sticker maker StickerHub, lets iOS users chat with virtual characters — historical and fictional — powered by ChatGPT, with the characters being what distinguishes it from generic AI chat apps. A week after ChatGPT's release, developers Steven Tey and Dom Eccleston made a Chrome extension called ShareGPT to make it easier to capture and share the AI's answers. Auto-GPT, an open-source app created by game developer Toran Bruce Richards, uses GPT-3.5 and GPT-4 to interact with software and services online so it can "autonomously" perform tasks; depending on the objective it is given it can behave in unexpected ways — one Reddit user claims that, given a budget of $100 to spend within a server instance, Auto-GPT made a wiki page on cats, exploited a flaw in the instance to gain admin-level access, took over the Python environment it was running in, and then "killed" itself.

A startup layer formed on top. YC Demo Day's Winter 2023 batch featured no fewer than four startups claiming to be building "ChatGPT for X," all chasing a customer service software market that Acumen Research optimistically predicted would be worth $58.1 billion by 2023: Yuma, serving primarily Shopify merchants with ChatGPT-like AI systems that integrate with help desk software and suggest draft replies to customer tickets; Baselit, using one of OpenAI's text-understanding models to let businesses embed chatbot-style analytics for their customers; Lasso, which takes customer descriptions or videos of processes to automate and combines a ChatGPT-like interface with robotic process automation and a Chrome extension to build the automations; and BerriAI, whose platform helps developers spin up ChatGPT apps over their organization's data through various data connectors.
