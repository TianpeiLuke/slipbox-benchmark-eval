# Leiderman's "There Are No Sacred Numbers" Deficit Argument

Leo Leiderman's claim is that a country at war is permitted to raise its fiscal deficit by whatever is judged to be the right amount, and that no particular deficit ratio is sacrosanct: "I mean a deficit of 3% of GDP, or 5%, or 8% - there are no sacred numbers."

His grounds are purposive. Besides the hope of financing part of the deficit through aid from the US — Globes noted that President Biden approved $8 billion in military aid that week — it is permissible to widen the deficit "in order to ensure that the economy functions properly, to carry out rehabilitation actions, and to prepare for recovery and a return to growth in the near future." His warrant is the precedent of the Covid pandemic: as was said then, the state has to do whatever it takes to achieve these goals.

The argument carries explicit conditions that keep it from being open-ended. It is important to make sure the growth in expenditure and in the deficit is temporary, until economic activity gets back to normal. Leiderman is also sure that various approved budget items do not support economic growth and need not be implemented in the new reality. On those terms, he argues, such a policy will be accepted with complete understanding by investors overseas and in Israel, and also by the credit rating agencies.
