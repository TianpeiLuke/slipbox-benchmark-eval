# Why 1989 Had To Abandon Country: Swift's Case For A Clean Break

Taylor Swift argued that a decisive break from her country sound was necessary rather than optional, and 1989 enacts that argument: after building her reputation in Nashville, the album jettisons the banjos in favour of insistent, needling beats and tales of bohemian nightlife — "a farewell to twang," as the New York Times called it. The album opens with a mission statement: the old Taylor can't come to the phone right now, she's dead.

Her grounds were about credibility rather than taste. "I don't have the option of making music that sounds just like what I've done before," she told Popcrush. "People will see right through it. They'll see that I was lazy." The warrant joining that claim to the decision is a refusal to hedge: her label begged her to add three country-leaning songs to the album, but she stood her ground, on the reasoning that "at a certain point, if you chase two rabbits, you lose them both."
