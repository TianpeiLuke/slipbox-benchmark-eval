# Darren Waller As A Week 6 2023 Start At Buffalo

CBS Sports listed Darren Waller under "Start Him" for Week 6 of the 2023 season at Buffalo, but only as a low-end starter: the claim is that Waller is a safe bet to lead the Giants in targets while everyone in the stadium will know it, and the warrant is that hoped-for volume is the only thing putting him ahead of comparable tight ends, since Buffalo can easily drape a second defender near him to force the Giants quarterback to throw elsewhere.

The volume grounds are qualified in both directions. In Week 5 Waller was a focal point of the offense, catching 8 of 11 targets for 86 yards at Miami, with four targets coming from Tyrod Taylor for a 3-31-0 line, all in the fourth quarter — a 33% target share from Taylor alone — and he could have had a bigger game, coming close to corralling some deep targets from Daniel Jones. Taylor has a career target rate of 22.4% to his tight ends, which is real nice; as recently as 2020 he leaned on Hunter Henry with the Chargers, and in two of his heyday years with Buffalo he worked tightly with Charles Clay, so he shouldn't be a detriment. On the season Waller has the highest target share on the Giants, but it is only 20.8%, and his average depth of target sits at 8.9, both numbers lifted by the Giants' decision to force-feed him targets the previous week, when he also ran more downfield routes than usual. If the Giants stick with that, Waller can meet some of his lofty expectations — but the author thinks that if Brian Daboll had it his way he would try to find explosive plays from all his receivers and spread the ball around.

The matchup grounds explain the ceiling. Buffalo got its first test against tight ends the previous week in Evan Engram and swarmed him, holding him to 4 of 8 targets for 28 yards; Engram is used similarly to Waller, which makes Buffalo's plan for slowing Waller down easy to carry over. Their pass rush should also affect the Giants' passing game and may force Waller to run shorter routes so he gets open quicker. The breakdown lists the Giants at Buffalo, the Bills favoured by 14, an over/under of 44.5, an opponent ranked 4th versus tight ends, 10.2 projected points, a seventh tight end ranking, and year-to-date totals of 23 receptions on 34 targets for 239 yards, no touchdowns and 9.4 fantasy points per game. The verdict places Waller in the same range as Kyle Pitts and Cole Kmet, ahead of them only because of the volume the author hopes he gets.
