# Which Teams Met With Ohtani And Who Remained In The Race

The field of teams pursuing Shohei Ohtani narrowed during the 2023 winter meetings, with Bob Nightengale of USA Today reporting on Tuesday, Dec. 5 that the Cubs' optimism had faded, leaving the Dodgers, Blue Jays, Angels and Giants as the remaining finalists, per Sporting News' tracker.

Earlier, on Friday, Dec. 1, Jeff Passan of ESPN reported the field was already growing smaller: little had been leaked about the top suitors beyond the usual suspects, but at least three teams initially believed to be in the running — the Mets, Rangers and Red Sox — were believed to be out, with the teams most likely still in the mix being the Dodgers, Cubs, Blue Jays and Angels, and the Giants' status unknown. On Sunday, Dec. 3, Morosi said it was unlikely the Blue Jays would land both Ohtani and Juan Soto: they had been among the most active names in both markets, and fans had dreamed of a lineup featuring Ohtani, Soto, Vladimir Guerrero Jr., Bo Bichette and George Springer, but Morosi reported they might land one of the two and would not add both that offseason.

On Monday, Dec. 4, Ken Rosenthal of The Athletic reported Ohtani was "believed" to have met with the Blue Jays at the team's complex in Florida, far away from the meetings in Nashville; the Blue Jays had been pursuing Ohtani and Soto in hopes of striking a deal for one of the two stars. The same day, Morosi reported the Braves were "engaged" in talks with Ohtani's camp, prompting Sporting News to imagine a lineup of Ohtani, Ronald Acuna Jr., Ozzie Albies, Austin Riley and Matt Olson. On Tuesday, Dec. 5, Heyman reported that even before Ohtani went to talk with Toronto he had a "positive feeling about Toronto," along with other cities and teams including the Dodgers, and that he had been linked to Toronto and Los Angeles throughout the process. Also on Dec. 5, per Alden Gonzalez of ESPN, Dodgers manager Dave Roberts told reporters the team met with Ohtani for two to three hours over the weekend, said Ohtani was "clearly" the Dodgers' top priority, added that the team was confident the two-time MVP would pitch again, and could not offer a timeline for the decision.
