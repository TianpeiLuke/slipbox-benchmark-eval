# Choosing A Power Bank For Phones And Tablets

Polygon's selection rule is simple: get a power bank that lists Power Delivery capability, noted as "PD" in the specs, and it should ideally match, or come close to, the peak charging speeds in watts of the devices you intend to charge with it.

The reason the rule is workable is that today's phones and tablets do not differ much at the top end. Some outliers such as Samsung's Galaxy S23 can recharge at 25 W or higher depending on the model, but many other Android phones and iPhones recharge over a wired connection at under 20 W — even less when charging wirelessly. Tablets cluster similarly: all iPads currently ship with 20 W power adapters, and so far tablets from other manufacturers such as Samsung and Amazon have not deviated far from that speed, so either of Polygon's two recommended banks should be a good fit. Two scope conditions matter. The batteries recommended for phones and tablets are also a good pick for the Nintendo Switch, but the Steam Deck needs more juice, as do some mightier laptops that can receive power over USB-C. Finally, whichever bank you choose, it never hurts to get extra USB-C-to-USB-C cables — Polygon points to a two-pack at Amazon for $15.99 so you can keep one at home and put the other in a bag you carry for everyday travel.
