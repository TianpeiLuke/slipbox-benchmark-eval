# Objections to Blue Halloween Buckets

The objection to blue Halloween buckets attacks the premise that visibly marking a child's disability is a benign act of awareness: some parents are concerned that blue buckets unnecessarily point out their child's disability, and could even make them a target for abuse. The National Autism Association recognised the movement as well, warning in a 2020 blog post that not all parents find the colour coding necessary.

The argument continued into the 2023 Halloween season, with some X users pointing out the controversy. "DO NOT BUY YOUR CHILDREN BLUE HALLOWEEN PUMPKIN BUCKETS," one X user posted on the Monday before Halloween. "This idea, aside from being horribly ableist, as it's intended to denote the child as 'different' (read: autistic) harms children w/dietary needs. Teal pumpkins = hypoallergenic needs/options & this idea muddies that message." That post adds a second line of attack: not only is the signal itself objectionable, it interferes with an existing colour-coded signal for allergy-safe treats.

A further objection grants the good intentions but denies that identification is what the children need. Another user posted: "I know this has the best of intentions ... But we don't need blue pumpkin buckets for Halloween. Non-verbal kids don't need to be identified during a fun holiday, they just want to participate like everyone else."
