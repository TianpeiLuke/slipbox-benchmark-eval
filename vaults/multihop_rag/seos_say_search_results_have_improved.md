# SEOs Also Say Search Results Have Improved

The strongest challenge to the article's premise is that Sullivan was not the only person insisting search results have improved significantly: out of the dozen-plus SEOs the writer spoke with at length, nearly every single one said search results are way better than they used to be — just objectively more accurate, more useful, more difficult to manipulate.

What gives that testimony force is the absence of an incentive to flatter Google. Except for Sullivan, these were not people with a reason to praise the company; if anything, these were folks who lamented how much harder it had become for them to take advantage of Google.

The effect on the writer was disorientation rather than conviction. This was not what she had been noticing, and certainly not what she had been hearing from friends and journalists and friends who are journalists. Were all of us wrong, or engulfed in some kind of Baader-Meinhof frequency bias delusion? Had she been researching a nonexistent problem? Were Google results actually amazing? Truly, she had lost the plot — was the premise of the piece completely off, and was she the asshole who deserved to be attacked by an alligator?
