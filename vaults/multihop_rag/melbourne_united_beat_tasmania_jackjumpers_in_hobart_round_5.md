# Melbourne United Beat Tasmania 90-82 In Hobart

Melbourne United withstood a hot start and finish from the Tasmania JackJumpers but were outstanding in between on the way to a 90-82 NBL victory at a sold-out MyState Bank Arena on the Friday night of round 5.

The league's top two teams locked horns and it was the JackJumpers who raced out of the blocks with a 17-4 start inside the opening five minutes, including three-balls to Jordon Crawford and Jack McVeigh, but United took control from there — outscoring Tasmania by 78 points to 49 after the first five minutes and leading by as many as 16 midway through the fourth quarter on the back of a three from Luke Travers (14 points, 13 rebounds). Tasmania, who had pulled off a remarkable comeback against Melbourne back in round two, got as close as four points in the final minute, but United held firm for a fifth straight win to stay on top at 7-1.

Dean Vickerman was celebrating his 300th game coaching in the NBL and was full of praise: "Tonight was a little bit like my career. We didn't start great but once we found our rhythm and especially the way we came out of the half and had a great third quarter, and held on down a few players at the end there. But we held on." NBA and NBL champion Ian Clark top-scored with 19 points, rookie Kyle Bowen added a career-best 17, and Jo Lual-Acuil also had 17 along with 10 rebounds. Jack McVeigh led Tasmania with 20 points and seven rebounds, Crawford contributed 16 points and four assists, and Milton Doyle 12 points and five assists.

The swing came through rebounding and shot selection. Tasmania coach Scott Roth was philosophical after giving up 18 offensive rebounds and losing the battle on the glass by 16: "We got a little uncharacteristic with our shot selection, which was a credit to them. Then they beat us up on the glass. It's just little things when you get in those kind of games that are going to make the separation points." United steadied after the JackJumpers' fast start once Lual-Acuil was introduced, closing the quarter outscoring Tasmania 17-4, and three-pointers midway through the second from Bowen and Clark put them up five; they still led just 43-42 at halftime on the back of 11 extra rebounds and 10 second-chance points. Captain Chris Goulding opened his account from downtown at the start of the second half, Clark hit his second long ball soon after to push the lead to nine and his third to double figures, and Bowen landed two surprise ones of his own, before Tasmania's trademark 14-2 run fell short.
