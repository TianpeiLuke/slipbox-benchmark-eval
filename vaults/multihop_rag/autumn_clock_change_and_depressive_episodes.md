# The Autumn Clock Change And Depressive Episodes

A Danish study from 2017, covering more than 185,000 people over 15 years, found that the transition from summer time to standard time was associated with an 11 per cent rise in depressive episodes — while the switch from winter to summer time was found to have no effect. The asymmetry is the point: the harm is attributed to the autumn change specifically, not to clock changes as such.

The study's authors offered a mechanism for that asymmetry: "This study shows that the transition from summer time to standard time was associated with an increase in the incidence rate of unipolar depressive episodes. Distress associated with the sudden advancement of sunset, marking the coming of a long period of short days, may explain this finding."
