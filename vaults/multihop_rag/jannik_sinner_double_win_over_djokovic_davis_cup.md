# Jannik Sinner's Double Win Over Djokovic In The Davis Cup

Jannik Sinner beat Novak Djokovic in both the singles and the doubles in the space of five exhilarating hours in Italy's Davis Cup semi-final against Serbia on the Saturday (Sunday AEDT), saving three consecutive match points on serve before winning 6-2, 2-6, 7-5 and then partnering Lorenzo Sonego to a 6-3, 6-4 doubles win, The Age reported on 24 November 2023.

Djokovic had held three match points in the singles rubber and was on the verge of completing a relatively workaday 2-0 victory for the Serbs. But for only the fourth time in his unparalleled career he was beaten from match-point up: after soaking up immense pressure throughout the deciding set, Sinner stared at 0-40 on his serve at 4-5, then proved absolutely nerveless, firing down an ace to save the third match point and immediately breaking Djokovic in the very next game amid wild Italian celebrations at the Palacio de deportes Martin Carpena before serving out. The win ended Djokovic's cherished unbeaten 21-singles winning streak in the Davis Cup. It was the 22-year-old's second victory over Djokovic in 12 days, reprising the win in the Turin group stages when he became the only man since Djokovic's Wimbledon final loss to Carlos Alcaraz to defeat the 24-time grand slam champion; world No.4 Sinner, coached by Australia's Darren Cahill, is the first player to topple Djokovic twice in the same season since Russia's Daniil Medvedev in 2019.

Both teams then drafted their top singles players into the doubles decider. Sinner was again magnificent and still looking ominously fresh after more than four hours on court, spraying massive forehand winners around liberally as he and Sonego outplayed a weary-looking Djokovic and Miomir Kecmanovic; Djokovic's serve was broken in the first set, his court positioning left a big hole for Sinner's forehand, and his weak backhand into the net off the Italian's second serve on match point seemed to sum up his disappointment. After telling fans to "shut up" earlier in the week, he again got irritated with the crowd, pretending to be the conductor to the Italian fans' deafening chorus — though he may be the greatest player who ever picked up a racquet, doubles looks an alien game to him. It was a crushing end to a season featuring three grand slam victories and an ATP finals showdown win over Sinner just six days earlier. Kecmanovic had earlier worn down Lorenzo Musetti 6-7 (7-9), 6-2, 6-1 in the tie's opening singles. The beneficiaries were Lleyton Hewitt's Australian side, enjoying a day's rest at their team hotel further down the Costa del Sol and watching the drama on TV; ahead of the final, the query was whether Italian captain Filippo Volandri would turn to Musetti, who battled a leg issue at the end of his loss, or one of Sonego or Matteo Arnaldi, while if Alexei Popyrin could get the job done in the No.2 singles then Australia would at minimum reach the doubles with a live and good chance, with 2022 Wimbledon champions Max Purcell and Matt Ebden to be unleashed.
