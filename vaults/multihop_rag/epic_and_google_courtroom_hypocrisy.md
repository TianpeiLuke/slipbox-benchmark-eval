# Hypocrisy On Both Sides Of The Courtroom

Each side's central talking point is undercut by its own conduct, so neither party's framing should be taken at face value. Epic says it is not in this for the money, only suing to end Google's monopoly — but Epic would surely profit if it does not have to pay Google's cut. Epic also looks set to argue that you are locked into Android after buying an Android phone, though switching back and forth between the platforms is easier than ever.

Google's talking points have the same problem. Google will say that every Samsung Galaxy phone has "a competing app store right there on the homescreen," even though Google apparently tried to quash that store too, fearing it could cost the company billions. Google was caught trying to play the China card in a pre-briefing with press, reminding journalists that Epic is 40 percent owned by Chinese company Tencent — even though Google itself allegedly considered teaming up with Tencent to eliminate the Epic threat. The parties cannot argue that in court, but Google can attempt to play the China card subtly: "Google may ask a witness once about Tencent's domicile," reads one of Judge Donato's orders.

In the same press briefing, Google suggested to journalists that Epic should have joined the company's "User Choice Billing" program if it wanted an alternative payment system, instead of suing — but that program did not exist until 2022, long after the lawsuit, and it only knocks 4 percentage points off Google's fee.
