# X As The Outlier Against Ofcom's Rules

X (formerly Twitter) is the one big outlier to Whitehead's generally encouraging read on industry cooperation, because its direction of travel runs against what the guidelines assume: Ofcom's guidelines specify that users should be able to easily block users, but Elon Musk has publicly stated his intentions to remove X's block feature.

The premise under attack is that the act's requirements can be met by platforms as they are currently evolving. The UK's efforts with the legislation long predate Musk's acquisition of Twitter, but it was passed as he fired large swaths of its trust and safety teams and presided over a loosening of moderation standards, which could put X at odds with regulators. Musk has clashed with the EU over similar rules and reportedly even considered pulling out of the European market to avoid them.

Ofcom would not be drawn on the specific case. Whitehead declined to comment when asked whether X had been cooperative in talks with Ofcom, but said the regulator had been "broadly encouraged" by the response from tech firms generally.
