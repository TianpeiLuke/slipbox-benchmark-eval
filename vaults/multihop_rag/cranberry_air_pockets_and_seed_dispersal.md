# Cranberry Air Pockets and Seed Dispersal

Every cranberry contains four air pockets, which is why the berries float when farmers flood bogs to harvest them — and the pockets serve a biological role: they enable the berries to float down rivers and streams to disperse their seeds.

The air pockets also make raw cranberries bounce when dropped on a hard surface, a good indicator of whether they are fresh. The dispersal route matters because the usual alternative is closed off: many other plants disperse their seeds via animals and birds that eat their fruits and excrete the seeds as they move around, but as anyone who has tasted them raw knows, cranberries are ultra-tart, so they have limited appeal for wildlife.
