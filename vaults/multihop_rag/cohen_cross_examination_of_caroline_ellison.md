# Cohen's Cross-Examination Of Caroline Ellison

Mark Cohen's cross-examination of Caroline Ellison on October 12th, 2023 aimed at the premise that her direct testimony was reliable — she had recounted many conversations held in person or on auto-deleting messaging platforms, and she had an incentive to flip on Bankman-Fried in the form of possible leniency at sentencing — but The Verge's reporter judged that the defense absolutely biffed it. In Cohen's disorganized cross-examination he mostly bored the jury; at one point two different jurors appeared to be asleep, and midway through the morning the reporter began wondering whether there was a mercy rule for cross-examinations.

The lines of attack were opened and dropped. Cohen established that Bankman-Fried had a much larger appetite for risk than Ellison, that the two reacted differently to stress, and that they took different approaches to media — Ellison avoided it while Bankman-Fried sought it out — without building on any of it. He drew out that Alameda had one accountant in 2021 and hired two more junior accountants in 2022, and had a problem retaining accountants, then dropped that too. He appeared to be trying to suggest the government had coerced Ellison's testimony by getting her to plead guilty to defrauding investors, conduct she could not have been involved in since she did not prepare materials for them; unfortunately she said she had had conversations with investors as part of their due diligence, and Alameda was in any case taking on losses from FTX to keep FTX's balance sheet pristine. Cohen also pressed the hedge he had blamed her for not taking out in his opening — hedging being long crypto by selling Nasdaq futures — which raised the question of whether the defense was that she should have taken more customer funds, or taken them sooner.

Small failures accumulated. Cohen asked Ellison to define what "buy on the way down" meant as though it were a term of art, which seemed to puzzle her, and at other times he seemed to forget what she had testified to and brought up things she had not said; like FTX employee Adam Yedidia before her, Ellison was fastidious about making sure a question was clear and her answer precise. She appeared at one point to be toying with him: having testified on direct about the token Luna, she noted when Cohen asked about Terra/Luna that she had only spoken about Luna, leaving him to fumble the relationship between the two. There were many sidebars, enough that several jurors looked entertained when one occurred, and in one the day before the prosecution complained that Bankman-Fried was visibly scoffing at Ellison's answers, per the court transcript. What Cohen was up against was strong direct evidence: fake balance sheets, one sent to crypto lender Genesis, after which a Genesis representative texted Ellison to say he had spoken to Bankman-Fried — strongly suggesting Bankman-Fried knew the contents of the fake balance sheet. Afterwards, the defendant's mother Barbara Fried engaged Christian Everdell in an animated conversation, gesticulating and clearly holding a strong opinion; Everdell walked off and Cohen talked to her for a bit.
