# Moneyline Odds Examples By Sport

Typical moneyline prices look the same across sports — a minus number on the favorite, a plus number on the underdog — but the shape of the market varies with the sport, most obviously where draws or large fields exist. In an NFL game between the Dallas Cowboys (-130) and the New York Giants (+110), the Cowboys are the favorites: a $130 bet on Dallas returns $100 profit if they win, while a $100 wager on the Giants earns $110 profit on an upset. An NBA game between the Los Angeles Lakers (-150) and the Miami Heat (+130) works the same way, with $150 on the Lakers profiting $100 and $100 on the Heat profiting $130.

Baseball and hockey illustrate slight favorites and even money. For an MLB game with the New York Yankees at -120 and the Boston Red Sox at +100, the Yankees are slight favorites — a $120 bet returns $100 profit — while the Red Sox are at even odds, so a $100 bet also returns $100 profit. For an NHL game with the Toronto Maple Leafs at -140 and the Chicago Blackhawks at +120, $140 on Toronto profits $100 and $100 on Chicago profits $120.

Soccer adds a third selection. In an MLS match between the Seattle Sounders (+110), the Draw (+230) and Atlanta United (+140), neither team is a clear favorite though the Sounders have slightly better odds: $100 on Seattle profits $110, $100 on the draw profits $230 if the match ends tied, and $100 on Atlanta profits $140. In soccer, the possibility of a draw is a common outcome to bet on, which is different from many other major sports.

Combat sports, tennis and golf round out the pattern. A UFC fight between Conor McGregor (-200) and Dustin Poirier (+170) pays $100 profit on a $200 bet on McGregor and $170 profit on a $100 bet on Poirier. A tennis match between Serena Williams (-150) and Maria Sharapova (+130) makes Williams the favorite, with $150 on her profiting $100 and $100 on Sharapova profiting $130. PGA Tour odds price individual golfers to win a tournament — Tiger Woods +500, Rory McIlroy +600 — so a $100 bet profits $500 or $600 respectively; in golf, with usually many players in the field, the odds can be much higher than in head-to-head matchups.
