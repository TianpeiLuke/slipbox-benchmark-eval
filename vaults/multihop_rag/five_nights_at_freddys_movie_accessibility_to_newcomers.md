# Is the Five Nights at Freddy's Movie Accessible to Newcomers?

Polygon's claim, published 2023-10-26, is that the Five Nights at Freddy's movie is not hard to follow for newcomers — but that newcomers may still be confused, and that the confusion is the movie's fault rather than a gap in fan knowledge.

The producer's framing invites the opposite conclusion. At a Blumhouse panel for the recent New York Comic Con, producer Jason Blum explained that he thinks a lot of adaptations — books, comics, and games alike — falter when creators try to simultaneously please hardcore fans and remain accessible to new audiences, and said his company finally cracked FNAF by deciding not to make it with new audiences in mind: in other words, to just please the fans. Polygon calls that a clear applause-bait line at a convention filled with fans.

The grounds against reading it as inaccessible are structural: the movie starts from scratch, mimicking the premise of the first game, in which a guy named Mike, played by Josh Hutcherson, is hired as overnight security guard for an abandoned Chuck E. Cheese-style arcade restaurant and encounters some animatronics with strange and eerie origins. There is no comics-style backstory that demands pre-movie study.

The warrant Polygon draws is a distinction between comprehension and caring: complete newcomers may still be confused because the backstory is parceled out awkwardly, characters' decisions do not always make sense, and there is as much family melodrama as thrill-ride scares. Any confusion is not the viewer's fault — the movie is kind of weird, and not because of missing fan-only information that unlocks its inconsistencies. The for-the-fans vibe Blum described applies more to whether the average person will care about this story than to whether they can understand it.
