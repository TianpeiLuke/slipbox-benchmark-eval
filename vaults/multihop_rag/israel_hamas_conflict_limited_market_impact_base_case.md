# Israel-Hamas Conflict: The Limited-Market-Impact Base Case

As of mid-October 2023, the Indian stock market's base case was that the Israel-Hamas hostilities would not escalate, and on that assumption the conflict was expected to have only a limited impact on markets. The grounds are threefold. First, the world can ill-afford another full-fledged war while economies are already battling high inflation and interest rates, an ongoing Russia-Ukraine war and a slowing China, which is precisely why many countries are trying to restrict any escalation; the market movement in the week before 14 October 2023 indicated that non-escalation was the presumed base case. Second, the US treasury secretary stated that the current conflict is not likely to significantly impact the global economic outlook. Third, history supports the same conclusion: a study of past conflicts between Israel and Hamas found that they tend to have limited impact on global financial markets, across crude oil, US 10-year yields, US equities and market volatility.

The warrant joining the evidence to the claim is that market impact tracks escalation, not the mere existence of hostilities: because the incentives of major countries point towards containment and because previous Israel-Hamas episodes transmitted little into the four channels that matter (oil, yields, equities, volatility), the priced-in outcome is a contained conflict. The argument is explicitly conditional on the non-escalation premise holding.
