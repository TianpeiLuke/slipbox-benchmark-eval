# Harm Reduction

## Definition

Harm reduction, in the corpus's usage, is the mission co-founder Philip Wilkins attaches to Keep Labs' medication-storage device.
The corpus pairs the term with "medication management" and, in the enterprise pivot, with "medication adherence," but never glosses harm reduction on its own terms.

## Context

The phrase "harm reduction" appears three times in TechCrunch's profile of Keep Labs: once in the article's own framing of the device, once attributed to Wilkins, and once in the reporter's description of the enterprise pivot.
Before Wilkins is quoted, the article frames the Keep as more than a secure box: "it's a solution to harm reduction and medication management," folding the device's dose-tracking and missed-dose notifications into a harm-reduction purpose rather than treating the lockbox function as the point.
The phrase reappears when Wilkins explains the company's shift away from cannabis branding: the CES 2020 innovation award the company had won was later rescinded after the trade group behind CES noticed the device was marketed as a cannabis storage device, and even with cannabis dropped from the website in favor of "medicine," he says the company is "still on the same mission of harm reduction."
The same phrase carries into the reporter's account of Keep Labs' enterprise pivot, which describes the company as "exploring an enterprise offering focused on medication adherence along with harm reduction" for partners such as the McKesson Digital Health Network.

## Key Characteristics

- **Paired with medication management, not opposed to it** — the corpus never treats harm reduction as an alternative to adherence tracking; the device's core function (weight-sensed dose tracking, missed-dose alerts) is presented as the mechanism of harm reduction.
- **Mission-level, not vocabulary-level** — Wilkins distinguishes explicitly between the company's marketing language (which dropped "cannabis" for "medicine") and its underlying mission (harm reduction), which he says stayed constant across that rebrand.
- **Extends into the enterprise offering** — the same phrase recurs in the reporter's description of the post-pivot enterprise product to healthcare partners, tying harm reduction to population-level adherence data rather than only the consumer device.
- **Never defined in the corpus** — TechCrunch uses the term three times without glossing it; the corpus assumes reader familiarity with the phrase rather than explaining it.

## Background (external)

The corpus uses "harm reduction" without defining it, so the following fills the gap from outside the corpus (quarantined from the sections above).
Harm reduction is a public health philosophy encompassing practices and policies aimed at lessening the negative social and physical consequences of a behavior — legal or illegal — without requiring that the behavior stop entirely; it treats reductions in harm as worthwhile even when abstinence is not achieved (Wikipedia, *Harm reduction*, accessed 2026-09-02).
In the drug and medication context specifically, the approach is commonly associated with measures like adherence tracking, safer-use tools, and peer or user involvement in designing services, rather than punitive or abstinence-only responses (Wikipedia, *Harm reduction*, accessed 2026-09-02).
