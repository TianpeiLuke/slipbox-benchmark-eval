# Disney's Legacy Projects Are Financially Safe

From a moneymaking standpoint it is easy to see why Disney sticks to legacy-linked projects: they are all but guaranteed to make bank.

The evidence Polygon gives is the gap between reception and revenue — Disney's live-action remakes and sequels consistently get mixed to poor reviews, yet make hundreds of millions of dollars, because people will pay to see their faves on screens again or to try to share their happy childhood memories with their own children. The argument's limiting clause is the important part: they will, at least until they get tired of the diminished returns. Polygon's claim is that the remakes might make a lot of money in the short run, but all they are really doing is reiterating what Disney has done in the past without meaningfully adding to the original movies' legacy.
