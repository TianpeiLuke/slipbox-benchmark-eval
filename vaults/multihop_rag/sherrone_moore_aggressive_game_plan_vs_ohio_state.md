# Sherrone Moore's Case For Attacking Ohio State

Sherrone Moore argued that being conservative against Ohio State would have been a disservice to his players, and he called the most aggressive game of his career on that basis. "It goes back to how they prepare, how they attack things and how they work," Moore said. "I would be doing them a disservice if I went out there and was conservative." His grounds were the week's practice: "Let's go. That's it. The players just practiced so well this week, and when you have confidence they are going to execute at a high level, it's just like first down."

The shift was measurable against his own recent history. Moore had favored a conservative plan the previous two weeks against No. 11 Penn State and Maryland, amounting to 91 running plays and 31 pass attempts, yet he was the aggressor in The Game: Michigan converted three fourth-down conversions in the first half, and J.J. McCarthy — 16 of 20 for 148 yards and a touchdown — threw a 22-yard touchdown to Roman Wilson between two defenders for a 14-3 lead.

The players read the aggression as a statement of trust. "Coach Moore said from the get-go that he was going to the most-aggressive game he's ever called," McCarthy said. "For the big boys, for Blake, for myself, that's music to our ears that he has the confidence in us to go get the extra yard or two yards. That's who we are."
