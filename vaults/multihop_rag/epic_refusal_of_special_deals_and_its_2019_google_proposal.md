# Epic's Refusal Of Special Deals And Its 2019 Google Proposal

Asked whether he would have sued at all had Google offered 0 percent for Epic's own payments system or 4 percent for Google Play billing instead of a $147 million deal, Tim Sweeney answered no — Epic has always turned down special deals just for Epic, fighting on the principle that all developers should be given the same opportunities.

His grounds were a document in evidence: a 2019 email he sent to all of Google's senior executives, right before Fortnite launched the Marvel season and the Star Wars event featuring J.J. Abrams, saying Epic wanted to bring Fortnite to the Google Play Store in time for that event and wanted Google to allow Epic and all other developers to process certain payments and keep them all. Had Google said yes, Sweeney argued, it would have been awesome for all developers, the Android ecosystem would have become much stronger, and Google would be in a better position in the smartphone industry today; there would never have been a dispute because the problem would have been solved. The warrant tying this to the present is his claim that the fix has always been within Google's reach: Google makes several billion dollars a year in what he calls unfairly earned profits from imposing this tax, which is nothing compared to what it makes from search, and given all the other benefits it gets from Android, "Google could solve this problem today if they wanted to."
