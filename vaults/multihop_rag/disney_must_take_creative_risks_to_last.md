# Disney Must Take Creative Risks To Last

Polygon's closing argument is that you do not get to a hundred-year legacy by playing it safe: it takes faith and trust in storytelling, in the creative minds behind the great movies, and in the power of progress — with a little pixie dust not hurting either.

The grounds are historical. With 100 years of creative life behind it, Walt Disney Studios has a lot to be proud of, but Mickey Mouse did not become a staple overnight: before Mickey was an icon, before the Disney Princess brand was even a thing, and before Disney owned a million other entertainment companies, Disney filmmakers were trying daring things while making a lot of costly mistakes along the way. The warrant is that each of those mistakes was an opportunity, a legacy of its own that helped usher in the great movies the studio is known for. Polygon acknowledges the audience problem for this conclusion: it is not a message that executives and shareholders want to hear.
