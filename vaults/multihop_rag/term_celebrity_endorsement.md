# Celebrity Endorsement

In the corpus, "celebrity endorsements" is named directly only once — listed alongside "campaign trail rallies" as one of several online-campaign tactics that, on their own, are "not enough to engage voters online" — and everywhere else the corpus treats it as a family of concrete cases rather than a defined category: FTX's paid partnerships with athletes, Anna Wintour's sponsorship request to Sam Bankman-Fried, The Epoch Times' political endorsements and CPAC sponsorship, and Vivek Ramaswamy's TikTok launch via a Jake Paul endorsement.

## Context

The corpus documents celebrity endorsement as a recurring marketing and political tactic across unrelated domains. FTX bought "mainstream recognition with branding deals and partnerships," most visibly by partnering with celebrities like Tom Brady and his ex-wife Gisele Bündchen, Steph Curry, Shaquille O'Neal, and Naomi Osaka; a follow-up account puts a price on two of those deals, with Tom Brady paid $55 million and Steph Curry $35 million to "lend FTX legitimacy and edge." Anna Wintour — described in the corpus as "the icy fashion doyenne" — separately reached out to Sam Bankman-Fried to ask him to sponsor her Met Costume Ball, a related but distinct pattern of soliciting a sponsor rather than a spokesperson. The Epoch Times pursued the same logic in politics rather than commerce: a hired Republican strategist "achieved the goal" of legitimizing the outlet by securing interviews and introductions with conservative politicians and media figures at the Conservative Political Action Conference, where the outlet was also billed as a "supporting sponsor." In the 2023 Republican presidential primary, Vivek Ramaswamy tried the same move digitally, launching his TikTok presence "with an endorsement from Jake Paul" to reach young voters. Corporate sponsorship of events and venues appears as a companion pattern to celebrity endorsement rather than a synonym for it: FTX separately bought the naming rights to the Miami Heat's home arena, and English football's League Cup has been renamed after a succession of corporate sponsors (Coca-Cola, Worthington's, Carling, and currently Carabao) with no celebrity attached.

## Key Characteristics

- **Transfer of legitimacy or attention** — the endorsing figure's fame is meant to "lend legitimacy and edge" to the endorsed brand, as stated of the Tom Brady and Steph Curry deals with FTX.
- **Often explicitly paid** — reported dollar figures ($55 million for Brady, $35 million for Curry) show the arrangement is frequently a straightforward commercial transaction, not a spontaneous show of support.
- **Can misfire when authenticity is doubted** — Jake Paul's endorsement of Ramaswamy "only drove animosity from TikTok viewers," because the Paul brothers have "a reputation of promoting anyone and anything for a paycheck," and viewers "immediately questioned how much Ramaswamy paid him."
- **Distinct from, but adjacent to, event/venue sponsorship** — naming-rights deals (FTX and the Miami Heat arena) and tournament title sponsorships (the Carabao Cup) trade a brand name for association with an audience, without necessarily involving an individual celebrity endorser.
- **Can trigger public backlash independent of the endorsed party** — Web Summit's corporate sponsors and speakers withdrew their association after the event's founder, Paddy Cosgrave, made controversial comments about the Israel-Hamas conflict, with fintech entrepreneur David Marcus vowing "I'll never attend/sponsor/speak at any of your events again," showing that sponsor/endorser relationships run in both directions and can be revoked.

## Background (external)

Outside this corpus, celebrity endorsement (sometimes called celebrity branding) is a marketing method that uses a famous person to promote something, on the theory that "the positive image of the celebrity transfers to the brand," boosting public attention and trust; its success typically hinges on how well the celebrity fits the product and how genuine the association seems. (Wikipedia, "Celebrity branding," accessed 2026-09-02: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Celebrity_branding)
