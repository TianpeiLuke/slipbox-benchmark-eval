# Nike Wholesale Partner Relationship Reset

Nike's relationship with its wholesale partners has been rocky: as the company pivoted to a direct-to-consumer model, it focused on driving sales online and in its stores at the expense of its wholesale accounts. The dependency reasserted itself when Nike grappled with excess inventories throughout 2023 and relied on those partners to move the merchandise, and it has now restored its relationship with both Macy's and DSW — accounts it previously cut in favour of the DTC strategy.

The quarter's numbers reflect a sector-wide caution as well as Nike's own pivot. Some analysts expected wholesale revenue to be sluggish because excess inventories have been a problem throughout the retail industry, with some wholesalers being more particular in what they order to avoid another backlog. Wholesale revenue during the quarter was flat compared with the year-ago period at $7 billion.

The relationships are structured to avoid concentration, and both CEO John Donahoe and finance chief Matthew Friend made clear that Nike is ready to meet customers in all channels, including through wholesalers and directly. The company singled out Dick's Sporting Goods as a key partner and noted it is still in the process of resetting its business with Foot Locker, which has seen two quarters in a row of plunging sales and profits. Friend framed the portfolio rule: "Ultimately, we have a segmented portfolio of strong partners across price points and channels. With no single partner representing more than a mid-single digit of Nike's total business." Despite the shift in how it works with wholesalers, Nike insisted direct sales will pave the way to future growth: "While the ultimate landing spot of digital and direct isn't as clear, we do believe we're going to be a more direct and a more digital company, and a more profitable company. And there's a channel mix and channel profitability opportunity that comes with that as well."
