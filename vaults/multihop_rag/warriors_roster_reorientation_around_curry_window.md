# Warriors Roster Reorientation Around Curry Window

Yardbarker argued on Nov. 29, 2023 that maximizing what is left of Stephen Curry's championship window will prompt some tough decisions in Golden State — that whether it comes before the trade deadline or next summer, something is bound to change.

The grounds are converging contract deadlines: for years the picks and players had been a luxury for the Warriors, but the age problem was coming to a head — Thompson would be a free agent in seven months, Paul's contract for the following season was not guaranteed, and Green's new contract, though a bargain then, might not look as good when he was 37. The warrant is that a shrinking window makes holding developmental assets more costly than trading them. Hence the specific move the piece contemplates: the Warriors might have to cut bait on the two remaining lottery picks who shared a stage two years earlier, Kuminga and Moody, either of whom could have value as a "second draft" candidate for a younger team — Kuminga's $6 million salary can be added to Payton to build $14.7 million in outgoing salary, and Moody's $3.9 million is some sugar to sweeten a deal for any of Golden State's higher-priced players. Available big names like Zach LaVine might not make a ton of sense given his age and contract, but everything from All-Star acquisitions to trading for helpful role players should be on the table; if the Warriors decided to reorient the roster around Curry between then and February's trade deadline, new GM Mike Dunleavy Jr. would be tasked with rebuilding a jet mid-flight. The piece closes on the difficulty: the draft picks had not panned out and Curry's supporting cast was showing its age, but finding solutions in the middle is easier said than done.
