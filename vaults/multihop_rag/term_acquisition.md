# Acquisition

## Definition

Across the corpus, "acquisition" denotes one company buying another and taking ownership and control of it — whether framed as an all-cash purchase of a target's shares (Amazon–iRobot), an outright corporate purchase (OpenAI–Global Illumination, Microsoft–Activision Blizzard King, Palo Alto Networks–Talon/Dig), or referred to interchangeably as a "merger," "buy," or "deal."

## Context

The corpus uses "acquisition" (and its synonyms "purchase," "buy," "merger," "deal") across a wide range of industries and deal sizes, from an AI startup's first tuck-in purchase to a $69 billion mega-deal, showing the term denotes a general transaction type rather than one specific event.
Amazon's proposed acquisition of iRobot was priced as an all-cash offer, later cut by 15% during a lengthy EU antitrust review, illustrating how the announced acquisition price can move before a deal closes.
OpenAI's purchase of Global Illumination was described as the company's "first public acquisition," showing the term applies equally to small, non-controversial tuck-ins as to headline-grabbing mega-deals.
Microsoft's $69 billion purchase of Activision Blizzard and King — the largest acquisition in video game history — took nearly two years to clear antitrust review before completing, and the corpus notes Microsoft's prior $26 billion purchase of LinkedIn as an earlier acquisition of comparable scale.
In cybersecurity, Palo Alto Networks' pursuit of Talon Cyber Security and Dig Security for roughly $1 billion combined is framed as one of several "mega deals" and smaller tuck-ins (CrowdStrike–Bionic, IBM–Polar, Cisco–Splunk) that the corpus groups together as an industry-wide acquisition trend driven by consolidation pressure.

## Key Characteristics

- **Change of ownership and control** — the acquirer gains control of the target's assets, operations, or equity; in the iRobot case the EU's concern was specifically that Amazon would gain the ability and incentive to influence competitive outcomes on its marketplace once it controlled iRobot.
- **A negotiated price that can shift** — the iRobot deal's price fell 15% from its original $1.7 billion all-cash offer as the target took on more debt during a delayed close, showing acquisition price is not fixed until the deal actually closes.
- **Regulatory review as a gating step** — both the Amazon–iRobot deal (EU statement of objections, in-depth probe) and the Microsoft–Activision Blizzard deal (nearly two years of antitrust review before an October 2023 approval) show large acquisitions routinely require competition-authority clearance before completion.
- **Ranges from tuck-ins to mega-deals** — the same term covers OpenAI's small, low-profile purchase of Global Illumination and Microsoft's $69 billion Activision Blizzard purchase, differentiated by disclosed deal size and public scrutiny rather than by transaction mechanics.
- **Driven by consolidation dynamics** — in cybersecurity, acquisitions cluster as companies buy up specialized startups (e.g., Palo Alto pursuing Talon and Dig) partly in reaction to competitors' own acquisitions, a pattern the corpus explicitly attributes to a "swing" toward large all-in-one platforms.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, "acquisition" is a standard mergers-and-acquisitions term: it occurs when one entity takes ownership of another entity's share capital, equity interests, or assets, as distinct from a merger, which combines two entities into a single new legal entity. (Source: Wikipedia, "Mergers and acquisitions," accessed 2026-09-02.)
