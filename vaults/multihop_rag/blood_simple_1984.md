# Blood Simple (1984)

Blood Simple — Frances McDormand's screen debut and the Coen brothers' first movie — is a must-see for neo-noir fans, the kind of tightly plotted, intensely acted, strikingly stylish movie the Coens would continue to be known for in the decades that followed. It streams on Max and The Criterion Channel and is rentable at Amazon, Vudu, Alamo, and YouTube.

A Texas woman played by McDormand cheats on her thuggish husband, played by Dan Hedaya, who sets out to kill her and her lover; but everyone involved seems to have a hidden agenda, and the threads get tangled as they surface, with twist after twist making the action memorable. Polygon's Tasha Robinson singles out its use of darkness: Blood Simple puts the visual darkness implied in "noir" to particularly good work, being the kind of movie where someone is always either lurking in deep shadow or forced off into a symbolic darkness they might or might not come back from alive.
