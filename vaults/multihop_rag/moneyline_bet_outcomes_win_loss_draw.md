# Moneyline Outcomes: Win, Loss, Draw And Push

A moneyline wager can end in several ways: the most straightforward outcomes are a win or a loss, determined by whether the team or individual you backed was victorious or defeated, and there is also the possibility of a draw if the event ends in a tie. Winning is simply a matter of picking the eventual winner — bet on Boxer B in a bout between Boxer A and Boxer B, and if Boxer B wins you are paid based on the odds at the time you placed the wager. A loss means the side you backed did not win: bet $100 on Team A in an NBA game and, if Team B wins, the bet on Team A loses.

Some sports price the draw as its own selection. In soccer especially, games can end in a draw and bettors can wager on that outcome, so a moneyline typically shows three sets of odds — one for each team to win and one for the draw. For a match between Team A and Team B, the odds might read Team A +150, Draw +220, Team B +180; a $100 wager on "Draw" that lands returns $220 in profit on top of the $100 stake, totaling $320, while a win by either team loses the $100.

A tie handled as a "push" is a different case, and the scope condition matters. When a push occurs bettors are refunded their original wager, with no money won or lost; but sports that typically allow a tie, like soccer, often offer the draw as a bet instead, and if the game ends in a draw and you did not bet that option, you lose. It is therefore essential to understand the specific rules of the sportsbook and the sport to know how ties are handled. Finally, in most sports and with most sportsbooks — including new betting sites — overtime or any extra time or tiebreakers do count toward a moneyline bet, though you should always check your sportsbook's specific rules.
