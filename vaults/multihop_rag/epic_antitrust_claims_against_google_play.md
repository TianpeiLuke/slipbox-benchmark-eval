# Epic's Antitrust Claims Against Google Play

Epic's claim is that the Android operating system's Google Play Store constitutes an unlawful monopoly, and that Google is separately tying its Google Play payments platform to that store, keeping other potential app payment mechanisms from competing.

The grounds Epic offers are the frictions it says Google creates: Google makes it so difficult for developers and users to get around its Android app store, and that app store's standard fees, that it has created an illegal monopoly which unjustly enriches Google while artificially driving up app prices because other stores cannot compete. The warrant joining that to the legal claim is the competitive-harm chain — no viable rival stores means no fee discipline, which means the "Google Tax" is paid either by developers or passed along to consumers. Epic wants to break up Google's alleged monopoly on Android app stores and payment methods so developers are not stuck paying that tax.

Google's answer contests the warrant rather than the fees: it says Epic's demands would damage Android's ability to offer a secure user experience and to compete with Apple's iOS. Both sides agree the future of Google's app store could depend on this trial. Epic sued in 2020 after a fight over in-app purchase fees.
