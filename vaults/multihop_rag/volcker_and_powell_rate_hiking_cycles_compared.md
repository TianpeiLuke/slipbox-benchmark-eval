# Volcker's And Powell's Rate-Hiking Cycles Compared

The consumer price index rose at a peak of around 14% in 1980 before then-Fed Chair Paul Volcker's hawkish policies sent mortgage rates to 18% within a year — cooling inflation but sparking a recession, and causing a serious downturn in housing in which home sales and building levels cratered while national home prices actually remained stable. At the start of Volcker's term as Fed chair in August 1979 the median U.S. home sales price was $64,700, and even after a near doubling of mortgage rates that figure rose to $69,400 by the second quarter of 1981. A second account puts the average 30-year fixed mortgage peaking at about 18% by late 1981, and records that home sales activity plummeted from 1978 to 1982, with existing-home sales dropping 50% over that period according to the Office of Policy Development and Research.

Over the 18 months preceding October 2023, current Fed chair Jerome Powell has been following a very similar game plan, raising interest rates aggressively to quash inflation and setting the tone for the most aggressive global hiking of rates in the modern era. The average 30-year fixed mortgage rate, the most common type in the U.S., soared from 3.8% in March 2022 to over 7.5% by 2023-10-06, and by the week of 2023-10-20 mortgage rates hit 8%, the highest in more than 20 years. This in turn slowed mortgage purchase applications and caused home sales to plummet, just as in the '80s; but home prices, echoing the dynamic of that era, have yet to collapse. While home sales activity levels this low had not been seen since the 2000s, the housing market more closely mimics that of the '80s, according to a report published in the week of 2023-10-20 by Fortune 500 financial services company First American.
