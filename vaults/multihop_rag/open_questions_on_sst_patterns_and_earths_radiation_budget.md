# Open Questions on SST Patterns and Earth's Radiation Budget

The article argues that pressing, fundamental questions for climate dynamics concern how surface temperature patterns come about, how Earth's radiation budget depends on the details of those patterns, and the extent to which the two depend on each other.

Three tasks follow from that claim. First, understanding must improve of the drivers of sea surface temperature patterns — decadal coupled variability; the pace and spatial structure of ocean heat uptake; and forcing by aerosols, greenhouse gases, and volcanoes — as well as the relative timing of these drivers, with the goal of explaining historical patterns quantitatively and predicting their likely future evolution. Second, the dependence of local and remote top-of-the-atmosphere radiative fluxes on the magnitude, spatial scale, and sign (positive or negative) of surface temperature changes must be quantified. Third, it must be explained how ocean heat uptake and radiative feedback are connected on various timescales, both globally and regionally.
