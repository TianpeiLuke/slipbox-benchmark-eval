# Historical Warming Does Not Bound Climate Sensitivity

The foremost implication of the pattern effect is that historically observed climate change does not constrain the upper limit of climate sensitivity (Sherwood et al., 2020; Forster et al., 2021).

The grounds for that claim are physical: the observed surface warming pattern caused feedbacks that were more stabilizing than the ones projected for the future. Warming was particularly pronounced in the sweet spots of the western Pacific and the subtropical eastern Pacific — a perfect combination for enhanced radiative damping — and in particular, low-lying cumulus clouds in the eastern Pacific covered a larger area and reflected more sunlight back to space than studies suggest they will in the future. The warrant joining the two is that a record produced under unusually stabilizing feedbacks cannot bound sensitivity under the different feedbacks expected later. More broadly, research on the pattern effect has exposed limitations of using equilibrium climate sensitivity to constrain future warming and, conversely, of using recently observed warming to constrain equilibrium climate sensitivity.
