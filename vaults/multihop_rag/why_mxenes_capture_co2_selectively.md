# Why MXenes Capture CO2 Selectively

MXenes are excellent at snagging CO2 while chemically ignoring other common gases such as nitrogen, the most abundant gas in Earth's atmosphere — a selectivity that could allow them to suck climate-warming CO2 out of the air while leaving the rest of the atmosphere alone.

Per Persson reports that in the Linköping experiments, "CO2 stuck to the MXene surface like refrigerator magnets. It was absolutely amazing," and his group has shown that MXenes can sponge up as much as half their weight in CO2. The explanation is not settled: some researchers think it is because CO2 molecules are just the right size to fit between a MXene's tight layers, while Persson suspects it is because CO2 and MXenes like to share electrons, allowing CO2 to chemically stick to a MXene's surface. Several research groups have been exploring MXenes' potential to capture CO2 from the air. One envisaged application is at coal-fired power plants, where MXene nanomaterials could be installed at or near the top of each smokestack to collect much of the CO2 leaving the stacks before it escapes into the open air.
