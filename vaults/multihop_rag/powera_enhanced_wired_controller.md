# PowerA Enhanced Wired Controller

The PowerA Enhanced Wired Controller is the budget pick: a wired controller for Xbox and PC that connects via Micro USB and features two customizable buttons on its rear, offered in many different colors and licensed designs. Specifications: wired connectivity, Micro USB connector, two mappable rear buttons, no software customization, wired power. Listed at $38 at Best Buy; full retail price is $37.99, though some color schemes can dip as low as around $25.

Its biggest downside is the Micro USB connection. You at least get a lengthy, detachable cable, but not having a reversible connector is annoying. Nothing else about the controller feels so outdated: its build quality is just fine, and it even has two mappable rear buttons built into the grips, which is great to have at such a low price and easy to operate without getting in the way.

The rest is table stakes executed well for a ridiculously affordable price. There is no impulse trigger rumble, but if there is any feature to cheap out on, that is the right choice. On the flip side, it comes with a two-year warranty, twice as long as pricey options like Microsoft's Elite Series 2 offer. The colors include licensed designs from game franchises like Mass Effect and Fallout; some may make you suspect cheap, cringe-inducing knockoffs, but the quality may surprise you when you use it.

PowerA also now sells the Advantage Wired Controller, essentially a newer version with USB-C and hair-trigger lockouts. The Advantage could be a shoo-in to dethrone the Enhanced Wired as the best budget controller, but at $37.99 it cannot yet match the Enhanced Wired's near-perpetually discounted price.
