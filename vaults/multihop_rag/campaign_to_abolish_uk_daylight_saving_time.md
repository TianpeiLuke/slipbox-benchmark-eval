# The Campaign To Abolish UK Daylight Saving Time

Many academics, scientists and campaigners have called for the abolition of the UK's clock-turning tradition, citing improvements to mental and physical health, public safety and wellbeing as the grounds. The UK first introduced Daylight Saving Time in 1916, when the government brought in British Summer Time to encourage people to spend more time outdoors in daylight, and the debate over whether to keep complying with DST protocols continues to run rife.

The reform has institutional momentum but no settled public mandate. The European parliament voted in favour of scrapping DST in 2019, a change due to take effect for the first time in 2021, but those plans have been stalled. And British opinion has not followed the campaigners: a 2019 YouGov poll found that a majority of Britons were marginally in favour of keeping DST, with 44 per cent electing to keep the current system against 39 per cent voting to ditch the change.
