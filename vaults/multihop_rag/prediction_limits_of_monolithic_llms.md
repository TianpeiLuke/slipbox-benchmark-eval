# Prediction: The Limits Of Monolithic LLMs

TechCrunch predicts that 2024 will be the year something gives in large language models, though exactly where is impossible to predict, as research is active at the frontiers of this field. So far there has been great optimism about the capabilities of LLMs, which have indeed proved more capable than anyone expected and have grown correspondingly more so as more compute is added.

Two specific claims are offered. First, the seemingly magical "emergent" capabilities of LLMs will be better studied and understood in 2024, and things like their inability to multiply large numbers will make more sense.

Second, in parallel we will begin to see diminishing returns on parameter counts, to the point where training a 500-billion-parameter model may technically produce better results but the compute required to do so could provably be deployed more effectively. The reasoning is architectural: a single monolithic model is unwieldy and expensive, while a mixture of experts — a collection of smaller, more specific models, likely multimodal ones — may prove almost as effective while being much easier to update piecemeal.
