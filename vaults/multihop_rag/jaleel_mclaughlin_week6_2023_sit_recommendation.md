# Jaleel McLaughlin As A Week 6 2023 Sit At Kansas City

CBS Sports listed Jaleel McLaughlin under "Sit Him" for Week 6 of the 2023 season at Kansas City: the claim is that there isn't a Broncos running back anyone should have much confidence in starting because they all split the proverbial pie too much, and the warrant is that the Chiefs build leads, which pushes Denver into the exact game state where McLaughlin's snaps disappear.

The usage grounds are precise. In Week 5 McLaughlin split first-half snaps almost evenly and then lost second-half snaps considerably, playing just 10 snaps after halftime while Denver trailed, yet still made three splash plays of 21, 22 and 38 yards, including a touchdown on a tipped-then-secured designed screen that pierced the middle of the Jets defense; his other nine touches each failed to exceed three yards. When the Jets led by eight or more in the second half he played 4 of 15 snaps with two carries, in line with a season-long trend: across the 117 snaps Denver has played while down by eight or more, McLaughlin has played 26 snaps for 13 touches, seven of them at Chicago, Javonte Williams 27 snaps for 15 touches almost all against Miami, and Samaje Perine 58 snaps for 13 touches — more than both backs combined. The irony the column notes is that McLaughlin is the only one averaging a strong number on the ground in that situation, at 6.1 yards per carry. On film he has good vision and speed, both essential to reading the Jets at the first and second levels and picking the right paths on his big plays, and his lateral agility was a differentiator on the touchdown catch; his worst asset is power, since he won't finish runs with the physicality his backfield mates do, and the biggest detriment is that he can't consistently overcome bad blocking, a persistent problem for Denver's line that also affected Javonte Williams in his first handful of games.

The opponent grounds explain why the game state is likely. The Chiefs tend to build leads on their opponents, have given up just four rushing plays of 12-plus yards this season including a Vikings fake-punt run the previous week, and have allowed one rushing touchdown to a running back — impressive given they concede 4.5 yards per carry and rank bottom-10 in yards before and after contact; the secret is that teams don't run much against them, 16.4 attempts per game, fourth-fewest, because they build leads. Only David Montgomery and Alexander Mattison have reached 10 fantasy points, PPR or non-PPR, against Kansas City this year, and both scored. Denver's own precedent is discouraging: when McLaughlin, Williams and Perine were all in the game plan during the Week 3 blowout loss, Williams had the most work with 13 touches and the most PPR points with 8.5, and the only time a Denver back reached 15-plus touches was Week 1, when Williams had 17 against the Raiders for a season-high 9.7 PPR points. The breakdown lists Denver at Kansas City, the Chiefs favoured by 10.5, an over/under of 47, an opponent ranked 6th versus running backs, 11.8 projected points, a 26th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 160 rushing yards, seven receptions, 46 receiving yards, three touchdowns and 9.3 fantasy points per game. The ranking is bounded: McLaughlin over Williams, but Brian Robinson, Alexander Mattison, Rachaad White, Jerome Ford and Rhamondre Stevenson over McLaughlin.
