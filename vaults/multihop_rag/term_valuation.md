# Valuation

## Definition

Across the corpus, "valuation" denotes the dollar figure attached to a company at a given moment — most often the headline number a startup or company is described as "valued at" following a funding round or share sale.

The corpus also uses the term for the analytical output public-stock analysts arrive at by pricing a stock through one of two competing methods, relative valuation or absolute (DCF) valuation.

## Context

The corpus repeatedly ties valuation to funding rounds and their trajectory: FTX's valuation rose to $32 billion at its peak after a $400 million Series C round in January 2022, before its collapse into bankruptcy; Flexport was valued at $8 billion amid the turmoil around Dave Clark's ouster; Adaptive, an AI startup based in Amsterdam and co-founded by French AI researcher Julien Launay, was about to raise a seed round at a $100 million valuation; and OpenAI closed a $300 million share sale at a $27–29 billion valuation in April 2023, separate from an earlier Microsoft investment. Scalable Capital's $65 million raise came in at a "flat" $1.4 billion valuation — unchanged from its prior round despite the company being four to five times larger, illustrating that valuation is a negotiated figure set at the moment of a deal, not a mechanical output of size or growth alone. Separately, the corpus's "Fundamental Investing" piece treats valuation as a discipline: analysts price stocks either through absolute valuation (a discounted cash flow model, "an interesting story backed by credible and validated numbers") or relative valuation (comparing a stock's PE, P/B, or EV/EBITDA ratio against peers, "an interesting number backed by a credible and validated story"), with about 85% of equity research and 50% of M&A valuation work using the relative approach because full DCF modeling across hundreds of stocks is impractical.

## Key Characteristics

- **Point-in-time and deal-driven** — a valuation is set at the moment of a funding round or share sale (e.g., FTX's $32B peak after its Series C, OpenAI's $27–29B share sale, Scalable Capital's raise), not a continuously updated market price.
- **Can be "flat"** — a company can raise new capital at the same valuation as its prior round even after growing substantially, as Scalable Capital did at $1.4 billion despite being four to five times bigger than at its 2021 round.
- **Detached from later outcomes** — a peak valuation (FTX's $32 billion) does not protect against later collapse; valuation reflects investor sentiment and negotiated terms at the time, not guaranteed durability.
- **Two competing methodologies for public stocks** — absolute valuation (DCF, modeling a company's own cash flows) versus relative valuation (comparing multiples like PE, P/B, EV/EBITDA against peers, growth, and history); the corpus reports relative valuation as the more prevalent in practice despite absolute valuation being considered theoretically superior.
- **Applies across scales** — the term covers everything from a seed-stage AI startup ($100 million) to a crypto exchange or foundation model company (tens of billions of dollars), with the same basic concept of "worth implied by a deal" underlying both.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, valuation in finance is the general process of determining the worth of a potential investment, asset, or security, typically through discounted-cashflow, relative, or contingent-claim approaches. (Source: Wikipedia, "Valuation (finance)," accessed 2026-09-02.)
