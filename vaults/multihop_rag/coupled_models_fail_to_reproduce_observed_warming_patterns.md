# Coupled Models Fail to Reproduce Observed Warming Patterns

Coupled climate models seem unable to simulate observed surface warming patterns across some key regions in which surface sensitivity modulates clouds: most importantly, models do not re-create observed cooling in the equatorial and subtropical eastern Pacific and Southern Ocean, and it is concerning that all models have the same sign of error in the trend patterns (Wills et al., 2022).

Coupled climate models simulate a freely evolving ocean surface and hence have their own expression of internal variability — one would not expect a coupled model to simulate El Niño events at the same time as they happened in nature, except by chance — which complicates comparisons between models and observations, especially on decadal timescales and beyond, for which little is known about the spectrum of internal variability in the real world. Even accounting for internal variability, the pattern failure persists, and models strongly disagree about the timescales over which future warming may occur in these regions. Limited knowledge of the drivers of surface warming pattern biases hampers the ability to evaluate them: it is not known whether the models' inability to reproduce observed trends is due to biases in how they represent the spectrum of internal variability, aerosol forcing, ocean-atmosphere interactions, deep and shallow atmospheric convection, Southern Ocean cloud-radiative properties, ocean thermocline depth, and ocean circulation, or something else entirely.
