# Twitch Streamer Playtime Versus the Average Player

A serious Twitch streamer who goes live daily or logs a full workday during their schedule will typically play much, much more than the people who are watching them, Polygon reported on 2023-10-19.

The reason is structural pressure: Twitch streamers cannot stop, since a mere two-day pause might mean losing thousands of paying subscribers. There is also a big incentive to keep up with live-service updates, partly FOMO — while certain events with special prizes happen yearly, other big occasions like season finales only happen once — but mostly because viewership is definitely affected by new patches that float a game to Twitch's front page.

StreamElements' public relations director Chase describes the pattern: "Multiplayer games will start decreasing in viewership after the first week as the launch hype dies down and then it's a question of how good the game is perceived to be that will determine its long term fate. With those in for the long haul, viewership tends to plateau and then spike when new content and patches are released or if it is supported by competitive events."
