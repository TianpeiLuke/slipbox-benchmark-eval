# Chinese Government Ban And Crackdown On Falun Gong, 1999

China banned Falun Gong altogether in 1999, after thousands of Li's followers silently protested its repression in Beijing, and the state then set about eradicating the movement.

Labelling it a cult, the Chinese government confiscated Falun Gong books, blocked websites, closed teaching centres and arrested practitioners. Human rights groups reported a brutal crackdown: some adherents were sent to labour camps, while others were tortured and killed.

International human rights organizations condemned China's attempted eradication. In the United States, where Li and some of his practitioners had fled, a new collection of followers organized a campaign to raise awareness and sympathy, using parades, demonstrations and pamphlets that touted the benefits of Falun Gong and the brutality of the Chinese Communist Party.
