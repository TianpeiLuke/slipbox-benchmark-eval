# M3 Max Benchmark Results

In TechCrunch's testing the M3 Max 16-inch MacBook Pro outperformed the M3 on everything and beat the M2 Ultra on several single-core tests, while often coming within spitting distance on multi-core tests.

The specific numbers: with Blackmagic Disk Speed, which measures SSD performance, the 16-inch Pro scored 6367 write and 5057 read, versus the Mac Studio's 6648/5716. On Geekbench 6 the system scored 3149 single-core and 21269 multi-core, a nice bump over the M3 iMac's 3024/11810, while in TechCrunch's Mac Studio review the M2 Ultra got 2819 single and 21507 multi-core. On Geekbench 5, which TechCrunch has run on M-series systems from the outset, the M3 Max gets 2323 single-core and 23190 multi-core — a big increase over the M3 iMac's 2191/10619 and the M2 Max 14-inch Pro's 1952/15249. On Cinebench 2024 it scored 142 single-core and 1693 multi-core, compared to the M3's 138/617. With GeekBench 6 Metal, which tests GPU performance during complex tasks, the M3 Max scored an average of 153273 — a nice increase over the M2 Max's 131883 average, while still getting trounced by the M2 Ultra's 208584; AMD's cards still best even the top-performing Apple chips by a good margin, with the Radeon RX 6900XT scoring 242924.

The conditions matter for reading these results. The M3 Max powering the review unit has 16 CPU cores — 12 performance and 4 efficiency — and a GPU packing 40 cores, whereas the M2 Ultra tops out at a 24-core CPU, 76-core GPU and 32-core neural engine, and the Mac Studio's large body and airflow system are much more equipped to dissipate heat than the MacBook Pro's compact profile. The reviewer had so far only triggered the fan during benchmarking. The Cinebench test was run with the system in high-power mode, a setting that prioritizes performance over battery life; low-power mode does the inverse, while automatic, the default, does its best to split the difference.
