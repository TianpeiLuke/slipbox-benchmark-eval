# Raheem Mostert As A Week 5 2023 Bust Candidate Against The Giants

CBS Sports flagged Raheem Mostert as a "Bust Candidate" for Week 5 of the 2023 season against the New York Giants: the claim is that starting Mostert means hoping he has not fumbled away half his touches and can still get a chance to score, and the warrant is that Miami's recent usage shows De'Von Achane has taken over the goal-line work that generates Mostert's fantasy points.

The grounds come from snap-by-snap usage. In Week 4, before he fumbled twice, Mostert was splitting reps with Achane unfavourably: he held a 16-to-9 edge before the Dolphins got inside the 10-yard line, then Achane played every single snap inside the 10; after Mostert's second fumble of the half he played just nine of the remaining 35 snaps and nothing near the end zone, and coach Mike McDaniel immediately afterwards preached the importance of his offense not having "self-inflicted wounds." Over the past two weeks Achane played all but two snaps inside the 10, which looks like a durable part of his role. On third downs Mostert holds a one-snap edge, 6 to 5, so those valuable touches are split, and targets have favoured Mostert only because he had more of them in Week 3 against Denver, 7 to 4. Total touches have been close — Achane 11 to Mostert's 10 in Week 4, Achane 22 to Mostert's 20 the week before, and before that Achane wasn't a thing and Mostert had 19 touches to Salvon Ahmed's six — so the Dolphins have used both backs rather evenly in volume even though where they get their touches has not been equal.

The matchup is the reason the column stops short of a sit. The Giants are about to get run over: their 4.3-yard rushing average allowed is a shade worse than league average, but they rate poorly in rushing touchdowns allowed (five), rushes of 10-plus yards allowed (14) and missed tackles on runs (24, fourth-worst in football). The breakdown lists Miami at home versus the Giants, the Dolphins favoured by 11, an over/under of 48.5, an opponent ranked 23rd versus running backs, 16 projected points, a 17th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 249 rushing yards, 13 receptions, 115 receiving yards, seven touchdowns and 22.4 fantasy points per game. Miami is also capable of giving Mostert more short-yardage work out of the blue. The verdict is therefore a risk warning rather than a bench order: understand the risks in starting Mostert as a No. 2 running back who could be doghouse-bound with another fumble; he sits behind Isiah Pacheco and Breece Hall in the rankings but ahead of Jahmyr Gibbs, Dameon Pierce and Najee Harris, while Achane is clearly far ahead of Mostert.
