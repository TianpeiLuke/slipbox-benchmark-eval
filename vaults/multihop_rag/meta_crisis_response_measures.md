# Meta Crisis Response Measures

Following a content moderation warning from European Union regulators earlier that week, Meta published on 13 October 2023 an overview of how it is responding to risks on its platforms stemming from the Israel-Hamas war: a temporary expansion of its Violence and Incitement policy, prioritised livestream checks, demotion of borderline content, hashtag blocking, a special operations center, and a temporary suspension of strikes for some violations. The blog post frames much of this as "ongoing efforts" and rehashes some existing policies and tools, but the company confirms it made some changes in light of fast-moving events in Israel and Gaza.

The first change is the temporary expansion of the Violence and Incitement policy, in order to prioritize the safety of Israelis kidnapped by Hamas. Under it, Meta will remove content that "clearly identifies hostages when we're made aware of it, even if it's being done to condemn or raise awareness of their situation." Blurred images of victims are allowed, but, "in line with standards established by the Geneva Convention, we will prioritize the safety and privacy of kidnapping victims if we are unsure or unable to make a clear assessment."

The second is livestreaming. Meta says it is prioritizing checks on livestreaming functions on Facebook and Instagram, including watching for any attempts by Hamas to use the tools to broadcast footage of captured Israelis or other hostages. The precondition it invokes is prior violation history: "We recognize that the immediacy of Live brings unique challenges, so we have restrictions in place on the use of Live for people who have previously violated certain policies. We're prioritizing livestream reports related to this crisis, above and beyond our existing prioritization of Live videos." Meta drew the comparison to measures it took after the 2019 Christchurch attacks in New Zealand, when a single shooter livestreamed a killing spree targeting two mosques on Facebook. On the specific threat, it added: "We're also aware of Hamas' threats to broadcast footage of the hostages and we're taking these threats extremely seriously. Our teams are monitoring this closely, and would swiftly remove any such content (and the accounts behind it), banking the content in our systems to prevent copies being re-shared." The urgency is not hypothetical: in a particularly disturbing report on Israeli media that week, widely recirculated on social media, a girl recounted how she and her family learnt of her grandmother's death after Hamas militants uploaded a video of her dead body to Facebook, apparently using the dead woman's own mobile phone to post the graphic content to her Facebook page.

Further steps cover ranking and search. Meta says it is making it less likely that its systems will actively recommend potentially violating or borderline content, and reducing the visibility of potentially offensive comments; it is also applying hashtag blocking to render certain terms related to the conflict non-searchable on its platforms. The blog post does not specify which hashtags Meta is blocking in relation to the Israel-Hamas war.

Operationally, Meta says it established a special operations center staffed with experts, including Arabic and Hebrew speakers, to increase its ability to respond quickly to content reports, and that it is taking feedback from local partners such as NGOs on emerging risks and "moving quickly to address them."

Meta states the limit of the procedure itself: given the heightened attention and concern, it says non-violating content may be removed "in error." Its mitigation is to relax the penalty rather than the removal — "for some violations we are temporarily removing content without strikes, meaning these content removals won't cause accounts to be disabled" — and it says it continues to provide tools for users to appeal decisions they believe were wrong.
