# Unfriended: Horror Contained Entirely Within A Computer Screen

Unfriended, directed by Levan "Leo" Gabriadze and starring Shelley Hennig, Moses Storm and Renee Olstead, is a masterfully contained horror movie that makes full use of its — at the time — groundbreaking gimmick: a tense teen horror movie that takes place entirely on a character's laptop screen. Polygon recommends watching it on a laptop if you can. It is available on the Criterion Channel, and both it and its sequel are available for digital rental or purchase.

The 2014 supernatural horror film is viewed entirely from the perspective of a computer desktop and centres on a Skype call between a group of high school students who are joined by an unknown presence known only as "billie227". What at first appears to be a prank swiftly morphs into something much more horrific, as the mysterious stranger begins to reveal terrifying secrets about each of the friends before killing them off one by one. It is a thoroughly gripping extrapolation of our always-online world, where vengeful poltergeists and doxxing exist side by side and no secret or offense goes undiscovered or unpunished — as the reviewers put it, there aren't that many things scarier than a video call you can't leave.

The follow-up, Unfriended: Dark Web, was directed by Stephen Susco and stars Colin Woodell, Rebecca Rittenhouse and Betty Gabriel; Polygon calls it a very good sequel and recommends it to anyone who dug the first film.
