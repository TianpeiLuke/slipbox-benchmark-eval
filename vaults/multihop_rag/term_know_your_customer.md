# Know Your Customer (KYC)

## Definition

The corpus names Know Your Customer (KYC) only as a commercial product category, not as a defined process: TechCrunch's fintech roundup reports that "CLEAR launches reusable KYC solution." The corpus treats KYC as something a company can build and sell as a discrete offering — CLEAR's launch item sits alongside other fintech product news such as an electric-car loan and a biometric checkout pilot — rather than defining what KYC itself entails. ## Background (external)

The corpus names a "KYC solution" without stating what KYC verification actually involves. Per Wikipedia's summary of the concept, KYC refers to laws and guidelines requiring regulated businesses to verify the identity, suitability, and risk profile of a customer before and during a business relationship (Wikipedia, *Know your customer*, accessed 2026-09-02). Its components typically include a Customer Identification Program (collecting name, date of birth, address, and an identification number when an account is opened), Customer Due Diligence (verifying identity, understanding the purpose of the relationship, and assessing risk), Enhanced Due Diligence for higher-risk customers, and ongoing transaction monitoring for suspicious activity (Wikipedia, *Know your customer*, accessed 2026-09-02). KYC sits within the broader framework of anti-money-laundering (AML) and counter-terrorism-financing regulation, aligned with standards set by the Financial Action Task Force since 1989 (Wikipedia, *Know your customer*, accessed 2026-09-02).
