# Eyes Without a Face (Georges Franju)

Eyes Without a Face is Georges Franju's influential 1960 French horror film, described by Polygon as a master class in supernatural fantasy horror and as a dark horror fable about love, guilt, and madness built out of what would otherwise be a standard mad scientist tale. It stars Pierre Brasseur, Édith Scob, and Alida Valli, and can be watched on Max and the Criterion Channel.

The plot follows Dr. Génessier, a renowned plastic surgeon wracked with guilt over a car accident that disfigured his beloved daughter Christiane's face. He fakes her death and confines her to the grounds of his mansion, then devises a desperate and horrifying scheme: perform a series of skin grafts to repair Christiane's face using the faces of abducted women who resemble her. As each attempt fails the body count rises, prompting the authorities to mount a desperate manhunt to apprehend the culprit. Christiane meanwhile grows tired of her father's obsession, wishing instead to reunite with her lover Jacques and resume her life.

Its significance is partly contextual: the late 1950s were not a booming era for French horror, and although Henri-Georges Clouzot's psychological thriller Diabolique received an effusive reception on its 1955 release, the prevailing attitude among French critics at the time was that horror was antithetical to the high-minded standard French cinema should aspire to. Eyes Without a Face bucked that perception. Polygon calls it fantastical and atmospheric, a beautiful horror thriller with masterful cinematography by Eugen Schüfftan — who previously worked on Fritz Lang's Metropolis — and a memorable score by Maurice-Alexis Jarre, who would later win an Academy Award for Lawrence of Arabia. It is recommended as an intelligent and gorgeous horror movie that still dabbles in its share of gore. Édith Scob's iconic face mask in the film was later referenced in her role in the also-excellent Holy Motors many decades on. Polygon placed the film at Oct. 23 in its Halloween countdown, listing it on the Criterion Channel and Max.
