# Dropout's Place In Improv History And Wayne Brady's Verdict

Wayne Brady, of Whose Line Is It Anyway?, appeared on Make Some Noise this season and told the Dropout team, in a line Sam Reich recounted in a Tumblr post, "There are only two people doing taped improv correctly: us and you." Asked whether being a notable part of the history of improv performance activates impostor syndrome or fulfils a dream, Reich's claim is that Dropout's improv identity was substantially a happy accident that Hollywood had said was impossible.

His grounds are the platform's own history: "We set out with a lot of scripted content on Dropout, and it didn't really work. It's once we broke away from IAC that we started to do more unscripted stuff, simply because it was what was working on the platform. And I think it's allowed us really to explore the world of improv more than we ever thought we would get to." He knew Mulligan as a world-class improviser and comedic performer even before he knew he was a GM, and Mulligan has just shot something for a new improv-based show that Reich calls one of his favourite things he has ever seen him do. The warrant is a claim about received wisdom being wrong: both have been through Upright Citizens Brigade, and so have many of their colleagues, "But Hollywood told us for years and years that there was no great way to capture this on film. And here we are. It's kind of serendipitous and magical."

Mulligan holds two positions at once. "When Wayne Brady says something like that, you sort of can't accept something that nice. So you deflect it away. But also, I feel like there's something that would be too glib and dismissive to not be cognizant about the incredible fortune that we've had." His supporting evidence is scarcity: a sober look at the entertainment landscape finds few other places able to celebrate improv and spontaneous comedy and push the envelope with the form, whether on Game Changer or Make Some Noise — and one of his favourite things to do with Dimension 20 is taking improv comedians whose entire creative life has been about following game and comedic beats in UCB sketch structure and letting them open their narrative wings and dip into storytelling in a way they often don't get to on stage. "So on the one hand, yes, very much impostor syndrome... And then on the other hand, I do recognize the rare air we are breathing with having a space like this."
