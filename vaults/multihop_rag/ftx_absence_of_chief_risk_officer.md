# FTX's Absence Of A Chief Risk Officer

The Verge's trial reporter argued that FTX's lack of a chief risk officer was either evidence of stupidity or a deliberate choice — and, since stupidity was unlikely, that Bankman-Fried probably chose not to hire one because he was worried about what such an officer might find. The grounds are that FTX ran a business in which risk management is structurally central, and the warrant is that any competent operator of such a business knows this, so the omission has to be explained by intent rather than oversight.

The claim about centrality rests on what FTX was. FTX was named as it was because it was a futures exchange, which, to borrow Bloomberg columnist Matt Levine's phrase, "sits between the winners and losers of bets" — meaning FTX could not pay out what it owed the winners unless the losers paid up. Risk officers exist to identify a business's potential risks, monitor them, and mitigate them, and those risks run from reputational to regulatory to financial, to say nothing of the regulatory risks specific to crypto. As the reporter put it after Bankman-Fried's own testimony, risk is an inherent part of a futures exchange, which is even more like a casino than regular cryptocurrency, so not having a risk team when you are any kind of financial anything is certainly a choice — and especially a choice when you go around telling everyone your crypto exchange is very good and safe.

Two things sharpen the argument. First, the omission was conceded: Bankman-Fried testified that he made a point of "periodically" handling support tickets himself because "I worried if I didn't, I would lose touch with the actual concerns of the customers," while what he did not do was create a risk team — something he now characterises as a "big mistake." Second, the defense raised the subject itself. Defense lawyer Mark Cohen's opening told the jury FTX "didn't have a chief risk officer, which became an issue when the storm hit," and offered a metaphor of FTX as a plane; the reporter's response was that if FTX was a plane, it was a plane flying with a key component missing, and that bringing this up was a tremendous mistake because the prosecution had not mentioned it.
