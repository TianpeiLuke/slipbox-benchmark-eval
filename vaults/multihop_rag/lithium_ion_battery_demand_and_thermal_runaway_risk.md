# Lithium-Ion Battery Demand and Thermal Runaway Risk

Demand for rechargeable batteries has grown steadily over the last decade with the growth of the personal electronics and electric vehicle markets, and lithium-ion batteries are one of the technologies that emerged and matured because of it: Statista estimated in 2020 that lithium-ion batteries accounted for 185 gigawatt-hours (GWh) of energy consumed, with that demand projected to reach 2,035 GWh by 2030.

Most of that growth is accounted for by increased desire for electric vehicles as people turn away from fossil fuels. While it is undeniable that lithium-ion batteries are more environmentally friendly than fossil fuels, and while they are also relatively inexpensive, these batteries are not perfect: one of their major drawbacks is the inherent risk of burning or even explosion as the result of thermal runaway, with overheated lithium-ion batteries deemed responsible for a number of fires in the past.
