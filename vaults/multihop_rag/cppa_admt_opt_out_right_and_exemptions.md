# The CPPA ADMT Opt-Out Right And Its Exemptions

The planned regulations deal with access and opt-out rights in relation to businesses' use of ADMT. Per an overview of the draft regulation, the aim is to establish a regime that will let state residents request an opt-out from their data being used for automated decisionmaking.

The exemptions where the opt-out does not apply are a relatively narrow set, available only where use of the data is necessary and solely intended for one of: security purposes ("to prevent, detect, and investigate security incidents"); fraud prevention; safety ("to protect the life and physical safety of consumers"); or a good or service requested by the consumer. That last exemption comes with a string of caveats: the business "has no reasonable alternative method of processing," and must demonstrate "(1) the futility of developing or using an alternative method of processing; (2) an alternative method of processing would result in a good or service that is not as valid, reliable, and fair; or (3) the development of an alternative method of processing would impose extreme hardship upon the business."

Where the procedure does not apply, in short: a business that intends to use ADMT and is trying to use a crude argument that, simply because the product contains automation or AI, users cannot opt out of their data being processed or fed to the models, looks unlikely to wash — at least not without the company going to extra effort to stand up a claim that, for instance, less intrusive processing would not suffice for their use-case. Basically, the aim is for there to be a compliance cost attached to trying to deny consumers the ability to opt out of automation or AI being applied to their data.
