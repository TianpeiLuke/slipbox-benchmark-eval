# Red-Teaming

## Definition

Red-teaming, as it appears in the corpus, is a named testing practice whose adequacy Paul Barrett says the federal government would need additional resources from Congress to actually assess, alongside the complexity of an AI system's training process.
The corpus itself never explains what the practice involves — it treats the term as already familiar to readers.

## Context

In the corpus the term surfaces in reactions to Biden's October 2023 AI executive order, where it is invoked as a benchmark the federal government would need resources to actually evaluate rather than a practice the EO itself defines or mandates in detail.
Paul Barrett, deputy director of the NYU Stern Center for Business and Human Rights, argued that without fresh resources from Congress, "it's not clear that the federal government has the resources to assess the vastly complicated training process or the adequacy of red-teaming and other necessary testing."
That framing ties red-teaming to the broader argument (also captured in the corpus) that an executive order alone cannot supply the institutional capacity — funding, staff, technical expertise — needed to verify that AI developers' safety testing is sufficient.

## Key Characteristics

- **Named but undefined in the corpus** — doc_0098 uses "red-teaming" only inside Barrett's quote, grouped with "other necessary testing," without describing what the practice involves (doc_0098).
- **One of two things Barrett says need assessing** — Barrett names "the adequacy of red-teaming and other necessary testing," alongside "the vastly complicated training process," as the two things the federal government lacks the resources to assess (doc_0098).
- **Resource-dependent oversight** — the corpus frames evaluating red-teaming adequacy as something the federal government currently lacks the resources to do without additional congressional funding, underscoring that red-teaming claims by AI developers are not independently verified by government within the EO's framework (doc_0098).

## Background (external)

The corpus mentions red-teaming only as a term assumed familiar to readers, without defining the practice itself.
Per NIST's AI Risk Management Framework, red-teaming is a structured exercise in which testers deliberately attempt to identify flaws and vulnerabilities in an AI system, such as harmful or discriminatory outputs, security weaknesses, or ways the system's safeguards can be circumvented, typically under adversarial or unusual conditions (NIST, *AI Risk Management Framework*, accessed 2026-09-02).
Biden's October 2023 executive order itself later directed NIST to set guidelines for red-team testing of AI models before public release, which is the broader regulatory backdrop the corpus's commentators are reacting to.
