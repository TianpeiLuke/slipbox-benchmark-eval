# DeepMind's Founding And Thiel's Investment

In 2010 Demis Hassabis and two colleagues, all living in Britain, were looking for money to start building "artificial general intelligence", a machine that could do anything the brain could do, at a time when few people were interested in AI because after a half-century of research the field had failed to deliver anything remotely close to the human brain. Some scientists and thinkers had nonetheless become fixated on AI's downsides, and many, including the three young men, had a connection to Eliezer Yudkowsky, an internet philosopher and self-taught AI researcher who led a community calling themselves Rationalists or, later, effective altruists; they believed AI could find a cure for cancer or solve climate change but worried that AI bots might do things their creators had not intended, arguing that machines more intelligent than humans could turn on their creators. Peter Thiel, enormously wealthy through an early investment in Facebook and his work with Musk in the early days of PayPal, had developed a fascination with the singularity, the science-fiction trope describing the moment when intelligent technology can no longer be controlled by humanity, and with his funding Yudkowsky had expanded his AI lab and created an annual conference on the singularity; one of Hassabis' colleagues had met Yudkowsky years before and snagged them speaking spots at that conference, ensuring an invitation to Thiel's party, where Yudkowsky introduced Hassabis to Thiel. Assuming many guests would be trying to squeeze their host for money, Hassabis' strategy was to arrange another meeting: he told Thiel there was a deep tension between the bishop and the knight, pieces carrying the same value whose strengths the best players understood to be vastly different. Charmed, Thiel invited the group back the next day, gathering in the kitchen where their host, just finished with his morning workout and still sweating in a shiny tracksuit, was handed a Diet Coke by a butler; the three made their pitch and soon Thiel and his venture capital firm agreed to put £1.4 million, about $A2.7 million, into their startup, making him their first major investor.

They named the company DeepMind, a nod to "deep learning", a way for AI systems to learn skills by analysing large amounts of data, to neuroscience, and to the Deep Thought supercomputer from The Hitchhiker's Guide to the Galaxy, and by the spring of 2010 they were building their dream machine, wholeheartedly believing that because they understood the risks they were uniquely positioned to protect the world. Mustafa Suleyman, one of the three founders, said "I don't see this as a contradictory position. There are huge benefits to come from these technologies. The goal is not to eliminate them or pause their development. The goal is to mitigate the downsides." Having won over Thiel, Hassabis worked his way into Musk's orbit: about two years later they met at a conference organised by Thiel's investment fund, which had also put money into SpaceX, and Hassabis secured a tour of SpaceX headquarters, after which, with rocket hulls hanging from the ceiling, the two lunched in the cafeteria; Musk explained his plan to colonise Mars to escape overpopulation and other dangers on Earth, and Hassabis replied that the plan would work so long as superintelligent machines didn't follow and destroy humanity on Mars too. Musk was speechless, not having thought about that particular danger, and soon invested in DeepMind alongside Thiel so he could be closer to the creation of the technology.
