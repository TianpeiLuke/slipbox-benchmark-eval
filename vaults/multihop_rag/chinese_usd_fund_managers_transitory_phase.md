# Chinese USD Fund Managers' Transitory Phase

The premise under attack is that the Bay Area influx represents a strategic pivot. Venturing out of China is not a "pivot" for the USD fund managers, said one of the investors TechCrunch spoke to; rather, the partners and their associates are mostly looking for something to get their hands on in the midst of a tepid market. Some are contemplating a career change, but it is difficult to find any job that matches their enviable pay.

The motive that investor offers is closer to reconnaissance than redeployment: "Chinese VCs are most anxious about the U.S. building cars behind closed doors. They don't want to fall behind, especially given the speed AI is evolving, so they are going to the Bay Area to figure it out themselves."

There is also a mundane explanation for the timing. The recent influx of Chinese VCs into the Bay Area should be viewed in a broader context: many of these investors, who have family ties in the U.S., have been going stateside regularly for years, and COVID-19 — which shut down trans-Pacific flights and introduced costly and harsh quarantines — created a pent-up demand for travel. Naturally, many investors rushed to the Bay Area as soon as the borders reopened, but the surge in activity might soon start to subside, said a partner who spent the past summer in California.
