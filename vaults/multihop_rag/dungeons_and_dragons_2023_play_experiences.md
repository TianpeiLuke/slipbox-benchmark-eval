# Dungeons & Dragons In 2023

Two Polygon contributors describe their 2023 with Dungeons & Dragons, and both accounts are about how the game is actually played rather than about its rules.

Mike Haracz, corporate R&D chef and host of Heroes' Feast, is in the seventh year of a homebrew campaign played with friends locally in the Chicagoland area. The campaign is called The Heartsguard Saga and is based on Deven Rue's map of a place called Euphoros; his players are around level 15, and he frequently gives them amazing and unique gear he has dreamed up, which they go on to forget they have and never use. The group used to play in person but since the pandemic he runs it virtually from his house, with a camera hovering over his gaming mat and a labeled grid so players call out moves like "Move me from A5 to B10," and during important rolls he makes players face their camera toward their dice for added suspense. Petrana Radulovic, entertainment reporter, had wanted to play since high school but never knew how to get in; in the doldrums of 2020 someone she knew knew someone who knew someone interested in running a game, which kicked off her D&D career, but 2023 was the year everything clicked and it went from a hobby she enjoyed to one she spends every free minute thinking about. She credits reliable groups — after some initial shuffling of members she had two separate groups in 2023, both with enthusiastic members committed to at least trying to meet regularly — and also Baldur's Gate 3, which helped her understand some of the more complicated D&D mechanics and made her excited about combat in a way she never was before. She has still yet to play in person, since her groups are spread across North America, and hopes to manifest an in-person session soon.
