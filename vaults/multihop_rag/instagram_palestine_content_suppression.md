# Reported Suppression of Palestine Content on Instagram

Instagram users reported several distinct forms of restriction on Palestine-related content following the October 2023 escalation.

Palestinian-American filmmaker Khitam Jabr was restricted from using her account for 24 hours after posting travel Reels from the West Bank containing no content about the occupation; she suspects other users reported the videos. Leen Al Saadi, a Palestinian journalist based in Jordan, had her account restricted after posting a trailer discussing a documentary about Palestinian street art under occupation.

Users reported Stories about Palestine receiving far fewer views than unrelated Stories posted the same day. One user in Egypt whose posts normally drew about 300 views reported one to two views on pro-Palestine Stories, with views recovering after posting an unrelated image. A user in the United Kingdom reported views falling "from 100s to zero or a handful," recovering after posting a cat photo.

7amleh, the Arab Center for Social Media Advancement, reported Instagram inconsistently filtering comments containing the Palestinian flag emoji as "potentially offensive." It also documented journalists posting identical news in Arabic, Hebrew and English and being flagged only for the Arabic. As advocacy manager Jalal Abukhater put it: "It's literally journalistic content, but the same wording in Hebrew and English does not get restricted."

The Intercept reported Instagram and Facebook flagging images of the al-Ahli Hospital as violating guidelines on nudity or sexual activity. Al Saadi reported a comment saying she should be "raped" and "burned alive"; Instagram responded that it did not violate its guidelines.

Bella Hadid alleged in 2022 that Instagram "disabled" her from posting Stories "pretty much only when it is Palestine based," with views dropping by almost a million.
