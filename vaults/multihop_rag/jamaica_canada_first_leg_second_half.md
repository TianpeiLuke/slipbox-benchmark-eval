# The Second Half Of Jamaica 1-2 Canada

Jamaica equalised early in the second half but Canada won it late through Stephen Eustaquio, finishing 2-1 in Kingston. Jamaica restarted the action, and by the 48th minute Richie Laryea and Shamar Nicholson both dove to the floor in a full-throttle tussle on the flank in a scene better suited to WWE, with referee Mary Penso rushing in to calm things and Nicholson — booked in the first half — needing to keep his cool. Canada went close at 51 minutes when Jonathan David lashed a dipping shot from a central position that Andre Blake did well to tip behind.

At 56 minutes Nicholson burst onto a pass inside the penalty area and produced a cool, low finish; the visiting bench's celebrations were cooled by an offside flag and a VAR check followed. Penso checked the pitchside monitor and agreed with Nicholson's protestations that the goal should count, making it 1-1 at 58 minutes — a goal that came from a quick piece of thinking by Daniel Johnson, who directed a free kick onto Nicholson's run while Canada appeared to have switched off.

The middle of the half was cagey. Canada won a free kick just outside the Jamaica area on the left at 60 minutes, with Blake standing at his near post directing team-mates, but Eustaquio's delivery created little danger — and Canada, who had won only one of their last 11 visits to Jamaica, would not improve that record without doing better with such chances. Jamaica's own free kick beyond halfway at 64 minutes ended with Milan Borjan catching the cross. Mark-Anthony Kaye came on for Ismaël Koné at around 67 minutes. Canada had never lost at home to Jamaica, so a draw looked acceptable to them, though their opponents had shown enough to suggest a surprise in the return game. Leon Bailey was booked for an altercation with his marker at 76 minutes, after which clear chances dried up; Alphonso Davies showed some invention on the left at 79 minutes but the ball ended in Blake's arms, and at 82 minutes fans in green shirts carrying yellow pompoms danced in the stands, with the official attendance a little more than 3,500. A drinks break came at 73 minutes, as it had in the first half, after which Canada had emerged stronger.

The winner arrived at 85 minutes: Laryea charged clear on the right, approached the penalty area and cut a perceptive low pass back into the path of the onrushing, unmarked Eustaquio, who slotted in. Six minutes of added time were signalled at 90 minutes. At full time it was Jamaica 1-2 Canada — the hosts a little unfortunate, having equalised and held out until five minutes from time, now facing a huge task to reach the semifinals, while Canada would reach Copa America 2024 if they preserved their unbeaten home record against Jamaica on the Wednesday.
