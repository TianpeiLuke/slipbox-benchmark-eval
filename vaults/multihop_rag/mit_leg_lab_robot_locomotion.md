# MIT's Leg Lab And Dynamic Robot Locomotion

The Leg Lab was a robot locomotion research lab founded by Marc Raibert at Carnegie Mellon University (CMU) in 1980 before moving operations to MIT, and its work would pave the way for Boston Dynamics. Its defining idea was to treat dynamics and stored energy as central to legged movement rather than something to be avoided.

Raibert set the concept against the state of the art at the time: "When we first got started, all robots that had legs were very slow moving, crab-like things that would hug the ground and then tentatively take a step and hope that things didn't topple over and then move like a slow-moving spider," he says. "I was interested in how animals work, and I looked at that and said, 'Wow, this is about as far from right as you can get.' I went the other way to see if we can do something where the dynamics and the energy in the machine [were] part of the story, and springs and bouncing were part of the story. My lab worked on that. We built pogo stick robots."

Raibert ran the lab for 15 years, and a number of future robotics luminaries made their way through the program over the course of its existence, including AI ethics professor Joanna Bryson, research scientist Jerry Pratt, Wobbleworks co-founder Peter Dilworth, artist Daniel Paluska, CSAIL professor and Toyota Research Institute (TRI) VP Russ Tedrake, UC Berkeley professor Ken Goldberg, Boston Dynamics CEO Rob Playter, and Agility Robotics co-founder Jonathan Hurst, who was a visiting student from CMU one summer.
