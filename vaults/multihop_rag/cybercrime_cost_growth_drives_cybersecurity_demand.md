# Cybercrime Cost Growth Drives Cybersecurity Demand

The financial damage from cybercrimes will reach an astronomical $10.5 trillion by 2025 — a figure that, as global economies go, would rank third behind only the US and China. Global cybercrime damage costs are expected to increase 15% annually over the following two years, according to Cybersecurity Ventures.

Seeking Alpha framed this on 18 November 2023 as bad for global conglomerates and the average person but an opportunity for cybersecurity firms like Check Point Software. The demand signal holds even as rivals such as Palo Alto Networks and Fortinet cite an uncertain macroeconomic environment for weak billings forecasts.

Check Point President Rupal Shah Hollenbeck put it this way in a Zoom interview that week: "There's never been a better time to be in cyber. It's fortunate in that organizations are understanding the business value of cybersecurity in a really big way. Unfortunately, it's because there are more hacks out there than ever before."
