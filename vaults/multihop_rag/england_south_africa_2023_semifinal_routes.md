# How England And South Africa Reached The 2023 Semi-Final

England arrived at the 2023 Rugby World Cup semi-final as the only side still competing that had yet to lose a game, while South Africa had already beaten two of the tournament favourites. Dominant wins over Argentina, Japan and Chile sealed England's passage to the last eight, and after being given an almighty scare by Samoa in their final pool match, they overcame a Fijian fightback in Marseille to triumph 30-24 — outscored by three tries to two, but carried by captain Owen Farrell's 20 points at the expense of 2023's breakout stars.

The Springboks had done it tough. Comfortable defeats of Scotland, Romania and Tonga could not mask the disappointment of a 13-8 loss to Ireland, which consigned South Africa to second in Pool B. With Ireland then sent home by the All Blacks in the quarters, the Bokke showed their World Cup mettle to edge hosts France 29-28 at the Stade de France, sneaking past Les Bleus in one of the best games of rugby you're ever likely to see.

Both journeys had twists, turns and bumps in the road but left each side within 80 minutes of a Rugby World Cup final. England would be looking to echo the spirit of 2019, when they beat the All Blacks 19-7 to reach the final, but in South Africa they faced a side who had taken their game to new heights just to reach the semis.
