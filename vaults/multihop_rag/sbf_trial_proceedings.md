# The SBF Trial Proceedings

Sam Bankman-Fried is in court for alleged fraud and money laundering while leading crypto exchange FTX and sister company Alameda. TechCrunch's 2023-10-02 preview placed the case in the Manhattan Federal Court, describing the disgraced cryptocurrency entrepreneur as accused of orchestrating "one of the biggest financial frauds in American history": the multibillion-dollar collapse of FTX, the crypto exchange he founded and led. SBF, as he is known, has pleaded not guilty to some seven charges of fraud and conspiracy.

The trial kicked off with jury selection October 3. By TechCrunch's 2023-10-06 update the case was entering its next chapter, with proceedings resuming Thursday: as confirmed during a teleconference Wednesday, SBF would be taking the stand along with a handful of other witnesses, as the onus shifted toward the defense after the prosecution brought its case to its conclusion.

Access to the proceedings is limited: no devices are allowed, so reports are being physically put together within the courtroom, or sent to overflow rooms. The jury's composition ranges from a train conductor to a retired investment banker who attended Stanford University, and TechCrunch pointed readers to a special joint episode of its Equity and Chain Reaction podcasts for a better sense of the vibe between the jurors and the rest of the court.

A second account of the same events was arriving in parallel: "Going Infinite," a book by Michael Lewis chronicling the rise and fall of SBF, was set to debut on the first day of the trial.
