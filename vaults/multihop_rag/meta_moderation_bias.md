# Meta Moderation Bias

TechCrunch's claim, published 19 October 2023, is that Meta has a moderation bias problem rather than a "bug": the suppression of Palestinian voices is the latest in a lengthy history of incidents on Meta platforms that reflect an inherent bias against Palestinian users in its processes, documented by years of complaints from both inside and outside the company. The argument's careful qualifier is that intent is not asserted — the company may not intentionally suppress specific communities, but its moderation practices often disproportionately affect Palestinian users.

The first ground is Meta's own commissioned evidence. During the last violent outbreak between Hamas and Israel in 2021, a report commissioned by Meta and conducted by a third party concluded that the company's actions had an "adverse human rights impact" on Palestinian users' right to freedom of expression and political participation.

The second ground is the pattern of disproportionate scrutiny during that outbreak. Amid the violence following the May 2021 court ruling to evict Palestinian families from Sheikh Jarrah, a neighborhood in occupied East Jerusalem, users across Facebook and Instagram accused Meta of taking down posts and suspending accounts that voiced support for Palestinians. The digital rights nonprofit Electronic Frontier Foundation described Meta's actions in 2021 as "systemic censorship of Palestinian voices," and in its 2022 report on Palestinian digital rights, 7amleh said Meta is "still the most restricting company" compared with other social media giants in the extent of its moderation of the Palestinian digital space.

The third ground is internal. That same month, a group of Facebook employees filed internal complaints accusing the company of bias against Arab and Muslim users; in internal posts obtained by BuzzFeed News, an employee attributed the bias to "years and years of implementing policies that just don't scale globally."

The fourth ground is the measured asymmetry between languages. At the recommendation of its Oversight Board, Meta conducted a third-party due diligence report about the platform's moderation during the May 2021 conflict. The report found that Arabic content was flagged as potentially violating at significantly higher rates than Hebrew content was, and was more likely to be erroneously removed. It noted that Meta's moderation system may not be as precise for Arabic content as for Hebrew, because the latter is a "more standardized language," and suggested that reviewers may lack the linguistic and cultural competence to understand less common Arabic dialects like Palestinian Arabic.

The warrant joining grounds to claim is duration and convergence. Because the disparity is recurrent across separate escalations, is corroborated from inside the company as well as by outside nonprofits and by Meta's own commissioned reviewers, and is traceable to standing properties of the moderation system rather than to a one-off failure, it is better described as bias inherent in the processes than as an isolated technical error.
