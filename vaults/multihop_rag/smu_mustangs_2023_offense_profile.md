# SMU Mustangs 2023 Offense Profile

SMU came into the North Texas game on a five-game winning streak and tied atop the AAC with fellow 5-0 squads Tulane and UTSA, having accumulated 1,685 total yards of offense in its past three games, including 91 first downs via passing, running or penalty, and 18 touchdowns. By happy coincidence for second-year boss Rhett Lashlee, the defense had flexed over that same span, yielding just 668 total yards, 39 total first downs and a mere four touchdowns.

Preston Stone was the engine. In his past four games the 6-foot-2 sophomore from Dallas had thrown 122 passes with 10 touchdowns and no interceptions, run in two more scores, and accumulated 1,171 aerial yards, including a 300-yard game against Prairie View A&M — heating up at perfect timing to improve SMU's bowl prospects after a 2-2 start that included keen tests and defeats at Oklahoma and TCU. When last seen at Gerald J. Ford Stadium two weeks earlier, playing near where he was raised, Stone threw touchdown strikes of 74, 62 and 17 yards in a 69-10 homecoming belting of Tulsa; by halftime it was 52-3, the most Division I points tallied in the first 30 minutes that year, though rain may have kept fans away as only 20,800 filled the 32,000-capacity stadium.

The supporting cast was spread out. Receivers RJ Maryland and Jordan Hudson had five touchdown catches apiece and Roderick Daniels Jr. four, with three others on two, while Jaylan Knighton paced the ground game with 511 yards and four touchdowns.
