# Public Health Guidance

## Definition

Public health guidance, in the corpus, is the population-level recommendations and standards that named authorities — the CDC and the WHO — issue on vaccination, disease-eradication targets, dietary/consumer advice, and antibiotic-resistance standards, as distinct from a clinician's advice to an individual patient.

## Context

In the corpus this guidance takes several concrete forms. The CDC recommends that everyone 6 months and older get a flu vaccine every season, an estimate it grounds in a documented burden of nine million illnesses, four million medical visits, 10,000 hospitalizations, and 5,000 deaths in the 2021-2022 season alone.
The WHO similarly issues disease-eradication targets, such as its aim to eradicate Hepatitis C by 2030, and consumer-facing advice, such as advising against sugar substitutes for weight loss or disease prevention.
On antibiotic resistance, the CDC tracks 18 concerning bacteria for rising drug resistance and the WHO defines a "priority" list of bacteria that new antibiotics should target, and both agencies' guidance underpins hospital-level antibiotic stewardship efforts such as auditing prescriptions and requiring special clearance for the broadest-spectrum drugs.

## Key Characteristics

- **Issued by named authorities** — the corpus attributes guidance specifically to the CDC and the WHO, not to unnamed "experts" or generic health advice.
- **Population-level rationale** — guidance is justified by aggregate burden data (illnesses, hospitalizations, deaths, or bacterial deaths worldwide) rather than any single patient's case.
- **Spans prevention and treatment-adjacent domains** — the corpus shows guidance covering vaccination recommendations, dietary/consumer advice, and antibiotic-prescribing standards.
- **Operationalized locally** — guidance translates into concrete institutional practice, such as hospital antibiotic stewardship programs auditing individual prescriptions to lower "overall population-level risk."
- **Distinct from individual clinical advice** — Dr. Gregg Sylvester's public comments urging flu vaccination echo the CDC's population-level recommendation, but they are expert commentary given to the press rather than a treating clinician's advice to a specific patient.

## Background (external)

More broadly (outside this corpus), public health is "the science of protecting and improving the health of people and their communities," concerned with protecting the health of entire populations — from a neighborhood to a country or region — through prevention, policy recommendations, and health communication, distinct from healthcare professionals like doctors and nurses, who "provide care to individuals and meet their individual needs" (CDC Foundation, "What is Public Health?", accessed 2026-09-02, https://www.cdcfoundation.org/what-public-health).
