# Tfue (Turner Tenney) and the End of a Fortnite Streaming Era

"I feel like the majority of my life has been in front of a screen," Turner "Tfue" Tenney said amid tears in the 2022 retirement video that ended his career as Fortnite's golden boy — a moment Polygon, writing on 2023-10-19, calls the end of an era for the games industry.

A fateful kill against Tyler "Ninja" Blevins back in 2019 had catapulted Tenney to fame, and his audience went on to outgrow that of Twitch's most mainstream star; his name is synonymous with the battle royale game's most recognizable character, Jonesy. For many, his retirement video was a sign of yet another Twitch star grappling with burnout — sad, yet familiar.

Polygon presents his arc as a summary of gaming's biggest growing pains during the influencer era. Tenney was the real deal, a perfect nexus of undeniable charisma and tenacious talent on display at least eight hours a day for years. He came up as an early Fortnite esports champion, and esports is now known to be a bubble that has burned millions of dollars. He rode a Fortnite wave that raised billions of dollars for Epic Games; a few years later the publisher laid off 830 employees. When he blew up, everyone was salivating over Drake and what his livestream appearance signaled for Twitch's future; now both the rapper and many of the platform's stars are better known for promoting gambling over hits. He spurred headlines after joining FaZe Clan, a gaming lifestyle brand so lucrative it was endorsed by celebrities like Snoop Dogg; FaZe Clan is now an infamous penny stock that Tenney says tried to rip him off.

"I wouldn't trade it for the world," Tenney said in his goodbye video, which overflows with footage of esports casters and viewers in awe of his Fortnite plays. "I've been doing this since I was a kid, man," he continued, shaking his head. "I feel like my childhood and even some of my adulthood kind of flushed away. I feel like this is the end of the journey, man."
