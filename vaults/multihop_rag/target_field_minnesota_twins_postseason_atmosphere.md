# Target Field: Minnesota Twins Postseason Atmosphere

Target Field ranked No. 6 among the eight remaining 2023 playoff teams' home-field environments in The New York Times' 8 October 2023 ranking, judged more an awesome place to play than one that strikes fear in the opponent. The park opened in 2010, has a capacity of 38,544 and drew an average attendance of 24,371 in 2023.

Its memorable postseason moment is only days old: on the Tuesday the Twins snapped an 18-game postseason losing streak with two blasts off the bat of rookie Royce Lewis, their first playoff win since Oct. 5, 2004, and on the Wednesday they completed the Wild Card sweep of the Blue Jays for their first postseason series win since 2002. The roars heard at Target Field were more than two decades in the making.

The crowd's effect was measurable on the field. After Game 1 against Toronto, Twins manager Rocco Baldelli said, "I thought the place was going to split open and melt, honestly. It was out of this universe out there on the field. The fans took over the game. They helped us win today." The next day they did it again: early in Game 2, Carlos Correa told Sonny Gray that, because of the crowd noise, Jays baserunners couldn't hear their third-base coach screaming "BACK!", so with Vladimir Guerrero Jr. on second and a full count to Bo Bichette they executed a perfect timing pick. "The crowd was incredible," Gray said after the game. "They were incredible yesterday. They were incredible today from the moment I stepped on the mound an hour before the game to the moment — they're probably still out there." The caveat is the small sample of postseason games at the park and the question of whether it is intimidating: it is certainly better than its predecessor, the Metrodome, but it does not have the same potential for overwhelming volume — though Twins fans could prove that judgement very wrong that month.
