# Twilight: 2000

Twilight: 2000 is a game about surviving in the aftermath of a fictional nuclear conflict in the heart of Europe — an alternate history where the Cold War went hot, swallowing up an entire United States Marine division, with players taking the roles of soldiers and other hangers-on just trying to make it out alive. Polygon senior tabletop editor Charlie Hall picks it as his game of 2023.

Hall prefaces the pick with a caveat about the format: he believes tabletop role-play should always take into account the audience that sits down at the table, which is part of why he dislikes best-of lists for this category — what difference does a high score on an arbitrary rubric make if the game does not resonate with the people in your community — and so treats year-end lists as a menu from which clever game masters pick the right flavour for a given table. His table was a group of neighborhood dads he was reconnecting with after years locked inside during the pandemic, assembled through a Discord for friendly games of Call of Duty's DMZ mode and a few visits to the local brewery; one night he broke out Twilight: 2000 and it was an instant hit. Mechanically, the heady mix of dice-driven gunplay and playing-card-style encounter creation makes it easy to run, and the fun begins even earlier at character generation, which uses old school randomized tables to simulate an entire military career. His recommendation is the all-inclusive starter set, aimed at people looking to get video gaming buddies around a table for something that does not require a headset or shouting down pre-teens in a glitchy public lobby.
