# Catholic Major and Minor Exorcism

The Catholic Church distinguishes between two forms of exorcism — "major" and "minor" — said Fr. Dan Reehil, exorcist of the Diocese of Nashville.

The office itself is defined first: an exorcist "is a priest chosen and installed by a bishop to exercise the office of deliverance ministry. Exorcism is a specific form of prayer that the [Catholic] Church uses against the power of the devil."

The discriminating features are the subject and the permission required. "A major exorcism is performed only for one, who is demonstrably possessed by a demon or demons, and may only be performed by a priest the bishop has specifically permitted to do so," said Reehil. Conversely, a minor exorcism is associated with sacraments and blessings.
