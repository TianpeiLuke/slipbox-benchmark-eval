# Vincent Namatjira On Choosing His Own Path From Albert's Legacy

Vincent Namatjira argues that inheriting Albert Namatjira's name obliges him to broaden it rather than copy it: "He chose his own path. Well, I choose mine," he told The Guardian on 16 October 2023.

The grounds are in his own practice. He eventually settled in the community of Indulkana in the Anangu Pitjantjatjara Yankunytjatjara lands, started a family and began to paint; the image of his great-grandfather has loomed large in the work he has made since 2012 — from the 13-panel series Albert's Story in 2014 to a 2023 portrait forming part of his Desert Songs series — yet he has never sought to imitate his ancestor's recognisable style. "We have both had to fill our legacies," he says. "I want the audience to see my work and my name, Vincent Namatjira, with the work of modern art – more portraits, figurative. It's colourful and bold."

The warrant is that continuation, not replication, is what honours a legacy — and the evidence that it works is that, a few decades after that classroom prize for his sketch of the Endeavour, his success means that for many younger audiences the name "Namatjira" brings to mind his name, not Albert's. Of those who followed his great-grandfather's technique he says: "They tried to do what Albert Namatjira left them, that kind of technique with watercolour painting. To be honest, I'd like my family and my culture and tradition to change their ways, and go forth and broaden the name."
