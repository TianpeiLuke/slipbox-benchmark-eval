# Alexei Popyrin's Davis Cup Breakthrough

Alexei Popyrin's straight-sets victory over 171st-ranked Finn Otto Virtanen in the 2023 Davis Cup semi-final was hailed as a potential springboard for his career, with Lleyton Hewitt animatedly embracing him afterwards, The Age reported on 24 November 2023.

Assessing the final ahead, Arthurs identified Popyrin as decisive: "I'm assuming they will back in Popyrin because he played pretty well a couple of nights ago, and with his firepower – he's probably the key to the tie. He's showing all the signs of becoming a top-25 player in the next couple of years. His junior career suggested he was going to be a very good player, and it's unusual for someone to win the junior French Open and not go on to something big. These moments can be a big springboard for someone like Alexei. He's a confidence player, and this is a very big moment – whoever he plays, Musetti or Sonego. But he's got to go out and embrace the moment, and he's the type of player who will enjoy it." Arthurs added: "I'd love to give de Minaur a chance with his fighting abilities, but the way Sinner has played the last six months, you'd have to say Sinner is the favourite."

On the head-to-head record, Popyrin won his only ATP Tour clash with Lorenzo Musetti — who has been as high as No.15 in the world — in a tight two-setter at the previous year's Miami Masters, but the emerging Italian claimed three of their four showdowns at the next level down; Popyrin is yet to play Lorenzo Sonego, whom Arthurs expects to play. Australia could also turn to an alternative No.2 singles option, with Jordan Thompson and Max Purcell waiting in the wings. "It's everybody's dream to play for their country," Popyrin said. "I looked up to Lleyton, looked up to all the older Aussie legends. To be able to put on the green and gold, to be able to go out there and play for your country, no matter the format, if it's this Davis Cup or the old Davis Cup, you give your all."
