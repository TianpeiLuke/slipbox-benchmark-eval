# Smart Home Device

## Definition

A smart home device is a connected household product — a speaker, camera, thermostat, light, plug, lock, sprinkler controller, or storage container — that can be controlled remotely (typically via a smartphone app or voice assistant) and often coordinates with other such devices through a shared smart home platform.

## Context

The corpus covers smart home devices mainly as a deal category in seasonal shopping roundups: The Verge devotes an entire Prime Day roundup (doc_0205) to smart home products, grouping the category into robot vacuums, security cameras and doorbells, smart speakers and displays, and smart lighting/locks/thermostats/sprinklers/plugs — all controllable from a phone and, increasingly, interoperable through a common home-automation platform.

The Verge's Black Friday under-$25 roundup (doc_0090) similarly carves out a "Smart home tech" section for budget smart plugs and a smart lamp, placed right alongside networking gear like a Wi-Fi extender.

The term also surfaces as a regulatory subject: the EU's antitrust probe into Amazon's iRobot acquisition explicitly gathered views from market participants including "suppliers of RVCs and other smart home devices," treating robot vacuum cleaners as one instance of the broader smart-home-device category.

Keep Labs' medicine-storage device (the Keep) shares the same connected-appliance characteristics described above — an app to unlock the lid, plus a scale and phone notifications to track medication use — even though its own category (secure medical storage) is unusual for a smart home device.

## Key Characteristics

- **Remote/app control** — devices are opened, adjusted, or monitored via a companion smartphone app (e.g., the Keep's lid opens via an app button; the Meross smart garage door controller lets you control your garage door from anywhere, and the MyQ Chamberlain Smart Garage Control opens the garage door from any location using a smartphone).
- **Voice-assistant integration** — many devices work with a voice assistant such as Amazon's Alexa; the Echo smart speaker is described as "a great way to get started with the smart home" and doubles as a smart home hub.
- **Interoperability via shared protocols** — devices such as the Govee LED Strip Light M1 and the Google Nest Thermostat support the Matter protocol specifically so a single hub can control gadgets from different manufacturers ("a wide array of smart home platforms" / "every major smart home platform, including Apple Home").
- **Hub/bridge role** — some devices (the fourth-gen Echo) act as more than an endpoint: it is also a Matter controller, Thread border router, and Zigbee bridge, connecting other gadgets to the home network.
- **Regulatory relevance beyond consumer tech** — the category is broad enough to draw antitrust scrutiny of channel access: the EU's iRobot probe frames robot vacuum cleaners as one of the "other smart home devices" whose competing suppliers depend on Amazon's marketplace for distribution.
