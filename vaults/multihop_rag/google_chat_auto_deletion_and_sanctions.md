# Google's Auto-Deleted Chats And The Sanctions Ruling

Google employees up to and including CEO Sundar Pichai were caught setting sensitive chats to auto-delete to keep them out of a court's hands, and the court has already decided Google should be sanctioned in some way for making potential evidence disappear — enough that the judge has decided to explicitly tell the jury they are not seeing all the evidence.

Bornstein used it to plant persistent seeds of doubt in the minds of jury members: "All we know is whatever is in the destroyed chats, as bad as the documents are, is worse. Or at least it was worse, before they were destroyed."

The best Google could do in response was to plant its own feeble seed with the jury — "Is Epic using the chats to distract me from all the evidence I do see?" — alongside Pomerantz's legal point that it is true Google could have automatically saved all chats for all relevant employees, but just because Google did not save some chats did not mean it violated antitrust laws.

The issue is not confined to this case. It has also come up in the US v. Google antitrust trial in Washington, DC, still ongoing at the start of Epic's trial.
