# Alternative Investment Funds Under The SEBI Framework

Alternative Investment Funds are pooled vehicles launched in 2012 after the market regulator SEBI issued guidelines for their management, guidelines that enable AIFs to identify and make investment opportunities available to investors in the public and private markets. AIFs can, for example, invest in listed and unlisted equities via private equity, infrastructure assets, venture debt, stressed assets, start-ups and so on; it is from this breadth that the term "alternatives" came about. The guidelines divide these funds into three categories, I, II and III, to make it easier for investors to understand a fund's investment objective.

The discriminating feature against traditional funds is correlation: AIF returns are generally uncorrelated with publicly traded equity or debt markets, providing a natural hedge against volatility and genuine portfolio diversification. The boundary on who may invest is the minimum investment threshold of ₹1 crore, which makes AIFs available only to HNIs, though not all funds must collect ₹1 crore in a single transaction; they can collect the money in tranches, called drawdowns, over 2-3 years, and that drawdown mechanism makes these funds easy on the wallet. AIFs have amassed an AUM of ₹8.30-lakh crore in the 11 years since inception, demonstrating the shift of HNI money to this asset.
