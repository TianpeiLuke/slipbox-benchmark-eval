# Smart Glasses Category Expectations And Echo Frames Positioning

The purpose of the Amazon Echo Frames is obvious — quick access to Alexa no matter where you are, because it's on your face — but The Verge argued that is not why anyone wants smart glasses, let alone a pair costing $389.99 like the Carrera Cruiser model the reviewer wore for a week.

The category expectation the Echo Frames sit against is cinematic: most people's vision of smart glasses is informed by Tony Stark, James Bond, and other iconic sci-fi and spy movie characters — sleek, discreet devices with some sort of hidden mixed reality display or the ability to capture the world around you. The original Google Glass cemented that image, while successors like the Snap Spectacles and the Ray-Ban Meta Smart Glasses have leaned heavily into content creation. The discriminating feature of the Echo Frames is that they have never been quite that ambitious: like the Bose Frames, Amazon's approach has primarily been a pair of open-ear headphones you can take calls on, listen to music with, and ask the occasional Alexa query. There is no camera, no screen, and nothing to clue anyone in that you're not just wearing ordinary glasses.
