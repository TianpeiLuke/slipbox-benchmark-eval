# The Exorcist III

The Exorcist III, written and directed by William Peter Blatty — the original screenwriter of The Exorcist and author of the novel it was based on — is, in Polygon's judgment, the sequel that came closest to capturing the same lightning in a bottle as William Friedkin's original, which nearly 50 years after its theatrical release has withstood the test of time as one of the most terrifying and influential horror films in American cinema. It was the Oct. 31 pick in Polygon's 2023-10-10 Halloween Countdown, listed on Prime Video and the Criterion Channel.

When a series of gruesome murders matching the modus operandi of an infamous serial killer begins to terrorize the city of Georgetown, Lt. William F. Kinderman, played by George C. Scott, investigates the psych ward of a local hospital in search of leads. Confronted with a suspect whose appearance matches that of his late friend Father Damien Karras, Kinderman is thrust into a battle for his very soul as he confronts an evil beyond his comprehension.

Polygon's Toussaint Egan calls the production history a tangled mess of false starts, studio interference, and thwarted artistic ambitions: it was originally conceived with Friedkin in mind to return as director, but he left over creative differences with Blatty; John Carpenter was at one time tapped to direct before he too stepped aside to let Blatty take the reins; and financier Morgan Creek Productions demanded Blatty reshoot the final act, which originally ended without an exorcism. Even so, Egan calls it a fascinating and terrifying film in its own right, with a hodgepodge of fascinating creative choices and literary dialogue that makes it feel like a sister piece to Blatty's The Ninth Configuration — maybe not the Exorcist sequel audiences at the time wanted, but still a damn fine picture.
