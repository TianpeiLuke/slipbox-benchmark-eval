# Dave Clark's Ouster From Flexport

On Sept. 13, 2023, Flexport founder Ryan Petersen took the stage at North America's premier supply chain conference in Phoenix — exactly a week after he had forced out his hand-picked successor as CEO, ex-Amazon executive Dave Clark, so Petersen could once again run the show — and Clark, whom he had ousted just a year into the job, was sitting in the first few rows.

Petersen was surprised that Clark showed up, according to people with knowledge of the matter. Days earlier Petersen had excoriated Clark, alleging he had secretly expanded the company's headcount and taken on unnecessary leases without Petersen's or the board's knowledge, and posting on X, formerly Twitter, "Strategic Plan, Day 1: Make better decisions!" With Clark a few feet away, Petersen struck a different tone: "I think we're going to look back and go, 'Wow I'd probably do that all over again because of the progress that we've made,'" he said on stage, and personally commended Clark for orchestrating the $1.3 billion purchase of Deliverr from Shopify, announced in May, which picked up supply chain technology for last-mile deliveries. "I'm very, very lucky because I wouldn't have had the courage to go and do that acquisition, but I give all the credit in the world to Dave Clark," Petersen said. "There's no one probably in the world who would be better at running that last-mile e-com fulfillment network." The mixed messaging from the 43-year-old founder underscores the dysfunction surrounding the sudden firing of Clark, who previously spent 23 years at Amazon and built its mammoth logistics network on the way to becoming one of Jeff Bezos' top deputies. It is also indicative of a bigger challenge facing Flexport, whose software is designed to simplify transporting goods: valued at $8 billion by private investors in early 2022, just as the economy was turning and the 10-year tech bull market was ending, the company has been trying to operate in Silicon Valley startup growth mode while restraining expenses to reflect new economic realities and cope with supply chain bottlenecks. CNBC's account is based on conversations with people close to Clark and Petersen who requested anonymity, corroborated by internal documents and communications. Petersen has publicly said Clark overspent, overhired and overpromised, burned through cash and kept Petersen in the dark about key financials and an ambitious expansion into end-to-end supply chain tools for small and medium-sized businesses — something his allies echoed to CNBC. But documents viewed by CNBC and sources close to Clark undermine those claims, showing that Clark, who arrived when the company was struggling to bill customers and track containers, worked closely with the board and Petersen to implement decisions Flexport now suggests were ill-advised; evidence to support the claims of financial mismanagement is lacking, raising questions about whether that narrative was put forward to justify Clark's exit. A Flexport spokesperson rejected that characterization: "Ryan Petersen returned as CEO in order to restore Flexport's culture of customer engagement, and drive the growth and cost discipline required to return the company to profitability."
