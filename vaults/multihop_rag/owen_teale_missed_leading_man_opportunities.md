# Owen Teale's Missed Leading Man Opportunities

Owen Teale had several chances to become a leading man and each fell away — financing collapsed, an American star was demanded, and he chose family over Hollywood networking — The Sydney Morning Herald reported on 20 October 2023.

When The Fifteen Streets attracted a massive TV audience in Britain, Teale was offered a lead role in a movie playing a bare knuckle fighter, but the financing fell through and he signed up for a four-year stint with the Royal Shakespeare Company instead. Years later a producer offered him the lead in a big film being shot in Britain, but the money men demanded an American star and William Hurt got the gig. When Teale won a Tony Award in 1997 for his performance in Ibsen's A Doll's House, he was told he needed to spend time in Hollywood wooing producers and decided to fly home to his family in London instead: "My wife was about to give birth, so I said 'I'm off'. I'm sure part of it was I just didn't believe that I was going to waltz into leading man roles in Hollywood." Despite the plaudits, awards and steady offers of work, the piece observes, he has never been certain of his gifts — probably why he is so consistently good, since complacency is not in his nature.

He is far better at recognising brilliance in others, such as Toni Collette, with whom he worked on Dream Horse, a 2020 movie about an unlikely champion racehorse. "Toni just raised my game. That's a huge talent, huge," he says, shaking his head in wonder. "I recently received an award from BAFTA for the film and part of me thinks they should give it to Toni." He is happiest in a room full of actors working on a play, a film or a TV production: "What turns me on is the thought of collaborating with talented people. I'm very sociable with the rest of the company and fully intend to do that when I come to Melbourne."
