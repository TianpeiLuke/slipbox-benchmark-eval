# Three Clinical Presentations Of The Canine Respiratory Illness

According to Oregon's Cantu-Schomus, the dogs' illnesses largely develop in three ways: as an inflammation of the tubes that connect the throat to the lungs, as chronic pneumonia, or as acute pneumonia that rapidly becomes severe.

The first two forms share a discriminating feature — they are minimally or not responsive to antibiotics. Inflammation of the tubes that connect the throat to the lungs is minimally or not responsive to antibiotics, and chronic pneumonia is likewise minimally or not responsive to antibiotics. What separates them is chronicity and location: airway inflammation in the first, established pneumonia in the second.

The third form is distinguished by speed rather than by antibiotic response. Acute pneumonia rapidly becomes severe and often leads to severe illness or possibly death in as little as 24 to 36 hours. That 24-to-36-hour window is the boundary marker that sets the acute presentation apart from the chronic one. The scoping matters: this is Cantu-Schomus's characterization of how the illnesses largely develop, so it describes the dominant patterns rather than an exhaustive classification.
