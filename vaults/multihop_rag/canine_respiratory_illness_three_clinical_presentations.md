# Canine Respiratory Illness: Three Clinical Presentations

According to Oregon's Cantu-Schomus, the dogs' illnesses largely develop in three ways, and the three are told apart by where the disease sits and how fast it moves.

The first is an inflammation of the tubes that connect the throat to the lungs that is minimally or not responsive to antibiotics. The second is chronic pneumonia that is likewise minimally or not responsive to antibiotics. The third is acute pneumonia that rapidly becomes severe and often leads to severe illness or possibly death in as little as 24 to 36 hours.

Poor antibiotic response is the shared feature of the first two forms, so it does not discriminate between them; the site of disease does, with the first confined to the airway connecting throat to lungs and the second reaching the lungs themselves. What sets the third apart from the second is tempo rather than site — both are pneumonia, but the acute form escalates within a 24-to-36-hour window. The characterisation is Oregon's, described as how the illnesses largely develop rather than as an exhaustive classification.
