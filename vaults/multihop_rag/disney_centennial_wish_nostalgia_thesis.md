# Disney's Centennial And The Wish Nostalgia Thesis

Polygon argues that while living in and echoing its own past has been profitable for Disney, it is not sustainable — and that harping so hard on its own glory days is antithetical to Disney's legacy, so to move forward the company needs to shed the nostalgia, or at least stop making it the primary selling point.

Wish, Walt Disney Animation's newest movie, is the culmination of the studio's celebration of its own centennial, a project that stretched throughout 2023 through the short Once Upon a Studio, specialized merchandise drops centered around each decade of the company's existence, and the rebrand of the iconic Disney castle logo. Polygon's complaint is that Wish leans so hard on celebrating the better Disney films that came before it that it does not have much identity of its own — openly a nostalgia-fest, pretty much on par with the other ways the company marked its 100-year anniversary. The article concedes the appeal: in 2023 Disney drilled in harder than ever on looking admiringly at its own history, and why not, given a full century of beloved movies, characters, and storytelling to mine for big emotion, with even people who have outgrown Disney retaining residual fondness for at least one animated movie from their youth. But it insists the pattern is not confined to the birthday celebration: nostalgia-bait is basically what the company has been selling for decades.
