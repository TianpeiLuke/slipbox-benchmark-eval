# Anker 321 Travel Power Strip

The Anker 321 Power Strip was $15, down from $26 — 42 percent off at Amazon. It is an all-in-one 20W USB-C power cube with three AC outlets, two USB-A ports, and one USB-C port, and it is deputy features editor Sarah Jeong's pick.

Her case for it starts from a specific failure. Nobody wants to carry a power strip or extension cord on vacation, and it is probably unnecessary in relatively modern buildings and definitely unnecessary while camping — but sometimes you stay in a charming historical hotel or a lovely cabin in the woods and discover at bedtime that the only electrical socket in the bedroom is in the corner farthest from the bed, with two lamps already plugged into it. Older buildings especially suffer from what she calls loose socket syndrome, where those convenient modern boxy socket extenders with five different USB and USB-C charging ports cannot stay in place and fall right out of the wall because they are too heavy. After one otherwise pleasant vacation spent charging her phone, watch, AirPods, and laptop in a weird corner of the room with the charging hub propped on a strategically balanced mountain of books and sham pillows, she bought this Anker combination extension cord and power strip.

It is not a full power strip — just a cube with a few sockets plus USB and USB-C charging ports at the end of a five-foot cable. It takes up extra space in her suitcase, but each trip has left her feeling vindicated about the purchase.

The design detail that matters is that the three prongs at the end of the cable are static rather than folding flat for easy packing, which is key because the loose sockets of older buildings reject the beautiful convenience of folding prongs. There are more than enough sockets for one person, and with some finagling — and maybe an extra charging brick — it can accommodate two people's devices. If you are traveling overseas, do not forget to buy a different plug type for the region or pack an adapter.
