# Antibiotic Stewardship Practices

Until next-generation bacteria slayers make it to market, immediate measures must be taken to stall the rise of superbugs by preventing the misuse of antibiotics that pressures bacteria to evolve resistance in the first place — beginning with doctors being more diligent about confirming that bacteria, not viruses, are behind a patient's infection before prescribing antibiotics.

Dr. Shruti Gohil, a lead investigator of four INSPIRE-ASP Trials — federally funded research aimed at improving hospitals' antibiotic use — described the further safeguards: auditing doctors' prescriptions to see if narrower-spectrum drugs could be used instead of broad ones, and requiring special clearance for the broadest-spectrum drugs. These steps are essential not only in hospitals but everywhere antibiotics are prescribed, from primary care to dentistry, Gohil said, and each interaction between a doctor and their patient matters. The rationale for working case by case is population-level: "by reducing individual risk, you anticipate that you will drop the overall population-level risk," she stressed, and eventually slash the prevalence of multidrug-resistant bugs.
