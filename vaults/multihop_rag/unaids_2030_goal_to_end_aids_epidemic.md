# The UNAIDS 2030 Goal to End the AIDS Epidemic

Countries around the world have signed onto an ambitious United Nations program whose goal is to end the AIDS epidemic by 2030 by reducing the rate of new HIV infections and AIDS-related deaths to below the reproductive rate of 1, country by country — meaning each person living with HIV would infect fewer than one additional person in their lifetime.

The framing comes from Quarraisha Abdool Karim, associate scientific director of the Centre for the AIDS Programme of Research in South Africa and a joint United Nations Programme on HIV/AIDS (UNAIDS) special ambassador, who described the target to Live Science. If the program is successful, the world would see 200,000 new HIV infections and 130,000 AIDS-related deaths in 2030 — 90% fewer than in 2010. The distinguishing feature of the goal is that it does not require eradication: while eradicating the virus would require a vaccine and a cure, infections and death rates could eventually be driven to near zero without those tools, Abdool Karim said. Reaching that point is what would head off the risk of infections rebounding by the end of the decade. "We do have the tools to end AIDS as a public health threat. We do have the biomedical interventions," she said. "The challenge is, how do we all get to that point?"
