# Alameda Absorbed FTX's Losses, Including MobileCoin

Alameda Research functioned as FTX's sin-eater: when FTX took a loss, the loss was moved onto Alameda's balance sheet so that FTX would not look bad. Gary Wang testified that in 2021 FTX was facing a "several hundred million dollar" loss from an exploit in MobileCoin — apparently in April 2021, though Wang did not give a month — and Bankman-Fried directed him to have Alameda take it on instead, because FTX's balance sheets were visible to investors and Alameda's were not.

The relation was general, not a one-off. Ellison testified that her tenure as CEO of Alameda Research was an exercise in optics: when FTX took a loss in the hundreds of millions of dollars because a trader had exploited its system, Wang moved that loss onto Alameda's balance sheet, and FTX's "insurance fund," meant to keep traders from getting stuck with other traders' losses, was totally made up, with Alameda simply picking up the tab. The concealment held all the way to investors: Paradigm Capital's Matt Huang showed the court the balance sheets he had received from Bankman-Fried, and they did not include the loss, which should have appeared in the second-quarter numbers he was given — those instead showed $63 million in trading expenses and $63 million in "other expenses" for the quarter, and an estimated profit of $732 million.

Bankman-Fried offered a benign reading of the same arrangement from the stand: he said the MobileCoin loss, the one Wang said Alameda took to keep off FTX's balance sheet, was a totally innocent matter in which he thought it appropriate that Alameda take the position as a backstop liquidity provider.
