# MacBook Pro M3 Generation Deals

Apple announced Macs using a new M3 processor at its "Scary Fast" event the night before Halloween, including a new 14-inch MacBook Pro that replaces the 13-inch M2 model — meaning Touch Bar Macs are finally dead, though some outgoing M2 models remain available. The new entry point is a MagSafe-equipped MacBook Pro that uses the existing 14-inch design and slightly pares it down: the 14-inch M3 MacBook Pro has the same 3024 x 1964 resolution display and 120Hz refresh rate as its pricier siblings and adds an SD card slot and HDMI port, but starts with just 8GB of RAM and lacks the third USB-C / Thunderbolt port found on the M3 Pro and M3 Max models, as well as Thunderbolt 4 speeds.

The M3 MacBook Pro is a bit of an odd middle child but still a very good laptop, The Verge wrote — especially on a deal that puts more price distance between it and the M3 Pro version. The base model with 512GB of storage had already been seen at $150 off during Black Friday and Cyber Monday, and was available for an even better $1,399 at Amazon, Best Buy, and B&H Photo, $200 off; the 1TB configuration was at its all-time low of $1,599, $200 off, at Best Buy and B&H Photo, and the configuration with base storage and 16GB of RAM was $1,599, $200 off, at B&H Photo. Anyone planning a higher-spec configuration than that would likely want to spring for the M3 Pro model instead.

The 14- and 16-inch MacBook Pros for late 2023 are another round of spec-bump models, much like their 2022 incarnations, now starting with the M3 Pro chip or the speedier M3 Max and 18GB of base RAM instead of 16GB, and remain targeted at creatives doing video editing, photo processing, and other graphical work. Like their predecessors and the M1 Pro generation before them, they offer MagSafe charging, three USB-C / Thunderbolt 4 ports, HDMI-out, and a full-size SD card slot, with prices starting at $1,999 for the 14-inch and $2,499 for the 16-inch — and they can be specced up considerably, as with the 16-inch review unit Apple sent The Verge, which cost an eye-watering $7,199. Discounts had begun on the versions actual humans buy: the 14-inch in its base M3 Pro configuration (11-core CPU / 14-core GPU, 18GB of RAM, 512GB SSD) was $1,749 at B&H Photo, $250 off; a similar version with 1TB of storage and a 12-core / 18-core M3 Pro was $2,149, also $250 off, at B&H Photo; and the base 16-inch with the M3 Pro (12-core CPU / 18-core GPU), 18GB of RAM, and a 512GB SSD was $2,249 at Amazon and B&H Photo, $250 off.
