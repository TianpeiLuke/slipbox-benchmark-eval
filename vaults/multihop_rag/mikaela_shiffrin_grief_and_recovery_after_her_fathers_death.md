# Mikaela Shiffrin's Grief And Recovery After Her Father's Death

Mikaela Shiffrin was left questioning how she could ever ski again after her father's death, and returned to competition only in 2021, at the first of the two slalom races at Levi, after a lengthy period of absence from the sport, as reported by Essentially Sports on 3 November 2023.

The grief is described in detail. Shiffrin said skiing was "the place where I feel most connected to him. It's kind of hard to feel that anywhere else". She shared that she was in the middle of a Sports Illustrated photoshoot when her dad had the accident, which left her feeling immense pain every time she saw the pictures from the shoot. Without her anchor, her mother said, the grief was so immense that she couldn't even get out of bed; Eileen stated, "She was so devastated. She couldn't stop crying".

The turning point given is her brother's intervention. Taylor, unable to see his sister reeling from the pain, one day walked over to his mom and Mikaela and told her "You can't quit on us"; he spoke in an interview calling it his moment of "selfishness," and said it's what his dad would also want and how he would have handled the situation, quoting the phrase his dad often used when they were children: "When you're going through hell, keep going. Don't stop". In 2022, when she set the record for the most World Cup wins in Alpine skiing history, she honoured her dad on Instagram: "Thank you. Dad, I hope you had a good view. Mom, your strength, love, support, and belief in me is the greatest treasure I'll ever know".
