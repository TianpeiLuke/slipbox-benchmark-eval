# JP Morgan's Note Against The HSF Board's Handling Of The Valuation

JP Morgan analyst Christopher Brown, one of the closest watchers of Hipgnosis Songs Fund in recent years, issued a damning note on 19 December 2023 attacking the board's attempt to shift responsibility for the valuation. He wrote that the delay "is an early blow to the credibility of the new [Hipgnosis Songs Fund] Board, and casts further doubt over the credibility of the independent valuer, Citrin Cooperman", and concluded that "[it] is up to the [Hipgnosis Songs Fund] Board to satisfy itself that the independent valuer, whom it hires, is up to the job, rather than try and pin the blame on [Hipgnosis Songs Management]", adding that in JP Morgan's view "a breakdown in the relationship between Board and manager would not be optimal for maximising shareholder value".

Brown said he was surprised that a second valuation expert had not been drafted in to value the HSF portfolio for the latest interim results, especially as Kroll had been appointed to consider the "reasonableness" of Citrin Cooperman's assumptions for HSF's previous set of results to the end of March 2023. He suggested that the easiest solution now for the board is to apply an additional discount rate to the Citrin Cooperman valuation to reflect current market uncertainty, on the basis that it is probably too late to employ another valuer, and quantified the effect: a 0.5% rise in the discount rate, from 8.5% to 9.0%, would "reduce the fair value of the [HSF] portfolio by 7.9%, which on a leveraged basis is [approximately a] 10% reduction in NAV [Net Asset Value]". Should the board go down that route he expected the shares to weaken, given that it represents another "unforced error", and he added that JP Morgan would not be surprised to see Citrin Cooperman resign, having been very publicly undermined by the board that day. Despite all this, JP Morgan retained its "overweight" rating against HSF's share price "in spite of yet another [HSF] own goal".
