# Spotify's Podcast Business Efficiency Pivot

Spotify reported an operating profit for the first time in a year, sending the stock up nearly 10 percent, with the revenue bump largely due to the streamer's $1 price increase earlier in 2023 — but executives also pointed to the downsizing of the podcast operation, which included hundreds of layoffs and the dissolution of Gimlet and Parcast, as a contributing factor. On the investor call, CFO Paul Vogel said podcasting, after being a real "drag" on profit margins in the past, will soon break even and turn toward profitability, and CEO Daniel Ek echoed him: "We're constantly finding new ways to bring more efficiencies out of the business… We've seen some improvements, but you should expect us to continue to look for more improvements going forward because that's just our modus operandi."

The Verge's October 25th, 2023 analysis argued that the adage "content is king" no longer applies at Spotify, which is increasingly focusing on tools that will scale the business rather than content that will attract listeners. Ek and Vogel hammered the efficiency theme — by the writer's transcript they said the word more than 30 times over the 50-minute call — but what was left unsaid about podcasting spoke louder: in the past they would excitedly tout how many millions of podcasts were on the platform, the latest new celebrity show, and the area's explosive growth, and as investors have run out of patience for the time and money such ambitions required, leadership changed its tune. The writer's summary of the new iteration is leaner, less splash, and more scale; Spotify's contributions to podcasting will not necessarily be insignificant — one audio industry professional told her the translation thing could be a total game-changer for the medium, if it is not a complete dud — but they should not be expected in the form of original content.

The open question she was left with is where Spotify's licensing deals fit. She wished someone on the call had asked Ek about the plan for big-name talent like Joe Rogan, Alex Cooper, and Dax Shepard as their deals come up for renewal: on one hand they have the scale, Rogan especially; on the other, they cost a chunk of cash, Rogan especially. It will be interesting, she wrote, to see how much Spotify and its investors can stomach spending on the biggest names in podcasting.
