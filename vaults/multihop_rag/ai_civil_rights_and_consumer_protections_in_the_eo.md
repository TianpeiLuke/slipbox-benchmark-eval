# Civil Rights, Privacy And Consumer Protections In The AI Executive Order

The executive order extends anti-discrimination guidance to "landlords, federal benefits programs and federal contractors" to prevent AI systems from exacerbating discrimination within their spheres of influence, and directs the Department of Justice to develop best practices for investigating and prosecuting civil rights violations related to AI — including, per the announcement, "the use of AI in sentencing, parole and probation, pretrial release and detention, risk assessments, surveillance, crime forecasting and predictive policing, and forensic analysis."

That builds on earlier work. The first Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights, released by the White House last October, directed agencies to "combat algorithmic discrimination while enforcing existing authorities to protect people's rights and safety," the administration official said. "But there's more to do."

On privacy, the EO calls for prioritizing federal support to accelerate development of privacy-preserving techniques that would let future large language models be trained on large datasets without the current risk of leaking personal details those datasets might contain. Such solutions could include "cryptographic tools that preserve individuals' privacy," developed with assistance from the Research Coordination Network and the National Science Foundation. The order also reiterates its calls for bipartisan legislation from Congress addressing the broader privacy issues AI systems present for consumers.

In healthcare, the EO states that the Department of Health and Human Services will establish a safety program that tracks and remedies unsafe, AI-based medical practices. Educators will also see support from the federal government in using AI-based educational tools like personalized chatbot tutoring.
