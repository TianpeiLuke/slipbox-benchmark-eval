# Why Small Self-Administered Ketamine Doses Are Not Safer

The intuition that small doses of ketamine are safer than the larger doses given via IV is the premise these clinicians attack: when the drug is self-administered, Johnson warned, there is a danger of exceeding safe limits.

"The total cumulative dose could end up being higher than a standard dose of a medically administered intravenous dose," he told Fox News Digital.

The route also changes the behavioral risk. Intranasal use can lead to "more aggressive drug-seeking" behaviors, Johnson warned, as the "high" of taking the ketamine is paired with cues in the user's environment -- which are not present in a medical office.

Like other medications, such as opiates or benzodiazepines, ketamine has the potential to be abused, Sullivan agreed. "Ketamine should only be used within the confines of a close relationship between a patient and an experienced prescriber," he said, citing the position reaffirmed by ASKP3.
