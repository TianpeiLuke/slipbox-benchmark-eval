# Tate McRae's Greedy TikTok Breakthrough

Tate McRae's song Greedy went viral in August 2023 after being used in a video by popular TikToker Tube Girl and then in countless hair-flip transition clips, eventually amassing over 320 million streams globally, topping charts internationally and becoming the 20-year-old's proper breakthrough, The Age reported on 30 November 2023.

Until then her career, the review says, had "a stench of desperation about it" — a wild thing to say about someone only 20, but the description of an artist trading explicitly in classic pop moves while failing to grab the popularity such expression necessitates. The Calgary-born musician had already had a journey: a professionally trained dancer since 6, at 13 she finished third on the US version of So You Think You Can Dance and attracted record label attention with songs uploaded to YouTube. In 2020 her weeper You Broke Me First had a chart moment, leading to her 2022 debut I Used To Think I Can Fly, an intriguing album of thrashy bangers and post-Billie Eilish sad-pop featuring production from pop titans Greg Kurstin (Adele) and Finneas (Eilish). It had at least one could've-been smash — the spiky What Would You Do? — but, fairly listless despite her swaggering delivery, it failed to ignite with either mainstream audiences or pop aesthetes.

Greedy also landed her a slot on Saturday Night Live, "that pop superstar rite-of-passage", and sold out shows for her Australian tour the following November. That it was the song to push her to the next level is somewhat confounding, the reviewer writes: it is not particularly memorable and her debut had better tracks that slipped listeners by, but its sassy sentiment — "I would want myself", particularly suited to clips of youthful coming-of-age expression — caught on with the TikTok crowd, "which is how pop stardom happens these days". In another popstar move, McRae channelled an alter ego, Tatiana, who is "ballsy, loud and obnoxious", for Greedy's vibrant music video, a fun throwback to choreographed Y2K-era aesthetics with McRae dancing all lustily on a Zamboni.
