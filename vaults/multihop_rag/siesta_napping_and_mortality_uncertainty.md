# Siesta Napping and Mortality Uncertainty

The siesta, a brief afternoon nap and a traditional habit in Mediterranean countries, is the component scientists remain undecided about: in the current study, specific napping habits were found to be associated with higher risk of mortality and cardiovascular disease, cutting against the presumption that every element of the Mediterranean lifestyle is protective.

The researchers themselves qualify that finding. Similar to the case of olive oil consumption, they say they did not have detailed information on frequency and duration of naps or sleep structure — how often and for how long — which weakens their conclusion. "Moreover, reverse causation is also possible, with people napping because of subclinical conditions or other chronic diseases (for instance, if they had more prevalence of sleep apnea), especially since napping faces a stigma in Anglo-Saxon countries," said Sotos-Prieto. She also points to a different shape of relationship in earlier work: "Previous studies have found a J-shaped relationship — a smile-like curve — between napping and cardiovascular diseases," a chart type in which low values of one variable (short nap duration) lead to a decrease in the second variable (incidence of cardiovascular events), while the trend reverses at higher values. "Short naps, which range from 0 to 30 minutes, were associated with lower incident cardiovascular disease," she said. "An inverse association between short naps and mortality and cardiovascular diseases was found in our previous publications." Asked for a definitive answer as to whether napping is beneficial, Sotos-Prieto indicated that more research is needed.
