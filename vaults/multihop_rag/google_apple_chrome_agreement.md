# Google's Agreement Not To Promote Chrome To Safari Users

As part of its search deal with Apple, Google agreed not to promote Chrome to Safari users, according to Bloomberg — a restraint that mattered because Google would otherwise have been able to do this with banners, pop-ups and other annoying means in its other Google apps. The same agreement also meant that Apple never switched to a Google competitor, and never allowed users to choose their browser when setting up their iPhones.

The observation surfaced in the antitrust trial, where the amount Google spent on homepage market share has been a fixing point. Earlier in October 2023, the CEOs of Microsoft and DuckDuckGo testified that their search engines would have been far more successful, even competitive with Google, had they been able to make similar deals with Apple. Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella even said he was willing to spend $15 billion per year to get Bing into Apple's default search, per The Information — a stated willingness to pay, not a deal that happened.

A second, related admission came from Google's side: Pichai admitted to attempting to get Tim Cook to preinstall Google on every iOS device back in 2018, according to The Verge. His aim, as reported, was to make Google's and Apple's services so connected as to be inseparable.
