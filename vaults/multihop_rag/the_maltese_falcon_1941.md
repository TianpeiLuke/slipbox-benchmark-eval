# The Maltese Falcon (1941)

John Huston's The Maltese Falcon is probably the most famous noir movie of all time, and Polygon's Tasha Robinson presents it as the film that covers the genre's basics — an adaptation of Dashiell Hammett's 1930 crime story, rentable on Amazon, Vudu, Apple, and YouTube.

The basics it covers are a hard-bitten detective, Sam Spade played by Humphrey Bogart, on a case full of twists, turns, and double-crosses; a beautiful woman who lures him in and turns out to be full of lies; and a McGuffin everyone is after that turns out to be more than it seems. Robinson calls it a prime example of a noir that is more about atmosphere, cinematography, and a complicated plot than about edge-of-the-seat thrills — a moody movie with a lot of great performances. Her qualification is that it has been imitated and iterated on so much that it can feel a bit basic compared with its followers, which is also what makes it one of those films where watching it suddenly makes a thousand later cultural references and media in-jokes make sense.
