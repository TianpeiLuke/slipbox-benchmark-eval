# The Smiths' Promise That Divorce Would Never Be Necessary

Across their 26-year marriage Will Smith and Jada Pinkett Smith have argued that divorce is simply not an available outcome for them, on the grounds that they removed it as an option at the outset and have treated that promise as binding ever since. Pinkett Smith restated the claim in a preview of an interview with Today's Hoda Kotb that aired on 13 October 2023: "I made a promise that there will never be a reason for us to get a divorce. We will work through, whatever. I just haven't been able to break that promise." In the same interview she confirmed that the couple had been living "completely separate lives" for seven years, and, asked why they did not "just amicably divorce," replied simply: "We don't want to."

The warrant joining the promise to the outcome is that removing the exit changes how the couple treat the problems. Pinkett Smith recalled setting the terms before the 1997 wedding, in a 2018 Red Table Talk episode: "I told Will from the gate, I said let me tell you something: 'If you marry me, know this: we're gonna be together. We're going to be under the same roof.' For me personally, I'm not mature enough to have a divorce, I'm just not. I don't think I would ever be mature enough, I don't." Will Smith — who had divorced his first wife, Sheree Zampino, in 1994 — made the same argument in different terms. In 2006 he told MTV News: "With Jada, I stood up in front of God and said: 'Til death do us part.' So there are two possible outcomes. One, we are going to be together 'til death, or two, I am dead." On the Ellen DeGeneres Show he put it as an argument about choice architecture: "divorce just can't be an option... It's really that simple. And I think that's the problem with LA — there are so many options. So a huge part of the success for [Jada] and I is that we just removed the other options." As of October 2023 it remained unclear whether the couple planned to end their separation and live in the same home again.
