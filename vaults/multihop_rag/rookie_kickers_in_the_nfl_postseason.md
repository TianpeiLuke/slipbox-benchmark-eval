# Rookie Kickers In The NFL Postseason

Postseason field-goal make rates since 2000 stack up pretty evenly between rookie kickers and veterans — 86% (67 of 78) for rookies against 84% (720 of 862) for veterans — so there is no statistical indication that rookies are less effective in the postseason, The New York Times reported on 16 October 2023.

The question arose because the 49ers lost that Sunday when their rookie kicker missed from 41 yards. San Francisco had raised eyebrows among some when they used their second-highest pick in the 2023 draft — a third-round choice, 99th overall — on Michigan's Jake Moody, a cost-effective successor to veteran Robbie Gould; general manager John Lynch called Moody a potentially "foundational" player whose stock in the draft was so high that other teams were trying to trade ahead of San Francisco for a shot at him. The season had seen Moody and Saints rookie Blake Grupe miss game-winning tries. By distance band, the postseason rates since 2000 are: under 33 yards, rookies 97% (31 of 32) versus veterans 94% (330 of 351); 34-44 yards, rookies 75% (21 of 28) versus veterans 82% (230 of 279); 45-50 yards, rookies 78% (7 of 9) versus veterans 73% (121 of 165); 51-55 yards, rookies 100% (8 of 8) versus veterans 59% (33 of 56); and 56-plus yards, rookies 0% (0 of 1) versus veterans 55% (6 of 11).

A veteran evaluator entered the caveat that the numbers do not capture the moment: "Playoff teams have a different aura, different feel in the locker room. Percentages are one thing, but who is the guy who trots out there to try the game-winner? It's a different pressure shooting free throws up by 20, down by 20, than in that type of situation." Fans might recall rookie kickers missing playoff kicks — Rodrigo Blankenship with the Colts in 2020, Harrison Butker with the Chiefs in 2017, Nate Kaeding with the Chargers in 2005 — but the NFL has also seen plenty of rookies kick effectively in the postseason: Evan McPherson made all 14 postseason tries with the 2021 Super Bowl Bengals, the 2017 Eagles won a Super Bowl with rookie Jake Elliott performing well, the 2012 Super Bowl champion Ravens had rookie Justin Tucker, and the 2006 Patriots trusted rookie Stephen Gostkowski.
