# Scott McTominay's Advanced Role And Goal Threat

Under Ten Hag, Scott McTominay functions as a marauding midfield engine and late-running goal threat with a licence to create overloads wherever he finds them — the job United once paid Paul Pogba to do.

The structure of the role differs from his previous, more conventional midfield positions. Against low blocks and structured defences — and, the piece notes, nobody is remotely accusing Chelsea of possessing one of those — he provides the extra body in a tightly marked penalty area; but he also pops up in the channels and on the wing, either supporting the man in possession or offering himself for the quick switch of play. The relation between that role and his output shows in the numbers: against Chelsea he scored his fifth and sixth goals of the season, having previously never got more than seven. The piece attributes this partly to opponents still not quite cottoning on to his goal threat — those late runs to the far post, the blur on the edge of the 18-yard box you do not spot until it is too late — and partly to how Ten Hag is using him. The range over which the relation holds is the current team: for all the undoubted leadership qualities of Bruno Fernandes and Harry Maguire, tactically it is McTominay running the show right now, a development with numerous interesting consequences. The Pogba comparison is drawn with the caveat that McTominay is a different kind of player with a different kind of skillset; Pogba's United career never quite worked out, largely — if you believe his most vehement critics — on account of his hair.
