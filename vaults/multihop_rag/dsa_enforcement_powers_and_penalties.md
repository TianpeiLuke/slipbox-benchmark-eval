# DSA Enforcement Powers And Penalties

The European Commission is responsible for enforcing the DSA on larger platforms such as X, which was designated a very large online platform (VLOP) under the DSA back in April 2023. Confirmed breaches of the online governance regime can face a range of major sanctions, including fines of up to 6% of global annual turnover.

The Commission's powers are not limited to fines. The EU can also apply interim measures where it believes there is a risk of serious harm for users, and may even seek to have access to infringing services temporarily blocked. Its 18 December 2023 announcement was careful to caveat the development by affirming that no conclusions had yet been reached.

There is also no confirmed timeline for the probe to conclude. But the fact of its being opened means other tools are put within reach of regulators and could be applied if the EU sees the need to act quickly — such as the interim measures above — so the Commission's official scrutiny of X could have real world implications for how the platform operates sooner rather than later.
