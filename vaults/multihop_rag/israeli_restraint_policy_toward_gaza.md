# Israel's Restraint Policy Toward Gaza

In an article five years before October 7, Brigadier General Eran Ortal argued that "Israeli restraint as regards proactive thwarting of offensive capabilities in the Gaza Strip stems from a clear Israeli strategy that strives to reduce the influence of the enemy on the current routine of life in Israel, even at the price of worsening of the threat in the future."

Ortal treated the policy as natural — "a Western nation cannot lead its life in an endless state of emergency" — but insisted that "alongside the understanding of the need for a containment policy, it must be recognized that the risks are high, as evidenced by the steeply worsening intensity of conflicts between Israel and the terrorist organizations in Gaza." His grounds are the adversary's adaptation: in recent decades Hamas and Hezbollah developed ballistic missiles and terror tunnels in response to Israeli policy that sought minimal friction with the enemy through separation and withdrawal from the security zone in southern Lebanon and the Gaza Strip. He points out that between Operation Pillar of Defense in 2012 and Operation Protective Edge in 2014, Israel significantly reduced targeted countermeasures against terrorist operatives, and none focused on Hamas operatives; his analysis is that because Iron Dome only came into use in 2011, the most dangerous threat posed by Gaza developed in the period preceding Operation Protective Edge. The understandings Israel reached with Hamas in 2012 were made "to stop the fighting," Ortal says, and to get life back to normal as quickly as possible — after air raid sirens sounded in Tel Aviv and Jerusalem for the first time since the Yom Kippur War and the education system shut down for more than a week. In 2018 Ortal wrote of "the barrier" that "it would be wrong to assume it will turn out to be more immune than its predecessors. The longer as we delay cutting the direct connection between the Hamas rockets and those of our other enemies, who make extensive use of this method of operation, and the idea of attacking our territory, the longer we will be condemned to watch from the sidelines and look at their constant enhancement." Ortal is currently in active military service and unavailable for interview.
