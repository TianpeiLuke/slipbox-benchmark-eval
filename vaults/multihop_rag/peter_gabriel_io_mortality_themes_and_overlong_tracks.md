# I/O's Mortality Themes And Overlong Tracks

The Age's 30 November 2023 review argues that I/O is animated by a palpable sense of mortality but undermined by songs left "in the oven too long" — stretching to five, six and seven minutes and in need of an edit or at least a change in tempo.

The grounds for the first half of the claim are biographical and musical. Gabriel is 73, and he is mining a seam of material about taking stock, feeling at one with the world and the admittedly not-exactly-original realisation that love is all we need. This reaches its most intense moment in And Still, a song he wrote to deal with the death of his mother in 2016 — a fragile, delicate thing with an almost Nick Drake transparency, as he remembers leaning his head against her skin and the feeling of her brushing his hair as a young boy. The reviewer also notes that in the late '90s Gabriel collaborated with Randy Newman on That'll Do from the Babe soundtrack, and suggests that should Newman be indisposed for the next Pixar film, Gabriel's Playing For Time could easily slot in for the reflective, tear-inducing, life-affirming ballad Newman routinely provides at the end of the second act.

The grounds for the criticism are two named tracks: Olive Tree and Live and Let Live overload the "one with the world" barrow he is pushing — the former a Sting-like concoction that seems to be two different songs bolted together, the latter coming from a fine place, inspired by Nelson Mandela and The Elders project, but littered with cliches such as "When we can forgive, we can move on" and "An eye for an eye, again and again, until the whole world is blind". On top of all this there are two mixes of the album, a "bright-side mix" by Mark "Spike" Stent and a "dark-side mix" by Tchad Blake. "Yes, it's a lot to take in," the reviewer concludes. "But then, it's been a while in the making. And who knows when, or if, we'll get a follow-up?"
