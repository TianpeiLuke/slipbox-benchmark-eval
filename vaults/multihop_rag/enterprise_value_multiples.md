# Enterprise Value Multiples

Enterprise value refers to the total value of a company or business, and the total value as perceived by the market is represented by the sum of its market cap plus net debt, which includes minority interest. What works in the case of EV is that it allows a capital-agnostic analysis: whether a company is funded only with equity or with a combination of equity and debt, the total value of the business is the same, because sources of funding do not matter. Using EV, the value of equity, the share price, is derived from the value of the business; since the capital structure of companies varies significantly, an EV approach enables valuing them on similar terms, arriving at the value of the business first and then subtracting net debt to reach the equity value. The advantage of EV-based metrics is that they give more options for valuing a company.

The boundary conditions are set by where in the statements one can anchor. Valuation accuracy is better the lower down the P&L and cash flow statement one can get to, but there are many times when company profits are low while operational performance, that is EBITDA, is not bad; in such cases, as revenue and EBITDA continue to grow, operating leverage will result in PE growing much faster than those two line items. Hence EV-based metrics are options to consider when equity-based valuation multiples do not give a clear picture. Where even EBITDA multiples are not clear, as was the case with many new-age company IPOs that were reporting losses even at the EBITDA level, investors have no option but to go further up the P&L to metrics like EV/revenue, or outside the P&L entirely to metrics like EV/subscriber.
