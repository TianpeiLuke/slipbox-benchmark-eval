# National Basketball Association (NBA)

## Definition

The National Basketball Association (NBA) is the top professional men's basketball league in the United States, grouped in the corpus among "the four major US pro sports leagues" alongside the NFL, MLB, and NHL, and described elsewhere as "the pinnacle of the sport" of basketball.

## Context

In the corpus the NBA appears in three overlapping roles: as a cultural institution with its own holiday tradition, as a subject of sports betting coverage, and as a point of contrast with other basketball leagues.
Its Christmas Day games date back to 1947 and are described as a tradition older than the Super Bowl; last season's slate averaged 4.27 million viewers across platforms — about 200% more than a typical regular-season game — and games are broadcast to over 200 countries in more than 50 languages.
The league is regularly set up as a rival tradition to the NFL's Thanksgiving Day games, with commentary noting the NBA "far outperforms" the NFL in global popularity even though the NFL draws more domestic viewers.
A large share of the corpus's NBA coverage is sports-betting content — moneyline, point-spread, prop, and futures betting sites all use the NBA as their running example, and articles track odds for annual awards such as NBA MVP and NBA Rookie of the Year.
The league is also used as a reference point by Australia's National Basketball League (NBL): NBL recaps describe several players as "ex-NBA" or "NBA hopeful," and note NBL clubs playing exhibition games against NBA opposition, distinguishing the two leagues while linking them through player movement.
Ticket-price reporting places the average 2022 NBA ticket at $94, versus $112 for the NFL, underscoring the NFL's larger domestic commercial scale even as the NBA is framed as more globally popular.

## Key Characteristics

- **One of the "four major" US leagues** — grouped with the NFL, MLB, and NHL as a top-tier US professional sports league.
- **Draft-based player pipeline** — teams acquire young talent through an annual draft, and draft outcomes ("draft misses") are tracked for their long-term effect on team success.
- **Regular season, playoffs, and marquee annual awards** — the league runs a regular season, playoffs, and honors such as the NBA MVP and NBA Rookie of the Year, each with dedicated betting markets and odds coverage.
- **Signature holiday tradition** — Christmas Day games, dating to 1947 and older than the Super Bowl, function as the league's answer to the NFL's Thanksgiving games and are a major global broadcast event.
- **Global broadcast reach** — Christmas Day games alone are broadcast to more than 200 countries and translated into more than 50 languages.
- **Distinct from same-named/similar leagues abroad** — contrasted in the corpus with Australia's NBL, whose rosters and coverage frequently reference NBA-experienced or NBA-aspiring players.
- **A dominant subject of the sports-betting content ecosystem** — used as the default example league across moneyline, spread, prop, and futures betting explainers.
