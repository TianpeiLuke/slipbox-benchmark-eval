# The SEO-Algorithm Update Cycle

Since the dawn of the internet there have been people attempting to manipulate search and then people decrying those manipulations as the end of search's ability to be useful, and it works in cycles: people doing SEO find loopholes in the algorithm; critics complain about search results; search engines innovate and close the loopholes. Rinse, repeat.

The cycle has turned several times before the current moment of widespread disillusionment. In 2003, as Google approached the deadline to disclose pertinent business information leading up to its IPO, the company quietly released an update cracking down. By 2011 SEO was once again oppressively pervasive — TechCrunch published a story called "Why We Desperately Need a New (and Better) Google," which argued that "Google has become a jungle: a tropical paradise for spammers and marketers." In the next year Google made two major changes to the algorithm, which came to be called Panda and Penguin.

The same event reads oppositely from the two ends of the relation. While the public might have experienced each of these updates as a relief, Mapstead and his SEO compatriots saw them as devastating. "They change the rules instantly overnight, and then you're out of business," he said. "Here you're trying to rely on this business model to feed yourself and your family, and they're pulling the rug from underneath you, and you've gotta scramble to pay rent."
