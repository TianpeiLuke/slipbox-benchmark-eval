# Why Dropout Retired The CollegeHumor Name

Sam Reich's case for dropping the CollegeHumor name is that the brand had become more curse than blessing and that the company had been trying to shed it from the start. "We've been wanting to do away with the name CollegeHumor almost since Dropout was first formed," he said. The grounds are in the original naming decision: the service was called "Dropout" — as opposed to "CollegeHumor Plus", or "Pro", or whatever else it could have been — specifically because they were itching to distance themselves from that brand name. The name has been a blessing and a curse for a long time, the blessing being that it is one of the few brands with quote-unquote "staying power" on the internet, giving it recognizability with a few different generations, and the curse being self-explanatory: "We could not be more distant from our frat-y roots at this point." He also noted the video predated the decision — they wanted to make it before they recognised they were going to do the name change, thinking five years would be a good opportunity for a callback to their original announcement video.

Brennan Lee Mulligan's supporting argument is about the audience the message is for. Internally, he says, "we're just 'us'" — a comedy company with enough glibness and irreverence to have a healthy sense of humour about a brand change, which is why the video is tongue-in-cheek while also being a very earnest celebration of the work everyone on the platform has done. But the confusion persists outside the core fan base: people very close to the company feel in on the joke and remember the 2018 launch, "But I guarantee you every 18 months, some video creator will be like [in a salacious YouTube thumbnail voice] 'What happened to CollegeHumor?' and the first 100 comments will be 'They're the most healthy and sustainable they've ever been as a company. What are you talking about?' But that video will still go viral." The warrant follows: there is a huge track of the mainstream for which an internal, well-made, funny statement is worth making — the story has saturated the fan base, but as a joke in the video puts it, your dad will still call them CollegeHumor.
