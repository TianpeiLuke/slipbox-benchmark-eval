# A Framework For Valuing US Pharma Stocks

Analysing pharma stocks in the US rests on three factors, taken in order: drugs about to expire, drugs in the ramping-up stage, and the pipeline. With a sharp fall post genericisation, drugs about to expire will be a major overhang for any company without a portfolio in ramping-up stages, and pipeline products cannot really offset the absence of products in ramp-up, because pipeline assets are given lower probabilities of success; generally Phase-I, Phase-II and Phase-III assets get 10, 20 and 50 per cent probability of success respectively.

The second step is pipeline valuation, and the article works an example. Biogen's and Eisai's Leqembi for Alzheimer's is expected to generate peak sales of $7 billion by 2030; it has announced a list price of $26,500 for an annual course, and the worked example assumes a 50 per cent rebate to insurers on average. Considering the 6 million patients with Alzheimer's disease in the US, assuming 50 per cent are mild to moderate, the applicable group according to the FDA label, and 15 per cent are on treatment by year 7, the drug alone can rake in $12 billion, halved to account for Eisai's share, by 2030 in cumulative sales, for a company whose market cap is $33 billion trading at 14 times FY24 EPS.
