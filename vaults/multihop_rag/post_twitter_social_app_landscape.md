# The Post-Twitter Social App Landscape

So many apps have sprung up over the year since Elon Musk's takeover in an attempt to become "the new Twitter," but no app has emerged as the dominant refuge for disillusioned Twitter users.

The scoreboard as of November 2023: Threads, Instagram's supposed "Twitter killer," got 100 million sign-ups within five days; the decentralized platform Mastodon has 1.8 million monthly active users; and the smaller upstart Bluesky hit 1 million users in September. Though still in beta, Spill's community of 200,000 users may seem small in comparison, but Spill's rise has shown that quality matters just as much as quantity — that building a strong core community is just as valuable as high-speed growth, even if the traditional venture model may not always incentivize thoughtful product development.

Not every entrant survived. Pebble, formerly T2, another app that spun out from laid-off former Twitter employees, recently shut down after a year, having grown to just 20,000 registered users; its CEO Gabor Cselle previously had exits to Google and Twitter but accepted that Pebble simply was not competitive enough to stay afloat. The contrast TechCrunch draws is that while Pebble sought to essentially clone Twitter, Spill's team is building something that looks very little like it.
