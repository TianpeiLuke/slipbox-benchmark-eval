# Craigslist Unbundling As A Model For Social Media

The Verge's model for what could happen to social networks is the "unbundling of Craigslist": a picture created more than a decade ago by venture capitalist Andrew Parker, which compares a few dozen startups with subsections of Craigslist, and which still goes viral on the web every once in a while.

The structure it describes is one incumbent that was all things to all people online and a generation of startups that figured they could do part of the job a lot better. The relation held over a decade of attempts: some of those companies failed — sometimes because of Craigslist — but some became gigantically successful, and the successes worked by beating the incumbent on a specific dimension. Airbnb worked because it was more searchable, reliable, and trustworthy than a random Craigslist listing; StubHub sold you secondhand tickets without the 50 percent chance those tickets were fake; Etsy offered a much more fun shopping and discovery experience than a bunch of text listings and crappy photos. The range over which the model is now being extended: for a decade startups tried to unbundle Craigslist, and now they can do the same for social networks. The image is credited to Andrew Parker.
