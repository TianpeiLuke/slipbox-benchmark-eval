# The Ending Of Packers-Chiefs Week 13 Was Marred By Blown Calls

Sporting News argued that the closing sequence of the Week 13 Packers-Chiefs game was decided in part by officiating errors — "then came the blown calls" — with three separate incidents on Kansas City's final possessions going the wrong way.

The grounds were three specific plays. Jonathan Owens was whistled for an unnecessary roughness that never was on the game's final drive, penalized for crashing into a scrambling Patrick Mahomes on the field of play. Soon after, Marquez Valdes-Scantling was seemingly impeded by Green Bay cornerback Carrington Valentine on a streak play, an unabashed pass interference call in most circumstances; the umpires fell silent. If that weren't enough, Owens then barged into Kelce's back on the final play of the game and Mahomes' Hail Mary tumbled onto the turf, and like the previous play the contact went unrecognized by the zebras.

The warrant joining the grounds to the claim is that the errors cut in both directions but were consequential either way, which is how the live log framed them in real time. At 11:18 p.m. the referees made what Sporting News called a baffling unnecessary roughness call on Jonathan Owens, "gifting the Chiefs an extra 20 yards. That could prove vital." At 11:25 p.m. the outlet described the non-call on Valdes-Scantling as a "proper make-up call by the referees there," noting he was almost certainly impeded illegally while streaking down the field and that it should have been pass interference — it wasn't, though, and now Kansas City could be on the brink of losing.
