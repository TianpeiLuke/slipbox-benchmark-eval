# Sports Betting Odds

## Definition

Sports betting odds are the numbers a sportsbook posts alongside a wager to show the payout a bettor stands to win relative to their stake, and they are set by oddsmakers using computer models and analytics before being released to the public. The same underlying odds concept takes different shapes depending on the bet type — a moneyline price on who wins outright, a point spread price on a handicapped margin, and a totals (over/under) price on the combined score — and can be displayed in American, decimal, or fractional format.

## Context

In the corpus, odds are the common thread across every major bet type: moneylines, point spreads, totals, and prop bets all price outcomes with the same odds conventions, and combined-market previews such as college bowl coverage post moneyline, spread, and total odds side by side for a single game. Odds are not static — sportsbooks move them ("line movement") in response to public betting patterns, injury or roster news, and sharp/professional money, with the goal of balancing action on both sides of a bet so the book keeps its built-in profit regardless of outcome. Because different sportsbooks can carry different odds for the same event, bettors practice "line shopping" — comparing odds and lines across multiple sportsbooks before wagering to find the best price. Odds also extend beyond single games into futures and award markets, such as season-long odds on the NFL MVP race between Brock Purdy and Lamar Jackson.

## Key Characteristics

- **American odds format dominates U.S. listings** — a negative number (e.g., -110) shows the favorite and how much must be wagered to profit $100; a positive number (e.g., +250) shows the underdog and how much a $100 wager profits.
- **Three display formats carry the same price** — American, decimal (multiply the odds by the stake for total payout), and fractional (numerator = profit, denominator = stake) are alternative notations for identical odds.
- **Odds move for identifiable reasons** — sportsbooks shift a line or its odds because of lopsided public betting, new information (injuries, weather), sharp bettor action, or a perceived initial mispricing.
- **Odds differ by bet type but share the sign convention** — moneyline odds price the outright winner, point spread odds price a handicapped margin near even money, and totals odds price the over/under of a combined score; all three commonly display as American odds.
- **Line shopping exploits cross-sportsbook odds variance** — because oddsmakers and sportsbooks don't always move in lockstep, checking odds at multiple books before betting can secure a better price on the same wager.
