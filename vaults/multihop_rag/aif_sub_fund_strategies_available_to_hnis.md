# AIF Sub-Fund Strategies Available To HNIs

Alternative Investment Funds present a family of sub-strategies that are available only to HNIs, each defined by the stage or credit segment it targets. Start-up funds serve angel investors looking to invest in ideas with the potential to grow into full-scale businesses, providing a platform that brings promoters and investors together; these funds handpick start-ups after extensive due diligence and build a portfolio with the potential to multiply capital several times. Mid-stage private equity funds suit investors hesitant to take start-up risk, letting them invest in companies in the middle of their evolution cycle whose business valuations grow exponentially as they mature and enter the late-stage or pre-IPO stage. Late-stage or pre-IPO funds allow investment in mature companies preparing to list, building a portfolio of companies about to go public and planning to exit after their anticipated blockbuster listing, which can magnify returns in a relatively short period; such funds gained immense popularity after the 2020 stock market rally that witnessed the blockbuster listing of some popular B2C IPOs.

The remaining strategies are defined by instrument rather than stage. Hedge funds invest in both publicly traded and unlisted stocks and manage money using long-only and long-short strategies, the latter allowed to use complex derivative techniques to hedge the portfolio; long-short funds try to optimise returns in every market situation and hence tend to rise and fall less than their peers, so investors with stock and mutual fund portfolios can diversify with them to reduce volatility. Performing credit funds lend to operating companies that cannot raise funds from banks and NBFCs due to their size or credit ratings, specialising in credit risk assessment and offering credit at relatively higher rates after thorough due diligence, which produces higher overall returns than AAA/AA-rated bonds and appeals to investors seeking more from their fixed-income portfolio. Infrastructure funds invest in long-term infrastructure assets owned by PSUs or private companies, such as roads, power transmission lines and solar power plants; most such projects provide essential societal services and thus have intrinsic value, and their cashflows come as annuities backed by Central and State governments, which lowers future cash-flow risk significantly, gives the fund a yield characteristic through regular distributions, and can add capital gains when assets are sold to another fund or infrastructure trust at maturity. Venture debt funds serve start-up promoters who want to raise money without selling equity and cannot approach banks and mutual funds, providing credit to start-ups at high rates, with the higher returns and risk management appealing to risk-taking, yield-hungry investors. The list is explicitly not exhaustive: many more options exist, such as special situation funds and real estate yield funds.
