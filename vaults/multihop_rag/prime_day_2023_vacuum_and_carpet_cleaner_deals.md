# Prime Day 2023 Vacuum And Carpet Cleaner Deals

WIRED's cleaning picks include the Dyson V15 Detect, Lupe's cordless vacuum, a budget Dyson, a Dyson pet model worthwhile between $300 and $400, the Shark AI Ultra 2-in-1, the Eufy X9 Pro, a Roborock, a Samsung stick vacuum, the Bissell Little Green machine, an upgrade carpet cleaner, and a portable cordless spot cleaner.

The V15 Detect holds the top spot in WIRED's Best Dyson Vacuums guide: 7 pounds, lightweight, simple to convert into a handheld, and, as a Detect model, fitted with a head projecting a green laser to spot microscopic dust invisible to the naked eye. Lupe's cordless vacuum (8/10, WIRED Recommends) is one of the best tried and cheaper than a Dyson but still very expensive even on sale; its cleaner head has a standard rotating brush and a composite foam roller that creates a strong seal against the ground on the highest setting, and while most cordless vacs are best used between deeper cleanings with a powerful upright, the Lupe might be all you need — unfortunately it sounds terrible. The budget-friendly Dyson converts into a hand vacuum, runs about 40 minutes, and includes attachments for nooks and crannies. The pet-owner Dyson, which WIRED's guide says is worthwhile priced between $300 and $400, is similar to the Animal 3 but adds a self-adjusting cleaner head that raises and lowers the base plate to seal in suction on all floor types, plus a motorized brush for efficiency. The Shark AI Ultra 2-in-1 (8/10, WIRED Recommends) is WIRED's favorite vac-mop combo, vacuuming well and scrubbing floors thoroughly with the included mopping bin without wetting carpets, using laser navigation to map the home and subdivide vacuuming and mopping zones in an easy app — and it has amazing functionality for the price, needing no replacement bin bags; the sale price is specifically for the black and gold colorway, with black and silver also on sale though not as deeply. The Eufy X9 Pro (6/10, WIRED Review) doesn't work well as a stand-alone robot vacuum but is the best mopping vacuum WIRED has tried, with two mops rotating at about 180 revolutions per minute; senior associate reviews editor Adrienne So says it took only 30 minutes to mop her kitchen — including a spilled half-bottle of syrup, without leaving the floor sticky — plus laundry room and bathroom, and the docking station dries the mops. Roborock recently launched an upgrade to WIRED's favorite robot vacuum and current top pick, which after several years is still the one vacuum So hasn't unplugged and consistently calls on after other robot vacuums have failed. The Samsung stick vacuum is the version that does not purport to use AI, and the reviewer noted (7/10, WIRED Review) that you probably don't really need machine learning in a vacuum; it's a great vacuum if your midcentury modern home needs one to fit the decor. The little green machine matches the lowest price typically seen and made both WIRED's Best Carpet Cleaners and Best Viral TikTok Gadgets lists, being great for furniture, stairs, and vehicles. The smart carpet cleaner has a clever design, is fairly easy to maneuver, sucks grime out of carpet, has a drying mode, and comes with upholstery attachments; as the upgrade pick in the Best Carpet Cleaners guide its relatively high price is WIRED's main criticism, and it has never been seen lower. The portable, cordless Bissell spot cleaner spares you lugging a heavy machine to a spill or pet accident and is mercifully easy to take apart and clean afterward, appearing in the same guide as WIRED's favorite spot cleaner.
