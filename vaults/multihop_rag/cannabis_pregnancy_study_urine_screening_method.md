# The Cannabis Pregnancy Study's Urine Screening Method

The researchers screened frozen urine samples that had been collected from 9,200 pregnant people at eight U.S. medical centers, then reviewed the participants' medical records for pregnancy outcomes.

The participants were originally recruited between 2010 and 2013 for a long-term study on pregnancy outcomes, and their samples have been stored since then — a precondition for the analysis. Samples were taken during three time windows that fell respectively within the first trimester, the early second trimester, and the late second trimester into the third, and for the new research each sample was screened for a specific molecule that appears in urine when the body breaks down tetrahydrocannabinol (THC), the psychoactive compound in cannabis. On the outcomes side, the researchers took note of preterm births, stillbirths and instances when a newborn was born small for their gestational age — "small" being defined as below the fifth percentile for their age and sex — and also noted when the mother experienced pregnancy-related high-blood-pressure disorders such as gestational hypertension or preeclampsia.
