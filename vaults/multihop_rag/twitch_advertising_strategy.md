# Twitch Advertising Strategy

Twitch's advertising model rests on paying streamers enough to opt in, then reshaping when ads run so they cost the stream less audience. Its monetization executive told TechCrunch on 2023-10-26 that the two moves were coupled from the start: "one of the things we knew with ads, if we're going to get streamers to buy into ads as a good way to monetize, is that we had to increase the rev share, which we did, and launch the ad incentive program." That rev-share change was itself part of a decision to prioritize ad revenue, and creators and viewers have said that integrating ads in their streams is very frustrating.

The revenue relation Twitch teaches streamers is a function of consistency rather than volume in any one stream: "We're helping streamers understand that with ad running, you have to think about it consistently over time in order to earn predictable revenue. It's a very reliable source of income for many streamers."

The scheduling layer addresses the specific failure mode of live content. Twitch announced in its keynote two new features to allow moderators to know when an ad break is coming and adjust the ad break around the content, "because the problem in livestreaming, as you're aware, is that people don't want to miss out." The stated direction is improving the ad experience by allowing the ad breaks to be synchronized with the content, and enrolling moderators and eventually the community in adjusting those ad breaks.

The model is scoped to livestreaming and does not transfer from static-content platforms: tactics for monetization that work for Instagram or TikTok just don't really work for Twitch or any livestreaming platform. Alongside ads sits a direct-support layer Twitch claims to lead: "One thing we've done since the beginning, where we are a leader, is getting people to take their wallets out and support creators through subscriptions, gifting, cheering. I think that sets us as a unique community-based platform where viewers are directly supporting creators in that way."
