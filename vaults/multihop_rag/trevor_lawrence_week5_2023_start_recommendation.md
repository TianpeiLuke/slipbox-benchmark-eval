# Trevor Lawrence As A Dicey Week 5 2023 Start At Buffalo

CBS Sports listed Trevor Lawrence under "Start Him" for Week 5 of the 2023 season against Buffalo while conceding the call was dicey: the grounds are that Lawrence has more upside than all but about 10 quarterbacks that week, and the warrant is that Buffalo's pass rush may be diminished enough to let that upside surface, even though Buffalo is savvy at defending the pass.

The supporting grounds are specific. Buffalo lost top cornerback Tre'Davious White to a torn Achilles the previous week, meaning some combination of Christian Benford, Dane Jackson and Kaiir Elam — who had been benched for the first four games — would work at outside cornerback with Taron Johnson in the slot; the column says it cannot assume the Bills will fall off just because White won't play. The Bills pass rush is among the fiercest in football with a top-10 pressure rate of 38.2% while blitzing little (a 15.3% blitz rate, second-lowest in football), but pass-rush terror Greg Rousseau won't play and Von Miller, if he plays, would be sub-100 percent, lowering Buffalo's dominance in that area. The listed weekly breakdown has Lawrence at Buffalo with the Bills favoured by 5.5, an over/under of 48.5, an opponent ranked 1st versus quarterbacks, 15.7 projected points and a 12th quarterback ranking, on year-to-date totals of 943 passing yards, 101 rushing yards, four touchdowns, two interceptions and 16.4 fantasy points per game.

The case against Lawrence is carried in his own splits. On third downs he has completed just 50% of his throws — only Geno Smith is worse among qualifying quarterbacks — averaging 4.4 yards per attempt with a 6.9-yard average depth of target, both bottom-six in the league, with only one dropped pass on third down; the column calls this a drain on his fantasy production. Under pressure he is neither well protected nor thriving, with a 45.7% completion rate, 6.7 yards per attempt and a 14.3% off-target rate, and worst of all an EPA per dropback of minus-0.72, better only than Jimmy Garoppolo, Deshaun Watson and Ryan Tannehill, meaning he hurts his team more than he helps when pressured; he is also among the least-blitzed quarterbacks on third downs. In the previous week Lawrence didn't have to do much as the Jaguars rolled past the Falcons with his top receiver blanketed by a heavy dose of man coverage and a favourable game script — his off-schedule touchdown throw to Ridley was awesome, but he set season lows in pass attempts (30) and in throws of 10-plus air yards (a 20% rate), and Week 4 was the first time all year a defense chose to play significant man-coverage snaps against Jacksonville. The verdict is bounded: Lawrence is a decent starter, but the author would rather roll with Anthony Richardson, Jordan Love and Justin Fields.
