# The Israeli Case For Continuing The Gaza War

Eylon Levy argues that Israel must end the war by destroying the capabilities of Hamas, and that the legitimacy for doing so comes from 7 October. He rejects the premise that everyone in the world hates Israel or that all are antisemites: Israel enjoys a great deal of support in the world, and people understand that what happened on 7 October crossed a line with atrocities that cannot be repeated and for which there can be no excuse, which is what gives Israel legitimacy in this war against Hamas and the understanding that it is not possible not to respond.

The difficulty he identifies is one of images and of unshared context. Israel faces difficult images coming out of Gaza; Israeli TV does not show what viewers abroad see of the destruction resulting from the military campaign to destroy terrorist infrastructure built beneath houses, hospitals, mosques and schools, so the world sees these pictures and wants to understand why Israel is doing this to civilians in Gaza, asking for how long and what the limit is. Israeli spokespeople therefore have to explain things that sound self-evident to Israelis but are incomprehensible to those who do not live in that reality: that this is a war imposed by Hamas through the most terrible massacre since the Holocaust, and a war Israel must win because Hamas has repeated that it wants to carry out more October 7s until it murders every man, woman and child in the country. Levy says the explanations do produce greater acceptance, with an understanding that the war must end by destroying Hamas' capabilities or Israel will be in the same situation in another six months. He makes this case particularly to the Irish, who have long pressed for a ceasefire: if Israel had taken their advice and stopped firing then little Emily Hand would still be in captivity, and if it agrees to a ceasefire now Hamas will commit more atrocities because it will feel empowered and come out of the war feeling the world has its back. On the dead in Gaza, he says everyone killed there over the last month would be alive today if Hamas had not started the atrocities on 7 October and then decided to fight in populated areas while preventing people from leaving, despite Israel's efforts over more than a month to evacuate civilians from the battle zones.
