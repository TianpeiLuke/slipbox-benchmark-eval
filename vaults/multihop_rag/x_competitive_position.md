# X's Competitive Position In October 2023

X was facing more competition than ever before at the time it introduced its bot-countering fee, and its own numbers were going the other way: a report published that week by market intelligence firm Similarweb found that X's web traffic and monthly active users on mobile had declined post-acquisition. TechCrunch reported this on 18 October 2023, noting that as Musk's chaotic product and policy changes took root, some X users abandoned the platform for rivals or decreased their posting.

The competitive field had widened on two fronts. Twitter-like startups and tech companies gave users far more options for microblogging and public discourse — Spill, Bluesky, Pebble, Countersocial, Spoutible, Hive, and the open source platform Mastodon — and tech giants had entered too, with Meta's new app Threads. On pricing, X was close to alone: few of the other social apps currently charge a fee, unless it's for premium features, as some third-party Mastodon apps do.

One scope condition limits how far the fee comparison can be pushed. X's charge was at that point only an experiment, and TechCrunch noted it was not clear the $1 fee would be a permanent requirement when the new subscription policy rolled out more broadly to other markets.
