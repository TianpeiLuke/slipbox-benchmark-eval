# Box Jellyfish Associative Learning Finding

Despite lacking a central brain, Caribbean box jellyfish learned to spot and avoid obstacles in a new study — the first evidence that jellyfish can do associative learning, the ability to make mental connections between events, such as seeing something and running into it, and then change one's behaviour accordingly.

For these animals, as the article puts it, learning is literally a no-brainer. Jan Bielecki, who studies animal nervous systems at Kiel University in Germany, and his colleagues shared the findings on September 22 in Current Biology.
