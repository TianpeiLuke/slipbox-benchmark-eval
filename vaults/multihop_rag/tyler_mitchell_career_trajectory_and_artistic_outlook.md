# Tyler Mitchell On His Career Trajectory And Artistic Outlook

Interviewed by The Age ahead of the NGV Triennial and published on 27 November 2023, Mitchell says early success does not unsettle him — "The idea of the road ahead only excites me" — and that he deliberately allows himself space for quiet periods and for work that does not come off.

He traces his choice of medium to impatience: "I was impatient when I got started and just wanted to make visual things now, and I didn't want to wait for permission, or didn't want to wait on equipment, or didn't want to lug large amounts of equipment up flights of stairs. I just wanted to make something that I felt compelled by, immediately." His career has only blossomed in the years since the trip that started him in photography, and where some artists might feel trepidation at so much success so early, he is clear-eyed about the rhythm of a long career: there will be "very quiet, sleepy periods, and frenetic periods, or hyperactive periods", and it is "always refreshing as a young person to see how the career of a much older, more senior artist has really gone" — senior artists we know and love today "had decades where they were out of style, out of fashion, people thought their work was done, their best work was behind them, and yet, often had resurgences".

Crafting a career, he says, means "allowing space for bad and good, allowing space for artists to make and work through complicated and sometimes not-so-favourable ideas. And I try to give myself that same space. It's only about giving yourself grace and space and thinking one day at a time." Asked about a dream project or collaborator, he says there are too many; with one monograph behind him — 2020's I Can Make You Feel Good — he is hungry to make more.
