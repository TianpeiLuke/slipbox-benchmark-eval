# Longfellow's "I Heard the Bells", 1863

Henry Wadsworth Longfellow, one of America's greatest poets, wrote his famous poem "I Heard the Bells on Christmas Day" on Christmas Day 1863 — exactly 160 years before this December 2023 account — at a moment when the Civil War was a ravaging storm, ripping a gashing hole in the fabric of the Republic, and when he was also suffering personal tragedies.

Fanny, his wife of 18 years and the mother of his six children, had died in a fiery accident in their home after her dress caught fire from a lit candle. Longfellow's son Charlie was critically injured fighting for the Union army near Washington, D.C. So on that day, the sound of the church bells heralding Christmas seemed to mock the very thought of "peace on Earth."

Yet through his personal grief and the torment of a war that would claim nearly a million lives, Longfellow penned the poem that has become a Christmas carol for the ages. The story of the poem is now the subject of a feature-length film, "I Heard the Bells," available on demand through most cable and streaming platforms.
