# Alameda's Special Privileges Written Into FTX's Code

Gary Wang told the court that Alameda received advantages through code baked into FTX's software granting it special access to the exchange — "We gave special privileges on FTX that gave unlimited withdrawals on the platform to Alameda" — privileges that ultimately resulted in Alameda owing FTX $8 billion worth of customer deposits.

The structure had several linked parts. Alameda was allowed to withdraw and transfer funds and had a $65 billion line of credit. "When customers deposited USD, it went to Alameda," Wang said. "It existed in the computer code. Alameda could have negative balances and unlimited withdrawals." That "bug" in the code was written by Nishad Singh, FTX's director of engineering, and reviewed by Wang, with Bankman-Fried calling the shots. Wang also told the court about a $1 million personal loan he received and a $200 million to $300 million loan in his name from Alameda that was never deposited into his account but was used to make investments into other companies on behalf of FTX — all done by Bankman-Fried, he testified. The escalation was visible over time: in early 2020 Wang discovered for the first time that Alameda's negative balance exceeded FTX's revenue, an indication that Alameda was taking customer funds, and he brought this to Bankman-Fried's attention several times; in late 2021 he discovered Alameda had withdrawn $3 billion from its $65 billion line of credit. The ownership and authority around it: Wang owned 10% of Alameda while his boss owned the other 90%, his compensation was a base salary of $200,000 per year plus stock, and he owned roughly 17% of FTX — and even though they were co-founders, "ultimately it was Sam's decision to make" when there were disagreements. Born in China, Wang moved to the U.S. at age 7 and grew up in Minnesota before going to MIT to study math and computer science, worked at Google after college, and first met Bankman-Fried during high school at a summer camp.
