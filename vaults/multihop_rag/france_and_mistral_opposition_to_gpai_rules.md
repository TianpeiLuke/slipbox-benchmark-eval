# France And Mistral's Opposition To The GPAI Rules

Opposition to the inclusion in the AI package of tiered rules for general purpose AIs was led in the weeks before the deal by France — and by the French AI startup Mistral, which had been lobbying for a total carve out from obligations for foundational models and GPAIs.

That attack failed on its own terms: the deal agreed by the Spanish presidency does contain some obligations for GPAIs and foundation models, so it is not the total carve out Mistral and its lobbyists had been pushing for. Responding to news of the political deal, the office of France's digital minister put out a statement attributed to Jean-Noël Barrot which said, translated from French using AI: "We will be carefully analyzing the compromise reached today, and in the coming weeks we will ensure that the text preserves Europe's ability to develop its own artificial intelligence technologies, and safeguards its strategic autonomy."

How much of a carve out Mistral's business might enjoy under the deal remained unclear. Asked about this during the press conference, Carme Artigas suggested the French AI startup would — once commercialized — be likely to fit in the "low tier" for GPAIs, meaning only limited transparency obligations, since she said it does not hit the high capacity compute threshold triggering the systemic risk obligations: it is using what is thought to be 10^23 of compute, not 10^25. And because Mistral was still in an R&D and pre-training phase for its models, she said it would be excluded from even the low tier compliance requirements.
