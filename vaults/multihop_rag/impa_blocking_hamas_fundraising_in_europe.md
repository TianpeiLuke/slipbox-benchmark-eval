# IMPA's Blocking Of A Hamas Fundraising Pipeline In Europe

Two weeks into the war, the Israel Money Laundering and Terror Financing Prohibition Authority received secret intelligence from two European countries warning that "A large, well-known organization is raising funds for Hamas through posts on social networks, and fintech company platforms outside Israel, under the guise of donations to Gaza residents" — and in a swift joint international action, at IMPA's request, one of the European countries blocked the organization's money pipeline by immediately freezing its financial activities.

The intelligence included the name of the organization, its fundraising methods, and the identity of the entities behind it, and the concern was that within days the millions of dollars raised would go directly to the military arm of Hamas. "We are talking about a matter of minutes here. From the moment the authorities in Europe received the information, and we requested the money be blocked, with no court orders or delay, they blocked the transfer of millions of dollars to one of the most active organizations, and from it to Hamas. This is a real-time deterrence," IMPA director general Adv. Ilit Ostrovitch-Levi said. "And then there was a domino effect. Following this blocking, more countries began to look around, and blocked funds intended for terror. All this information, which is now shared at an international level, leads to the closing of more and more Hamas funding pipelines, in real time. With worldwide cooperation, Israel's security and enforcement authorities are thwarting the transfer of millions of dollars earmarked for terrorism, every day."
