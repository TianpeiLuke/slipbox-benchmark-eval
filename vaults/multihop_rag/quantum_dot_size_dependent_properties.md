# Quantum Dot Size-Dependent Properties

"Quantum dots are a new class of materials, different from molecules," said Heiner Linke, a member of the Nobel committee, at the award announcement: each dot is a few billionths of a meter across, and at that tiny scale its qualities are governed by the weird laws of quantum mechanics, which rule that changing a dot's size alters its properties.

The properties that change with size include how the dots interact with light and electricity, their magnetic properties, and the temperature at which they melt. One useful quality that changes with size is colour. Usually, "if you want to make different colors with molecules, you would choose a new molecule," Linke said — that is, a new set of atoms arranged in a different structure — but changing a quantum dot's size can change its colour without changing its molecules. Quantum dots give off fluorescent light of different colours when bathed in laser light: smaller dots give off bluer light and bigger dots give off redder light. Material matters too, since dots of the same size made from different materials may give off slightly different colours; quantum dots are usually made from semiconductors such as graphene, selenite or metal sulfides. By adjusting the sizes and materials of quantum dots, chemists can alter their properties for a huge range of uses.
