# Climate Grief Conversation at the Homestead

On a steamy August morning, Kalmus huddled around a faded picnic table at the centre of the Possibility Alliance homestead in Belfast, Maine, sipping coffee with a rare breed — people as alarmed by climate change as he is — and his grief began to flow as he shared findings from unpublished research projecting heat in moderate emissions scenarios through the year 2300.

Ethan Hughes, the charismatic, folksy co-founder of the Possibility Alliance, started the day with a poem about a deer in the forest and then began to outline daily chores — chopping wood, building a new deck, caring for goats — before talk turned to climate change. "Even in the coolest time of day, people will be dying if they don't have air conditioning," Kalmus told the group. "Everything is already spinning out of control," said Sarah Wilcox-Hughes, dismayed. Kalmus opened up to the table: he had been procrastinating for months on finishing the research and was running into his "psychological limitations," feeling he was letting the planet down. "Anyone want to rub Peter's shoulders as he writes?" Ethan said. Later Kalmus relayed a recent, gloomy conversation with a fellow scientist; crickets trilled, roosters crowed, and a tear dripped down Ethan's cheek. "Let's dedicate our work today to the Amazon," Ethan said, outlining for the group how the planned work would sequester carbon and show people they could live without fossil fuels and take meaningful action against climate change, even as world governments failed them.
