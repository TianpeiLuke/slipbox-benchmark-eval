# Startups' Over-Reliance On OpenAI's Proprietary Models

The claim, from Luis Ceze, University of Washington computer science professor and OctoML CEO, is that "part of the frenzy around the future of OpenAI is due to too many startups over-relying on their proprietary models." The observed grounds: up until the week of the crisis OpenAI was still the AI darling everyone wanted to dance with, countless startups and scale-ups had built businesses atop OpenAI's proprietary GPT-X large language models (LLMs), and over the weekend hundreds of OpenAI customers reportedly started contacting OpenAI's rivals — which include Anthropic, Google and Cohere — concerned that their own businesses might be impacted if OpenAI were to disintegrate overnight. The panic has been palpable.

The warrant is drawn by analogy to an earlier concentration episode: there are precedents from elsewhere in the technology sphere, perhaps most notably the cloud computing industry, which became renowned for the way it locked companies in to centralized, vortex-like silos. As Ceze put it: "It's dangerous to put all your chips in one basket — we saw that in the early cloud days which led to companies shifting to multi-cloud and hybrid environments."

The prescription follows from the analogy: "Open source today offers a wide variety of models for companies to essentially diversify," Ceze added. "By doing so, these startups can quickly pivot and minimize risk. There is also a major upside — many of these models already outperform the likes of OpenAI's in terms [of] price-performance and speed."
