# ChatGPT's Durability And Regulatory Outlook

Engadget argues that ChatGPT is unlikely to fade as a fad, and that regulation, not public fickleness, is the main variable in its future. At the start of the year, ChatGPT was regularly derided as a fad, a gimmick, some shiny bauble that would quickly be cast aside by a fickle public like so many NFTs; those predictions could still prove true, but as 2023 has ground on and the breadth of ChatGPT's adoption has continued, the chances of those dim predictions coming to pass feel increasingly remote.

The grounds are financial commitment and competitive fear: there is simply too much money wrapped up in ensuring its continued development, from the revenue streams of companies promoting the technology to the investments of firms incorporating it into their products and services, and there is also a fear of missing out among companies — S&P Global argues — that they might adopt too late what turns out to be a foundationally transformative technology, which is helping drive ChatGPT's rapid uptake. The scope condition is stated directly: the calendar resetting for the new year shouldn't do much to change ChatGPT's upward trajectory, but looming regulatory oversight might, since President Biden has made the responsible development of AI a focus of his administration, with both houses of Congress beginning to draft legislation as well, and the form and scope of those resulting rules could have a significant impact on what ChatGPT looks like this time next year.
