# Owner And Veterinarian Actions Recommended By The AKC

If a dog has a cough that won't quit and other respiratory symptoms, it is recommended that the owner contact a veterinarian right away. Beyond that trigger, the American Kennel Club's chief veterinarian offered advice for both dog owners and their vets — Dr. Jerry Klein, a practicing vet for 35 years, who was emergency department head emeritus at MedVet Chicago and currently works at the McKillip Animal Hospital in Chicago.

The steps run in this order. First, owners should make sure their pets are fully vaccinated and boosted against the known canine diseases, Klein said. Second, when people bring in sick dogs, Klein recommends that veterinarians suggest a full respiratory panel — though he acknowledges that may be an expense some owners can't afford. Third, if a dog's veterinarian doesn't suggest the panel, then owners should request it; that will help determine if the germ is one that vets already know about or is something new. Fourth, people can call their local emergency vets to find out if there has been an increase in respiratory infections coming in for treatment. "That would be a way to find out whether there's been an uptick in your area," Klein said.

The final step is conditional on what that call reveals: "If there is, then they should try to protect against exposure by avoiding grooming establishments and daycare." Avoidance is therefore keyed to a local uptick, not recommended unconditionally. Klein also recommends that groomers and daycare facilities not allow dogs to share food and water dishes — an instruction directed at the facilities themselves rather than at owners. The cost caveat on the respiratory panel is the one acknowledged limit on the procedure: Klein grants that the expense may put it out of reach for some owners.
