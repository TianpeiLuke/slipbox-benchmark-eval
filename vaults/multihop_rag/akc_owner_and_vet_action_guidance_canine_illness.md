# AKC Owner and Vet Action Guidance for Canine Illness

The American Kennel Club's chief veterinarian offered advice for both dog owners and their vets, and the triggering precondition is a specific one: if a dog has a cough that won't quit and other respiratory symptoms, it's recommended that the owner contact a veterinarian right away. The advice comes from Dr. Jerry Klein, a practicing vet for 35 years who was emergency department head emeritus at MedVet Chicago and currently works at the McKillip Animal Hospital in Chicago.

First, owners should make sure their pets are fully vaccinated and boosted against the known canine diseases.

Second, at the visit, Klein recommends that veterinarians suggest a full respiratory panel when people bring in sick dogs. He acknowledges that this may be an expense some owners can't afford, which is the stated limit on the step rather than a reason to skip it silently.

Third, if a dog's veterinarian doesn't suggest the panel, then owners should request it; that will help determine if the germ is one that vets already know about or is something new.

Fourth, people can call their local emergency vets to find out if there has been an increase in respiratory infections coming in for treatment. "That would be a way to find out whether there's been an uptick in your area," Klein said. The next step is conditional on the answer: "If there is, then they should try to protect against exposure by avoiding grooming establishments and daycare."

Finally, a step directed at facilities rather than owners: Klein also recommends that groomers and daycare facilities not allow dogs to share food and water dishes.
