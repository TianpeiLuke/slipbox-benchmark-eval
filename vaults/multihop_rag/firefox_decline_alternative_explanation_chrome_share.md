# Firefox's Decline: The Chrome Share Alternative Explanation

TechCrunch attacks the causal premise in Mozilla's Yahoo story — the inference that Firefox lost users *because* Yahoo was the default. As it puts it, that is not the full story.

Two grounds are offered. First, Yahoo was already well behind Google in terms of search technology at that point, so the switch conflated the default change with a pre-existing quality gap. Second, and more damaging to the inference, Firefox itself was very well behind Chrome: Chrome ended 2014 with nearly 50% market share and ended 2017 — when Google got prime default search position on Firefox — at nearly 65% of all web browser usage on desktop, with mobile even more skewed to Chrome. In other words, the number of Firefox users might have been declining for other reasons.

The counter also flags the interest at stake: pushing the blame on Yahoo certainly works in Google's favor here, since the story is being told in Google's defense.
