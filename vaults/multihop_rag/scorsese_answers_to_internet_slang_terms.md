# Scorsese's Answers To The Internet Slang Terms

Martin Scorsese got most of his daughter's slang terms right. The first was "tea," with the example "I'm going to spill the tea"; the Killers of the Flower Moon director answered "That means you're gonna tell all you know," and a green check mark emoji appeared over their heads as Francesca's face lit up in shock. For "ick" — "He clapped when the plane landed and that gave me the ick" — he answered correctly, "You were thoroughly repulsed by it," making his daughter burst out laughing. On "sneaky link," a term for discreetly meeting a romantic interest, he offered: "These are our personal peccadillos that you may have. Things that you don't want other people to know about, or just people close to you or something like that." When Francesca explained it was similar to a "booty call," the Goodfellas director's eyes lit up: "Oh, really? We never use that, we never saw specific people in my day," before curtly asking, in his signature Italian-American accent, "What else you got?"

For "hits different," illustrated with "Watching a movie in 70 mm film hits different," he said: "It's an easy one. You perceive it in a totally different way. It's another perspective of the image, so to speak, and the effect the film has on the audience" — with Francesca correcting him toward "like, it's much better. Hits different, like: 'Oh, this is amazing.'" He answered "slept on" incorrectly, though not unreasonably: "I was thinking about some stuff so I slept on it." For "ate," with the example "Lily Gladstone ate in Killers of the Flower Moon," he first took it literally — "Consumed the screen" — before landing on "Ruled." He answered "no cap" instantly and correctly as "no lying," then asked, in classic dad fashion: "How many more of these?" "Ship" defeated him: the Taxi Driver director replied "A ship is a boat," and even given the example "I ship you and mom" still asked, "Take us around somewhere?" For "simp" he invented his own definition — "Sympathise, a simpy person. A person who's whining all the time, they're simps" — adding, "Simp used to mean a wimpy person, a person who's always complaining and stuff like that. I do that a lot, complain a lot." He immediately understood "throw shade" from the example "That critic threw so much shade," and was quick on "slaps" — "This dessert slaps" — answering, "Obviously, it's good." The final term was "slay," given as "This video slays. We slayed in this video," to which the Gangs of New York director replied: "That means it's really good?"
