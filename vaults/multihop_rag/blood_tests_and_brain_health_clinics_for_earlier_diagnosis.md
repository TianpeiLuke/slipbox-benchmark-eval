# Blood Tests and Brain Health Clinics for Earlier Diagnosis

Doctors argue that diagnosis must move much earlier in the disease course — through blood tests that could pinpoint the disease quickly and effectively, and through new brain health clinics — because, as Cath Mummery puts it, "By the time you have dementia, you will have had Alzheimer's disease developing in your brain for at least 20 years."

Blood tests are not yet available: "These are under research, but we are several years away from having them in widespread use," said David Thomas. "Meanwhile, we have got to get the NHS to a state where it is much more focused and better organised in getting better and earlier dementia diagnoses. That will be crucial in our battle against the condition." Mummery agreed, continuing: "Dementia is the latest stage; we need to pick up signals much earlier. So we need to be thinking about how to diagnose people at the very earliest stages of disease, when they may only have very, very subtle symptoms. Our current service isn't good for doing that, and we need to develop brain health clinics where we can pinpoint the disease much earlier, then help to build resilience against dementia in a patient when it is only in a very early stage."
