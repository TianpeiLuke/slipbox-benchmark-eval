# Missy Ward, Daron Babin And Darren Blatt: The Early Operators

Three veterans at the alligator party illustrate how the first generation of SEO made its money. Missy Ward, in an orange bandage dress so tight she said she could not take full steps — ordered on Amazon and untried until the day of the party — started doing SEO in 1998, when it was "five girls and all dudes," and eventually sold her company for $40 million. She was patient about explaining the history of SEO and suggesting other people to talk to, and pointed the writer toward a man in a sky blue polo with a long-running SEO podcast.

That was Daron Babin, whose SEO career got going in 1994, before Google even existed. "The air of manipulation was insane," Babin said. "We had this weird community of geeks and nerds, and we all talked to each other about how we were beating the algorithms up. People were trying to outrank other people just for bragging rights."

The party's host, Darren Blatt, has been friends with Babin for decades — since the era when Darren "D-Money" Blatt would throw rap star-studded internet marketing shindigs during the Adult Video News Awards in Vegas, back when sex sites were among the most advanced in technology, and Babin was using SEO to promote offshore casinos and Viagra ("We were outranking Pfizer!"). Together, Darren and Daron managed to milk all three of the early online cash cows: porn, pills, and gambling.

The consulting turn came as the internet became more regulated and mainstream around the turn of the century, when Darren noticed Daron's SEO skills were increasingly in demand. "I told him that he was missing the boat, that he needed to be a consultant and charge a few grand," Darren said. Daron took the advice, asking for $2,000 a day, and watched his career explode — "I would wake up in a city and not know what time zone I was in." To slow the pace he raised his rate to $5,000 a day, but "it seemed the more I raised my rates, the more gigs I was getting." Nowadays he mostly invests in cannabis and psychedelics, telling the writer SEO just got to be too complicated for not enough money; Ward said the same, that she had stopped focusing on SEO years ago.
