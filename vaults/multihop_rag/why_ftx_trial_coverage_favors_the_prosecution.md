# Why FTX Trial Coverage Favors The Prosecution

Asked by some people why the reporting coming out of the FTX trial seemed so skewed toward the prosecution, The Verge's reporter answered that the prosecution had put together a strong, comprehensive indictment of Bankman-Fried's behavior at FTX and Alameda, while the defense had so far managed to do almost nothing in response. She said she did not know whether Cohen and Everdell had bad facts, a bad client, or were simply untalented themselves — or some combination of all three — but that she had not yet seen any good reason to doubt the very convincing story she was hearing from the prosecution.

The supporting ground is the state of the cross-examinations. Throughout the entire prosecution's case, the defense struggled to shake the credibility of witnesses such as Yedidia, Wang, and Ellison; the hits the defense made on Singh did not make her doubt the overall truth of his story — they just made her think Singh was kind of self-serving and trying to secure himself a lenient sentence. She also read Bankman-Fried's willingness to go to trial as a sign of his own belief: he must still think he can get away with it all, or he would not be dragging his closest companions' names through the mud in a jury trial.
