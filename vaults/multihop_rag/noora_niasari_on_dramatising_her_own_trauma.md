# Noora Niasari On Dramatising Her Own Trauma

Noora Niasari describes working on Shayda as "long-term exposure therapy", and says even doing interviews to promote the movie is difficult — "I have to sit with it and process it" — The Guardian reported on 1 October 2023.

The film is described as tender and revealing, balancing Shayda's discovery of inner strength with the sacrifices she makes for her daughter as she tries to create a new family for her; it is understated, relatable and drawn from such personal memories that the director finds it hard to discuss.

What has changed, she says, is that the material is no longer only hers: "But the thing is, now that it's a film, it has a really different energy in the world. People bring their own experiences to it, it's a very universal experience. We've screened it in Europe, North America and Australia and there is a real sense that it connects beyond my mother and I, beyond our experience. It's not about us any more. That feels liberating and cathartic." After screenings, adult men have approached her with their own tales of growing up in shelters, and other audiences have said they left with a deeper understanding of different Iranian women's experiences.
