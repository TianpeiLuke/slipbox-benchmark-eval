# Insomnia: Definition And Prevalence

Insomnia is a sleep disorder characterised either by difficulty falling asleep or by difficulty staying asleep, to such an extent that it disturbs the sufferer's ability to function during the day — the daytime impairment being the criterion that separates it from ordinary poor sleep. Globally, an estimated 10 per cent of adults meet the criteria for insomnia, and millions struggle with it in the UK alone.

The risk is unevenly distributed by sex: studies have found women are 40 per cent more likely to be affected than men. Sleep therapist Dr Lindsay Browning locates the onset of that gap after puberty: "This sex difference in insomnia emerges after puberty, suggesting that hormonal differences such as menstruation, pregnancy and menopause may be the cause of this increased risk in women." Symptoms are also persistent — a longitudinal study conducted in the UK found that 69 per cent of respondents affected by the disorder at the start of the study still had insomnia 12 months later.
