# Risks And Limitations Of Clinical AI

Against the case for AI's clinical benefits, Patel acknowledged that AI in health care also presents challenges and limitations -- evaluation burden, inherited bias, automation bias and data privacy -- each of which qualifies the premise that a capable model can simply be put to work.

Because AI is a branch of science, it requires rigorous evaluation before it can be applied by doctors, he said: "This ensures that we're not just implementing technology for the sake of innovation, but are truly enhancing patient care in a tangible, evidence-based manner."

AI models also run the risk of bias, he warned. "AI models are, by nature, a reflection of the data they're trained on," he said. "If this data contains biases -- whether racial, gender-based or from other sources -- the models may perpetuate these biases."

There is the additional risk that humans will become overly reliant on AI, leading to a phenomenon called "automation bias." "Essentially, this means giving undue weight to AI-generated results without adequate human scrutiny," Patel said. "In health care, understanding the 'why' behind a diagnosis or recommendation is as crucial as the result," he said. "As providers, we must always contextualize AI outputs within the broader patient picture, leveraging our clinical judgment and experience."

It is important to protect data privacy and security as well, Patel noted, due to the large amounts of information that must be fed to AI models.
