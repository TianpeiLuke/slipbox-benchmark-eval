# Israeli Founders And Investors On Operating Through The War

Israeli startup founders and their investors told TechCrunch in October 2023 that, alongside the war effort, keeping the businesses running mattered, and they expected the immediate commercial damage to fall on product development rather than sales. For most Israeli startups the customer base is global and for many the go-to-market teams are in the U.S. and hence not directly affected by the war, so the current situation may slow down product development but won't directly affect sales in the short run.

Lazer Cohen, whose company Concrete Media works with many startups, said people are contributing however they can while the businesses also need to keep running: "Looking at our clients and my agency, 10-25% of Israeli team members have been called up to reserve duty." Others on the team were helping by donating blood, hosting survivors of the attacks, packing supplies for soldiers, or covering for a team member who was called up. Cohen framed continued operation as an obligation: "As we come out of the initial shock and horror of the attack, many tech workers and executives see it as their duty to keep the tech industry up and running. Technology is Israel's biggest export and the main engine of our economy and with a long and difficult war ahead, it's crucial that we keep working and innovating."

Customers had been understanding for the most part, but companies were working to continue operating under difficult conditions. Sharon Seeman, a partner at YL Ventures, stressed that companies maintain communication with customers: "Ensuring the continuity of business operations is vital for Israeli startups in maintaining their customer relationships."

The trickier question was deferred rather than answered. Startups eventually come around to the inevitable cadence of their existence — raising money to scale their operations, then doubling down and scaling those operations — and the article left open how that would play out with would-be startup investors and the LPs of those investors should the region and country continue to be at war.

Investors quoted took the long view. OurCrowd founder and CEO Jon Medved believes the ecosystem will remain resilient: "There is no doubt that in the long run — a year or more — Israel's tech ecosystem will emerge from this war, stronger, bigger, with more investment and returns for investors," pointing to proven resilience in past conflicts with Hamas and to Israel's focus on deep tech, semiconductors, AI, mobility, fintech, cybersecurity, quantum, agtech, the cloud, devops and clean energy. Oren Yunger, managing partner at GGV, said his firm will do what it takes to make sure the Israeli startups in its portfolio don't go bankrupt because of the war: "I can say with absolute confidence — even if we don't know how long it's going to take and who's going to be affected — I am 100% sure that none of our companies will go bust as a result from this moment in time."
