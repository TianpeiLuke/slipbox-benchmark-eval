# Allison Miller's Case For Getting An Annual Flu Vaccine

Allison Miller argues that everyone should get the flu vaccine every year, because even though it does not guarantee you will avoid the flu, it shifts where you land on a continuum of severity -- and the worst end of that continuum, as she experienced, is catastrophic.

Her grounds are her own omission. Miller did not get the flu vaccine the year she became critically ill. "I had missed the memo that flu vaccines were for everyone, and being 33 at the time and otherwise healthy, it didn't even register as something I should consider doing," she said. The experience has given her a "renewed sense of how quickly things could change" and has taught her to be as proactive as possible about her health to prevent a terrible outcome, and she is now looking to spread awareness of the importance of getting the flu vaccine.

The warrant joining her experience to the recommendation is a proportionality argument about cheap prevention against severe downside: "If there's anything you can do proactively to minimize the risk of something that severe happening to you, you absolutely should do it, I think." She acknowledges that the vaccine does not guarantee that someone will not get the flu, but noted that "it's more of a continuum, rather than either you're sick or you're not." She said, "You still might get it, but you may not end up in the ICU like I did ... Considering the benefits that you could get and what the extreme, worst outcome could be, it is an easy thing to do that is proven to make a tremendous difference for people."

In addition to getting yearly vaccines, Miller takes steps to minimize her exposure and makes healthy lifestyle choices, such as eating nutritious foods and exercising.
