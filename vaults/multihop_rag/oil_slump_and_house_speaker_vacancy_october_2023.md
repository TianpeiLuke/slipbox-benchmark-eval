# The Oil Slump And House Speaker Vacancy, Early October 2023

Oil prices tumbled on Wednesday 4 October 2023, taking some heat off inflation: benchmark US crude fell $US5.01 to settle at $US84.22 per barrel for its worst drop in just over a year, having topped $US93 the previous week, while Brent crude, the international standard, lost $US5.11 to $US85.81. Prices for crude had been generally charging higher from $US70 during the summer following announcements of production cuts by some oil-producing countries. The services report of the same day offered hints of sticky pressure on inflation, with prices paid by services companies rising last month at a similar rate as in August.

Wall Street was also absorbing the ouster of Kevin McCarthy as speaker of the House of Representatives. The unprecedented move to remove a speaker likely does not change much in the short term, with funding for the US government set until 17 November, but according to economists at Goldman Sachs, "a leadership vacuum in the House raises the odds of a government shutdown when the current funding extension expires"; a shutdown would drag on the US economy and raise the risk of a recession, though financial markets have held up relatively well through past shutdowns. On Wall Street, big tech stocks helped support the market after leading it lower a day earlier, since they tend to move more sharply with expectations for rates because high-growth stocks are seen as some of the biggest victims of high yields, while stock indexes abroad were mixed across much of Europe.
