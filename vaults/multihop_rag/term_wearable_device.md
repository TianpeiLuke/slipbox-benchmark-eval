# Wearable Device

## Definition

In this corpus, "wearable device" (or "wearable") is the umbrella term outlets use for a body-worn consumer or health-tech gadget — a smartwatch, fitness ring, fitness band, or medical-sensing device — interchangeably with specific products like the Apple Watch, Google Pixel Watch, Whoop 4, Ultrahuman Ring Air, and Acurable's respiratory monitors.

## Context

The corpus uses "wearable" as the umbrella term spanning consumer smartwatches (Apple Watch, Google Pixel Watch, Samsung Galaxy Watch), fitness rings (Ultrahuman Ring Air), fitness bands (Whoop 4), and specialized medical-sensing gadgets (Acurable's respiratory monitors, the Willow Go wearable breast pump). Some wearables are purely consumer-facing fitness/lifestyle products sold through holiday deals coverage, while others sit at the boundary of consumer electronics and regulated medical devices — the Apple Watch's SpO2 blood-oxygen sensor and EKG feature are the clearest example, since they triggered patent litigation with medical device makers Masimo and AliveCor over the underlying sensing technology. Acurable is described explicitly as "a medical device company that makes patient-friendly wearable devices to diagnose and manage respiratory conditions at home," showing wearables also serving a clinical diagnostic role outside the smartwatch category.

## Key Characteristics

- **Body-worn form factor** — worn on the wrist (Apple Watch, Pixel Watch, Whoop 4, Galaxy Watch), finger (Ultrahuman Ring Air), or elsewhere on the body (Acurable's respiratory monitors, Willow Go breast pump).
- **On-board sensing** — wearables carry sensors such as accelerometers, gyroscopes, optical heart-rate sensors, and SpO2 (blood-oxygen) sensors; the Apple Watch Series 9's double-tap gesture, for instance, is detected from accelerometer, gyroscope, and optical heart-rate data collected at the wrist.
- **Companion-app dependence** — many wearables (Whoop 4, Ultrahuman Ring Air) have no display of their own and instead offload data to a smartphone app for analysis and coaching.
- **Health/medical crossover** — some wearables function as, or overlap with, regulated medical devices: the Apple Watch's SpO2 and EKG features became the subject of ITC patent disputes with medical device makers Masimo and AliveCor, and Acurable markets its wearables as home-use medical devices to diagnose and manage respiratory conditions such as sleep apnea.
- **Rapid feature iteration** — newer models add capabilities like Sleep Profiles, abnormal heart-rate tracking, and gesture controls (double tap), often gated behind newer chips (the Apple Watch Series 9's S9 chip with four neural engines).

## Background (external)

Outside this corpus, the general definition is broader than any single corpus device: wearable technology comprises small electronic and mobile devices, worn on the body or built into clothing or accessories, that commonly detect and transmit information such as vital signs, sometimes with immediate biofeedback to the wearer (Wikipedia, "Wearable technology," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wearable_technology).
