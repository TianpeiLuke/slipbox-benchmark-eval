# Peter Kalmus's Profile and Radical Turn

Kalmus, 49, thinks civilization is on the path to break down, that the Biden administration is clueless on climate, and that he might get fired from his job at NASA if he is arrested for a third time protesting what he views as downright madness: the continued use of fossil fuels.

Kalmus, whose strident tweets reach some 340,000 followers on X, is at the vanguard of a group of scientists who are increasingly disillusioned and growing convinced that more radical action is needed to wake up political leaders. He has been on a 15-year quest to halt climate change and has already oriented most everything in his life to minimize his own climate footprint and maximize what he can do to push society to change — and nothing has been enough. The changes he made were not enough: he wrote a book on climate change and chained himself to a Chase bank in protest of fossil fuel financing, and twice he has been arrested during climate protests. So this summer, as extreme heat and rainfall battered many American communities, he visited Maine searching for something different — not for climate solutions, but for new ways to withstand climate change's harms and to make peace with the grief that often feels inescapable for those who reckon with global warming daily; he visited Belfast, Maine in July and August to see the Possibility Alliance homestead as he looked for inspiration on how best to live with the effects of climate change. Now a married father of two teenagers, Kalmus is introspective, earnest and narrowly focused on climate change in both his personal and professional life; tall with an athletic build, a neat beard and a tangle of messy hair with a skunklike streak of white, he can sometimes come off like an overgrown teenager — the smartest person in the room, brimming with idealism and still indignant, with a zeal many adults can no longer muster, about how life's most annoying systems work, like the U.S. medical system, a symptom of what he calls the "capitalist death cult." His social media posts often evoke heartbreak: "We're in the process of losing basically everything," he wrote recently on X.
