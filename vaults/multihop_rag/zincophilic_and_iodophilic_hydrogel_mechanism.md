# The Zincophilic and Iodophilic Hydrogel Mechanism

The anode and cathode sides of the hydrogel carry different functional groups that make it zincophilic (with an affinity for zinc) and iodophilic (with an affinity for iodine) respectively, and this asymmetry is what lets one electrolyte fix problems at both electrodes.

On the anode side, the design repels dissolved polyiodides — negatively charged ions composed entirely of iodine atoms — allowing the zinc to be deposited uniformly and protecting the anode from iodine-caused corrosion. Ensuring that zinc accumulates evenly on the anode side is the purpose of the hydrogel modification, Hongjin Fan explained, and it mitigates the diffusion back and forth of polysulfide between anode and cathode, the so-called "shuttle effect," which can cause poor cycling stability and severe anode corrosion in batteries. On the cathode side, the polyiodides are immobilized, and redox reactions — in which iodine is both stripped of electrons (oxidation) and granted electrons (reduction) — are sped up thanks to the synchronized rapid transport of ions and electrons, so the hydrogel electrolyte also accelerates the iodine conversion reaction. Finally, the solid nature of the hydrogel electrolyte limits the growth of dendrites through its mechanical strength, acting as a physical barrier not present in liquid electrolytes.
