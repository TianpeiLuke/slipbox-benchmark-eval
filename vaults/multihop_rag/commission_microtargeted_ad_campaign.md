# The Commission's Microtargeted Ad Campaign For CSAM Scanning

The European Commission's advertising campaign for its CSAM-scanning proposal was microtargeted so that the ads did not reach privacy-minded, eurosceptic or Christian audiences, and EU home affairs commissioner Ylva Johansson admitted to MEPs that the EU's executive is investigating the matter — after she had earlier claimed the campaign was "100%" legal. TechCrunch reported the admission on 25 October 2023, from an exchange of views with the European Parliament's civil rights, justice and home affairs (LIBE) committee that afternoon.

The targeting detail comes from Mekić, who wrote that X's Transparency Report shows the European Commission used "microtargeting" to ensure that the ads did not appear to people who care about privacy (people interested in Julian Assange) and eurosceptics (people interested in "nexit", "brexit" and "spanexit" or in Victor Orbán, Nigel Farage, or the German political party AfD). For unclear reasons, people interested in Christianity were also excluded.

Mekić's account of the mechanism is that after excluding critical political and religious groups, X's algorithm was set to find people in the remaining population who were indeed interested in the ad message, resulting in what he called "an uncritical echo chamber". On that basis he argued the microtargeting on political and religious beliefs violates X's advertising policy, the Digital Services Act — which the Commission itself has to oversee — and the General Data Protection Regulation.

Johansson's position moved between two dates. In a tweet earlier that month, on 13 October 2023, she had sought to dismiss criticism over the legality of the microtargeting, writing that as her services had been directly accused of illegal acts she thought it important she step in, and setting out three points: that @EUHomeAffairs "have followed the guidelines & the law 100%"; that "the promotion of our proposal is standard normal practice"; and that "this proposal is about protecting children from sexual abuse".

Asked by the LIBE committee about the discrepancy between that tweet and the existence of an investigation, Johansson said she had been given "new information" related to DSA compliance that merited looking into. "When I made a tweet on the 100% legal [point] that was based on the information I had. But I have to be very open; then I got other information that there could be question marks on the compliance with the DSA — and I take this very seriously," she told the committee. "If that is the case then of course there has to be consequences on that. So that's why it's important that we have to look into [it]. Of course we always have to comply with the regulation. There's no question about that."
