# Adam Yedidia's First Testimony And His Immunity Deal

Late in the afternoon of the second day of Sam Bankman-Fried's trial, the government called Adam Yedidia to the stand, and lead prosecutor Danielle Sassoon disclosed that this college friend of Bankman-Fried had legal immunity during his testimony. Asked why he had made such a deal with the government, Yedidia answered: "I was concerned that I had unwittingly written code that contributed to a crime."

Yedidia, described as a fast-talking graduate of MIT, testified that he and Bankman-Fried were close friends in college. After Bankman-Fried left Jane Street — the high-frequency trading firm where the former billionaire got his start in finance after MIT — he persuaded Yedidia to join him, first as a trader at Alameda and then as a developer at FTX.

The testimony was cut short when the clock neared 4:30 p.m. and court adjourned for the day. Yedidia was scheduled to continue his testimony on Thursday, to be followed by Matt Huang, a former partner at the venture capital firm Sequoia Capital, and then by Gary Wang, a key Bankman-Fried lieutenant and one of the government's star witnesses.
