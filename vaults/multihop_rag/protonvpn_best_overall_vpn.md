# ProtonVPN, Best VPN Overall

ProtonVPN was Engadget's best VPN overall. The VPNs tried out ranked pretty consistently across all tests, but ProtonVPN stood out because of its overall security and usability: the Proton Technologies suite includes mail, calendar, drive and a VPN known for its end-to-end encryption, making it a strong contender for overall security, while the VPN itself came across as a well-rounded independent service. Specifications: 10 simultaneous connections across 10 devices; Windows, Mac, Android, iOS and Linux; Chrome and Firefox browser extensions; dedicated IP addresses for business users only; no trial but a 30-day money-back guarantee; $4.99/month at ProtonVPN.

Its privacy record is the core of the case. ProtonVPN's no-logs policy has passed audits and the company has proven not to comply with law enforcement requests; because it is based in Switzerland there are no forced logging obligations, according to the company. It is also built on an open-source framework and has an official vulnerability disclosure program along with clear definitions of what it does with personal information.

The free version is limited compared with other options, with access to server networks in just three countries. The paid version, starting at about $5.39 per month, includes VPN server locations in more than 65 countries on 10 devices at a time; dedicated Proton Technologies users can pay closer to $8.63 for a monthly plan covering the entire suite.

In testing it passed the geoblock, streaming and gaming tests with only a very small toll on connection speeds. It also comes with malware-, ad- and tracker-blocking as an additional service, plus a kill switch feature on macOS, Windows, Linux, iOS and the latest version of Android, and it is available on most major operating systems, routers, TV services and more, including Firefox, Linux and Android TV. For dedicated Linux users, ProtonVPN added a new Linux-specific app to support its services in August.
