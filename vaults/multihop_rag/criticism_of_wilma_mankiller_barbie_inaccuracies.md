# Criticism Of The Wilma Mankiller Barbie's Inaccuracies

Critics attacked the premise that Mattel's Wilma Mankiller doll faithfully represents Cherokee culture, pointing to specific errors in the product and its packaging. Chuck Hoskin Jr, Principal Chief of the Cherokee Nation, told The New York Times that the doll's basket was not authentically Cherokee, and that the wrong sets of symbols for the Cherokee language were used on the packaging — with the result that the symbols read "Chicken Nation" rather than "Cherokee Nation." "To someone who doesn't read Cherokee syllabary, they're not going to notice it," he said. "To the Cherokee people for whom Wilma is of such enduring significance and we have such enduring love for her, to see our seal incorrect, it's very disappointing because it would not have taken much effort or thought to avoid that." Hoskin said he learned of the doll's creation six months earlier and that Mattel did not work directly with the Cherokee Nation, though he added the company has been understanding of the criticism. The tribe's own statement said: "Regrettably, the Mattel company did not work directly with the tribal government's design and communications team to secure the official Seal or verify it."

Cherokee women raised a second objection — that Mattel overlooked the problematic details on both the doll and the packaging. "Mixed emotions shared by me and many other Cherokee women who have now purchased the product revolve around whether a Wilma Barbie captures her legacy, her physical features and the importance of centering Cherokee women in decision making," Stacy Leeds, the law school dean at Arizona State University and a former Cherokee Nation Supreme Court justice, told The Associated Press in an email. Regina Thompson, a Cherokee basket weaver who grew up near Tahlequah, does not think the doll looks like Mankiller, and said Mattel should have used traditional pucker toe moccasins instead of black shoes and included symbols on the basket that Cherokees use to tell a story. "Wilma's name is the only thing Cherokee on that box," Thompson said. "Nothing about that doll is Wilma, nothing."
