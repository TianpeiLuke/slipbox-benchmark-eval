# December 2023 On Binge And Stan: Vigil, Bump And The Roberts-Smith Documentary

Vigil returns to Binge on December 11 for a second season. The Scottish crime thriller's first season, in which a police detective was assigned to a murder investigation on board an on-duty British nuclear submarine, ended with a nerve-shredding cliffhanger the reviewer still thinks about far too often. Season two stays on dry land but retains the military focus: when an attack drone demonstration at a British army base goes tragically wrong, Detective Chief Inspector Amy Silva (Suranne Jones, Gentleman Jack) and her now pregnant partner, Detective Sergeant Kirsten Longacre (Rose Leslie, Game of Thrones), are called in to investigate. Expect a fast pace and significant twists — this series doesn't do quiet contemplation. Binge's November highlights included comedy troupe Please Don't Destroy's The Treasure of Foggy Mountain, the picaresque comedy Bookie, and Julia, the celebrity chef biography of Julia Child.

On Stan, the top recommendation is Bump (December 26), continuing what the Herald calls one of the best new traditions in Australian television: a new season of the Sydney-based comic-drama uploaded every Boxing Day. This fourth instalment of Kelsey Munro and Claudia Karvan's show again places its ensemble cast in a testing new light — the high-school students of season one, Oly (Nathalie Morris) and Santi (Carlos Sanson Jr), thrown together when a hook-up turned into a teenage pregnancy, are now a career woman and stay-at-home dad while their daughter Jacinda (Ava Cannon) is in primary school.

Also on Stan, Revealed: Ben Roberts-Smith Truth on Trial (December 10) is a feature-length documentary providing the inside story to one of Australia's most contentious and headline-making pieces of investigative journalism: the five-year quest to unearth the truth behind rumours that the country's most decorated living soldier committed war crimes in Afghanistan. With stories in The Age and Sydney Morning Herald, as well as on 60 Minutes, journalists Nick McKenzie and Chris Masters pushed to bring the truth to the public, navigating divisions in the SAS and eventually the defamation trial that Roberts-Smith brought to court and lost. Dora Weekley's documentary should include hitherto unknown details and unheard commentary.
