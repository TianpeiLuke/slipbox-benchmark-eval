# Michigan's Temporary Restraining Order Against The Big Ten

Michigan filed a temporary restraining order against the Big Ten on Friday night, Nov. 10, 2023, hoping to block Jim Harbaugh's three-game suspension, but no ruling came before the Wolverines' game at Penn State and the hearing was set for Friday, Nov. 17. ESPN reported that the Nov. 17 hearing would determine whether Harbaugh returned to the field for the final two games against Maryland and No. 1 Ohio State, after the Big Ten's Friday ruling barred him from the field for the final three regular-season games over the in-person scouting and sign-stealing scandal that led to staffer Connor Stalions' resignation.

The filing itself was made in Washtenaw County Trial Court on Friday night, with Jim Harbaugh and the U-M regents as plaintiffs and the Big Ten and Tony Petitti as defendants; Chris Vannini reported on Nov. 11, 2023 that the judicial officer was Timothy P. Connors, a U-M lecturer and adjunct professor at Wayne State and Vermont, and the case was also reported to be in the hands of Judge Carol Kunhke, a Michigan alumnus. If granted, the order would have delayed the suspension, but the clock was running before Saturday's game.

The drama ran to kickoff and then ended without relief. ESPN's Pete Thamel reported it was unclear whether Harbaugh would be granted a hearing in time to coach against Penn State, saying with kickoff scheduled for noon ET that "the drama's going to go right up 'till noon." Nicole Auerbach reported on Nov. 11 that there was no ruling on the TRO filing that day, so Harbaugh would not coach against Penn State, with the school saying: "We look forward to presenting our case next week where we intend to demonstrate that the Big Ten has not acted legally or fairly." Adam Rittenberg noted that Harbaugh was not on the team bus with other coaches and players when it arrived at Beaver Stadium on Saturday morning, and Michigan offensive coordinator Sherrone Moore was set to serve as acting head coach in his absence.
