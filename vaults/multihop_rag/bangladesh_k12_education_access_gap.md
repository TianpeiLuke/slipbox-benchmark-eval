# Bangladesh's K12 Education Access Gap

The founders of edtech 10 Minute School argue that access to education in Bangladesh is filled with inequalities, and that edtech is the way to close the gap. Their grounds are geographic and financial: many rural areas do not have good learning centers, so students travel to the capital Dhaka to prepare for their university admission exams — an option only available to families who have the financial means.

Asked what is driving interest in edtech in Bangladesh, COO Beg pointed to the scale of the affected population. "We have a huge student base of 42 million enrolled in the K12 system and the majority of students don't have access to quality teachers, quality content, because those who are actually living outside the capital city don't have good teachers in their area," he said. "The fundamental problem that needs to be solved is access to quality content and teachers in a very affordable manner." The warrant joining the grounds to the conclusion is delivery over phones: edtech has the opportunity to democratize access to education by reaching students wherever they are, which removes both the travel requirement and the cost of a capital-city coaching center.
