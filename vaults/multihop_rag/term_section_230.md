# Section 230

## Definition

Section 230 (of the Communications Decency Act) is a US law that shields online platforms from legal responsibility for the illegal actions of their users, and separately protects the platforms' own content moderation decisions.

## Context

In the corpus the statute surfaces in two distinct disputes. In the Snapchat fentanyl-death lawsuits, Snap's motion to dismiss cites Section 230 as its liability shield against claims that its platform facilitated illegal drug sales between users. In the Supreme Court case over the Texas and Florida content-moderation laws, Oregon Senator Ron Wyden — described as Section 230's co-author — invokes the statute's other function, protecting the moderation decisions platforms make about what content to host, arguing that the state laws would gut what Section 230 lets platforms do.

## Key Characteristics

- **Dual shield** — the corpus shows it protecting platforms on two separate fronts: liability for users' illegal conduct on the service (the drug-sale claims against Snap) and liability for how a platform moderates content (the basis of Wyden's objection to the Texas/Florida laws).
- **Conditional, not absolute, immunity** — per the Snap case, the statute "only offers immunity to companies that have acted responsibly and taken precautions to prevent illegal activity from taking place on their platforms," a condition the plaintiffs' attorney argues Snap failed to meet.
- **Legislative authorship tied to the moderation function** — Senator Ron Wyden is named in the corpus as a co-author of Section 230, invoked specifically to defend its role in enabling platforms to remove harmful content without losing legal protection.
