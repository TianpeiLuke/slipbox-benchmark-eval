# Roblox's Content Delivery Architecture Versus App Stores

Asked by Casey Newton of Platformer whether Roblox — which looks a lot like an app store and increasingly like it wants to be an operating system — is on a collision course with iOS, Android, or the Oculus Store, and how it can reach a billion daily active people without owning its own hardware, David Baszucki answered with an architectural argument rather than a competitive one. When you imagine millions of creators and the ability to go to ancient Egypt or to the Roblox office in immersive 3D, the content cannot all be shipped to the device: it is just impossible to have that much content there.

The structure that follows is hybrid. Content needs to come to the device in a very unique architecture with very low latency and a lot of local 3D simulation, while at the same time, on the cloud, that content needs to load and connect very fast. Baszucki called this the architectural inevitability of a 3D connection platform where you can go everywhere instantly, and suggested it is what Newton's question was really pointing at.

His claim about the relationship to platform owners is that it is complementary rather than adversarial: Apple and these platforms, he said, are very aware of this inevitable architecture for 3D and are actually very big supporters of it.
