# Prime Day 2023 Home Comfort And Air Quality Deals

WIRED's home comfort picks are the Tushy bidet, the Airmega 250 air purifier, the WiiM sunrise alarm, a 35-pint dehumidifier, the Vornado 460 fan, LifeStraw filters, a printer, astrology candles, and a pet fur roller steadily priced at $25.

The bidet pitch opens with a rhetorical question — if you fell in the mud, would you reach for a sheet of toilet paper or a hose? — and the Tushy is affordable, easy to install on practically any toilet, and needs no electrical plug or hot water hookup, which earned it WIRED's recommendation as best budget non-electric bidet. For larger rooms you want a larger air purifier, and WIRED recommends the Airmega 250 as best for living rooms: product reviewer Matt Jancer has used one for years to keep his New York City apartment's air clean, and while filters are expensive at $60 to $80 he gets nearly a year out of each, making the performance-per-price worth it; its automatic function kicks into high gear when heavy pollution is detected but normally runs whisper-quiet, unnoticed even a few feet away. A sunrise alarm wakes you at a set time by gradually brightening and changing color temperature to mimic real sunlight, a gentler alternative to a blaring alarm, and like most it has a sunset feature for winding down; of all those Jancer tested, the WiiM was easiest to set up and best at walking the user through operation in its app. Jancer has relied on the 35-pint dehumidifier to keep his East Coast apartment habitable during sweltering, humid summers: you can set the desired humidity in 5 percent increments from 35 to 85 and the automatic function turns the machine on and off as needed; it's not particularly loud, and he empties the water bin only once a day on his smaller model. An electric fan saves money on air conditioning and adds pleasant white noise while taking only a sip of electricity; Jancer has used the Vornado 460 for three summers and says its ability to move an impressive amount of air around a bedroom on the lowest setting belies its smallish size. WIRED readers love LifeStraw filters, included in its Best Home Emergency Gear guide, which remove 99 percent of waterborne bacteria and pathogens — and this is the lowest price they tend to reach, cheap enough to buy one per family member. On printers, navigating the sheer number of options is exhausting and WIRED doesn't have a full guide yet, but the writer bought this one on a fellow reviewer's recommendation after their last printer died and has been more than happy: good quality prints and scans, wireless, and printable from a phone. The candles you've likely seen floating around your feeds are 13.5 ounces, smell good, and are hand-poured in the US — and, writes Nena, astrology is fun whether you're into it or not; born on March 28, she is reportedly reclusive by nature, which she calls accurate. The pet fur roller isn't a huge discount at its steady $25, but if you have pets you need it: roll it across furniture to trap fur in its inner compartment, then open it and toss the fur, with no sticky lint roller refills.
