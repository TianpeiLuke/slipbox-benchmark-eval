# Space Mission

## Definition

In this corpus, "space mission" surfaces in two distinct forms: agency-run scientific observatories — NASA's Solar Dynamics Observatory and the European Space Agency's (ESA) Solar Orbiter — that monitor the sun, and the interplanetary ambitions of a commercial launch company, SpaceX, whose founder Elon Musk described a plan to colonize Mars.

## Context

The clearest corpus evidence for actual space missions is the sun-science roundup in doc_0325, which credits specific NASA and ESA instruments with the year's discoveries: images are attributed to "NASA/Solar Dynamics Observatory" for tracking the approaching solar maximum, to "NASA/SDO and the AIA" and "EVE... science teams" for revising the sun's measured size, and to the "European Space Agency's Solar Orbiter," which spotted tiny plasma jets ("picojets") that help explain the origin of solar wind. Doc_0027 describes SpaceX, Elon Musk's company, as the vehicle for a more ambitious kind of space mission: DeepMind co-founder Demis Hassabis secured a tour of SpaceX headquarters, where "rocket hulls" hung from the ceiling, and afterward the two men lunched together and Musk explained that his plan was to colonize Mars to escape overpopulation and other dangers on Earth. NASA also appears in the corpus in two mission-adjacent but non-mission roles: as the federal agency that employs climate scientist Peter Kalmus, who fears losing his job over climate protest arrests (doc_0233), and as one of roughly 45 organizations that pledged participation in Meta and IBM's AI Alliance (doc_0268) — neither passage describes a specific space mission NASA is running.

## Key Characteristics

- **Agency-run scientific missions** — NASA's Solar Dynamics Observatory and ESA's Solar Orbiter are named, active missions in the corpus, each producing data (sunspot activity, coronal waves, picojets) that fed 2023 solar-science findings.
- **Commercial/exploratory missions** — SpaceX represents a company-driven space mission model, with Musk's stated goal of colonizing Mars going beyond satellite observation into human settlement.
- **Distinct from institutional presence** — the corpus also uses "NASA" as shorthand for the agency as an employer (Kalmus) or a coalition member (AI Alliance) without describing any specific mission in those instances.
- **Observation vs. settlement** — the two space-mission threads in the corpus sit at opposite ends of the spectrum: unmanned solar observatories doing remote sensing, versus SpaceX's proposed crewed colonization of Mars.

## Background (external)

Outside this corpus, a space mission is generally defined as a spaceflight intended to achieve an objective, conducted with or without humans aboard and carried out by government space agencies as well as private companies (source: "Space mission," Wikipedia, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Space_mission, accessed 2026-09-02).
