# Zojirushi Micom Rice Cooker

The Zojirushi Micom Rice Cooker and Warmer was $194, down from $233 — 17 percent off at Amazon. It is a 5.5-cup-capacity rice cooker and warmer that not only cooks rice but comes with a steaming basket to double as a steamer and a cake menu setting to bake cakes, and it is senior reviewer Victoria Song's pick.

Her argument is that a lot of people will tell you rice cookers are single-use appliances meant only for rice, and they are wrong: a rice cooker is best at cooking rice, but it can do a lot of the same things as an Instant Pot — hardboiled eggs, oatmeal, steamed vegetables, porridge, one-pot meals, even a cake.

She grew up with giant 10-cup rice cookers at home but did not appreciate the appliance's versatility until she left the country for college, where a tiny two-cup rice cooker kept her fed in a cramped 250-square-foot Tokyo apartment. It was programmable, so she could wash her rice, stick it in the cooker, and know that when she woke up late for class she could still whip up ochazuke or oatmeal for a quick, cheap, nutritious breakfast — and it took the hassle out of steel-cut oats. Whenever she craved sweets, it was easy to take pancake mix and bake a Japanese-style cheesecake for one.

She has since graduated to the 5.5-cup Zojirushi Micom, one of the handiest tools she has for meal prepping: when sick this past winter she made ample use of its porridge setting for a congee-type dish with chicken and ginger, just like her mom used to make, and because it keeps something warm for days she could crawl out of bed, scoop out some porridge, and crawl back in with minimal effort. When feeling lazy she throws eggs in for extra hard-boiled protein, and hers comes with a little basket so it is easy to add veggies or steam frozen dumplings. What she likes most is that rice cookers are more space-efficient than Instant Pots: in her kitchen, the one spot where an Instant Pot could fit is instead occupied by a rice cooker, blender, and spoon rest — and a multitasking kitchen gadget that does not take over your entire counter is a must in a small space.
