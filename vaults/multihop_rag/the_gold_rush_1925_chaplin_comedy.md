# The Gold Rush (1925): Chaplin's Klondike Comedy

The Gold Rush is Charlie Chaplin's 1925 adventure comedy, running 1h 28m, directed by and starring Chaplin alongside Georgia Hale and Mack Swain. Chaplin's "Little Tramp" is a prospector living in a shack in the middle of the Klondike, and the film is built from extreme slapstick and farce: the Tramp is blown about by Canadian winds, stoops to eating a leather shoe for sustenance, and eventually performs his legendary fork dance.

Polygon's case for the film is that it is nearly 100 years old and still holds up for the modern sense of humour, and that Chaplin — even more than the muscle icons of the 1980s — is the key DNA of modern action entertainment, which makes The Gold Rush a hilarious entry point for anyone who has never seen one of his classics. It is available to stream on Max and Criterion Channel or free with ads on Freevee, Tubi and Plex, and for digital rental or purchase on Amazon and Apple TV.
