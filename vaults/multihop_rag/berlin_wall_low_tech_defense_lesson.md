# The Berlin Wall's Low-Tech Defense Lesson

Dr. Avner Barnea, a former Shin Bet senior officer and lecturer on intelligence and national security and on business intelligence at Bar-Ilan University, argues from the Berlin Wall that it was low-tech measures, not sophisticated ones, that actually stopped crossings: "These low-tech solutions made all the difference. None of the authorities ever took the risk of relying on advanced technologies."

The evidence is the wall's own evolution. Built in 1961 as a standard wire and concrete fence to make it difficult for East German residents to escape to freedom in the West, by the end of the 1970s its fourth generation included a 127 km long electrified fence, a strip of land made of sharp metal nails, a closely-packed row of watchtowers, a patrol road, an intrusion-tracking dirt road, guard dogs, deep anti-vehicle trenches, and two layers of concrete walls. "The fence was initially quite weak, so it was decided to place guards every few meters on top of the wall," Barnea told "Globes." "But until they mined the area, they didn't really succeed in preventing escapees."
