# Microtargeting

## Definition

In the corpus, microtargeting refers to using a platform's ad-targeting tools to aim political or advertising messaging at narrow, algorithmically selected audience segments — including by deliberately excluding groups defined by their interests or political views — rather than at a general audience.

## Context

In the corpus, microtargeting appears in two related EU controversies over data-driven ad targeting on major platforms.
Meta's "pay or consent" model is accused of forcing users to accept being "tracked and profiled" so the company can keep "profiting from microtargeting them," with nearly 20 consumer groups arguing this breaches EU consumer protection law.
Separately, the European Commission itself ran a microtargeted political ad campaign on X to build support for its CSAM-scanning proposal, which became the subject of a DSA-compliance investigation and a European Parliament committee hearing.
Both cases involve platforms designated Very Large Online Platforms (VLOPs) under the DSA — Facebook and Instagram in the Meta case, X in the Commission's campaign — though only the Commission's campaign was exposed using X's DSA-mandated ad-transparency tools, while the Meta case instead centers on a coordinated consumer-protection complaint.

## Key Characteristics

- **Exclusion-based mechanics** — technologist Danny Mekić's analysis of X's Transparency Report showed the Commission's campaign was configured to exclude specific interest groups (people interested in Julian Assange, in "nexit"/"brexit"/"spanexit," in figures like Viktor Orbán or Nigel Farage, and people interested in Christianity), after which the platform's algorithm found remaining users receptive to the message.
- **Echo-chamber effect** — Mekić described the resulting audience, once critical political and religious groups were filtered out, as "an uncritical echo chamber."
- **Regulatory exposure under the DSA** — the DSA prohibits using sensitive personal data, such as political opinions, to target ads; Mekić argued the Commission's campaign violated X's own advertising policy, the DSA, and the GDPR.
- **Basis for consumer-protection complaints** — separately, Meta's monetization of tracked-and-profiled user data for microtargeted advertising was central to BEUC's complaint that its pay-or-consent model is "unfair" and "illegal" under EU consumer law.
- **Exposed via VLOP transparency obligations** — the Commission's microtargeted campaign became visible only because Mekić used the public ad-transparency tools the DSA requires X, as a VLOP, to provide; Meta's microtargeting practice, by contrast, was already publicly known and became the basis of a separate consumer-protection complaint rather than something uncovered through ad-transparency tools.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, microtargeting is generally defined as the use of online data to tailor advertising or political messaging to individuals or small groups based on their personal characteristics or vulnerabilities, rather than addressing a broad, undifferentiated audience (Wikipedia, "Microtargeting," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microtargeting).
