# Minnesota Wild 2023-24 Slump Causes

Yardbarker's Nov. 27, 2023 account of Dean Evason's firing set out a multi-factor explanation of the Wild's collapse: with Minnesota far out of the playoff picture at 5-10-4 and sitting bottom five in the league in both goals for and against, a change was necessary and expected, and the causes ran through injuries, goaltending, luck, culture, star production and depth defense.

Injuries and goaltending had certainly played a factor in the poor play — captain Jared Spurgeon missed the first 13 games of the season, and Filip Gustavsson's and Marc-André Fleury's combined .878 SV% was near the bottom of the league. The Wild had likely been unlucky too: they controlled a slim majority of scoring chances and a strong majority of high-danger chances during 5-on-5 play, a common theme for an organization usually among the more defensively stout in the league. But the culture around the team was evidently turning sour quickly, Kirill Kaprizov was on pace for a career-low 26 goals and carried a team-worst -10 rating, and most of the depth defensemen had been significant liabilities. The roster was not all that different from the one that had finished top-three in the Central Division for three straight seasons, and it was certainly one that still had playoff aspirations. Minnesota had not won since Nov. 7 against the Islanders and was winless in seven straight.
