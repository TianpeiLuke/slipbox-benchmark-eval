# The Case For Treating Ultra-Processed Foods As Addictive Substances

Lead researcher Ashley Gearhardt argues that ultra-processed food addiction should be acknowledged as real, because doing so opens a route to improving global health.

"There is converging and consistent support for the validity and clinical relevance of ultra-processed food addiction," said Gearhardt, a professor of psychology at the University of Michigan, in a press release announcing the study. "By acknowledging that certain types of processed foods have the properties of addictive substances, we may be able to help improve global health."

She extends the claim beyond wealthy countries and beyond individual choice. "It's essential to understand the addiction to these ultra-processed foods globally, particularly in low- and middle-income countries," Gearhardt said in the release. "It will take courageous action to change these and other economic and structural factors that drive people toward ultra-processed foods."
