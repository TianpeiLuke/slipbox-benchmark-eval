# Switching From Book Value To Market Value Multiples

Once the metrics and their importance are understood there are no fixed limits on how they can be combined to pick stocks: one or two metrics, or a combination of many more, can be used to assess which stocks are undervalued or overvalued, and depending on specific cases further variations of the multiples themselves are available. The variation this note describes is the substitution of market value of net assets for book value in the denominator, applied when the market is repricing assets that the balance sheet carries at cost.

The illustrating case is the 2003-2007 bull market, when many textile stocks, for instance Bombay Dyeing, turned out to be multibaggers even though their textile business was not really booming; the stocks were zooming because there was a real estate boom and many textile mills had manufacturing facilities in prime real estate locations, so the markets started to factor the value of the company based on the market value of their land. In such a case one can deviate from the Price/Book multiple to price divided by the market value of net assets, that is assets minus net debt. The same holds for holding companies: if there is a reasonable case for underlying asset value to be unlocked and the Holdco discount to shrink, as has happened in bank holding companies over the last 2-3 years, then there is a case to move beyond book value to market value. The scope condition is that specific case for unlocking or repricing; absent it, the ordinary book-value multiple stands.
