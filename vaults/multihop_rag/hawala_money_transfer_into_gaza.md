# Hawala As The Main Money Transfer Channel Into Gaza

The main and easiest method for moving money to Gaza is hawala — a financial channel based on trust and connections that enables the transfer and exchange of funds between countries without the cash physically passing between them.

"Most of the money does not reach Gaza physically through the border crossings, but via hawala," Ostrovitch-Levi explains. "It's a channel for transferring the funds raised in other countries. In Gaza there are all sorts of wealthy individuals who can transfer large amounts in cash to Hamas upon receiving request from entities abroad." The mechanism runs on offsetting balances rather than movement: "Money changers in places all over the world, let's say Turkey, receive money, and inform the hawaladars (money changers) in Gaza who transfer the money on their behalf. When a customer comes to a hawaladar in Turkey and asks to transfer money to Gaza, he does not physically transfer money, but settles accounts internally with money changers in Gaza, and Judea and Samaria. The funds are de facto not transferred. Occasionally, or when the amount of the debt reaches a predetermined ceiling, a transfer is made between the money changers to reset the outstanding debt." That structure is what makes interdiction hard: "Transfers are made between two 'exchangers,' and don't include the identity of the beneficiaries, or the persons who gave the money. They don't know what the transactions were, they only know there are debts to offset. We are in a constant battle against this phenomenon. In recent years, Israeli authorities have designated several currency service providers in Gaza as conduits facilitating the transfer tens of millions of dollars a year to terrorist organizations in Gaza." Cryptocurrencies and trade are among the other methods by which money has reached Gaza and Hamas over the years.
