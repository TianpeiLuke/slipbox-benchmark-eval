# The Dodgers' Payroll Constraints Against Signing Both Ohtani And Trout

Essentially Sports argued on 18 November 2023 that keeping Shohei Ohtani and Mike Trout together would not be a feasible option for the Los Angeles Dodgers right now: the claim is that the payroll cannot carry both at full value, and the warrant is that the space freed by departing contracts only supports players well below either man's annual price.

The price grounds come first. Ohtani's next projected contract value is predicted at around $500-$600 million, which would break Trout's biggest MLB deal record of 12 years and $426.5 million. The Dodgers have deep pockets and maintained a costly lineup that season valued at $283 million. Trout's $426.5 million deal carries an annual salary of about $35.45 million with seven years left on his Angels contract, so to get him in blue the Dodgers would have to pay $248.15 million plus any extension value; Ohtani's predicted $535 million over 11 years, according to a Sports Illustrated segment, would cost a good $48.63 million annually.

The payroll grounds close the argument. The Dodgers already have six players they must pay above $10 million a year: Freddie Freeman at $27 million, Mookie Betts at $25 million, Clayton Kershaw at $20 million, Chris Taylor at $15 million, Max Muncy at $13.5 million and J.D. Martinez at $10 million. Among the top-paid players Kershaw is exiting the team that season, and that, along with a few other player offloads, will free up enough space to support two $25 million players or three $10 million-per-year players — a range neither Trout nor Ohtani falls within.
