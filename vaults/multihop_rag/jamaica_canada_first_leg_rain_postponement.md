# The Rain Postponement Of Jamaica vs Canada

Torrential rain in Kingston forced the CONCACAF Nations League quarterfinal first leg between Jamaica and Canada to be called off on the Friday evening and replayed the next morning at 10:30 a.m. ET (7:30 a.m. PT). An hour and a quarter before the original kickoff the weather was torrential and had been raining hard for some time, to the point that people wondered whether the match would take place at all; postponing international matches is extremely difficult, but player safety had to be considered, and starting lineups were due in 15 minutes. Canada Soccer, which had earlier joked about 10ft x 10ft tarps, posted that "You will need a rowboat."

The delays came in stages. An hour before kickoff, lineups had still not been released and Canada Soccer's social media admin went on Instagram Live to show apocalyptic conditions at Independence Park, saying "it's not looking likely" the game would be played. The first official update stated kickoff had been pushed back at least an hour and would not come before 8 p.m. ET, language that seemed to leave room for further postponement or calling the game off, with OneSoccer citing "on-field issues." Then Concacaf pushed kickoff back another hour, to no earlier than 9 p.m. ET, and it appeared more and more likely a game would take place. Rain tapered significantly at one point, prompting some Jamaica fans to take their seats in celebration, and speculation held that a 9 p.m. ET start was possible; if played, the rain-soaked conditions could have rivalled the wet and wild match Canada played in Honduras in the summer of 2022. But more rain then struck, leaving the match in further limbo while the players kept themselves loose in the hallway.

The contingency question was serious because of what was at stake. With no word inside an hour of the possible kick time, the concern was that officials were hashing out alternate plans: it was possible to cancel the first leg and simply play the second leg in Canada, but the teams would have to agree, and it would put Jamaica at a serious disadvantage in a matchup with Copa America qualification on the line.

At 8:25 p.m. ET the game was postponed until the next day, with journalists reporting a 10:30 a.m. ET / 7:30 a.m. PT restart, and at 8:35 p.m. ET Canada Soccer made it official — Concacaf had rescheduled the match due to adverse weather. Nothing was announced about what would happen if the pitch remained waterlogged, meaning either the federations had not sorted that out or were keeping it in-house rather than publicising plans that might never be needed. The following morning conditions were more promising, and the Jamaica Football Federation reminded fans that kickoff was at 10:30 a.m., that all patrons who bought tickets would be allowed to enter the Grandstand only, and that those with scanned tickets would be given appropriate credit to enter.
