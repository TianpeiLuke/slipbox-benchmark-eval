# Surveillance Camera Saturation In Occupied East Jerusalem

In the occupied Palestinian territories there are cameras everywhere, and in Silwan, in occupied East Jerusalem, residents say cameras were installed by Israeli police up and down their streets, peering into their homes.

One resident named Sara described the effect on domestic life: she and her family "could be detected as if the cameras were just in our house … we couldn't feel at home in our own house and had to be fully dressed all the time."

Surveillance cameras also cover the Damascus Gate, the main entrance into the old city of Jerusalem and one of the only public areas where Palestinians can gather socially and hold demonstrations. It is at that gate that "Palestinians are being watched and assessed at all times," according to the Amnesty International report Automated Apartheid. Amnesty investigators say these cameras have created a chilling effect not only on the ability to protest but also on the daily lives of Palestinians living under occupation; the organization had previously concluded that Israel has established a system of apartheid against Palestinians.
