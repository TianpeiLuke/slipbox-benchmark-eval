# Livestreaming

## Definition

In the corpus, livestreaming is used both as a distinct product category and as a feature bolted onto other apps: Twitch is covered as a dedicated livestreaming platform whose creator monetization is a recurring news topic (doc_0401, doc_0551), while Facebook, Instagram, and the Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses treat livestreaming as one feature among several rather than the product itself (doc_0335, doc_0364).

The corpus does not otherwise supply a formal definition of the term; see Background (external) for a general definition.

## Context

In the corpus, livestreaming shows up both as a distinct product category — Twitch positions itself as a livestreaming platform whose creator economy, subscription tiers, and monetization policy fights with streamers define much of its news coverage — and as a feature that other products bolt on: the Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses let a wearer livestream directly to Facebook or Instagram, and Blizzard held a livestream ahead of Diablo 4's Season of Blood to reveal quality-of-life changes. Meta explicitly named its Facebook and Instagram livestreaming functions as a moderation priority during the Israel-Hamas war, treating the feature as a specific risk surface distinct from ordinary posts. TwitchCon 2023's announcement that Twitch now permits simulcasting — streaming to Twitch and rival livestreaming platforms at the same time — shows livestreaming platforms directly competing for the same broadcasting creators, with Kick named as a competitor.

## Key Characteristics

- **Real-time and continuous** — unlike a VOD or a pre-recorded clip, a livestream is watched as it happens, which is why Meta describes "watching for" hostage footage in real time rather than reviewing it after upload.
- **Platform-gated by design** — the Ray-Ban Meta glasses can livestream, but only to Meta's own Facebook and Instagram, not to Twitch or TikTok, illustrating how a livestreaming feature can be walled off to a single ecosystem.
- **A moderation-risk surface** — Meta prioritized "checks on livestreaming functions" specifically, separate from its general Violence and Incitement policy, because live broadcast can surface real-time abuse (e.g., hostage footage) before moderators can intervene.
- **Multi-platform / simulcastable** — Twitch's 2023 policy change to allow simulcasting acknowledges that streamers livestream to more than one platform at once, and that livestreaming platforms compete directly for the same creator base (Twitch vs. Kick).
- **Supported by dedicated hardware** — accessories like Elgato's Stream Deck exist specifically to give livestreamers one-button macros and shortcuts during a livestream.

## Background (external)

Outside this corpus, live streaming is generally defined as the real-time (or near real-time) transmission of video or audio over the internet, distinguished from video-on-demand or pre-recorded clips by its live, ongoing nature, and spans uses such as social media, video gaming, sports coverage, and lifecasting on platforms including Facebook Live, YouTube, Twitch, and Kick (Wikipedia, "Live streaming," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Live_streaming).
