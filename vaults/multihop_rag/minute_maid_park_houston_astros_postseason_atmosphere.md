# Minute Maid Park: Houston Astros Postseason Atmosphere

Minute Maid Park ranked No. 5 among the eight remaining 2023 playoff teams' home-field environments in The New York Times' 8 October 2023 ranking, held down by the fact that the Astros have not responded to their own crowd. The park opened in 2000, has a capacity of 41,168 and drew an average attendance of 37,683 in 2023.

Its memorable postseason moment, chosen over Albert Pujols off Brad Lidge or Jorge Soler to the moon, is Chris Burke ending what was then the longest game in MLB postseason history in both time (5 hours, 50 minutes) and innings (18) with a walk-off home run with one out in the 18th inning of the 2005 NLDS. Burke did not start the game but pinch-ran for Lance Berkman in the 10th; Roger Clemens came into the game for Houston in the 16th and pitched three scoreless to pick up the win, but it is Burke's homer that will always be remembered.

The case for a higher ranking is strong on volume: one of the writers called it "the loudest stadium I've ever attended, by at least one standard deviation." The Astros sold out the building 23 times that year and drew 3 million fans for the first time since 2007; the park is big and loud, and visiting fans show up fully exhausted from walking in the south Texas sun. But in all those sellouts the Astros went 6-17, and despite the noise they were 39-42 at home. Before their final homestand the team added some green paint to the batter's eye in response to player complaints, and the Astros responded by going 1-5, with three of those losses coming against the Royals. So the ballpark is packed tighter than toes in a cowboy boot and gets louder than a Pantera concert in a concrete basement, but these Astros just haven't responded to it — raising the question of whether a visiting team should really be all that intimidated.
