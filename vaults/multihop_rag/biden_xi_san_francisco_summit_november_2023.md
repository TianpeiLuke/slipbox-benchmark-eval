# The Biden-Xi San Francisco Summit, November 2023

After another rocky year of U.S.-China tensions, the two countries' presidents were set to meet in person that week for the second time since Joe Biden took office, at a summit on the Wednesday local time in San Francisco alongside the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation meeting. CNBC, reporting from Beijing on 13 November 2023, called it a rare summit before the U.S. presidential election cycle kicks off in earnest, noting that taking a tough stance on China, the second-largest economy in the world, has become one of the few areas of bipartisan agreement and that Biden plans to run for reelection. Michael Hirson, head of China Research at 22V Research, said "the focus will be on expanding dialogue in order to low[er] tail risks in the relationship and prevent a crisis that neither leader is looking for", that flashpoints such as Taiwan and the South China Sea need to be managed carefully, and that for that reason the meeting is still important, especially ahead of a politically charged 2024 that will begin with an important presidential election in Taiwan in January and end with the U.S. presidential election. U.S.-China tensions have escalated over the last several years, beginning with tariffs under the Trump administration and spilling over into broader tech restrictions under the Biden administration.

Goodwill efforts increased in the weeks leading up to the summit: more direct flights between the U.S. and China are resuming from a low base; Chinese commodity importers in October signed the first agreements since 2017 to buy U.S. agricultural products in bulk, according to a release from the U.S. embassy in Beijing; and China's Ministry of Commerce announced the previous week that it was gathering information in an effort to address unequal treatment of foreign businesses in China versus domestic ones, a longstanding business complaint. On the cultural front, however, the three remaining giant pandas in the U.S. on loan from Beijing returned to China the previous week due to an expiring contract, pandas having been lent by China to countries around the world as a diplomatic tool.
