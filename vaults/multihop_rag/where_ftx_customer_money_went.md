# Where FTX Customer Money Went

Expert witness Peter Easton, a Notre Dame accounting professor who had also assisted in investigating Enron and WorldCom, traced the funds behind the $11.3 billion liability that Ellison, Singh, Wang, and Bankman-Fried had known about in June 2022, and presented the breakdown to the jury as a pie chart (the prosecution's exhibit 1045). Of that money, $1.2 billion went to repaying crypto lender Genesis; a substantial chunk went into investments including the celebrity-connected venture firm K5, the trading firm Modulo — co-founded by one of Bankman-Fried's ex-girlfriends — and the AI company Anthropic.

The chart also traced money to Bankman-Fried personally and to those around him: another $413 million went directly to Bankman-Fried through his wholly-owned company Paper Bird; "outflows to insiders," which suggested Singh's not-quite-loans were included, came to $192 million; real estate accounted for another $228 million; and the donations to Gabe Bankman-Fried's Guarding Against Pandemics, combined with the philanthropy from the FTX Foundation, totaled less than $40 million.

An earlier and separate use of customer money came in the summer of 2021, when Bankman-Fried bought back FTX shares from Binance, an early investor. Ellison testified that he told her it was "really important," otherwise "Binance would do things to mess with FTX"; she told him Alameda did not have the money, so Bankman-Fried took $1 billion of FTX customer funds to buy Binance out. It was the first time Ellison recalled an amount that large, and she said it was Bankman-Fried's decision, as he was CEO of FTX.
