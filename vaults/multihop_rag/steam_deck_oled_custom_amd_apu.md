# Steam Deck OLED's Custom AMD APU

The Steam Deck OLED has a die-shrunk version of the custom AMD APU found in the original Steam Deck — same CPU and GPU, just more efficient, produced on a 6nm process rather than the older 7nm process. Manufacturers typically use die-shrinks to either improve performance or efficiency, and Valve has chosen the latter; the max frequency of both the GPU and CPU is identical between the LCD and OLED models.

This is the process Sony takes advantage of to build the PS4 Slim and PS5 Slim, but instead of collapsing the Steam Deck around smaller hardware, Valve chose to add a bigger battery and larger fan to its existing chassis. For developers, this means the device has the same performance targets as the original and there's no need to create games specifically for the new hardware — a single performance target to tune for. In the end, the device weighs about 30 grams less than the original model, or the equivalent of five quarters.
