# Independent Research And Reviews Of ChatGPT's Flaws

Independent researchers and advocacy groups repeatedly found ChatGPT and GPT-4 to be flawed in ways OpenAI's own launches did not resolve — on child safety, on toxicity, and on the vision-enabled multimodal model.

An independent review from Common Sense Media, a nonprofit advocacy group, found that ChatGPT could potentially be harmful for younger users, flagging it as potentially unsafe for kids; ChatGPT got an overall three-star rating in the report, with its lowest ratings relating to transparency, privacy, trust and safety. On multimodality, OpenAI announced that GPT-4 with vision would become available alongside the upcoming launch of the GPT-4 Turbo API, but some researchers found that the model remained flawed in several significant and problematic ways.

Two toxicity findings stand out. A Microsoft-affiliated scientific paper examined the "trustworthiness" — and toxicity — of LLMs including GPT-4; because GPT-4 is more likely to follow the instructions of "jailbreaking" prompts, the co-authors claim it can be more easily prompted than other LLMs to spout toxic, biased text. Separately, a study co-authored by scientists at the Allen Institute for AI showed that assigning ChatGPT a "persona" through the ChatGPT API — for example "a bad person," "a horrible person" or "a nasty person" — increases its toxicity sixfold; the co-authors also found that having the chatbot pose as certain historical figures, gendered people and members of political parties increased its toxicity, with journalists, men and Republicans in particular causing the model to say more offensive things than it normally would. That research was conducted using the latest version, but not the model then in preview based on GPT-4.
