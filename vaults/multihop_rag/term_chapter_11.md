# Chapter 11 (Bankruptcy)

## Definition

Chapter 11 is the U.S. bankruptcy filing that insolvent companies in the corpus use to seek protection from creditors, and it does not guarantee a company survives: some filers are restructured under new leadership, while others wind down anyway once inside it.

## Context

In the corpus, Chapter 11 is the recurring endpoint for companies whose finances collapsed in 2022–2023. FTX and its trading-firm affiliate Alameda Research filed for Chapter 11 in the U.S. in mid-November 2022, after a published Alameda balance sheet exposed a huge FTT-token shortfall and a Binance-triggered bank run wiped out FTX's liquidity; the filing coincided with Sam Bankman-Fried's resignation as CEO and the appointment of Enron turnaround veteran John J. Ray III in his place. The same collapse pulled other crypto firms into Chapter 11: BlockFi filed in November 2022 and Genesis Global Trading followed in January, both noted by TechCrunch as part of the wider fallout from FTX's collapse. Outside crypto, WeWork filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy protection in November 2023, listing over $18.6 billion of debt; TechCrunch's Week in Review opened its coverage of the filing with "as predicted," without stating when or on what basis it had made that prediction. SmileDirectClub took the same route but did not come back from it: the company shut down three months after its Chapter 11 filing, citing $900 million of debt — showing that a Chapter 11 filing in this corpus can precede either continued operation under new leadership (FTX) or an eventual full shutdown (SmileDirectClub).

## Key Characteristics

- **Used by companies of very different scale and sector** — from a $32-billion crypto exchange (FTX) to a flexible-office-space firm carrying $18.6 billion of debt (WeWork) to a smaller consumer startup (SmileDirectClub).
- **Often accompanies a leadership change** — FTX's filing came with Bankman-Fried's resignation and Ray's appointment as the new CEO to run the estate.
- **Does not guarantee the company continues operating** — SmileDirectClub shut down three months after filing, despite having gone through Chapter 11.
- **Can spread across a sector after one high-profile filing** — BlockFi's and Genesis Global Trading's Chapter 11 filings followed FTX's within months, both noted by TechCrunch as part of the wider fallout from FTX's collapse.
- **Distinct from Chapter 7**, the other U.S. bankruptcy chapter appearing in the same corpus's 2023 tech-layoffs list, which cybersecurity startup IronNet filed as it ceased all business activities.

## Background (external)

The corpus repeatedly names "Chapter 11" as the filing route without stating what the chapter itself legally entails. Chapter 11, formally Chapter 11 of Title 11 of the U.S. Code, is the reorganization bankruptcy: it lets a debtor continue operating as a "debtor in possession" while it develops a plan to restructure its debts and pay creditors, rather than immediately liquidating its assets — which is the province of Chapter 7 instead (United States Courts, *Chapter 11 - Bankruptcy Basics*, accessed 2026-09-02).
