# Bolt's SEC Probe Closed Without An Enforcement Recommendation

Bolt Financial and its co-founder Ryan Breslow were subpoenaed by the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission to investigate whether the company violated any securities laws during its 2021 fundraising, and on August 23 the SEC said — in a letter viewed by TechCrunch — that it was not recommending an enforcement action for the company. Bolt CEO Maju Kuruvilla told TechCrunch in an interview that the one-click checkout company is putting the probe behind it and is moving on.

The fundraising at issue was Bolt's $355 million Series E round, which the company was seeking in 2021 at an $11 billion valuation. The subpoenas went out the year before this October 2023 interview. The investigation ran about 15 months, but news of the probe did not become public until July of 2023; the SEC's no-enforcement letter followed shortly thereafter, on August 23.

Kuruvilla, whose company provides checkout technology to merchants, framed the outcome as a release rather than a vindication: "It obviously is a very, very involved process, but we're really happy to put that behind us," he told TechCrunch. "We look forward to focusing on the momentum for the business and how we can help the retailers, especially the big retailers who are looking to us to innovate for them, because this is a tough year for retailers." He also spoke about how, exactly, a company can put something like this behind it, what Bolt told its customers, and what comes next.
