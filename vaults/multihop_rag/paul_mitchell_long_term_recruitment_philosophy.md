# Paul Mitchell's Long-Term Recruitment Philosophy

Paul Mitchell argues that recruitment should be judged over the long term rather than by immediate returns, because young signings need time to settle and a player's prior record is better evidence than the narrative that forms around him in his first season — a case talkSPORT set out on 16 October 2023 using Heung-Min Son.

The grounds are Mitchell's own record and his stated standard. Having worked in football management for over a decade, his career has always been about the long term for his clubs, with the young signings he has made often only paying off after he has left. He frames the job as continuous improvement across the whole operation: "I want to continue to drive standards in each and every department. Psychology, sports science, nutrition, medicine, the way we travel, the way we refuel," he said, adding that "everything, every element I think we need to keep pushing to its maximum to try and be the best we possibly can."

The warrant joining evidence to claim is the Son case, which perfectly sums up his philosophy. "I took quite a lot of criticism in my first year at Tottenham for some of the decisions and Sonny was kind of at the centre of that," he explained. "Sometimes players need time, they're human beings, they need to settle, Sonny had a great track record at Hamburg and Leverkusen but there was this narrative that started to grow internally and externally that he wasn't a good finisher." He recalled telling a coach: "you're wrong, his history shows in two different clubs he's a very astute finisher off both sides." Son went on to be the 2022/23 Premier League top scorer, vindicating the judgement made against the prevailing internal and external narrative.
