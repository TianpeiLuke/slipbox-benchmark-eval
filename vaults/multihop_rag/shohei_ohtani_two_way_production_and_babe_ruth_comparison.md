# Shohei Ohtani's Two-Way Production And The Babe Ruth Comparison

In 2023 Shohei Ohtani led the American League in home runs, on-base percentage and slugging percentage — the same three categories Babe Ruth led in 1920, his first season exclusively as a hitter — while still making 23 starts with a 3.14 ERA, The New York Times reported on 9 December 2023.

Ohtani swatted 44 homers with a career-best 1.066 OPS, and did this while making starts and dealing with a torn ligament in his elbow. He demonstrated his potential as the American League Rookie of the Year in 2018, but his two-way hopes were delayed after his first elbow surgery, and it was not until 2021 that the full flower of his ability bloomed. He has won the American League MVP in two of the past three seasons; in the intervening season, he led all American League pitchers in strikeout rate while hitting 34 home runs with an .875 OPS. To create a comparison for him involves inventions that sound freakish — "It's like if Judge went out and was a 20-game winner as well," former teammate Kole Calhoun said.

On the pitching side, Ohtani is committed to being a starting pitcher, and if he cannot stay healthy enough for that role he could aid his new team as a reliever; he closed the final game of the World Baseball Classic, securing the crown for Japan by striking out Mike Trout. His four-seam fastball averaged nearly 97 mph in 2023, and the velocity figures to improve in short bursts. Even if he never pitches again, his value as a hitter is immense. Of his ambition, St. Louis Cardinals outfielder Lars Nootbaar, who played with Ohtani in the World Baseball Classic last spring, said: "My sense is that he wants to be the best ever, but I don't think he would ever publicly say that."
