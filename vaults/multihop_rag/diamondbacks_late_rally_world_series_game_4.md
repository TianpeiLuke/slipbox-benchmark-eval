# The Diamondbacks' Late Rally In World Series Game 4

Arizona scored six runs across the eighth and ninth innings of Game 4 of the 2023 World Series on Oct. 31, cutting an 11-1 deficit to 11-7 and forcing Bruce Bochy to bring in Jose Leclerc for the final out, but the Rangers still won and took a 3-1 series lead.

Texas scored first in this stretch: at 10:29 p.m. Jonah Heim blasted a home run to right field — the Rangers' first run since the third inning — to extend the lead back to 10 at 11-1. The game was briefly paused at 10:33 p.m. while the public address announcer asked fans to stop throwing paper airplanes onto the field. Arizona's eighth then began with three consecutive singles: Corbin Carroll, who had struggled in the series, singled with one out at 10:40 p.m., Moreno followed at 10:41 p.m. with the crowd still cheering loudly, and Walker delivered the third single at 10:44 p.m., forcing Cody Bradford out of the game with Chris Stratton coming in with the bases loaded and one out. Tommy Pham worked a long at-bat against Stratton ending in a sacrifice fly at 10:50 p.m. to trim the lead to nine, and at 10:52 p.m. Lourdes Gurriel Jr. launched a three-run home run to cut the Rangers' lead to six and give the crowd something to cheer about; Alek Thomas hit a bouncer at 10:54 p.m. to end the inning.

The Rangers added one more in the ninth. Corey Seager led off with a ground-rule double at 10:56 p.m.; after Mitch Garver struck out, Andrew Saalfrank came in at 10:59 p.m. to face Evan Carter and hit him at 11:02 p.m., a call upheld after Torey Lovullo asked for a review, before Josh Jung grounded into an inning-ending double play at 11:04 p.m. — leaving Arizona needing six runs in the bottom of the ninth to extend the game.

The Diamondbacks got two of them. Chris Stratton had warmed up to start the inning but Bochy made a change at 11:07 p.m. after seeing Pavin Smith enter as a pinch-hitter, so rookie Jordan Lawlar led off against Will Smith at 11:09 p.m. and drew a walk at 11:12 p.m. in what the blog called a very professional plate appearance for a player called up in September. Perdomo singled into the gap at 11:13 p.m. to send Lawlar to third, but Smith struck out Carroll at 11:17 p.m. to leave Texas an out away. Bochy brought in Leclerc for the final out despite the six-run lead at 11:18 p.m., and at 11:22 p.m. Gabriel Moreno singled in two more runs to trim the lead to 11-7. Walker popped out in foul territory at 11:24 p.m. to end it, leaving Texas one win from its first championship, with the chance to win it all as early as the next night at Chase Field.
