# Danny Elfman's Return to Live Concert Performance

What brought Danny Elfman back to live performance — and rebirthed him as a man who could share a stage with Billie Eilish and Limp Bizkit's Wes Borland — was, in Polygon's view on 2023-10-13, Henry Selick's The Nightmare Before Christmas: a Burton-produced stop-motion project that was a black sheep for Walt Disney Animation before blossoming into a cult favorite, and, based on the last few years of Elfman's life, a transformative milestone.

In 2013 the former Oingo Boingo frontman returned to the stage at the Royal Albert Hall to perform his scores from Tim Burton movies with a full orchestral backup, including select numbers from The Nightmare Before Christmas, which he sang in full. The concert was such a hit that Disney invited him to appear alongside co-stars Catherine O'Hara, who voices Sally, and Ken Page, who voices Oogie Boogie, at the Hollywood Bowl in Los Angeles for a night purely dedicated to Nightmare — and that concert was such a hit that the group has performed it regularly every October since, and would do so again that Halloween.

The hype around his various Burton and Nightmare concerts sparked the idea among Coachella programmers that Elfman could play alongside pop acts of the moment. As Elfman tells it, there was immediate and obvious hesitation on his part, since he was not a rock star or a pop star or hip-hop star, but after watching Childish Gambino and Janelle Monáe unleash pure theatrical mayhem at Coachella 2019 he saw his way in, and the then-66-year-old began immediate work on a set blending his film music, Oingo Boingo hits, and experimental songs fit for a rowdy crowd. Polygon appended a correction noting that a previous version of the story said Elfman performed at Royal Albert Hall in 2014; he performed his Music of Tim Burton concert in 2013.
