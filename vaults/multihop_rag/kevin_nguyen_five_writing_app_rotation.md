# Kevin Nguyen's Five-Writing-App Rotation

Kevin Nguyen, a deputy editor at The Verge, uses a rotation of five different writing apps — iA Writer, Google Docs, Bear, Apple Notes, and Scrivener — each assigned to a stage of the work rather than chosen for overall quality. He warned Installer he had four writing apps on his homescreen, then followed up to say it was actually five.

The assignment runs like this. iA Writer is for trying to get words down as quickly as possible. Docs is for revising and fidgeting. Bear is for thoughtful notes. Notes is for garbage notes — he really hates that app, but it also syncs the household grocery list. Scrivener is its own beast, built from the ground up for real sickos, meaning authors. They all have more weaknesses than strengths, and each feels designed for a fairly specific use case, but that is not really the point.

The model came from a Donna Tartt interview he read years ago about her writing process. He assumed a famous person of letters would have an elaborate, possibly pretentious system — at least an especially fancy leather Moleskine — but she just kept four different notebooks, cheap ones sporting different Beatles album covers, each serving a different purpose: something like "Revolver is for characters, Sgt. Pepper's is for plot."

He grants that five apps doing essentially the same thing sounds like a nightmare to some people, but for him writing and editing is a messy process, like trying to capture lightning in a bottle — or in this case, five different bottles. His phone is an iPhone 15 Pro, already lightly scratched; the wallpaper is Toshiro Mifune, standing in for his partner, who would be mortified to be posted next to a bunch of apps.
