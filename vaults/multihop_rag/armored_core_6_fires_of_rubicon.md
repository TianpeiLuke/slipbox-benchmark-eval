# Armored Core 6: Fires of Rubicon

Armored Core 6: Fires of Rubicon, developed by FromSoftware for PlayStation 4, PlayStation 5, Windows PC, Xbox One, and Xbox Series X, is ranked 10 on Polygon's best video games of 2023 list of 2023-10-31. Polygon's refrain is that nothing else feels like Armored Core — and that of the franchise's many games, none have reached the heights of this one.

The feel is the argument: the giant robot you pilot heaves with the weight of a six-story building but flies into the sky as nimble as a hummingbird, skating along the ground, rocketing into the air, and changing direction in the blink of an eye. With hands tightly gripping the controller you can unload four different weapons at once, picked out of an armory that could rival a small nation, targeting, aiming, and firing while moving faster than a fighter jet, dodging streams of missiles, arcs of gunfire, bazookas, and flames.

Critic CA writes that the levels have never had such ruined beauty, the enemies have never been as satisfying to fight, and the characters have never been more endearing, while an ingenious new game plus mode packs the story with surprises as you continue building out your arsenal. He suggests the decades-long wait for a new Armored Core is part of the reason, because the lessons FromSoftware learned creating and popularizing Dark Souls are evident: bosses put you through dastardly skill checks in classic Soulsian fashion, many towering over the landscape to make you, the pilot of a giant robot, feel small, in set-piece fights that are some of the most thrilling moments playable that year.

The gameplay lingers as much as the one-liners, and CA attributes that to customization: you decide exactly how your robot feels — how quickly its missiles get a lock, how fast its generator recharges, how far you boost when you swing your laser sword — and swapping components while scrutinizing competing weight and power requirements is an engaging puzzle all its own, making you intimately familiar with a screen-filling spreadsheet of stats because all those numbers add up to something that feels real, tactile, earned.
