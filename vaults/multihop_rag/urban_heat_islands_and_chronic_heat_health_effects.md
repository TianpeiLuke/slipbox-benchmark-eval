# Urban Heat Islands and Chronic Heat Health Effects

Urban heat islands — hot zones of a city where asphalt, high building density and infrastructure push temperatures higher than in surrounding areas — house millions of people who cannot escape the effects of relentless summer heat, and chronic exposure to that excess heat affects cardiovascular, respiratory and mental health.

The exposure pathway runs through daily life outdoors. "People who live in urban areas usually walk to do their daily errands. One of the advantages of living in a city is that things are close and you can use public transport, but that also means you have to spend more time outdoors," said Neelima Tummala, a physician and co-director of the Institute for Environmental Health at George Washington University, who was not involved in the study. In neighbourhoods without parks or large trees, where asphalt and buildings absorb and radiate summer heat, residents can find it hard to escape extreme temperatures either indoors or outdoors, and this kind of continuous exposure can be very dangerous because the body becomes unable to cool itself adequately by sweating. "Prolonged exposure to elevated night-time temperatures can affect sleep quality and mental and cardiac health," Tummala said. "You are constantly at an elevated temperature that your body has to deal with."
