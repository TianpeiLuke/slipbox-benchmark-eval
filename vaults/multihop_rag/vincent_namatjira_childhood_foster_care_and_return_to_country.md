# Vincent Namatjira's Childhood In Foster Care And Return To Country

Vincent Namatjira, born in Alice Springs in 1983, was moved to Perth with his sister and placed in foster care after their mother's death, and returned to central Australia at 18 with little connection to his mother's home beyond his surname, The Guardian reported on 16 October 2023.

His first art prize came while he was living in Perth as a teenage ward of the state: a school excursion brought him face-to-sail with a replica of the HMS Endeavour, the vessel that fatefully delivered James Cook to Dharawal country in 1770, and after his teacher announced a prize for whoever drew the ship the young Vincent got sketching. "Yeah, I won the prize," he tells Guardian Australia with a grin, decades later. "That's the first time." Back in the 1990s the teenage Vincent did not fully appreciate how art, Cook and those colonial legacies would shape his life; displaced from his Arrernte family, culture and country at a young age, he did not even know the significance of his last name. "At that time, I didn't know that I was related to this well-known artist," he says. "And I never met the guy."

That gap between white and black Australia also influenced his life. Of landing in Alice Springs at 18 he recalls: "Getting off that flight, you see a lot of people coming. A lot of old ladies, a lot of young people, everybody crying. They didn't actually know me, I didn't actually know them. It was from mentioning my [mother's] name, Jillian Namatjira."
