# Small Pathogen Lung Penetration Mechanism in Dogs

Jandrey's proposed mechanism runs from pathogen size to disease severity in three linked stages: smaller pathogens are likely to have an easier time making it past a dog's defenses in the upper respiratory tract; getting past those defenses lets them get down into the lungs; and "if it goes into the lungs, there's a risk of pneumonia."

The size of the organism is thus the upstream variable, the upper respiratory tract is the barrier it relates to, and pneumonia risk is the downstream outcome. Each link is stated as a likelihood or a risk rather than a certainty.

The model holds only under a stated precondition: it applies if it turns out that the New Hampshire researchers have found the right microbe. On that condition, Jandrey said, the mechanism may explain why some dogs are getting very sick. If the New Hampshire identification is wrong, the explanation does not attach to this outbreak, and the model says nothing about which dogs progress to pneumonia and which do not.
