# Why A Smaller Pathogen Reaches The Lungs

Smaller pathogens are likely to have an easier time making it past a dog's defenses in the upper respiratory tract and getting down into the lungs, said Jandrey — and "if it goes into the lungs, there's a risk of pneumonia," he added. The model is a two-step chain: small size defeats upper-airway defenses, arrival in the lungs raises the risk of pneumonia.

The model's explanatory target is severity. If it turns out that the New Hampshire researchers have found the right microbe, that may explain why some dogs are getting very sick, Jandrey said. Size is thus offered as the link between the identity of the organism and the seriousness of the disease it produces.

The range over which the model holds is explicitly conditional. It applies only if the New Hampshire researchers have in fact found the right microbe; Jandrey frames the explanation as contingent on that identification holding up. The relations are stated as likelihoods and risks — an easier time getting past defenses, a risk of pneumonia — not as certainties, so the model predicts a raised probability of severe outcomes rather than a guaranteed one.
