# Clean: The Imogen Heap Collaboration That Closed 1989

Clean, co-written with indie-pop auteur Imogen Heap, was 1989's original album closer and is very much the palette cleanser its title suggests. For once the cavernous 1980s drums are swapped for the plucked keys of a Zimbabwean mbira, while a weary Taylor Swift emerges from the ruins of a relationship.

The song prompts one of her best lyrics, establishing drought as a metaphor for emotional desolation ("the flowers that we'd grown together died of thirst") before she is reborn in the cleansing waters of a rainstorm: "When I was drowning, that's when I could finally breathe / And by morning, gone was any trace of you, I think I am finally clean."

Its underlying idea is the necessity of pain, she told Popcrush: "For most people that I've known who've fought through struggle, a lot of them who have really just faced the pain head on have come out OK a lot faster than the ones who just pretended to be in denial of it."
