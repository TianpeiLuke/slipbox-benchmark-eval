# The Scale Of Israel's Technology Sector In 2022

Technology is, without question, a giant part of Israel's economy: in 2022 it contributed more than 18% of the country's GDP, the most of any single sector, according to the latest annual report from the Israel Innovation Authority at the time of writing in October 2023. A total of 14% of Israeli citizens work in high tech, which in a country of just 9.2 million people works out to about 1.3 million.

The startup base is correspondingly large. The active number of startups in the country is 9,000, third-highest in the world after the Bay Area and NYC, and over the last five years those startups collectively brought $95 billion into the country by way of venture capital. Combining the business development of startups with the larger tech companies operating in Israel — Intel, Microsoft, Nvidia, Google and many others have operations there — they collectively exported $71 billion in the prior year, 48.3% of the total amount exported across all industries.

These figures are the pre-war baseline, and the article's own caveat is that all of them are going to be significantly lower this year, not least because the disruptions of the war are coming directly on the heels of a major protest movement.

The context for that qualification is that the world was still coming to terms with Hamas' deadly attacks on Israelis the previous weekend, and with everything that had unfolded in the aftermath, including the barrage of retaliation strikes on Gaza. TechCrunch was explicit that the ramifications for the technology ecosystem pale in comparison to the human toll the fighting is taking on both sides, and that the industry is worth considering only because it bears on how the wider region will weather the destruction and one day recover. In such a small country, just about everyone knows someone who was directly affected by the attacks, or someone involved in the subsequent defense and retaliation — and often all three.
