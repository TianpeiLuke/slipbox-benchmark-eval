# Kyncl Rebuttal: Labels Persist Like Ad Agencies After AdWords

Robert Kyncl's counterpoint at Code Media attacks the inference that easy direct distribution makes labels redundant, not the fact of that distribution. His parallel is the advertising industry a decade or two ago: at that time many predicted ad agencies were headed for the scrap-heap because Google's AdWords let any business set up a customised ad campaign on Google services. Yet ad agencies did not go extinct — today, Kyncl told the Code Conference audience, "they're the largest customers of companies like Google."

The mechanism he offers explains why the prediction failed. "In the year 2000, when AdWords came out, everybody was predicting their deaths because all the brands [could] just go direct to AdWords and buy ads… However, what happened was lots of different platforms emerged. Lots of ad tech emerged, complexity increased exponentially, and brands needed help with that, and the agencies provided that value." He sees a very close analogy with music: "Music is incredibly broadly distributed. Everybody on earth listens to music. We're on every platform. And the complexity is high. And the more people can upload content, and the more people can be heard, the greater the noise, which means it's harder to cut through the noise and sustain a career." Open access therefore raises, rather than removes, the value of the intermediary.

Kyncl also widened the label's job beyond breaking an artist. A label's value is not just about building an artist's career but about sustaining it once established: "When they become established, it is also hard to remain at the top. So their goals change. And again, we're there to help with that." He added later that a music artist "need[s] a team. You need an army behind you…. if you want a sustainable career, with repeatability and success." Music Business Worldwide framed this as a rational, level-headed explanation that was itself surprising coming from a techie like Kyncl.
