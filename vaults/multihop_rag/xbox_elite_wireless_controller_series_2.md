# Xbox Elite Wireless Controller Series 2

The Xbox Elite Wireless Controller Series 2 is Microsoft's fancied-up, premium version of the stock controller, and easily one of The Verge's favorite controllers. Specifications: Xbox wireless, Bluetooth and wired connectivity; USB-C connector; up to four mappable rear buttons; software customization; built-in rechargeable power. Listed at $140, down from $180 — 22 percent off — at Walmart and Microsoft.

It is older than the current console generation, having been released a year before the Xbox Series X/S arrived, so while it sports a USB-C port and wireless support it lacks the convenient Share button found on the latest Xbox Wireless Controller. It makes up for that with exceptional build quality, extra buttons, user-swappable sticks and D-pad, and lots of customization. Where the standard controller uses an unassuming plastic build, the Elite mixes higher-quality plastic with rubberized grips and bits of metal; combined with its built-in rechargeable battery it is noticeably heavier than its cheaper brethren, and it comes with a charging dock and zip-up case with passthrough charging. The metal, dish-like D-pad is a small work of art, though sadly a far cry from the best D-pad around, and the four removable paddles on the rear sit beneath the trigger locks and charging dock pins.

The competitive features are the hair-trigger locks and rear paddle buttons: in theory the hair trigger gets shots off faster when playing shooters, and the rear paddles let you jump, crouch or reload without taking your thumb off the right stick for aiming. Using that many buttons on the back definitely takes some adjustment — you have to train new muscle memory, and since it requires dexterity with more fingers, they are not for everyone.

The premium features come at a steep cost. The Elite Series 2 is normally $179.99, and even when it drops to $139.99 it is still more than twice as expensive as a regular Xbox controller; you have to really want its extra features and user customization, because if you are not using the rear paddles or the software adjustments for stick sensitivity you are not getting your money's worth. Customizing the colors runs to $209.99 for the Design Lab "Elite Package." Microsoft also has a stripped-down Elite Series 2 "Core" version for $129.99 — the same controller in white-and-black, red-and-black, or blue-and-black without the rear paddles, accessory charging case, or extra stick toppers and D-pad; you can get all of those in a $59.99 accessories pack, but that sets you back $10 more than just going for the Elite Series 2 to start with.
