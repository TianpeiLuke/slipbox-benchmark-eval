# Hard Problems As A Startup Moat

The claim is that the harder the problem a startup is tackling, the stronger its competitive advantage and the more indispensable it becomes in its industry. Startups exist to solve complex problems, not to be a quick moneymaking scheme, and their success lies in their unique value proposition.

The warrant is replicability: simple solutions are easily replicated, risking the startup's position and success, whereas a genuinely hard problem cannot be copied cheaply. The payoffs the columnist attaches to hard problems follow from that same scarcity — working on a difficult startup problem can bring industry recognition, ease sales and marketing efforts, and open the door to charging more for a premium product. The key to a successful startup lies in its team and their ability to answer pivotal questions about the startup's purpose and challenges.

The conclusion is stated as a diagnostic rather than a guarantee: if a startup seems like an easy win, the founders are likely overlooking something. Solve something that could at first seem impossible, and you may be on the right path — if there is not a very real chance of failure, you are doing it wrong.
