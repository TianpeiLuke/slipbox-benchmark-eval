# Peter Gabriel From Genesis To Eighties Pop Ubiquity

Peter Gabriel spent much of the '70s at the forefront of prog-rock as the singer for Genesis, and then went on to personify big, shiny '80s pop, The Age noted on 30 November 2023.

In the Genesis years he created sprawling concept albums and wore a series of increasingly bizarre stage outfits "that suggested he had ingested a truckload of acid on the way to a Halloween party", and was one of the main reasons punk had to happen. The evidence of his subsequent ubiquity is specific: the most played video on MTV was Sledgehammer, his body slam of a hit from 1986; the song blaring from the boombox John Cusack held aloft under Ione Skye's window in the iconic scene from 1989's Say Anything was Gabriel's In Your Eyes; and, somewhat bizarrely, you can barely watch an old episode of Miami Vice without hearing a Gabriel song in the background.

The same column places this in a contemporary context via its other subject: with a pop landscape led by serious singer-songwriters like Taylor Swift, SZA, Olivia Rodrigo and Billie Eilish, Dua "go girl, give us nothing" Lipa, the fame-trolling Doja Cat and the introverted-to-the-point-of-obscurity PinkPantheress, the reviewer sees a vacancy for Tate McRae's particular set of popstar skills — she is a throwback in the classic singing-dancing Britney mould, and "if she's greedy enough, the platform feels like it's hers to grab". Of Gabriel the verdict is blunter: after two decades he returns "overcooked and overlong".
