# Charade (1963): Stanley Donen's Comedy Thriller

Charade is a 1963 comedy thriller directed by Stanley Donen, running 1h 54m and starring Cary Grant, Audrey Hepburn and Walter Matthau. Its premise turns on a heist that succeeded years before the film begins: Reggie (Hepburn) has been living off the profits of her husband's crime without realising it, and when he is suddenly murdered she discovers she knew nothing about him — or about the new man in her life, Peter Joshua (Grant). The remaining money is missing, a lot of terrible people believe Reggie knows where it is, and as more of them are drawn into the money's orbit it becomes less and less clear who, if anyone, she can trust.

What distinguishes the film, in Polygon's account, is the pairing of its two stars: Hepburn can move from pragmatic to seductive to fearful within a single scene with believable ease, and their chemistry is immediate and undeniable, carrying the snappy dialogue and the mixture of flirtatious comedy, captivating mystery and genuine thriller — "His Girl Friday by way of Hitchcock," in reviewer Jenna Stoeber's phrase. It is also an unusual case on the question of age gaps: Grant's initial discomfort with the 25-year gap, a still-not-uncommon chasm in Hollywood, produced script rewrites making clear that Reggie was pursuing him, leaving it one of the few movies in which the gap is acknowledged and dealt with believably rather than taken for granted.
