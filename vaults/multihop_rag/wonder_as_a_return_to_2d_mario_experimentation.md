# Wonder As A Return To 2D Mario Experimentation

Polygon argues that Super Mario Bros. Wonder shares the inventiveness of the earliest 2D Mario games and their disregard for what should and shouldn't be in a Mario game — making it, for middle-aged fans, a candidate for the first "true new Mario game" since Super Mario World in 1990. The grounds lie in a history the reviewer sets out: with each Super Mario Bros. on the NES and SNES, Nintendo's designers learned from the previous entry, cut what didn't work, made audacious additions (there was a time when adding a dinosaur named Yoshi felt like a gimmick), and gradually accumulated the pieces and tropes that would recur for the next thirty-plus years. Then, with Super Mario 64, 3D Mario games became the de facto labs for Nintendo's greatest experimentation — magical hats, water guns, galactic travel — while 2D Mario defaulted to a vessel for nostalgia, including the cheekily named New Super Mario Bros. series.

The warrant is that Wonder revives the old spirit specifically by letting the designers break their own machine. The traditional layer is intact — every level can be enjoyed as a loyal continuation of the plumber's work in the 2D platformer genre, which has kept a healthy cadence since the series debuted in 1985: dash from left to right, jump over pits, concuss enemies, collect the occasional power-up, and leap onto the flagpole that ends one level and unlocks the next. But the wonder of playing Super Mario Bros. in 1985 came from discovery, with Nintendo anticipating that players would climb outside the confines of the level and placing shortcuts in hidden crevices to reward the most rule-breaking players; badges now build on that freedom with skills that let you soar over towering enemies or use the Piranha Plant grappling hook to climb above the stage. "This is the most significant difference between 1985 and 2023," the reviewer writes: "Mario's creators have joined in the fun of breaking the machine." He imagines the game's origin as a notebook in which, since the '90s, every Nintendo developer wrote down each idea that "didn't fit the tone" or "wasn't Mario enough," until someone had the gall to say: "Let's turn this stuffed notebook of chaos into a game. Because why shouldn't Mario be an elephant? Why shouldn't Wigglers wear roller skates? And why shouldn't Luigi skydive?"
