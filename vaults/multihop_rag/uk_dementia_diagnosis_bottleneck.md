# The UK Dementia Diagnosis Bottleneck

The main problem facing doctors is the difficulty of pinpointing dementia not just in its early stages but even in its later phases: most cases are first presented to GPs, who then refer patients to memory clinics for a dementia test, but waiting times for appointments at these centres run up to two years on average.

Diagnosing Alzheimer's and other forms of dementia is usually based on pen-and-paper tests, followed by lumbar punctures and brain scans before a final diagnosis, and about 65% of cases are confirmed this way — meaning the remaining third of dementia cases are never diagnosed. Yet patients can only expect to receive treatments, including the new drugs, if their condition is identified.
