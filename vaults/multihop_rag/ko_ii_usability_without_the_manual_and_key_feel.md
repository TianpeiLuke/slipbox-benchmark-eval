# K.O. II Usability Without The Manual And Key Feel

Engadget's reviewer likes to test music gear by seeing how easy it is to use without reading the manual, and was pleasantly surprised by the K.O. II: within minutes they had figured out basic navigation and how things are organized — sample groups, accessing shift functions, what the fader does and when.

The test matters because music gear has common tasks like sequencing, timing adjustment and automation, and how you achieve these on a drum machine might be very different from a keys-based synth — and Teenage Engineering in particular likes to do things its own way. The blind test also introduced the reviewer to the K.O. II's buttons and faders. It was obvious from the launch materials that these are not rubber MPC-esque pads; the reviewer would describe them as keys rather than buttons. They are satisfying to click and pressure sensitive, so you can give drum hits different velocities or play notes at different strengths — with the caveat that you should focus on the lower part of the key, as that seems to be where the sensor is.
