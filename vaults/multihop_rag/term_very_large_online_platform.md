# Very Large Online Platform (VLOP)

## Definition

A Very Large Online Platform (VLOP) is a service designated by the European Commission under the EU's Digital Services Act (DSA) as carrying obligations beyond the DSA's baseline, on the grounds of its scale. Designation is the discriminating feature: the DSA's general duties apply to digital services broadly, but a platform once designated a VLOP additionally must identify and mitigate the *systemic risks* its service poses — disinformation, threats to fundamental rights, and, for algorithmic services, the societal risks attached to their recommender systems. The parallel designation for search engines is the Very Large Online Search Engine (VLOSE).

## Context

In the corpus the term appears as the tier that triggers DSA enforcement against the largest platforms. The Commission designated Meta's Facebook and Instagram as VLOPs earlier in 2023, and designated X (formerly Twitter) a VLOP in April 2023; VLOP compliance obligations took effect over the summer of 2023. The Commission is the *sole enforcer* of the DSA over VLOPs, which is the basis on which it opened its own request for information and later a formal investigation into X, and weighed in on Meta's advertising consent model. Designation is also what obliges these platforms to mitigate the disinformation risks the EU raised around upcoming elections, and it is why the Commission treats the (voluntary) Code of Practice on Disinformation as underpinning the DSA, with its stated intention to transform the Code into a Code of Conduct forming part of a co-regulatory framework for addressing disinformation risks.

## Key Characteristics

- **Designation, not self-classification** — a service becomes a VLOP by a Commission decision, and only then carries the extra duties.
- **Systemic-risk duty** — beyond acting on illegal content, a VLOP must assess and mitigate systemic risks such as disinformation, even where the underlying content (e.g. disinformation) is not itself illegal in the EU.
- **Sole-enforcer model** — the European Commission, not national regulators, enforces the DSA against VLOPs directly.
- **Penalty exposure** — confirmed DSA breaches by a VLOP can draw fines of up to 6% of global annual turnover, with service blocking possible for repeated infringement.
- **Search-engine analogue** — VLOSEs are the equivalent designation for very large search engines, subject to the same systemic-risk logic.

## Background (external)

The corpus names VLOP designation but does not state the numeric threshold that triggers it. Per the European Commission, the DSA sets the bar at more than **45 million average monthly active users in the EU** (European Commission, *DSA: Very Large Online Platforms and Search Engines*, accessed 2026-09-02), and the first batch of VLOP/VLOSE designations was issued on **25 April 2023** (European Commission, *List of designated VLOPs and VLOSEs*, accessed 2026-09-02).
