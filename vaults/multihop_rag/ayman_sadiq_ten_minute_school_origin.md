# Ayman Sadiq And The Origin Of 10 Minute School

Co-founder Ayman Sadiq started 10MS as a YouTube channel in 2015, after teaching since 2012 and working as a teacher at a leading coaching center in Dhaka. During that period he observed that many of his students faced two obstacles: the cost of tuition and the cost of traveling to Dhaka from hometowns that had no coaching centers of their own.

"That's when the naive idea came into being that I could record all the videos and upload them onto YouTube for free, so no one needed to come to the capital city and no one needed to pay anything," Sadiq says. He was also heavily inspired by online learning platforms like Khan Academy and edX, and after reaching a following of 100,000 people on YouTube he said he realized the idea was scalable.

Between 2015 and 2019 the YouTube channel took on sponsors, including telecom operators, electronics companies and FMCG brands, and produced more than 25,000 videos covering the entire K12 syllabus. Then the pandemic hit and sponsors began dropping out, and Sadiq realized that making the project sustainable required a paid version, so 10MS produced its first premium product: a paid book and course. "Interestingly, that paid book and course individually had more than 100,000 paid users," he said. "So that instantly gave us an idea of, okay, we can actually monetize some of our offerings and solutions and make a sustainable business out of it." The fledgling company then started looking for investors and, in 2022, raised $2 million in seed funding led by Surge, Sequoia India's (now Peak XV) scale-up program.
