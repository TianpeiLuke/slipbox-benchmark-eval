# Chinese Capital Stigma In US Fundraising

A founder's Chinese links now operate as a screening filter in US fundraising, and the filter's scope keeps widening. Even having the Chinese branches of famed American VCs listed on the cap table might deter U.S. investors from funding Chinese founders in their backyard, three founders said; local investors are now shunning Chinese "links," of which the definition is ever evolving and broadening, at all costs to avoid geopolitical risks.

The reputational asymmetry compounds it. At home, the Western-educated and -trained Chinese entrepreneurs are darlings of local VCs; in Silicon Valley, they are little known to investors. Occasional media reports that stress their Chinese background further erode trust in potential investors and customers at a time when concerns about national security already run high.

A San Francisco-based Chinese founder set out the conditions under which the filter can be passed: "If you speak like a local, know how to pitch like a confident Silicon Valley founder, have not taken any money from Chinese VCs, have all your staff in the U.S., have generated good traction in the local market, and are working on getting a green card, you might get a chance to raise local money. Don't even think about it if you still run your R&D out of China."
