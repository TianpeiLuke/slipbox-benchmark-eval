# ChatGPT Overview And Significance

ChatGPT is OpenAI's text-generating AI chatbot, and it has taken the world by storm: what started as a tool to hyper-charge productivity through writing essays and code from short text prompts has evolved into a behemoth used by more than 92% of Fortune 500 companies for far more wide-ranging needs.

That growth propelled OpenAI itself into becoming one of the most-hyped companies in recent memory, even though CEO and co-founder Sam Altman's firing and swift return raised concerns about the company's direction and opened the door for competitors. The fallout was still settling, but it might empower competitors such as Meta and its LLaMA family of large language models, or help other AI startups get attention and funding as the industry watched OpenAI implode and put itself back together. Whatever the nefarious side of ChatGPT, it was clear that AI tools were not going away anytime soon: since its initial launch nearly a year earlier, ChatGPT had hit 100 million weekly active users, and OpenAI was investing heavily in it.

The capability boundary at the time was defined by model access and modality. GPT-4, which can write more naturally and fluently than previous models, remained largely exclusive to paying ChatGPT users, although it could be accessed for free through Microsoft's Bing Chat in the Microsoft Edge, Google Chrome and Safari web browsers. Beyond GPT-4 and the OpenAI DevDay announcements, OpenAI had recently connected ChatGPT to the internet for all users, and with the integration of DALL-E 3, users could generate both text and images right inside ChatGPT.
