# Enterprise Caution On Generative AI Adoption

A July 2023 Morgan Stanley survey of large company CIOs found that many were proceeding cautiously with generative AI: 56% of respondents reported that generative AI was having an impact on their investment priorities, but only 4% had actually launched significant projects, with most still in the evaluation or proof of concept phase. TechCrunch reported on 15 December 2023 that although this is a fast moving area, the survey fits what it was hearing in its own conversations with CIOs.

The gap between vendor hype and buyer behaviour is the context. To hear the hype from vendors, you would think enterprise buyers are all in on generative AI, but like any newer technology, large companies tend to move cautiously; throughout the year, as vendors feverishly announced new generative AI-fueled products, CIOs took note. Some companies have actually been looking to cut back on spending, or at least stay even, rather than looking for new ways to spend money, with the big exception being when technology enables companies to operate more efficiently and do more with less.

Generative AI certainly has the potential to do that, but it also carries its own costs — whether a higher cost for these features in a SaaS product or the price of hitting a large language model API when building software internally — so it is important for the people implementing the technology to understand whether they are getting a return on their investment.
