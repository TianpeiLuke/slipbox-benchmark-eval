# Fraud

## Definition

Fraud, in the corpus's usage, is the criminal or civil charge of knowingly deceiving others — misrepresenting facts, misappropriating funds, or overstating value — for financial gain, and it recurs across the corpus as the central charge in several 2023 prosecutions rather than a single incident.

## Context

The clearest instance is Sam Bankman-Fried's federal trial: he faced seven counts of fraud and conspiracy for his role at collapsed crypto exchange FTX, including wire fraud and conspiracy to commit money laundering and to misuse customer funds, and was ultimately found guilty on all seven counts including wire fraud.
The Securities and Exchange Commission's civil allegation sharpened the charge's scope, framing FTX as a fraud "from the start" with a "multi-billion-dollar deficiency caused by [Bankman-Fried's] own misappropriation of customer funds," and U.S. Attorney Damian Williams called the alleged scheme "one of the biggest financial frauds in American history."
Fraud also appears outside crypto: Judge Arthur Engoron ruled in a civil suit brought by New York Attorney General Letitia James that Donald Trump "defrauded banks with 'fantasy'" by deceiving banks and insurers through years of overvaluing his assets and exaggerating his net worth to secure financing — a civil, not criminal, fraud finding that led to rescinded business licenses and continued independent monitoring rather than jail exposure.
A related but distinct offense sits alongside these cases: Binance and CEO Changpeng Zhao pleaded guilty not to fraud but to anti-money-laundering and sanctions violations, paying $4.3 billion to resolve the charges — the corpus treats this as adjacent misconduct in the same 2023 wave of crypto-industry legal reckonings, but the DOJ's own framing (failing to maintain an anti-money-laundering program, not deceiving victims for gain) keeps it conceptually separate from fraud proper.

## Key Characteristics

- **Deception for gain** — the common thread across cases is knowingly misrepresenting facts (asset values, fund safety, company solvency) to obtain money or credit, as in Bankman-Fried's alleged misappropriation of FTX customer funds and Trump's overvalued asset filings.
- **Criminal and civil tracks both exist** — Bankman-Fried faced criminal wire-fraud and conspiracy counts with prison exposure, while Trump faced a civil fraud finding from Engoron with business-related penalties, not incarceration risk.
- **Often bundled with conspiracy or related charges** — Bankman-Fried's indictment paired substantive fraud counts with conspiracy counts (to commit money laundering, to misuse customer funds) rather than resting on fraud alone.
- **Distinct from anti-money-laundering violations** — Binance/Zhao's guilty plea was for AML program failures and sanctions violations, not for defrauding customers, illustrating that not all crypto-industry 2023 prosecutions in the corpus were fraud cases even though they are often discussed together.
