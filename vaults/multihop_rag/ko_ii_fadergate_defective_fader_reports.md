# K.O. II "Fadergate" Defective Fader Reports

Within days of the K.O. II's reveal, several users were complaining that their units had defective faders, and enough people were affected that the problem quickly became known as "fadergate."

The rollout was fast: barely 24 hours after the K.O. II was revealed, units started landing in buyers' hands, and YouTube was soon awash with first-look videos and tutorials — the complaints arrived before Engadget's own review unit did. One brave creator took their unit apart and possibly discovered the cause: the internal pins were bent and not making a connection. Engadget said it asked Teenage Engineering about the issue and would update the story once it heard back. Some buyers theorised the cause lay in assembly by the user: the K.O. II ships without the caps on its rotaries and fader, which come loose in the box so the packaging can be flatter, but the cap for the fader is unusually tight fitting — leading to speculation that the enthusiasm required to push the cap down might put too much pressure on components inside, raising the cruel possibility of users breaking their own devices before they got to play with them. The reviewer used extra caution and needle-nosed tweezers to support the fader while applying its cap, and reported: so far, so good.
