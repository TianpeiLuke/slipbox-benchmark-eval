# The Apple-Masimo Pulse Oximetry Patent Dispute History

The import ban is the result of a long-standing patent dispute between Apple and medical device maker Masimo, which is known for its pulse oximetry tech — generally referred to as SpO2 or blood oxygen saturation in the wearable world — and which claims Apple is using that patented technology without permission.

The story started about 10 years ago when Apple reached out to Masimo about a potential partnership around blood oxygen features on its wearables; soon after, Apple reportedly poached several Masimo engineers and its chief medical officer. In fall 2020 Apple released the Apple Watch Series 6, its first Apple Watch to feature an SpO2 sensor measuring blood oxygen saturation levels, and those sensors are at the heart of the dispute. In 2020 Masimo filed a lawsuit in the US District Court in the Central District of California accusing Apple of stealing trade secrets and infringing on 10 of its patents; when that case dragged on, Masimo filed a separate case with the ITC in 2021. Apple then sued Masimo back last year, claiming the company made an Apple Watch clone with its Masimo W1 Medical Watch. The decisive step is that the ITC sided with Masimo: in January 2023 it ruled that Apple Watches did infringe Masimo patents, and in October it issued an import ban along with an order to stop selling already-imported infringing products. As of The Verge's December 19th piece, the case was nearing the end of a 60-day presidential review period in which President Joe Biden or the US Trade Representative could veto the ban; absent a veto by the end of the period, the ban takes effect. The photo of the SpO2 sensors accompanying the article is credited to Amelia Holowaty Krales / The Verge.
