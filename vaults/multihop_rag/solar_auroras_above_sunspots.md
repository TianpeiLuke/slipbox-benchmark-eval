# Solar Auroras Above Sunspots

A study from November 2023 revealed that the sun itself has auroras — a phenomenon previously thought to occur only on some planets and moons within the solar system — although they are a little different from the ones seen on Earth.

Astronomers detected radio bursts crackling above a sunspot after pointing a radio telescope at the dark patch, and the frequencies of the radio waves were very similar to the wavelengths given off by auroras on Earth, which strongly suggests a similar process is occurring on the sun. The mechanisms differ, however. On Earth, auroras are born when solar storms and solar wind bash into the planet and temporarily weaken its magnetic shield, allowing radiation from the sun to excite gas molecules in the upper atmosphere. Researchers think solar auroras are instead created by electrons that are accelerated to incredibly high speeds along the sun's magnetic field lines.
