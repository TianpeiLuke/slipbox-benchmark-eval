# Marcus Urann, The "Cranberry King"

Marcus Libby Urann was the "Cranberry King" — a bookish boy from Maine who became an influential college scholar and a Boston attorney before giving up the bar for the bogs in 1906, FOX News Lifestyle reported on 23 November 2023.

He was born on 3 October 1873 to Marcus M. and Chestina Urann in Sullivan, Maine, a tiny Downeast town on Bar Harbor, and described himself in one letter as a "bookworm". He attended the University of Maine, where he became an undergraduate scholar of great renown and great vision, and became an attorney in Boston after graduation; in 1906 he decided to give that up to buy cranberry bogs in and around Cape Cod and Plymouth. He died on 1 April 1963 in Hanson, Massachusetts, where he rests in a family plot in Fernhill Cemetery. "I do believe that anyone can do anything that he really desires to accomplish," Urann wrote as a young man in 1924, explaining the history of Phi Kappa Phi, and he lived by that example throughout his life.

The defining summary is the one given in The Boston Globe's obituary of 4 April, which called him "a recognized giant of the cranberry industry" and dubbed him the "Cranberry King": "He bought his first cranberry bog on [Cape Cod] in 1906. During the next 30 years, he built his bog holding into a complex throughout southeastern Massachusetts." The report of his death added that "he formed a cooperative which eventually dominated the industry, and organized a canning operation which grew into the industry's single biggest buyer and distributor."
