# Nocturne's Animation Inconsistency And Late-Season Pacing

The Verge's reservations about Nocturne target two things: occasional moments where the fluid dreaminess of the show's overall art direction butts up against its application of 3D animation to make certain pivotal moments pop, and a late-season slide back toward the first show's habits.

On the animation, it can be jarring when traditionally animated characters who were just popping in and out of view using tricks of the eye suddenly turn to the camera with deeper faces accented by flat shadows that weren't there a second before. On pacing, as strong and different as the season starts, by its last two episodes Nocturne does start to feel a bit like it's rehashing moments from the first show — if not in exact form, then in how neatly it brings its plotlines to a close. The rebuttal is bounded: none of that is enough to keep the show from hitting all the right notes, and by the time you hit the finale there's a solid chance you'll already be hoping for more.
