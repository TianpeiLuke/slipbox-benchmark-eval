# Reserve Mobilisation Inside Israeli Tech Companies

Israeli tech founders told TechCrunch in October 2023 that, once they had ensured the safety of their teams, they gingerly started to approach how to resume work, with a notable share of staff called to reserve duty. Kfir ben Shooshan, founder and chairman of scooter startup Inokim, put it in national terms: "The call for reserve duty has affected a notable percentage of our workforce. I'd estimate that at least 10% of our citizens have either enlisted or volunteered for the army," adding that this "has inevitably strained many businesses."

Resuming work was not a simple formula. Omer Davidi, CEO and co-founder of the Israeli agritech startup BeeHero, said that "currently, the emphasis is on finding a balance between supporting Israeli team members, some of whom want to keep working to clear their minds a little from what's happening, and some who cannot even think of working," and that "the priority is to focus on core company operations, especially in the short term, as we await a clearer understanding of the unfolding situation."

Yonatan Cohen, co-founder and CTO of Quantum Machines, said the war is clearly affecting people but that they are returning to work: "I can't deny that we all had trouble concentrating on work during the first few days of the war." He pointed out that several of his employees had been called to active duty, while those who were not are beginning to concentrate on work again in spite of the circumstances.

The response extended beyond military service. Shuly Galili, founding partner at the venture capital firm UpWest, said the situation is complex but companies are doing their best to navigate the challenges of operating in war conditions. "People are being called up," she said, noting that some startup denizens who are in the U.S. are looking to get back and serve, and adding that "at this point many who are not called to serve are volunteering to provide equipment, medical supplies and food. Many technical developers are building tech to manage logistics and help communities deal with the aftermath of the situation."
