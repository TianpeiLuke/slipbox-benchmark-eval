# Paul Denino's Defense Of The Kick Stream

In a post days after the stream, Paul Denino disputed claims of a "hidden camera" and alleged that the interaction was legal — attacking the premise that the woman had not consented to being filmed and that the encounter broke the law.

His grounds were jurisdictional and evidentiary. Denino streamed from Brisbane, Australia, where sex work is legal in licensed brothels or if a sex worker is working in private and alone. He also posted footage of a call allegedly asking the sex worker whether he could film her, which she approved for an extra fee, and later in the clip he tells Andy that he cannot block the sex worker from leaving.

The rebuttal to that defense targets what it leaves out — consent to the hidden onlookers rather than to the camera. "Where's the mention that you two were hiding in the room? Creepy as fuck," replied Repzion, another creator who streams on Kick. "Did she consent to that? When she found that out, she left because she was uncomfortable. It's shitty regardless."
