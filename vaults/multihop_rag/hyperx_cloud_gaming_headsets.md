# HyperX Cloud Gaming Headsets

The HyperX Cloud Stinger 2, the top budget pick in Engadget's gaming headphones buying guide, was on sale for $35, down from $50 — a modest $15 listed saving that matched the lowest price seen. Two days later, after Prime Day, it was $36: a dollar more than its Prime Day price and only a modest $4 discount over its recent street price, but still close to the lowest price Engadget had seen.

Its qualities are the same in both write-ups: the Cloud Stinger 2 does not have the most balanced or detailed sound, but its V-shaped signature gives action scenes a nice level of impact, and its mic quality belies the dirt-cheap price. The design is comfortable too, though it is made from cheap-feeling plastic.

If you are willing to pay a little more, the HyperX Cloud Alpha is another decent value at $60, down from $100 — $15 or so lower than its typical street price. Engadget notes it as an honorable mention in its guide: it has been around for a few years now, but it is comfortable, its detachable mic is solid, and it is better built than the Cloud Stinger 2. At this price they do not sound bad either, though an underemphasized treble means they are not especially detailed.
