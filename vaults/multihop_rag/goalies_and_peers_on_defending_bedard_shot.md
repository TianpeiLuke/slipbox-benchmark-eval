# Goalies And Peers On Defending Bedard's Shot

For goalies, it's the angle change that gets them, according to the goaltenders and peers quoted by The New York Times on 17 November 2023.

Moose Jaw Warriors goalie Jackson Unger, whom Bedard scored on the previous year, said the toe-drag release in particular is a challenge: "He changes the angle so quickly that, as a goalie, you have to adjust to it, but when he does it so fast, it's easier said than done. It's a lot of different angles he can give you." Sabres first-round pick Zach Benson, who has played on teams with and against Bedard and skates with him in the summers, singled out the pre-shot agility: "He can move left to right like no one I've seen before." Scott Ratzlaff, Seattle Thunderbirds goalie and Buffalo Sabres prospect, said with a smile: "It's just trying to get out, face him, and hope it hits ya. He can shoot from anywhere and he's lethal from anywhere. You've just always got to be ready just in case he shoots it. And then he's got a really good toe drag release, so it's watching for that and making sure you're lined up."

Blackhawks goalie Arvid Söderblom would certainly call Bedard a shooter. He and Petr Mrazek have faced more shots from Bedard than any NHL goalies this season, and the challenge is that Bedard doesn't just shoot one way — he's unpredictable. "When you have that type of shot, quick release, moves his body and shifts angles on the puck, it makes it harder on the goalies," Söderblom said. "You just saw the two goals (on Sunday against the Florida Panthers), both were quick releases. He finds that open net. He's a pure goal scorer. It's fun to have on the team and face him every day and see him take steps, too."
