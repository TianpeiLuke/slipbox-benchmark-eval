# MXene Family Diversity and Tunability

MXenes are the largest group of flat, sheetlike nanomaterials — largely two-dimensional, like graphene — and they have "probably the largest diversity and tunability any 2-D material can offer," says Michael Naguib, meaning they can be tweaked to take on different properties.

Naguib is an engineering physicist at Tulane University in New Orleans, Louisiana, who made the world's first MXene in 2011, a titanium carbide. Scientists have now made more than 50 MXenes, with new ones arising when they mix and match different M and X atoms, and each MXene has slightly different properties, from how well it conducts electricity to how strong it is. Existing MXenes can also be modified by treatment with different chemicals, covering the MXene's surface with new atoms such as oxygen and fluorine to change its behaviour. For more than a decade, scientists have been tailoring different MXenes for use in batteries and other energy storage devices, but tweaking MXenes' chemistry can also alter their ability to collect CO2. In an October 4 paper in Chem, engineers at the University of California, Riverside reviewed the potential widespread use of MXenes, and in a statement lead author Mihri Ozkan noted, "Their unique properties make them excellent candidates for capturing CO2."
