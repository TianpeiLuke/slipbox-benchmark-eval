# Edenglassie: A Dual-Timeline Novel Of Colonial And Contemporary Brisbane

Edenglassie is Melissa Lucashenko's new novel, published the week before this 6 October 2023 profile by University of Queensland Press, and named after Brisbane's early moniker. It is a historical epic depicting the period in Brisbane's history when the former penal colony was morphing into a settler town and Aboriginal people still outnumbered white colonists; in the book the two groups coexist uneasily at the Queensland frontier, their interactions defined by mutual curiosity and suspicion, economic co-dependence and outbreaks of violence.

The novel runs on two narrative threads. In the historical part, set in the 1850s, Lucashenko tells the story of Mulanyin, a heroic young fisherman from saltwater country in the south who works with Thomas Petrie as a young man; Mulanyin falls in love with Nita, who has lived with the Petries as a servant girl-cum-ward since she was small, and dreams of buying a boat and taking his beloved home to Yugambeh country, though evading the colonial system to live a traditional life is becoming harder and more dangerous. The book uses the impending calamity of colonialism to tragic effect — the reader knows what is going to happen but the characters do not, and some of them think the British might still go back home. The second thread is set in Brisbane in 2024, where the centenarian grandmother Eddie trips on a tree root and goes to hospital, an event that brings together her feisty granddaughter Winona and young doctor Johnny, who has just discovered his Aboriginal ancestry and is playing at being a blackfella, much to Winona's contempt.

Its origin lies in a single source text. "I'd wanted to write a novel of colonial Brisbane for a very long time, since I read Tom Petrie's Reminiscences of Early Queensland," Lucashenko says. She devoured Reminiscences — a 1904 classic recording the memories of the Scottish-born explorer, gold prospector, logger and grazier who lived in the Moreton Bay penal colony, subsequently Brisbane, from 1831 to 1910 — back in the 1990s, and it stayed in her consciousness for decades. She also felt she had to be in place to write it: "I just had this vague awareness that I should be living in Brisbane to write this book, because it's very different to write a book when you're walking the streets every day and looking at the river every week, than trying to do it at a distance." Lucashenko is the only person ever to have won both the Miles Franklin Literary Award and TV's Millionaire Hot Seat.
