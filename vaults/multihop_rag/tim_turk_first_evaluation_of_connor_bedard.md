# Tim Turk's First Evaluation Of Connor Bedard

Tim Turk, a self-described "shooting and scoring coach" with more than two decades of experience working with NHL stars, national federations and half a dozen NHL teams, had no idea who Connor Bedard was when he first evaluated him at Gary Roberts' personal rink in Uxbridge, Ontario, as recounted in The New York Times on 17 November 2023.

The setup was a cold call. Agent Greg Landry of Newport Sports asked Turk what he was doing tomorrow; Turk said he was booked up a year in advance, twice, before Landry insisted "You have to see them" about two players coming in from out west, and Turk moved his schedule around. In the small locker room the redhead introduced himself as Nate and the other, standing shorter, as Connor. Turk gave them his usual spiel — an evaluation, some shooting, some fun, suggestions but not here to change anything. Nate — Nate Danielson, the Red Wings' 2023 No. 9 pick — went first, and Turk could tell he was a talented and hard-working player with a good shot and real promise.

Then came Connor's turn. Turk placed him in position and watched how he got the puck ready to shoot, only Connor didn't stickhandle and prepare — he just took the puck and shot it right away. After the same number of shots as Nate, Turk spoke for the first time: "Timeout one second, just give me a second here, I'm just trying to see what you're doing so I'm going to have you take an extra few shots so that I can analyze where the puck's starting and where it's finishing." Another round later he knew this Connor kid, whose last name he still didn't know and who was then 16, was very different even from his high-end peers, simply by his proficiency shooting from a "frontal position." In the motion drills that followed, every time Turk introduced a new formation and shot type, Bedard's application was "simplistic, and he made it look easy." Both kids thanked "coach Turky" and Turk drove home. The next day Landry called again asking about Thursday because the boys wanted him back; "Well who the f— are these guys?" Turk finally asked. "Turky, it's Connor Bedard," Landry said. "Who's Connor Bedard?" Turk asked. After that second session the same week, Turk didn't work with Bedard again until a stick manufacturer tried to pitch him and Bedard asked if Turk could be there for the test-drive — by then Turk knew the name, and came away with another takeaway, picking up on Bedard's eye contact and ability to change his mind mid-shot.
