# The EU's Marketplace Foreclosure Theory Of Harm In Amazon-iRobot

The EU's theory of harm has three linked parts: ability, incentive, and effect. Its concerns focus on whether the deal will give Amazon the ability and incentive to foreclose iRobot's rivals by deploying strategies aimed at preventing competitors from selling robot vacuum cleaners (RVCs) on Amazon's marketplace and/or making it harder for them to do so.

On ability, the levers the Commission enumerates are: delisting rival products; reducing their visibility in both non-paid (organic) and paid results (ads) displayed on its marketplace; limiting their access to certain widgets it operates, such as the "other products you may like" feature, or to "certain commercially-attractive product labels" such as "Amazon's choice" or "Works With Alexa"; and/or directly or indirectly raising the costs of iRobot's rivals to advertise and sell their RVCs on its marketplace. The ability holds over a specific geographic range: "Amazon may have the ability to foreclose iRobot's rivals because Amazon's online marketplace is a particularly important channel to sell RVCs in France, Germany, Italy, and Spain," the Commission writes in a press release, adding that "RVC customers in these countries particularly rely on Amazon both in terms of product discovery as well as for their final purchasing decision."

On incentive, the Commission is worried Amazon may find foreclosure economically profitable: "The merged entity would likely gain more from additional sales of iRobot RVCs, than it would lose from fewer sales of iRobot's rivals and other related products on Amazon. Such gains include benefits from additional data gathered from iRobot's users." On effect, and at this stage of the probe only, the Commission's view is that were Amazon to deploy such foreclosure strategies it could restrict competition in the market for RVCs, leading to "higher prices, lower quality, and less innovation for consumers."
