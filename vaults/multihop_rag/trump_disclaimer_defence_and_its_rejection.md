# Trump Disclaimer Defence and Its Rejection

Trump's defence attacked the premise that his financial statements could be relied on at all: he denied wrongdoing, arguing in sworn testimony that it did not matter what he put on his financial statements because they carry a disclaimer saying they should not be trusted. He told James at an April deposition, "You don't have a case and you should drop this case," adding: "Do you know the banks were fully paid? Do you know the banks made a lot of money? Do you know I don't believe I ever got even a default notice, and even during COVID, the banks were all paid? And yet you're suing on behalf of banks, I guess. It's crazy. The whole case is crazy."

Engoron rejected that argument when the defence previously sought to have the case thrown out. The judge said the disclaimer on the financial statements "makes abundantly clear that Mr. Trump was fully responsible for the information contained within" them, and that "allowing blanket disclaimers to insulate liars from liability would completely undercut" the "important function" that such statements serve "in the real world."
