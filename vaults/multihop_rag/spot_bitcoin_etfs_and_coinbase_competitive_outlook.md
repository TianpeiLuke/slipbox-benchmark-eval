# Spot Bitcoin ETFs And Coinbase's Competitive Outlook

Coinbase emerged from the crypto winter better placed than before, but a new competitive threat may be on the horizon in the form of spot bitcoin ETFs. On the positive side, analysts at Needham, who recommend buying Coinbase shares, wrote on 21 November that the company "exited the crypto 'winter' better positioned than in the prior up cycle", noting that in addition to FTX's failure and Binance's retreat, crypto trading platform Bittrex has also exited the market; Bittrex said on 20 November that effective 4 December "all trading activity on Bittrex Global will be disabled", encouraging customers to log in and withdraw assets as soon as possible, after the SEC charged Bittrex and its ex-CEO in April with operating an unregistered exchange. Both Coinbase and Binance still face legal battles with the Securities and Exchange Commission, which was noticeably absent from the Binance settlement, and Coinbase executives have floated leaving the U.S. altogether for a jurisdiction with hard-and-fast rules on crypto should they be unable to reach a resolution with the SEC, a concern Wall Street appears to be shrugging off.

The counter-consideration is the ETF. U.S. regulators are expected to soon approve the first U.S. spot bitcoin exchange-traded funds, which would let investors buy into digital currency directly through the same mechanism they use for stock and bond ETFs, with top asset managers including BlackRock, WisdomTree and Invesco having filed applications; approval would open up many more avenues to buy bitcoin. That matters for Coinbase because, while it allows investors to buy a variety of cryptocurrencies, bitcoin accounted for 38% of transaction volume in the third quarter and almost the same percentage of revenue, so casual investors wanting exposure could use their primary online brokerage instead. JPMorgan Chase analysts wrote the previous week that crypto ETFs would likely be good for Coinbase in the short term but more problematic as time passes: the initial boost would come from custody revenue tied to the ETFs, since most of the big asset managers entering the market, including BlackRock, Franklin Templeton and WisdomTree, have picked Coinbase for custody services, the storage and safekeeping of assets; the longer-term concern is that fewer people will need Coinbase accounts, leading to pricing pressure. "We see many novice investors never going beyond these flagship tokens and thus never needing the services of a Coinbase," wrote the analysts, who have a neutral rating and an $80 price target, adding that they see ETF markets as more transparent, efficient and lower cost to execute, with potential for a migration to ETFs for cheaper exposure and trading driving Coinbase to lower fees.
