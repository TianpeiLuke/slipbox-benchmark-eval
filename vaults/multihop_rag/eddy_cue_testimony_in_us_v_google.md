# Eddy Cue's Testimony In US V. Google

Eddy Cue took the stand as a witness in US v. Google, the landmark antitrust trial over Google's search business, and arrived with two messages: Apple believes in protecting its users' privacy, and it also believes in Google — whether those two statements can be simultaneously true became the question of the day.

Cue is one of the highest-profile witnesses in the case so far, in part because the deal between Google and Apple, which makes Google the default search engine on all Apple devices and pays Apple billions of dollars a year, is central to the US Department of Justice's case against Google. The Verge set the scene in the Washington, DC courtroom, where the screens had briefly malfunctioned and left witnesses with only binders before the tech came back up, showing an image of three iPhones each demonstrating a part of the phone's setup process. Cue, in a dark suit, squinted at the monitor and said, "The resolution on this is terrible. You should get a Mac," drawing laughs in an otherwise staid and quiet courtroom; Judge Amit Mehta, presiding over the case, leaned into his microphone and answered, "If Apple would like to make a donation…", drawing bigger ones, before everybody got back down to business.
