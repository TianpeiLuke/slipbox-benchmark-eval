# How Geomagnetic Storms Produce Auroras

During a solar storm, energized particles from the sun slam into Earth's atmosphere at speeds of up to 45 million mph (72 million km/h), and the planet's magnetic field funnels those particles toward the poles, where the supercharging of molecules in Earth's atmosphere triggers the colourful spectacles.

The chain of relations runs from solar storm, to particle influx, to magnetic funnelling, to excited atmospheric molecules, to visible light. Its usual range is high latitudes: the displays normally remain limited to those regions, appearing as the aurora borealis (northern lights) in the north and the aurora australis (southern lights) in the south.
