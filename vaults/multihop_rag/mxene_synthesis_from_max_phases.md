# MXene Synthesis from MAX Phases

MXenes do not exist in nature and must be made from other labmade compounds called MAX phases: like MXenes, MAX phases are layered crystals, but they have extra sheets of metal "A" sandwiched between their M and X layers, so the procedure is to eat away the A layers and leave behind just the M and X layers.

The classic etchant is a strong acid, commonly hydrofluoric acid — so harsh that it can dissolve the minerals in bone. Since the materials' discovery, scientists have found safer ways to make MXenes using milder acids or bases, one such chemical being sodium hydroxide, an ingredient in soap. Another recent method uses molten fluoride salts to etch away the A layer in MAX phases; no harsh compounds are needed there, although it does require high temperatures, up to 750 degrees Celsius (1,380 degrees Fahrenheit). A published diagram depicts the conversion: an acid etches out all but the M layers and X layers to yield thick MXene sheets, with a scanning electron microscope image of the result. Freshly produced MXene normally forms a dark powder when dry; one example contains alternating layers of vanadium (M) and carbon (X) atoms.
