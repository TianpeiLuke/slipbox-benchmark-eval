# From The Zero-Rate Era To 8% Mortgage Rates

The path from 2010 to the 8% mortgage of October 2023 runs through a single policy regime and its reversal. In 2010, the last time existing home sales were so low, the global economy was on life support and the Fed slashed interest rates down to zero to revive the flatlining patient — fueling an "everything bubble" that inflated all kinds of assets along the way, not least housing. That zero-rate regime persisted until the highest inflation in 40 years surprised many economists and prompted the massive series of rate hikes that produced the once-unthinkable 8% mortgage.

Some onlookers had anticipated the level if not the cause: Zillow warned in May 2023 that housing would enter a "deep freeze" if the debt limit standoff failed to resolve and America defaulted, since that would send mortgage rates up to 8%. The default was averted, yet 8% mortgage rates arrived anyway. The account's own framing of the resulting market is that this isn't like 2008 or 2010 again — it calls instead for a 1980s history lesson.
