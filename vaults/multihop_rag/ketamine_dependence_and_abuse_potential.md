# Ketamine's Dependence And Abuse Potential

Ketamine's recognized abuse potential does not, on the clinicians' account, carry over to supervised clinical dosing -- the premise they attack is that a drug legally classified as dependence-forming is therefore addictive however it is used.

The classification is real: ketamine is "legally recognized as having the potential for abuse and for both psychological and physical dependence," according to the American Addiction Centers website.

Johnson qualified how severe that dependence tends to be, calling ketamine a "moderately addictive substance." He told Fox News Digital, "Even long-term users typically only develop a behavioral or psychological dependence. Physical dependence can occur, but usually the withdrawal signs are mild to moderate."

Dr. Sandhya Prashad, president of the American Society Of Ketamine Physicians, Psychotherapists and Practitioners in Florida, made the conditions explicit: there is "an extremely low risk of addiction, craving or withdrawal" when ketamine is used in low doses in a monitored clinical setting. "Abuse of ketamine occurs when it is self-administered in high or frequent doses without supervision or monitoring, which leads to addiction," she told Fox News Digital.
