# Roblox Versus The Social Media Dopamine Critique

Asked at the Code Conference by Alex Kruglov, who runs the social gaming startup pop.in for a much older user base, what people will stop doing in order to spend their time on Roblox — a Clay Christensen "jobs to be done" question about who they are firing — and asked as a parent of three children, the middle of whom he described as treating Roblox like crack and being highly regulated by her parents, with Alex Heath adding "how dystopian is this going to be?", David Baszucki attacked the premise that all screen time is the same kind of screen time.

His answer was that Roblox is optimistic because, in the specter of social media, there is a wide range of things people do: some consume a lot of short video content and trigger dopamine; some do "compare my life with your life and accelerate FOMO"; and some do something closer to "my kid's on the phone with their friends — they're connecting, inventing, hanging out." He is somewhat optimistic, he said, that the future of Roblox's direction is the last of these: bringing people together when they cannot be together in real life.
