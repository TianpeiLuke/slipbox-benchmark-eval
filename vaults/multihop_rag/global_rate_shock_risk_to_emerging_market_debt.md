# Global Rate Shock Risk to Emerging Market Debt

Rajan Jain, Head of Credit Research at SBI Caps, framed the open question in a note: whether the yield curve dynamics mean the US is moving to a structurally higher-rate economy versus the post-GFC normal remains moot, with important implications for investment demand and system risk.

The transmission he describes runs outward from US rates. These movements have had a cascading effect on global economies, which have seen routs in their respective bond markets besides a fresh round of currency depreciation.

The vulnerable case is specific: emerging market economies with large volumes of USD-denominated debt, which could be caught in a vicious circle where their external debt in local currency terms balloons due to a strong DXY while their servicing ability is impaired by tepid economic activity owing to high rates. The stress is not confined to them — even advanced economies have felt the heat, and the ECB and others have maintained a hawkish narrative, Jain said.
