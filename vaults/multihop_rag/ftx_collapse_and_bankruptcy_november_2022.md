# FTX's Collapse And Bankruptcy, November 2022

FTX went bankrupt on November 11th, 2022, days after Gary Wang and Bankman-Fried had determined that about $8 billion in customer money was gone. On November 7th, Bankman-Fried tweeted "FTX is fine. Assets are fine," and another tweet in the thread said "FTX has enough to cover all client holdings." Wang testified flatly: "FTX was not fine and assets were not fine because FTX did not have enough assets for customer withdrawals."

Prosecutor Thane Rehn placed the trigger in the same month: when CoinDesk published its article in November 2022, people realized FTX was a house of cards, while Bankman-Fried was tweeting "FTX is fine. Assets are fine" and "We don't invest customer assets even in treasuries."

The day after the bankruptcy, on November 12th, Bankman-Fried directed Wang to send customer funds to Bahamian regulators. Customers could not take their funds off the exchange at that point because FTX had run out of money and its servers had been shut down. Bankman-Fried and his father met with lawyers while Wang waited outside with liquidators; the US lawyers wanted the remaining FTX assets transferred to the US, but Wang testified that Bankman-Fried told him to stall the US lawyers and transfer the funds to the Bahamian regulators instead, who were more likely to let him stay in control of the company. The collapse also propagated outward: the government put Zac Prince, the founder and former CEO of crypto lender BlockFi, on the stand just long enough to blame FTX's bankruptcy for BlockFi's subsequent bankruptcy.
