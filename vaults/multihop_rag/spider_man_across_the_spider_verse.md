# Spider-Man: Across the Spider-Verse

Across the Spider-Verse, directed by Joaquim Dos Santos, Kemp Powers, and Justin K. Thompson and starring Shameik Moore, Hailee Steinfeld, and Daniel Kaluuya, continues to raise the bar set by its predecessor. Polygon ranked it 8 on its best movies of 2023 list of 2023-10-07 and listed it on Netflix or for digital rental/purchase.

Polygon's account of the first film is that 2018's Into the Spider-Verse changed the game for superhero movies and animated movies, raising the bar for what a Spider-Man story could be and inspiring animators to push the boundaries of what their movies could look like — even if Hollywood as a whole may have taken away the wrong lesson, given how many multiverse movies followed. The sequel is a little busier, pulling in more characters, more frantic visuals, and more emotional throughlines, but it remains bold, daring, and unlike anything else in the superhero movie sphere.

Its animation is character-specific: as the characters bounce across the multiverse, the animation style shifts, with each character rooted in a different style. Spider-Gwen's world is rendered with watercolors that shift according to the tone of her story, and she gets a step up in this movie — a second chance for Gwen Stacy, whose previous iterations across media have not ended well. Spider-Punk, the brash counterculture rebel, is made up of collaged bits and animated against the norm so he stands out even more. Critic PR calls every frame a visual feast bursting with lovingly rendered details, and notes that with a bigger cast and higher stakes the film ends on a huge cliffhanger, with all the pieces in place for a trilogy finale.

Its central idea, as Polygon's 2023-10-16 Spider-Man 2 piece describes, is an obsession with "canon events": pivotal moments in a Spider-Man's life that the film's villain, Spider-Man 2099, is dedicated to maintaining across the multiverse — the upside-down kiss, the death of Uncle Ben or Jefferson Davis, or simply the spider bite that starts it all. Spider-Man 2099 believes upholding these events, even the painful ones, is key to preserving the stability of each strand of reality; Miles disagrees, believing it is Spider-Man's job to resist the seemingly inevitable, always doing what is right rather than what is preordained. The film ends with a promise of pushing back against Spider-Man 2099 and the established canon.
