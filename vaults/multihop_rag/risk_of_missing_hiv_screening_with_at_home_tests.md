# The Risk Of Missing HIV Screening With At-Home Tests

A challenge to the convenience case for at-home STI testing is that it may cost patients an opportunistic HIV test: sexual health clinics routinely test for HIV when a patient attends, even if they are there for another sexual health worry.

The premise under attack is that a convenient at-home alternative is straightforwardly better for the patient. If more people choose at-home testing thanks to Daye's convenient alternative, they may miss out on the chance to acquire important health information, since clinics or doctor's offices can often nudge an attending patient to take the opportunity to test more widely while they are there in person.

Asked how it is mitigating this risk, Daye told TechCrunch: "We enable our patients to book in-clinic appointments for HIV screening, and in the future, we may also expand to at-home blood testing for HIV. We ensure that our patients are well-educated on the risks associated with HIV and are reminded of the need for regular testing. We'd like to play a meaningful role in destigmatising sexual health and provide medically-backed advice on the recommended cadence of screening."
