# Mozilla.social's Trust And Safety Moderation Stance

Mozilla's claim is that its instance will not be a neutral platform, and that neutrality is an excuse rather than a principle. "We are saying out the gate that this isn't a neutral platform," O'Hara stresses. "We think that that's often used as this crutch to allow, or even amplify, really toxic content in the name of engagement… In some cases, platforms aren't just brave enough to actually just take things down."

The commitments backing that stance are concrete. Its content policies indicate strict measures around hate speech, impersonation, self-harm, harassment, misinformation, violent and sexualized content and more, in addition to illegal content like CSAM (child sexual abuse material) and promotion of illegal goods. The instance, still in private testing, has a small team of moderators that will grow when it launches to the public in the coming months. "It's not just a commitment, it's beyond foundational for us — that is the thing that is going to be true about this instance," O'Hara says of Mozilla's plans to heavily moderate its space; though Mozilla will also rely on technology to do some of the work, it wants to make sure the human moderators "feel protected, supported and respected for their work."

The warrant that makes strict rules acceptable is the decentralized structure itself: exit is available. Mozilla has been leaning into its values around inclusion, dignity, security, agency and community for 25 years, O'Hara says, and those will guide its efforts in the fediverse. "We're going to have rules on how people can engage with one another…it's the fediverse, you have lots of other places to go if you don't want to abide by those rules," she adds.
