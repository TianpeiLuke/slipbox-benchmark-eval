# Steam Deck OLED Hardware Refinements

Beyond the screen, the Steam Deck OLED adds a gaggle of minor physical upgrades that Polygon argues matter as much as the headline change. Since the original Steam Deck launched Valve has been improving the hardware through updates — its team estimates around 300 to date — and that continues for both models, with the original getting a fresh BIOS update to align it with some of the OLED's upgrades, including some battery life improvement, though not the OLED model's 30-50% jump.

Some changes demand new hardware. The Steam Deck OLED includes a Wi-Fi 6E radio, useful if you have a modern router and connection to make use of it, and a dedicated Bluetooth module, which allows improved audio quality with wireless headphones, the option to wake the Steam Deck from a controller, and the capability to connect many Bluetooth controllers for multiplayer. The haptics are noticeably improved with a wider frequency range, and the touchscreen is markedly better — responsive to the point where the reviewer no longer feels the need to plug in a USB-C keyboard to type a few words. Valve also claims the device is more repairable, which the reviewer did not have occasion to test, with parts again sold through iFixit. He did enjoy the new power supply, with faster charging — 45 minutes gets you from 20% to 80% — and a 2.5-meter cable that stretches the length of his couch. One charming detail: the carrying case now has a tinier carrying case within it, with the bulky original recommended for the cautious and the slim case for tossing the Deck in a backpack for a day trip.
