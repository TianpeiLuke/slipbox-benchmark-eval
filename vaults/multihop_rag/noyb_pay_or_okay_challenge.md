# noyb's "Pay Or Okay" Challenge

noyb, the European privacy rights group which has driven much of the strategic litigation against Meta's tracking and profiling, attacks both the price Meta has set and the authority Meta claims for setting it. Since 2021 it has been challenging similar 'pay or okay' practices by news publishers by filing a series of complaints with data protection authorities.

In a press release earlier that month, after the WSJ reported Meta plans to charge users for their privacy, noyb's founder and honorary chairman, Max Schrems, wrote: "The CJEU said that the alternative to ads must be 'necessary' and the fee must be 'appropriate'. I don't think €160 a year is what they had in mind. These six words are also an 'obiter dictum', a non-binding element that went beyond the core case before the CJEU. For Meta this is not the most stable case law and we will clearly fight against such an approach." The objection thus targets the inference from the CJEU line as much as the amount charged.
