# Possession: Żuławski's Autobiographical Phantasmagoria Of A Divorce

Possession is Andrzej Żuławski's 1981 psychological horror film, starring Isabelle Adjani, Sam Neill and Heinz Bennent, now watchable on Shudder, AMC Plus and Metrograph. Set in Cold War-era West Berlin, it stars Neill as Mark, a Russian spy who returns home to find that his wife Anna (Adjani) has left him and wants a divorce; when Anna refuses to divulge why, saying only that she has not left him for someone else, Mark grows suspicious and has her tailed, and what he eventually discovers is a horrifying secret beyond his comprehension — one which awakens a long-dormant wellspring of anxiety, resentment and despair between the two that threatens to tear apart not only their small family but their very sanity.

Its distinguishing quality is the fusion of the personal and the monstrous: inspired by Żuławski's own tumultuous divorce in 1976 and his subsequent struggles with suicidal ideation, Possession blurs the line between the autobiographical and the phantasmagorical, with hysterical performances by Neill and Adjani that vacillate between disturbing, comical and disquietingly sympathetic. It has been an inspiration for everything from Ari Aster's Midsommar to the 2016 music video for Massive Attack's "Voodoo in My Blood", and Polygon calls it an essential watch for any serious horror fan.

Access has long been the boundary condition on its reputation. Outside the most ardent cinephile circles, Żuławski is not a name that inspires enthusiastic recognition in the United States: known for his transgressive brand of arthouse cinema, his career was stymied by Communist authorities in his homeland of Poland, with many early films heavily censored, banned, or in one instance nearly destroyed upon release, and the few of his films released in the States have since gone out of print, though that appears to be changing. Possession's cult status among horror connoisseurs was only amplified in the decades since release by its difficulty to obtain on physical media or view online — which, fortunately, is no longer the case.
