# Antibody Drug Conjugates And Their Market

Antibody Drug Conjugates, or ADCs, combine the chemotherapy route of toxicity with the targeted action of monoclonal antibodies on cancer cells: they rely on an mAB to target cancer cells and then release a cytotoxin into them, and the link joining the mAB and the toxin makes an ADC a three-part drug.

The three parts involved, together with the fact that several mABs are available, have opened up the ADC market, which compared with mABs is dominated by smaller players. Gilead acquired Immunomedics for $20 billion for its leading ADC, Trodelvy; Roche's leading drug trastuzumab, branded Herceptin, for breast cancer is now paired with a cytotoxin and branded Kadcyla, expected to generate peak sales of $3-4 billion over the next two years; and Merck paid $4 billion in upfront payments to Daiichi Sankyo to collaborate on ADCs. As of 18 November 2023, the US FDA has approved 10 ADCs for cancer treatment, and more than 80 ADCs are under clinical trials.
