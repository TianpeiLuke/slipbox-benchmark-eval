# User-Generated Content

## Definition

User-generated content (UGC), in the corpus, is the term for content that ordinary platform users — rather than the platform itself or a news organization — create and publish, such as Reels, Stories, posts, and streams.
The EU's Digital Services Act (DSA) treats UGC as a defined regulatory object, requiring "social media platforms and other services that carry user-generated content" to act on reports of illegal content and disinformation (doc_0025).
Snap itself describes the public Stories submitted to Snap Map as "authentic user-generated content" offering perspectives that traditional news outlets may not reflect (doc_0124).

## Context

The corpus treats UGC primarily as the object of content-moderation obligations and disputes rather than as a neutral technical term: the EU's Digital Services Act (DSA) explicitly regulates "how social media platforms and other services that carry user-generated content must respond to reports of illegal content," putting X under a legal obligation to act on flagged posts and mitigate disinformation risk (doc_0025).
On Meta's platforms, individual users' Reels and Stories about Palestine became the flashpoint for accusations of moderation bias, with filmmaker Khitam Jabr suspecting that her own Palestine travel Reels were restricted for 24 hours after other users reported them (doc_0106).
On Snapchat, an X user described the crowd-sourced public Stories submitted to Snap Map as "a WEALTH of real-time information" for journalists who could not get on the ground in Gaza, and Snap confirmed a "moderate" increase in Stories submissions from the region since October 7 (doc_0124).
Mozilla frames the broader problem as one of governance: today's large, advertiser-funded social networks decide how UGC is moderated and monetized, which is part of why it is building a decentralized "fediverse" alternative (doc_0026).
On Twitch, UGC is also an economic asset — streamers' broadcasts are the content being monetized, and disputes over revenue splits and the Partner Plus program center on how creators are compensated for the content they generate (doc_0401).

## Key Characteristics

- **User-originated** — the content is authored and published by ordinary platform users (e.g., Reels, Stories, posts, streams), not by the platform or a newsroom (doc_0106, doc_0124).
- **Subject to platform moderation and reporting** — UGC can be flagged, restricted, or shadowbanned by other users' reports or automated systems, as Jabr suspected happened to her Palestine travel videos (doc_0106).
- **Regulatory trigger** — under the DSA, a platform's responsibility to act on illegal UGC and disinformation is a defined legal obligation, not just a policy choice (doc_0025).
- **Real-time information source** — one X user called Snap Map's crowd-sourced Stories "a WEALTH of real-time information" for journalists who weren't on the ground in Gaza, and Snap confirmed a "moderate" rise in submissions from the region (doc_0124).
- **Monetizable** — UGC is also the basis for creator revenue-sharing arrangements, making its governance a business as well as a moderation issue (doc_0401).

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, "user-generated content" (UGC) — also called "user-created content" (UCC) — is generally defined as content generated by users of the Internet, such as images, videos, audio, text, testimonials, software, and user interactions (Wikipedia, "User-generated content," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/User-generated_content).
