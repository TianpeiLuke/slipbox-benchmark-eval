# India's 1996 World Cup Semi-Final Against Sri Lanka

India's 1996 semi-final against Sri Lanka at the Eden Gardens in Kolkata on 13 March was called off by the umpires with India reeling at 120 for eight in 34.1 overs, after crowd trouble interrupted the match and no respite came from the onlookers' side. The 1996 Cricket World Cup is known for Sri Lanka's emergence as a cricketing power on the horizon of world cricket and for the beginning of Sachin Tendulkar's extraordinary dominance in batting history.

India were favourites again in that first semi-final and raised their hopes by restricting Sri Lanka to 251 for 8 in 50 overs, despite Man-of-the-Match Aravinda de Silva's 66 and Roshan Mahanama's 58; India seamer Javagal Srinath took three top-order wickets including Sanath Jayasuriya and Romesh Kaluwitharana. Fans at the Eden Gardens cheered loudly while Tendulkar was at the crease, but once he fell for 65 with the team at 98, India suffered one of the worst collapses in the batting history of the World Cups, and the crowd turned angry and hostile towards Sri Lanka, creating a ruckus at the stadium. The long-lasting memories of the crowd's bad behaviour and of a weeping Vinod Kambli returning to the pavilion are still fresh in the memories of cricket fans of that generation.
