# Discogs' 2023 Seller Fee Increase

Discogs raised its seller fee from 8 to 9 percent on May 22nd, 2023 and — crucially — started charging that same fee on shipping costs for the first time, which matters given how international the record market is. The Verge's reporter noted that one of the beauties of Discogs had previously been finding and purchasing rarities from sellers in Japan or Germany, citing his own most expensive purchase, a copy of Cannonball Adderley's debut album from a seller in Switzerland; the site now takes considerably larger slices of those kinds of sales. Discogs declined to comment for the story.

To make up for the lost revenue, Discogs suggested sellers use a tool it had created to raise the prices of all of their inventory by a percentage, and another Discogs email to sellers suggested they offer free shipping to avoid the fee — without accounting for the fact that the seller would then be either covering that cost out of pocket or integrating it into the price of the record, which would result in the same amount of money going to Discogs. Sellers were essentially told to raise their prices and/or offer free shipping, two options that threaten their bottom lines. "Their communication, too — it's like, 'I said what I said, and we're done,'" says Doug Martin. "Well, you're really not, because we all have to live with this and so do you."

The tension between Discogs' old-internet charm and its attempts at growth came to a head earlier in the summer around a since-deleted viral Twitter thread by artist and label head Mike Simonetti lamenting "the fall of discogs," which sounded the alarm about increasing fees and subsequently increasing prices, a growing influx of scammers, rising shipping costs, and the dysfunction of the website itself, among other issues. "We had kind of thought Discogs was on our side as sellers," says Gene Melkisethian, who runs Joint Custody, a record store in Washington, DC, and sells on Discogs. "But when they started charging fees on shipping, it just felt really punitive." The Connecticut seller put it more bluntly: "In their communication, it was beyond insulting the way they framed it. Like, 'Oh, you can just not charge for shipping.' The sudden fee increase was a huge, huge blow to a lot of people."
