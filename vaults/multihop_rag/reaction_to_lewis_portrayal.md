# Reaction To Lewis's Portrayal Of SBF

The backlash to Michael Lewis's 60 Minutes appearance attacks one specific premise of his account — that FTX was a sound business brought down only by an unwarranted loss of confidence — and, behind it, the inference that SBF's story is therefore a tragic fall rather than a fraud. The episode has fired up a lot of people, indignant at what they feel was too-easy treatment for someone who many believe brought down the whole crypto house. TechCrunch collected the reaction on 2023-10-02.

The premise is attacked head-on by Dan Held, who on October 2, 2023 quoted Lewis — "They had a great real business. If no one had cast aspersions on the business, if there hadn't been a run on customers deposits, they'd still be making tons of money" — and answered: "That's not true. They are not a bank, they're…", prefacing it with "Shameful that Michael Lewis is defending SBF." Ryan Selkis attacked the framing instead of the balance sheet, writing the same day that "The Michael Lewis interview is infuriating. SBF was a scumbag, and now we need to hear about his 'tragic fall' in the MSM because Sam told them he was an 'effective altruist' and people like Michael Lewis were so far up his ass they couldn't tell truth from fiction." Tobi Lutke attacked the coverage itself: "60 Minutes got rightly community noted here. How is it that SBF is still getting super positive media coverage when the media has nothing good to say about the founders that have actually created things and brought value into the world?"

Two qualifications sit against the backlash. Noff is defensive: she believes crypto is no worse than the stock market, which can also be manipulated and mishandled, adding that "Even when you identify corruption, I don't think that means we don't believe in the system in question." And TechCrunch's own reading is that the thing being attacked may not be quite what the critics take it for — Lewis's picture is a little less stark from the looks of the 60 Minutes episode, and it is not that clear at all whether SBF is getting a kicking or a coat of polish in the interview, given Lewis's celebration of SBF's self-proclaimed (yet not quite proven) idealism and the "Bankman-Fried-shaped hold in the world" that exists. The writer's closing point is that this confused message is very apt for cryptocurrency, where the process by which it works is forever murky and often misrepresented, at least to most people.
