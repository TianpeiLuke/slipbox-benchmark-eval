# Mozilla's Open Letter On AI Openness

The letter's claim is that openness makes AI safer, against the counter-position that proprietary control does. In an open letter published by Mozilla a few weeks before November 21, 2023, Meta's chief AI scientist Yann LeCun joined some 70 other signatories in calling for more openness in AI development, and the letter has since garnered more than 1,700 signatures. The backdrop is that Big Tech companies such as OpenAI and Google's DeepMind have called for more regulation, warning of catastrophic consequences if the AI levers were to meet the wrong hands — in other words, arguing that proprietary AI is safer than open source.

LeCun and his co-signatories disagree, and concede the risk before turning it around: "Yes, openly available models come with risks and vulnerabilities — AI models can be abused by malicious actors or deployed by ill-equipped developers. However, we have seen time and time again that the same holds true for proprietary technologies — and that increasing public access and scrutiny makes technology safer, not more dangerous. The idea that tight and proprietary control of foundational AI models is the only path to protecting us from society-scale harm is naive at best, dangerous at worst." The warrant is that scrutiny, not secrecy, is what makes technology safe.

On a personal level, LeCun has accused the big-name AI players of trying to secure "regulatory capture of the AI industry" by lobbying against open AI R&D — an argument about motive rather than safety. And on a company level, Meta is doing all it can to encourage collaboration and "openness," recently partnering with Hugging Face to launch a new startup accelerator designed to spur adoption of open source AI models.
