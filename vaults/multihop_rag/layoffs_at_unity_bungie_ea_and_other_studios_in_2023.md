# Layoffs At Unity, Bungie, EA And Other Studios In 2023

Beyond Embracer and Epic, 2023's cuts ran across the industry: Unity lowered its headcount three times, affecting about 900 jobs; Sony cut 100 jobs at Bungie; and Electronic Arts culled an estimated 1,130 jobs over the year.

Unity's November quarterly financial results reported a yearly revenue increase of 69 percent, and it told investors, "We continued to manage costs well." Sony had bought Bungie for $3.6 billion in 2022, and according to developers still there, Sony executives are attempting to use the upheaval to wrest more control of the studio from Bungie founders and leaders. EA was one of the first video game companies to institute significant layoffs that year, cutting 6 percent of its workforce — about 800 employees — in March, and later cutting jobs at Dirt and F1 studio Codemasters, which it purchased in 2021 for $1.2 billion. CD Projekt RED and Sega each laid off about 100 people in the past 12 months, while Ubisoft fired an estimated 255 employees. Microsoft cut 10,000 jobs across its businesses early in the year, including about 100 people at Halo studio 343 Industries.
