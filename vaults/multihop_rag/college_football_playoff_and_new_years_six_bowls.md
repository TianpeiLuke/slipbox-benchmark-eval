# The College Football Playoff And The New Year's Six Bowls

The College Football Playoff starts with the New Year's Six bowl games, two of which — on a rotating basis — act as the semifinals that determine which teams play in the National Championship.

The six games, also known as the NY6, are the Rose Bowl Game presented by Capital One (Pasadena, California), the Allstate Sugar Bowl (New Orleans, Louisiana), the PlayStation Fiesta Bowl (Glendale, Arizona), the Chick-fil-A Peach Bowl (Atlanta, Georgia), the Goodyear Cotton Bowl Classic (Arlington, Texas) and the Capital One Orange Bowl (Miami Gardens, Florida). These six are the most iconic and important bowl games in all of college football and usually invite the top-rated teams in the country. Qualification works in two tracks: a selection committee ranks the best four teams in the country and selects them for the semifinal games, where they compete for a chance to play in the National Championship, while the other four NY6 bowls are typically given to champions from the Power Five conferences and the top-rated teams and champions from the Group of Five conferences. The boundary on this structure is temporal — the semifinal-only role expands to include quarterfinal games as of the 2024-25 season, when the CFP expands to a 12-team format.
