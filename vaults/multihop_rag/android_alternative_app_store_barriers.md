# Epic's Argument That Google Blocks Alternative Android App Stores

Epic will attempt to demonstrate that Google makes it hard for alternative app stores to succeed and reach consumers. Its headline ground is a distribution fact: 90% of apps are still downloaded through Google Play.

The mechanism Epic offers to explain that share is contractual. Google bundles the Play Store with other Google apps that Android OEMs (original equipment manufacturers) have to agree to in order to use Android. Epic will also bring up "Anti-Fragmentation Agreements" (AFAs) that prevent the OEMs from modifying Android to allow for frictionless downloading of apps outside of Google Play. The warrant is that if OEMs are contractually barred from making outside downloading frictionless, rival stores cannot reach consumers on comparable terms, and Play's share follows from the contracts rather than from consumer preference.

Epic will offer two concrete instances. It will specifically reference an agreement it had made with the OEM OnePlus, to make Epic games available on its devices through the Epic Games app; OnePlus had to cancel the deal over concerns that the arrangement would have it bypassing the Google Play Store. The case will also examine a deal between Google and Samsung that Epic says was designed to prevent the Galaxy Store from being a competitive threat and to require Google Play Billing on apps distributed by Samsung.
