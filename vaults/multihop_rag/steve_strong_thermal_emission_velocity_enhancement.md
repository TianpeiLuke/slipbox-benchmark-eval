# STEVE: Strong Thermal Emission Velocity Enhancement

STEVE is an aurora-like glow that often accompanies the northern lights yet is a distinct phenomenon, according to the American Geophysical Union (AGU); the acronym stands for Strong Thermal Emission Velocity Enhancement.

What separates STEVE from an aurora is its cause: STEVE is produced by hot streams of gas — 5,400 degrees Fahrenheit (3,000 degrees Celsius) — snaking through Earth's magnetosphere, which emit light when energized by geomagnetic storms, according to spaceweather.com. During the peak of the November 2023 storm, not all the light shows were auroras: STEVE made an appearance for some observers.
