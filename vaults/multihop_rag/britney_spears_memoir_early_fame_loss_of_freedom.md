# Britney Spears' Memoir On Early Fame And The Loss Of Freedom

In *The Woman in Me*, as summarised by The Guardian on 24 October 2023, Britney Spears describes how fame converted her childhood escape into confinement: by the age of 16 she was "public property", unable to go outside without being mobbed, and her love of singing and dancing had become a lucrative resource for other people.

Spears writes that as a child she escaped a difficult home life — her alcoholic father often rowing with her mother — into the outdoors, which "gave me a sense of aliveness and danger", and that performing made her feel invincible. By the time of her second album she wanted for so little; skinny-dipping with her dancers after playing Rock in Rio in January 2001 is one of her happiest memories, "a taste of rebellion and freedom, but I was just having fun and being a 19-year-old". After her split from Justin Timberlake she dreamed about quitting pop and opening a shop, and writes with hindsight that she "hadn't given myself enough time to heal from the breakup" — yet her requests for a break were not only turned down but interpreted as a potential sign of sedition to be quashed and interrogated.

In that restrictive environment small kindnesses took on outsized significance, such as Paris Hilton encouraging her "to have fun for the first time in a long time", which led to infamous nights out with the heiress and Lindsay Lohan; once again Britney was seen as stepping out of bounds and potentially damaging her earning potential. One night, after her mother babysat her sons, she returned home tipsy and Lynne Spears screamed at her: "The shame I felt killed my heart. I stood there, reeling, and thought, OK, I guess it's forbidden for me to party." As the restrictions tightened she acted out, and ended up legally denied her freedom for 13 years under a conservatorship controlled by her father.
