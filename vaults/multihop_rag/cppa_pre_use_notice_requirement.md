# The CPPA Pre-Use Notice Requirement

The planned framework requires that businesses wanting to apply ADMT must provide so-called "pre-use notices" to affected consumers before the processing happens, so they can decide whether to opt out of their data being used, or indeed whether to exercise their access right to get more info about the intended use of automation or AI. The precondition behind the step is that a law letting consumers opt out of privacy-hostile data processing only works if the people involved are aware how their information is being used.

This looks broadly similar to provisions in the EU's GDPR, which put transparency and fairness obligations on entities processing personal data, in addition to requiring a valid lawful basis for them to use personal data.

TechCrunch flags where the European analogue breaks down. The European regulation contains some exceptions — such as where info was not directly collected from individuals and fulfilling their right to be informed would be "unreasonably expensive" or "impossible" — which may have undermined EU lawmakers' intent that data subjects should be kept informed. That may matter especially in the realm of AI and generative AI, where large amounts of personal data have clearly been scraped off the Internet but web users have not been proactively informed about this heist of their info; see, for example, regulatory action against Clearview AI, or the open investigations of OpenAI's ChatGPT.
