# Garrett Wilson's Fantasy Outlook In Weeks 5 And 6 Of 2023

CBS Sports moved Garrett Wilson from "Start Him" in Week 5 of the 2023 season to "Flex Starter" in Week 6, and both calls rest on the same limiting factor: Wilson's situation, specifically the share of Zach Wilson's throws to him that are uncatchable, holds him back from the potential he so clearly has.

The Week 5 case, published 4 October 2023 ahead of a game at Denver, asks whether the matchup is so easy that Zach Wilson will have tons of time in the pocket to make perfect throws, and answers that this is tough to bank on because he has rarely thrown with precision. Even so, the column likes Garrett Wilson's chances of seeing a lot of targets and coming through with at least a couple of big plays against a defense tied in knots for the past eight quarters. The breakdown lists the Jets at Denver, the Broncos favoured by 1.5, an over/under of 43, an opponent ranked 24th versus wide receivers, 13.5 projected points, a 15th receiver ranking, and year-to-date totals of 21 receptions on 36 targets for 225 yards, two touchdowns and 13.9 fantasy points per game. Week 4 was a breakout of sorts, with Wilson catching 9 of 14 targets to boost his PPR score, though he finished with 60 receiving yards, his second-highest of the season, and came close to a 29-yard touchdown that Zach Wilson missed. Of the 36 targets Garrett Wilson has seen from Zach Wilson, 11 — 31% — have been uncatchable, not counting another three incompletions thrown late or predetermined enough for defenses to sniff out. The silver lining is that Garrett Wilson is clearly the alpha of the passing game, with at least twice as many targets as anyone else on the Jets; the real silver lining is Denver, whose defense has been awful to watch — no defense has allowed a higher catch rate (85.1%) or a worse defensive EPA per dropback (minus-0.79) to wide receivers, and after yielding an 86.7% catch rate and 20.8 yards per catch to Miami in Week 3 they let up a 92.9% catch rate and 15.6 yards per catch to Chicago in Week 4, while even top cornerback Pat Surtain has allowed a high-for-him 63% completion rate and gave up four catches for 56 yards on six targets in his coverage the previous week, with Denver's pass rush practically non-existent. The verdict starts Wilson over Gabe Davis, Jakobi Meyers and any Broncos wideouts.

The Week 6 case, published 12 October 2023 ahead of a home game with Philadelphia, downgrades him: if he couldn't put up a monster game against the Broncos, it's a tough sell that he'll do so against the Eagles, though volume should be in his favour, making close to 16 PPR points — half that in non-PPR — a fair expectation. The breakdown lists the Jets at home versus Philadelphia, the Eagles favoured by 7, an over/under of 41, an opponent ranked 31st versus wide receivers, 11.7 projected points, a 29th receiver ranking, and year-to-date totals of 24 receptions on 43 targets for 279 yards, two touchdowns and 12.8 fantasy points per game. In Week 5 Wilson led the Jets with seven targets but trailed Tyler Conklin in catches and yards, and his 26.9% target share was a season low; being shadowed by Patrick Surtain on all but one target didn't help, nor did a pass bouncing off his hands within five yards of the end zone. Zach Wilson was off-target on 15.4% of his throws for the second straight week even with a higher completion rate (73.1%) and 7.7 yards per attempt, a byproduct of attempting just 26 throws after 39 the game before, and the uncatchable share has risen to 14 of 43 targets, or 33%. The Eagles have allowed a slew of receiving numbers simply by seeing so many passes — 27.4 targets per game to wide receivers alone — so they rank bottom-five in catches, yards after catch and touchdowns allowed, but their per-target metrics are fine: a 60.6% completion rate allowed is 10th-best, 11.4 yards per catch allowed is seventh-best and 4.06 yards after catch per reception is league average. Their pass rush has been solid too, pressuring at 37.6% and blitzing on 33.3% of snaps against the soft lines of Washington and Los Angeles, and given the state of the Jets line after losing guard Alijah Vera-Tucker to a season-ending injury it wouldn't be a surprise if Philadelphia spooked Zach Wilson frequently, leading to shorter throws, off-target throws and sacks. The column then does the arithmetic explicitly: if the Jets throw 39 times, roughly 30% of targets go to Garrett Wilson, which is 12 targets; a third of those figure to be uncatchable, so eight receptions at best; and although he averages 8.7 yards per catch on the season he deserves the benefit of at least 10.0 given the Eagles allow 11.4, so maybe he delivers just past 15 PPR points. That gives him flex appeal over Nico Collins and Tyler Lockett, but not over Jakobi Meyers, Zay Flowers or Gabe Davis.
