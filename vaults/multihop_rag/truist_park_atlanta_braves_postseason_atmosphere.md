# Truist Park: Atlanta Braves Postseason Atmosphere

Truist Park ranked No. 4 among the eight remaining 2023 playoff teams' home-field environments in The New York Times' 8 October 2023 ranking, as a ballpark packed with fans who know their team is good and will not be quiet about it. The park opened in 2017, has a capacity of 41,084 and drew an average attendance of 39,401 in 2023.

The Braves' championship history mostly happened elsewhere: when they won the World Series in 1914 they were still playing in Boston, and in 1957 in Milwaukee; when Sid Bream slid home to beat the Pirates in 1992 they were in the right city but at the wrong ballpark, since Fulton County Stadium doesn't count; the Max Fried-Jorge Soler game in 2021 happened in Houston. The iconic moment at Truist Park could be Eddie Rosario's dazzling catch at the wall in Game 4 of the 2021 World Series, but the most memorable — even though it didn't lead to a championship — might be rookie Ronald Acuna Jr. hitting a grand slam off Walker Buhler in Game 3 of the 2018 NLDS, which happened to be the first postseason game ever played at the ballpark.

The ranking notes that there are issues bigger than baseball here: the war chant and Tomahawk Chop are offensive and problematic, and it is all about to come front and centre again as the best team in baseball opens its postseason with a roster that would be plenty intimidating in a Little League park with metal bleachers. The atmospheric case is the sight of 40,000 screaming fans when the stadium lights go dark and the entire ballpark does the Chop in unison with lit cell phones — "It does add something to the offensive ambiance," Braves scribe David O'Brien noted. Truist Park had 54 sellouts that season and drew just over 3.19 million, the Braves' highest total attendance since the park formerly known as SunTrust opened in 2017. The Braves have won six straight division titles and have been to the playoffs 22 times since 1991, so this is not uncharted territory for them.
