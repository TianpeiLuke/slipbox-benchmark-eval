# Discogs' Stated Justification For The Fee Increase

Against sellers' complaint that the fee increase was extractive, Discogs attributed the need to raise fees to its "significant investments in recent years to ensure compliance with various regulatory programs, including tax support and privacy protection," and said the change would allow it to "continue to devote resources to maintaining the Discogs Marketplace and develop better tools for collecting, selling, and enjoying music."

Sellers attacked the premise that the money was going back into the product. Many who spoke with The Verge speculated, in line with the viral thread about "the fall of discogs," that the company was trying to pump up its valuation for a potential sale; all of them had the sense that Discogs was trying to increase its profit margins without necessarily offering any improvements to its product in return. "It just seems like they're actively trying to stop sales," says the Connecticut seller. "You can raise your fees, but maybe you could do some promotions, coupon codes, sales — something that offsets the shift. Sellers can do it on their own, but that's going to require them to lower their prices — it's going to be a race to the bottom. If you were trying to ruin a sales forum, this is how you'd do it."

The one promotional gesture the company did make drew the same objection. Discogs held a sale in late August 2023, but it featured just 11 of the site's largest stores. "When I first saw it, I thought, maybe they'll be randomly promoting stores or the best products," says Doug Martin. "I don't know a big sale that most people are not part of that you promote to further depress our prices is the right direction." The Verge illustrated the resulting price picture with Discogs' marketplace page showing copies of Olivia Rodrigo's Guts for sale, where prices are generally higher than buying the album elsewhere.
