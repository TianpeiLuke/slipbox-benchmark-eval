# FTX's Inner Circle: Family And Friends Implicated

The Verge's October 26th, 2023 account of the trial catalogued how everyone who loved Bankman-Fried most in the world was caught up in the case. Adam Yedidia, his college roommate, testified with immunity because he was concerned he may have unknowingly worked on code that contributed to the fraud; "I love you Sam," he had texted at one point, "I'm not going anywhere" — that was before he realized FTX had stolen customer funds, at which point he resigned. Nishad Singh, a longtime family friend, copped to what sounded an awful lot like classic embezzlement, saying he had backdated transactions on FTX to help create doctored balance sheets to show to investors, and pleaded guilty to six charges including wire fraud, money laundering, and conspiracy counts around his political donations. Caroline Ellison, Bankman-Fried's ex-girlfriend, told the court about the seven phony balance sheets she had created and was clear that Alameda took customer funds, that Bankman-Fried knew about it, and that he even invested them against her advice.

His family was drawn in too. Joe Bankman, his father, is among the people included in the "small group chat," the Signal group where the FTX inner circle strategized during the exchange's last days. Barbara Fried's political action committee, Mind the Gap, is among the entities that received FTX customer money as political donations; Fried and Bankman lived together in a Bahamas house purchased for them using FTX investor funds, according to a financial expert called by the prosecution, and in a lawsuit filed by FTX's bankruptcy estate the two are alleged to have been given $10 million of FTX customer funds. Gabe Bankman-Fried's nonprofit, Guarding Against Pandemics, got millions from FTX; he was also in a Signal group chat, Donations Processing, that Singh says was used to coordinate the transfer of FTX and Alameda money to various political causes, and in that chat he can be seen recommending that some donations be made "in the dark" to avoid public relations headaches. Singh also said Gabe had an assistant fly to the Bahamas with a stack of blank checks for Singh to sign.

The consequences outlast the trial. Wang, Singh, and Ellison are now felons; even if they never do time — and they may very well be imprisoned or subjected to house arrest — their lives are irrevocably changed, not least by whatever restitution requirements they face, they are barred from taking certain kinds of jobs, and there is the publicity. Bankman, Fried, and Gabe Bankman-Fried's reputations are tarnished, and in the political arenas where they once operated they are suddenly undesirable. Beyond the courtroom, everyone who had money trapped on the exchange when it collapsed — as a result of Alameda purloining customers' funds — had their lives altered: even if they are made whole by the bankruptcy proceedings, that is still money they should have had access to at any time, which may mean houses that were not purchased, medical procedures left undone, and life savings lost. The reporter's summary: it is a Greek tragedy, precipitated by one man's character flaw, the arrogance of Sam Bankman-Fried.
