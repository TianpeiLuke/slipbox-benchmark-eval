# The Microtransaction Revenue Rationale For The Activision Acquisition

The BBC's model of why Microsoft still wins even if Activision games stay on PlayStation runs through in-game spending rather than game sales: Ubisoft may hold the streaming rights for 15 years, but game companies increasingly make much more money from in-game purchases, also known as microtransactions, than from selling games.

The proportions come from Statista: the firm raked in $5.89bn in 2022 from microtransactions, downloadable content and royalties — more than three times the $1.6bn it made from selling the games themselves.

The relation that makes this decisive for platform rivalry is the revenue split on those purchases. Sony gets around a 30 percent cut of purchases made in-game, though the exact figures will change between games. Either way, as the BBC puts it, an in-game purchase in Call of Duty on PlayStation will now be directly funding a rival.
