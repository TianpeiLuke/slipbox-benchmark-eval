# Which Artists Joined "Dream Track", And The Licensing Questions That Follow

MBW's closing observation is that the artists who agreed, with their record companies, to enter the YouTube "Dream Track" trial are stars — but in the case of both Universal Music Group and Warner Music Group they are, currently anyway, not the biggest megastars on either company's books.

The reasoning is that you might not expect, at this early stage, for the likes of UMG to put forward Drake, Taylor Swift, or The Beatles — or for WMG to put forward Ed Sheeran or Dua Lipa — to be guinea pigs in any early AI music experiments, even ones run by "friends" of the music industry like YouTube. From that observation MBW raises two open questions: whether there have been significant licensing payments made by YouTube to UMG and WMG for the right to play with the AI vocals of artists who have signed off on Dream Track thus far, and if so, whether these payments are being placed against any as-yet-unrecouped advances that some of these artists may have on their label accounts. More broadly, MBW poses a bigger question: when the day comes that YouTube asks the world's biggest superstars to wholeheartedly embrace "Dream Track", will Google's recent US Copyright Office filing give those superstars — and their record companies — pause for thought?
