# Testimony At The SBF Trial

Across the first three weeks of the trial, a run of former colleagues, an outside investor and a forensic accounting expert testified that FTX customer money flowed into Alameda Research and out again to other uses, and that FTX's investors were never told about the two firms' special relationship. TechCrunch's running coverage, published 2023-10-06, summarises each week.

In week one, FTX's co-founder and CTO Gary Wang testified Thursday on the extent to which Alameda Research was used for whatever purposes he and SBF saw fit: according to Wang, the crypto trading firm pulled funding directly from FTX customers, whose transactions would be funneled toward Alameda and then directed elsewhere. Thursday also brought testimony from Matt Huang, co-founder and managing partner at crypto investment firm Paradigm, which invested $278 million in FTX across 2021 and 2022 and is part of a class-action lawsuit accusing it and others of defrauding customers by promoting FTX. His testimony centered around being left uninformed about FTX's utilization of customer money to keep Alameda Research afloat — a practice Huang says would have halted his involvement — and covered his relationship with FTX stretching back to 2019.

Week two's standout testimony came from Alameda Research's former CEO Caroline Ellison, who claimed she took $14 billion from customers to repay debts to lenders, using them as a line of credit under the instruction of SBF. Ellison also went into detail about how a $2 billion venture fund, FTX Ventures, was funded by money that had previously been allocated to Alameda from third-party lenders. And though there are no charges of bribery in the case, she also testified to paying Chinese officials $150 million to restore access to $1 billion in frozen trading accounts.

The third week featured a couple of noteworthy moments for the prosecution. FTX's former general counsel Can Sun, like many of SBF's former colleagues, opted to cooperate with authorities and testified that SBF asked him to create a theoretical "legal justification" for the use of billions in consumer funds just days before FTX filed for bankruptcy. Robert Boroujerdi, managing director of the hedge fund Third Point, testified about his experience working with SBF and FTX in a partnership that ultimately resulted in a $60 million investment and a lack of awareness about the special relationship between Alameda Research and FTX; had he known of such a relationship, he testified, Third Point would not have invested in FTX in the first place. And earlier in the week, Peter Easton — a University of Notre Dame professor who helped the government prosecution of Enron and WorldCom — testified about what he uncovered after tracing the flow of billions of dollars between Alameda Research and FTX: much of that funding came from customers, and Easton testified that he found user deposits were used by both FTX and Alameda to purchase real estate, make investments or direct funding toward political causes and charities.
