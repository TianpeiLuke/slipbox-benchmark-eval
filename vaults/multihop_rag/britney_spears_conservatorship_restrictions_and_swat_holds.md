# Britney Spears On Conservatorship Restrictions And Swat Holds

The Guardian's 24 October 2023 account of *The Woman in Me* reports that the conservatorship ruled by Spears' father medicated and surveilled her, vetted her dates with background checks, blood tests and NDAs, and that she was twice confronted by a Swat team and subjected to a forced hospital hold.

Fearing Kevin Federline would take away access to her children, she locked herself in a bathroom with one of her sons: "Before I knew what was happening, a Swat team in black suits burst through the bathroom door as if I'd hurt someone," she writes, and she was placed under a forced hospital hold. Soon after, her mother invited her to her beach house because "the cops are after you"; when she arrived, so did another Swat team. "I'm a five-foot four-inch pop singer who calls everyone sir and ma'am… They treated me like I was a criminal or predator."

The conservatorship was ruled by her father, whom she alleges was "an alcoholic, someone who'd declared bankruptcy, who'd failed in business, who'd terrified me as a little girl". Her dates were required to agree to background checks and blood tests, to sign NDAs, and to receive a full rundown of her sexual history before the first date: "The insanity of this system kept me from finding basic companionship, having a fun night out or making new friends – let alone falling in love… It was just the worst thing that could possibly ever happen to my music, my career and my sanity." She acceded in order to retain access to her boys, while questioning how she could perform at such a high level if she was "so sick that I couldn't make my own decisions". Her father sent her to rehab and Alcoholics Anonymous because she had been taking over-the-counter energy supplements; the women at AA inspired her to try to take control of her life, but she hit more walls. He would not let her remove her IUD to start a family with her boyfriend and later husband Sam Asghari (they have divorced since she completed the memoir), and after she objected to a new dance move suggested for her Vegas residency she was sent to a solitary rehabilitation facility for two months — the most upsetting part of the book.
