# How Ketones Might Act on Behavior

According to Masato Yoshizawa, there are two plausible ideas as to how the ketones produced by a ketogenic diet act on behaviour: the first involves the mitochondria, which use either carbs or fat to produce energy in our cells, and the other involves epigenetics, which simply refers to any non-genetic influence that turns genes on and off.

The epigenetic route has an observable footing: ketones are known to create detectable increases in gene expression in cells. Pulling apart exactly how diet, environment, genes and neurotransmitters are linked is incredibly difficult, but doing so could reveal which pathways are best to target for autism treatments, or could identify a specific ketone which works more efficiently than others.
