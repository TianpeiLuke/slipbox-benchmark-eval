# PayPal's Anti-Steering Class Action

On October 5, TechCrunch's Mary Ann broke the news that PayPal had been hit with a class action lawsuit by consumers represented by law firm Hagens Berman, alleging that the fintech giant's anti-steering rules stifle competition against lower-cost payment platforms such as Stripe and Shopify. According to an investigation conducted by the firm's consumer rights attorneys, PayPal has subjected consumers to excess charges when purchasing from online merchants that accept PayPal or Venmo.

The suit was covered as one over allegedly "Draconian" payment policies. When someone commented on social media that they did not see the problem with what PayPal was doing, and questioned whether Visa and Mastercard do the same thing, TechCrunch went back to the attorneys who filed the lawsuit. They noted that anti-steering rules are not the first of their kind, and placed PayPal's rules in that lineage: "Visa and MasterCard once imposed similar anti-steering rules on merchants accepting their cards but, after the Justice Department sued the networks for antitrust violations, they agreed in 2010 to eliminate their anti-steering rules as part of the settlement. With payments transitioning into the digital realm, PayPal has simply ripped a page right from the Visa and MasterCard [sic] playbook."
