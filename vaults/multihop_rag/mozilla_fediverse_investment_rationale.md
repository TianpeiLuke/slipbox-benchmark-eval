# Mozilla's Rationale For Investing In The Fediverse

Mozilla believes there is a better way forward for social networking and, more importantly, that now is exactly the right time to invest in that path. Its structural claim about why it can pursue that path: as a wholly owned subsidiary of a nonprofit, the company says it is not motivated by generating earnings for shareholders or returning a VC investment, allowing it to progress with a collaborative approach where it takes in input from a lot of different voices.

The grounds, per Mozilla Senior Director of Content Carolyn O'Hara, are a decade of observed behavior by incumbents: Mozilla's involvement came about because the company looked at the history of social media over the past decade and did not like what it saw. "I think that it's a pretty poor track record by existing companies that are only model motivated by profit and just insane user growth, and are willing to tolerate and amplify really toxic content because it looks like engagement," she says. "[They] aren't just putting forward the kind of standards that are good for people, but are just good for their bottom lines." The timing claim rests on a change in consumers: they are now becoming aware of this, which is new. "Consumers feel that the vibes are off a little bit…these platforms aren't necessarily working in their best interests, or satisfying them," O'Hara points out.

For Mozilla, that discontent presented an opportunity to enter the social networking space and compete for consumers' attention — but with a broader goal of helping the fediverse itself gain traction, not just its own Mastodon server. "We're not seeking to get, like 2 billion users on our instance. We want people to have choice and agency," says O'Hara, adding that choice is in line with Mozilla's values. Building directly on the fediverse is not the only way the company is prompting new forms of social networking: it has also financially backed a startup, Mammoth, the makers of a third-party app for Mastodon.
