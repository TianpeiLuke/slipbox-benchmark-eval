# Big Ten Commissioner Petitti's Punishment Dilemma

The New York Times set out on 3 November 2023 why any decision by Tony Petitti is constrained: the claim is that this is an unprecedented situation in which whatever he decides to do, or not do, will set a precedent, and the warrant is that the Big Ten has no investigators of its own and no punishment available that would fall only on those responsible.

The coaches' pressure supplies one side. "The Big Ten is so much more powerful than the NCAA," a third Big Ten coach said. "Why are you just sitting back and doing nothing about this? The Big Ten can't pound its chest for the last 30 years about how it does the right thing ethically (when other conferences like the SEC won't) and then have this go on. If this were a team in the bottom half of the Big Ten, would this be handled in the same way?" A source briefed on the coaches' call said Big Ten coaches are concerned about whether Michigan "should represent the Big Ten," adding: "No matter what happens, if Michigan continues to move forward, the clouds will follow. They're reading the tea leaves and wondering why the Big Ten hasn't done anything yet. Every week and every day that goes by, people are like, 'Something's gotta give.' It's getting a little bit out of hand when you see him (allegedly) on the Central Michigan sideline. The playing field is not level right now. How can you have a team that you know has a competitive advantage over you still being allowed to play? That's what the coaches are grappling with."

The constraints supply the other. The Big Ten itself doesn't have investigators, so it needs to rely upon the NCAA to do that part and to determine who else was involved in the alleged scouting scheme. And it's not clear exactly what the coaches would want the league to do to punish Michigan: banning the team from competing in the Big Ten championship, for example, would harm players who had nothing to do with the sign-stealing apparatus.
