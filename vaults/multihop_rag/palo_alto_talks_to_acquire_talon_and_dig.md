# Palo Alto's Talks To Acquire Talon And Dig

TechCrunch confirmed with multiple sources that Palo Alto Networks was in advanced negotiations to buy two Israeli security startups for around $1 billion altogether: Talon Cyber Security for between $600 million and $700 million, and Dig Security for between $300 million and $400 million.

Talon has developed an enterprise browser aimed at securing distributed workforces; Dig is a specialist in securing data across public clouds. Palo Alto's stock price had been on the rise on the back of strong earnings and growing demand for cybersecurity services, and the company was using that momentum to go shopping; it is publicly traded and at the time had a market cap of close to $70 billion. Both startups are less than three years old, and in both cases these would be strong outcomes compared to their existing valuations: Talon has raised around $143 million and Dig only around $45 million. Talon's investors include Entrée Capital, Evolution Equity and LightSpeed; Dig's include Signal Fire, Okta, CrowdStrike, Samsung and Felicis. The two share common investors too — the cybersecurity specialist investors Team8 and Cyverse Capital.

The approach was proactive: from TechCrunch's understanding Palo Alto approached both startups, neither of which was in the market to be acquired. Palo Alto Networks and Dig declined to comment, and Talon had yet to respond, but multiple sources close to the deal confirmed the prices and said the negotiations were in an advanced phase, although not closed. The Israeli publication Calcalist had in the past week reported on both deals with the same figures.
