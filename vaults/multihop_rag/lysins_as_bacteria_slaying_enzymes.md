# Lysins as Bacteria-Slaying Enzymes

Lysins are enzymes that tear open bacterial cell membranes and cause the microbes' contents to spill out, used in nature by phages to split bacteria open from the inside and now pursued as a treatment option alongside peptides such as PNAs.

They act like tiny swords that slice through the outer wall of a bacterial cell, spilling its guts, and these molecular sabers are unlikely to promote resistance because bacteria cannot easily change the essential cell-wall components that lysins target. Lysins slaughter bacteria quickly upon contact, and they can be very specific, killing some types of bacteria while sparing others; they can also be tweaked in the lab to change which bacteria they target, boost their potency and improve their durability in the body. The current boundary of the evidence is that some lysins have entered mid- and late-stage human trials with hundreds of participants, in which they have been tested as supplementary treatments to antibiotics but garnered mixed results.
