# Seahawks-Giants Second Half: Witherspoon's 97-Yard Pick-Six Ends The Contest

Seattle put the Oct. 2, 2023 "Monday Night Football" game away in the second half, winning 24-3 as Daniel Jones absorbed sack after sack. Geno Smith returned to the field for the Seahawks to start the half (10:03 p.m.). New York's defense forced a turnover on downs thanks to a major pass breakup from linebacker Micah McFadden, giving the Giants the ball at their own 15 (10:12 p.m.), but their drive was a three-and-out ending with Jones' sixth sack of the night, by eight-time Pro Bowler Bobby Wagner, and a punt from their own 12 (10:16 p.m.). Jason Myers missed a 53-yard field goal at 10:22 p.m., handing New York the ball at its own 34.

The decisive play came at 10:33 p.m. On fourth-and-1 from Seattle's 16, Brian Daboll had kept the offense on the field and Jones rolled out of play action for 11 yards to reach the five (10:30 p.m.); then Devon Witherspoon sat on an underneath route, intercepted Jones and returned it 97 yards for a touchdown, with the PAT good. What could have been a one-possession game became a three-score game, and Jones had two turnovers.

The rest was attrition. Wagner sacked Jones a seventh time to set up second-and-28 after a first-down penalty (10:45 p.m.); an eighth sack forced a punt on fourth-and-31 as fans began leaving the Meadowlands (10:48 p.m.); and after New York forced a three-and-out, Jones was sacked a ninth time on the first offensive play — a rough showing given Seattle had entered the week with just five sacks all season (10:56 p.m.). At 10:59 p.m. Seahawks safety Quandre Diggs intercepted Jones for his third turnover. Myers converted a 34-yard field goal at 11:04 p.m. for a three-touchdown lead with just over five minutes to play. Jones returned under center at 11:06 p.m., but was sacked for an 11th time at 11:09 p.m. for a turnover on downs, with Seattle taking over at the New York 35 and Drew Lock back in for Geno Smith. The game ended at 11:18 p.m. with Seattle 3-1 and New York 1-3; the Giants had now been dominated at home twice, following a 40-0 Week 1 blowout on "Sunday Night Football," and Jones' career primetime mark fell to 1-13, 0-3 on the season.
