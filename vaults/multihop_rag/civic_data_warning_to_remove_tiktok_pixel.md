# Civic Data's Warning To Remove The TikTok Pixel

Marketing and advisory agency Civic Data has warned its clients to remove the TikTok pixel from their websites on privacy grounds, arguing that the data the pixel collects cannot be matched to the consent consumers have actually given.

The grounds are the agency's own observations. In a client bulletin dated 20 December, obtained by The Age and the Sydney Morning Herald, Civic Data director Chris Brinkworth said his company had "repeatedly observed non-consensual collection of personal data on Australian wagering, telco, finance, supermarket, e-commerce, charity and media organisations' websites." The warrant joining that evidence to the recommendation is Australian law and its consent requirements: "This raises serious privacy concerns regarding the lack of transparency, misuse of personal information and disregard for consent requirements under current regulations such as the Privacy Act 1988. Civic Data's recommendation is that all Australian businesses consider removing the TikTok pixel and other TikTok integrations from their platforms if they cannot guarantee that the data usage matches the consent given by consumers."

The warning carries weight because of who receives it — Civic Data's clients include the accounting software company Xero, Ticketek, Carsales, RACV and BlueScope — and because the extent of data collected by TikTok's pixel without user consent has caused concern among Australian marketers more broadly. TikTok itself has rejected claims that the pixel breaches Australia's privacy laws, and describes the tool on its website as letting advertisers "track website visitor actions, like view page or purchase, and create audience segments to re-engage previous site visitors or model lookalikes to find new customers." Senator James Paterson has separately called for an urgent probe by Australia's information commissioner.
