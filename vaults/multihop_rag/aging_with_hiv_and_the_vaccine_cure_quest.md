# Aging with HIV and the Vaccine Cure Quest

About a quarter of people with HIV worldwide, and about half of adults with HIV in Western and Central Europe and North America, are already at least 50 years old — evidence that, as Dr. Anjali Sharma put it, "They're growing older with HIV; they're not dying from HIV or AIDS."

Quarraisha Abdool Karim, Sharma, Dr. Monica Gandhi and Dr. Raphael Landovitz agreed that many obstacles remain on the road to ending the AIDS epidemic, but that the tools to get there are already in hand; using those tools effectively could begin to meaningfully drive the number of new HIV infections toward zero, at which point HIV would become a manageable, chronic disease of the elderly. Sharma's own research focuses on aging populations with HIV. That prospect does not diminish the case for continued vaccine and cure research, Abdool Karim said, even though neither is likely to materialize in the next seven years. "We need to continue our investments to find a vaccine, to find a cure," she told Live Science. "Because that will then say, 'That's it.'"
