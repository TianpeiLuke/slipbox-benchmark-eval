# Retelling as a Narrative Strategy

Retellings are compelling, in the account Polygon gives on 2023-10-16, because they allow writers to toy with readers' preconceived notions of how the story "should go," both in terms of genre conventions and in terms of plot.

The illustration is Angela Carter, quoted not on Venom but on the wolf from "Little Red Riding Hood" in The Bloody Chamber, her collection of reimagined folk and fairy tales: "Those slavering jaws; the lolling tongue; the rime of saliva on the grizzled chops." The line comes from "The Company of Wolves," one of three retellings of "Little Red Riding Hood" in the collection, each with its own spin on the familiar tale.

The relation that makes the strategy work is shared foreknowledge. We know Little Red Riding Hood will go to her grandmother's house, where she will find her grandmother has been eaten by a wolf, just as we know Spider-Man will be bitten by a radioactive spider; the fun, both for the author and the reader, comes in deviating from those norms — in finding new ways to tell old tales.
