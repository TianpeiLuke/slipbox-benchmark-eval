# Data Breach

## Definition

A data breach, in the corpus, is an incident in which hackers or unintended exposure let unauthorized parties reach personal or organizational data — spanning the 2017 Equifax hack, the 2023 23andMe breach, an individual's hacked Roblox account, and, more loosely, TikTok's pre-consent data scraping, which a senator branded a "mass breach of privacy" (doc_0015, doc_0334, doc_0178, doc_0189).

## Context

The corpus uses the term across several registers of the same underlying event. The canonical reference point is the Equifax data breach of 2017, invoked in a discussion of Google's search-revenue deals as the event that made identity-theft-protection services like LifeLock a hot commercial topic (doc_0015). The clearest 2023 instance is 23andMe: hackers first accessed the personal data of about 14,000 users (0.1% of customers), a number the company later acknowledged undercounted the real scope, in a breach it disclosed in October 2023 (doc_0334). The same TechCrunch weekly roundup that covers 23andMe also reports a related but distinct exposure — patient scans and health records "spilling online" through security weaknesses in the decades-old DICOM medical-imaging standard, which made private data accessible on the open web without any hacker having to breach a perimeter at all (doc_0334). At smaller scale, the term extends to individual account compromise: Sarah Michelle Gellar described her son's Roblox account being "hacked," which she treated as serious enough to cancel a linked credit card (doc_0178). The corpus also shows the vocabulary of "breach" stretched to cover non-hacking exposure: Australian coverage of TikTok's tracking pixel, which scraped site visitors' names, emails, and phone numbers before consent was given, was branded a "mass breach of privacy" by a senator — a data-handling violation rather than an intrusion, but framed with the same breach language (doc_0189).

## Key Characteristics

- **Unauthorized access or exposure, not just data collection** — a breach denotes information reaching parties (or the open web) it was never meant to reach, whether via hacking (doc_0334, doc_0178) or unintentional exposure of insecure infrastructure (doc_0334's DICOM item).
- **Initial disclosures tend to understate scope** — 23andMe first quantified only 0.1% of customers as affected before acknowledging the wider group whose data was exposed (doc_0334).
- **Consequences reach beyond the breached company** — Gellar's response to her son's Roblox account being hacked was to cancel a family credit card, showing breach fallout propagating into unrelated financial accounts (doc_0178).
- **The term is applied loosely** — commentary uses "breach" both for classic hacking incidents (23andMe, Roblox) and for non-consensual data collection practices (TikTok's pixel), even though the latter is a policy/consent violation rather than an intrusion (doc_0189).

## Background (external)

More broadly (outside this corpus), a data breach is any security incident in which unauthorized parties access sensitive, confidential, or protected information (IBM, "What Is a Data Breach?", accessed 2026-09-02, https://www.ibm.com/topics/data-breach).
