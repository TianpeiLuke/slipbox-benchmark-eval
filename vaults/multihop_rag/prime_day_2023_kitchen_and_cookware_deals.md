# Prime Day 2023 Kitchen And Cookware Deals

WIRED's kitchen section spans blenders, a stand mixer, a sous vide cooker, an AeroGarden, a Zojirushi rice cooker, Le Creuset cast iron, reusable wraps and bags, a water bottle, snacks, All-Clad pans, a hand grinder, an air fryer, mugs, a kettle and a chef's knife.

The Vitamix 5200 is a wedding-registry staple you can simply buy, and it converted a writer who was never really a blender person, as WIRED contributor Joe Ray also was: powerful and durable for smoothies, soups, or sauces, and not cheap even on sale but worth it. The Explorian is Vitamix's cheaper entry-level alternative, still with a powerful two-horsepower engine that reduces the toughest nuts to a creamy paste. The NutriBullet Smart Touch has a 1,500-watt motor base with a 64-ounce pitcher, a locking lid with a spout, an included tamper, and presets for purée, soups, frozen drinks and smoothies. The KitchenAid Artisan Mini suits smaller kitchens and households, with the full-size 5-quart version also on sale for $380 ($60 off). Sous vide cooking stops you overcooking food, and the Nano 3.0 is one of WIRED's favorite starter precision cookers. The AeroGarden goes on sale all the time but the price is right; reviewer Louryn Strampe found it super easy to use — place seed pods in their holes, keep the bottom filled with water, and wait — though the light is bright, making it less than ideal in a studio apartment, and it grows herbs like crazy. Zojirushi's rice cookers are a WIRED favorite, using "fuzzy logic technology" with a microcomputer — hence "micom" in the product name — to make tiny adjustments in heat placement for perfectly done grains with no burned or wet spots. WIRED loves Le Creuset's enameled cast iron, and this roasting pan is a good size for everything from chicken to prime rib. Beeswax wraps of organic cotton and sustainably sourced beeswax, with jojoba oil and tree resin, replace annoying plastic wrap and are waterproof, though they should be rinsed in cold water since hot water could melt the wax. Stasher bags do everything a single-use Ziploc does without being thrown out, and are dishwasher-, freezer- and microwave-safe, useful too for organizing screws, crayons and bobby pins; Rezip reusable baggies are also loved — durable, freezer-safe, dishwasher-friendly and much cheaper — with a 5-piece set discounted among other options. The HydroJug (9/10, WIRED Recommends) is big and heavy but useful if you're unmotivated to fill smaller bottles, with the sale price extending to a variety of colors. Amazon also has cheap Flamin' Hot Cheetos multipacks, best bought via Subscribe and Save for the lowest price, cancellable after the first order, plus candy from $2. The All-Clad set includes 10-inch and 12-inch frying pans, a nice combo for families, with D3 stainless steel construction giving very even heat distribution and durability — reviewer Scott Gilbertson has seen these warp after about 10 years, but not his own. WIRED hasn't tried 1Zpresso's J-Max hand grinder but it gets high marks from espresso gurus for a very even fine grind, at over triple the price of its favorite hand grinder, the Hario Skerton Pro ($50). Ninja's Max XL is the compact pick in WIRED's air fryer guide, space-saving enough to leave counter room for prepping vegetables, with a max crisp setting perfect for homemade fries and the ability to adapt conventional oven recipes. Louryn Strampe's favorite coffee mugs are a brilliant shade of pink with construction that makes the drink appear to float and a 16-ounce capacity. The double-walled Zwilling kettle, recommended in WIRED's Best Kettles guide for households with kids who touch everything dangerous, has six presets and a dedicated baby formula button. And the Victorinox, once WIRED's top chef knife pick, is still great, with a nearly nonstick finish that hardly anything sticks to, not even fresh cilantro.
