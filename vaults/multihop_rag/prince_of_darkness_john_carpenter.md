# Prince of Darkness (John Carpenter)

Prince of Darkness is John Carpenter's apocalypse film and, in Polygon's view, possibly the scariest of the three apocalypse movies he made — The Thing being the best known and In the Mouth of Madness the zaniest. It was the Oct. 7 pick in Polygon's 2023-10-10 Halloween Countdown, listed on Peacock.

Polygon's framing is that there are not enough movies about the end of the world: plenty are about preventing catastrophe at that scale, but rarely do they have the follow-through to truly get existentially terrifying, and Carpenter is no coward.

The film follows a group of college researchers sent to study a strange canister found in the basement of a church; the canister is filled with some kind of liquid that defies science, and when it starts to be released a strange evil seems to come into the world with it. Critic Austen Goslin calls this Carpenter at his most ambitious: though most of the movie is set in just one building, all manner of horror makes its way inside — people made of bugs, horrific zombies, an extradimensional Satan, and things even worse to behold — in an onslaught of creepy, skin-crawling images built out of a unique mythology about the relationship between good and evil, and, most importantly, after that parade of horrors seems to end, Carpenter saves the biggest, weirdest, most dreadful scare for last.
