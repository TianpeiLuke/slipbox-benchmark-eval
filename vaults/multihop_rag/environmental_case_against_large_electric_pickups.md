# The Environmental Case Against Large Electric Pickups

Even though electric vehicles are generally better for the climate than their gas-guzzling counterparts because they have no tailpipe emissions, large electric pickups like the Cybertruck carry a higher environmental cost than smaller EVs — because extra-large vehicles typically require more energy, and thus more battery materials.

The grounds start with the fact that all cars are pollutive, including consumer EVs: they require lithium, release tire particles into the air, and demand more energy than more efficient means of getting around such as public transit, cycling and walking, while battery material mining is also linked to worker exploitation. Experts have raised red flags over the ballooning size and weight of vehicles in the U.S. specifically, and the warrant joining size to harm runs through two channels: more battery material per vehicle, and collision risk, since bigger and heavier vehicles are less safe for everyone around them, especially pedestrians.

The comparative conclusion follows directly: among EVs, vehicles with smaller batteries are generally better for the environment.
