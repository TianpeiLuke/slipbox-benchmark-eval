# Advantages Of Prop Bets

Sporting News makes three arguments for prop bets: their lines move more slowly than other bet types, they offer greater variety, and they are usually very easy to understand. Every bet has positives and negatives, and props are no different — but these three are the positives.

The slow-moving lines create a timing edge. Prop bet odds are developed from game lines and totals at sportsbooks, yet prop lines tend to adjust more slowly. If you are watching New York Jets vs. Buffalo Bills and see the total shift because of an incoming winter storm, many bettors will scramble to get on the "under" before the main line moves too far; the better move is to scope out the prop bets for individual players, because most betting sites do not have the personnel to keep tabs on every possible player, so there may be value in an "under" on a quarterback's passing yards while those lines have not caught up.

The variety argument rests on what the main markets lack. Moneylines, point spreads and totals are constant in every major sport but do not offer much variety; props do, and they can add interest to a game that seems one-sided in the major markets. Larger sporting events, especially the Super Bowl, offer a massive variety of bets ranging from the typical to the exotic and everything in between.

The comprehensibility argument is that props tend to ask a question of the bettor — will a specific player hit a home run, will Team X have over/under 3.5 field goals in a game, will Team Y give up a powerplay goal — and whatever the question, props are fairly easy for a newcomer to understand.
