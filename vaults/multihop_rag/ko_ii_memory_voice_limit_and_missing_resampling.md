# K.O. II Memory, Voice Limit And Missing Resampling

The K.O. II's practical ceiling is not its 64MB of memory but its 12-voice limit: the memory translates to about 11 minutes of samples at the 46kHz/16bit at which the device records — halved if you sample in stereo — whereas the voice limit means it can make only 12 sounds at once, so six stereo samples playing simultaneously already hits it.

Engadget's reviewer judged that even going all out, over five minutes of samples should be plenty for most songs, and that their own compositions weren't interesting enough to hit the voice threshold — but maximalists should keep it in mind. What sharpens the limit is a missing feature: resampling, the common technique of merging separate sounds down into one new sample to avoid hitting the voice limit on other devices, is also the technique for baking in effects and modulation. Since the K.O. II can only manage one master effect at any one time, its absence is all the more obvious, and it is possibly the biggest complaint among users the reviewer has seen so far, after fadergate. There are workarounds, but they involve recording out into another device and sampling that back in — and "no one should have a sampler for their sampler, not in this economy." Teenage Engineering does have a decent track record of adding functionality via firmware updates; the company added a new effect to the OP-1 Field as the review was being written.
