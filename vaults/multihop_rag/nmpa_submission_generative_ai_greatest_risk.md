# The NMPA Submission Calling Generative AI The Greatest Risk To The Creative Class

The National Music Publishers Association's submission to the US Copyright Office, dated October 30, 2023, argues that generative AI may be the greatest risk to the human creative class that has ever existed — and therefore that the Office should adopt proactive protections for human creators and, where uncertain, err on their side.

The argument is deliberately framed as pro-technology at its base. The submission starts by stressing that the NMPA's membership — US music publishers major and independent — are "not opposed" to AI: "There is widespread belief in the music industry that great benefits could come from generative AI systems that can assist human creators." It then goes for the jugular: "However, the development of the generative AI marketplace is marked by breathtaking speed, size and complexity. Hindsight may well prove that there is no hyperbole in saying that generative AI is the greatest risk to the human creative class that has ever existed." The warrant for urgency is temporal: "Even more alarming is that we do not know how long the window is to act before it is too late. We therefore implore the [US Copyright] Office to support proactive protections for human creators and, where there is uncertainty, to err on the side of protecting human creators." MBW notes the position is not a surprise from an organisation that earlier in 2023 joined the fledgling Human Artistry Campaign, whose goal in broadest terms is to ensure AI does not replace or "erode" human culture — yet the submission is hardly a Luddite diatribe against high tech; it advocates regulations and principles it sees as the right way to keep the interests of musical artists and rights holders from being subjugated in the frenzy to build an AI-powered world.
