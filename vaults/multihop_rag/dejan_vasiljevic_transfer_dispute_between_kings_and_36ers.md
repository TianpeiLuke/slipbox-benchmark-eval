# The Vasiljevic Dispute Between The Kings And The 36ers

Adelaide coach CJ Bruton accused Sydney of not looking after Dejan Vasiljevic as the war of words between the 36ers and Kings over the sharpshooting guard escalated in October 2023 — his claim being that the Kings had their chance and chose not to take it, so the grievance is theirs to drop.

The facts both sides argue from are the same. Vasiljevic, a two-time championship winner with Sydney, had originally been released from his Kings contract to chase his NBA dream, but when the Washington Wizards waived him just before pre-season training camp the 26-year-old decided to return to the NBL, signing with the Sixers on the Tuesday — which did not please Sydney's powerbrokers. Kings chief executive Chris Pongrass said: "We originally amended the contract in good faith and completed our roster with the understanding that 'DJ' (Vasiljevic) would play internationally this season. We are disappointed with how this has been handled and another NBL team's role in this process." Kings co-owner and Australian basketball legend Andrew Bogut posted on X that Sydney had granted the release "after he told us he wanted to 'chase his NBA dream', not his NBL dream with another club". Vasiljevic, fronting the press as a 36er for the first time on the Wednesday, fired back that "they made me look like a villain, they made Adelaide look like the villain".

Bruton's counter on the Thursday night rests on the release itself: "I've only got my perspective – you (Kings) had the chance to have him, you chose not to have him. He's moved on. Move on. If you wanted to do something more, look after the kid. Talk to him, championship ceremony or whatever … but that's on them (Sydney). That's not on us or me." On the court, Vasiljevic scored 17 points in his Adelaide debut that night, including 11 in a productive first quarter, but could not prevent the bottom-placed Sixers losing 102-85 to South East Melbourne Phoenix. "I thought his energy was good," Bruton said of his prized recruit. "With more time to settle in, the better he'll become."
