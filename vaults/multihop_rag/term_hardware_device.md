# Hardware Device

## Definition

A hardware device, in this corpus, is a physical consumer electronics product — a smart speaker, handheld game console, smart glasses, or similar standalone gadget — that a company designs, manufactures, and sells as a distinct unit, as opposed to software or a service.

## Context

The corpus covers hardware devices from two angles: unveilings/reviews and retail deals. Google's annual "hardware event" is where the company unveils an entire slate of new devices at once — the Pixel 8 and Pixel 8 Pro phones, the Pixel Fold, Pixel Buds, and the Pixel Watch 2 — showing that "hardware" in this reporting names the physical product line a company ships, distinct from the software layered on top (Android 14, Bard, Google Assistant) that runs on it.

Retail-deal roundups organize hardware devices into shopping categories — Amazon's Cyber Monday guide lists separate sections for Echo/Fire TV/Kindle devices, speakers, headphones, tablets, and gaming gear — treating "speaker" as one device sub-category among several, not a synonym for hardware in general.

Smart glasses form a recurring device category: the Ray-Ban Meta smart glasses combine a camera, open-ear audio, and a voice assistant, while Amazon's Echo Frames center on giving hands-free access to Alexa, and both trace the category back to the Google Glass Explorer Edition as its 2013 origin point.

Handheld gaming hardware is treated the same way: the Steam Deck OLED is discussed as a physical device with its own chip, screen, and battery specifications, refreshed mid-cycle the way a phone or headphone line would be.

## Key Characteristics

- **Physical and purchasable** — a hardware device is a standalone unit with a price and a retail listing (the Echo Frames at $314.99, the Ray-Ban Meta glasses at $299, the Steam Deck OLED at $549), not a subscription or app.
- **Distinct from the software it runs** — Google's hardware event unveils devices (Pixel 8 Pro, Pixel Watch 2) separately from the software updates (Android 14, Bard) that ship alongside them.
- **Organized into device sub-categories** — retail guides split hardware into categories such as Echo/Fire TV/Kindle, speakers, headphones, tablets, and gaming, each reviewed and discounted on its own terms.
- **Defined by internal components** — reviews describe a device's identity through its chip, screen, and battery (the Steam Deck OLED's die-shrunk APU, 7.4-inch OLED panel, and 50Whr battery), the specs that distinguish one hardware generation from the next.
- **Ambiguous surface form** — "speaker" in the corpus denotes both an audio hardware device (a smart speaker) and the office of Speaker of the House; only the device sense belongs to this concept.
