# VPN Services That Missed The Cut

Four widely known services were rejected, each for a specific defect rather than general weakness. NordVPN did not quite make the cut because it is overhyped and underwhelming: as Engadget wrote in its full review, the pricing — up to $14.49 for a "complete" subscription — seemed high compared with other services, and its free or lower cost plans just did not have the same wide variety of features as competitors.

TunnelBear was not a top choice despite its cute graphics and user friendliness, because it failed numerous basic security tests from Consumer Reports and had limited availability across platforms like Linux. It did, however, get a major security boost in July when it updated to support the WireGuard protocol across more of its platforms.

Bitdefender does not offer support for devices like routers, which limits its cross-platform accessibility, and it also lacked a transparency report or third-party audit to confirm its security specs.

Atlas ranked lower on the speed tests than the other VPNs, with a notably slower difference on web browsing and streaming tests — a good option otherwise, but one that could easily cause headaches for those chasing high speed connections. Security-wise, an Atlas VPN vulnerability leaked Linux users' real IP addresses.
