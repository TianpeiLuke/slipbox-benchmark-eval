# Silent Hill (2006 Film Adaptation)

Silent Hill is the 2006 film adaptation of the video game series, and Polygon argues it deserved so much better than the moment it arrived in. It was the Oct. 20 pick in the 2023-10-10 Halloween Countdown, listed on Peacock.

Polygon situates it in a no man's land for horror movies: by 2006 audiences were four years out from the blockbuster prestige of Gore Verbinski's The Ring and the J-horror import boom that followed it in America, and only just beginning to transition into the Saw-dominated years of "realistic" horror, still almost a decade away from the arthouse horror boom that would kick off closer to 2014 with films like It Follows.

The story follows Rose, whose daughter Sharon is plagued with awful episodes of sleepwalking and nightmares about the mysterious abandoned town of Silent Hill. Rose brings Sharon to the town in a last-ditch attempt to find answers, but things go horribly wrong when the fog-shrouded town seems to transport them, along with police officer Cybil Bennett, to a new monstrous dimension; meanwhile Rose's husband Christopher sets off to Silent Hill to find his family. Inside the town the film takes the most inspiration from the games, with some of Silent Hill's most recognizable enemies and monsters chasing Cybil, Sharon, and Rose through the town and eventually into the iconic Pyramid Head — segments critic AG calls impressively scary and steeped in the dreadful atmosphere that made the games famous.

Goslin's favorite part comes when Christopher arrives: rather than finding his family, all he finds is an abandoned mining town, so for most of the movie the women's frantic running and hiding from monsters is paralleled by Christopher wandering the exact same areas, filled with a dreadful sense that his family is close but completely unable to see them or the horrors threatening to kill them. He calls it not a subtle metaphor for the differences in the ways men and women go through the world, but an effective one that creates a level of crushing existential terror and defeat few movies ever come close to.
