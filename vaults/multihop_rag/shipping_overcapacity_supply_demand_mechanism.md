# The Shipping Overcapacity Supply-Demand Mechanism

After Covid's massive demand shock, the culprit behind shipping's collapse is now too much supply. The mechanism is a combination of economic forces: goods demand is returning to pre-pandemic levels just as supply is rising in the form of new, bigger ships. The structural reason the two cannot be matched is the industry's lead times — it can take two to three years to build a container ship, which typically operates for about 25 years, so timing their launch and retirement with the ebbs and flows of the business cycle is inherently difficult.

The severity of the resulting swing is unusual. Booms-turned-busts have been more abrupt and sensational elsewhere, but rarely has an established industry so tied to the global economy lurched from historic profits to below break-even levels more directly than the shipping lines that move 80% of the world's merchandise trade have in 2023.
