# Christopher Morel Cubs Trade Candidacy

Christopher Morel was ranked No. 22 on Yardbarker's Nov. 13, 2023 list of the top 25 MLB offseason trade candidates and described as likely to be a divisive player around the league: he had huge raw power and connected on 26 home runs while slugging .508 in 107 games for the Cubs, and the right-handed hitter owned a .241/.311/.471 line in just over 850 MLB plate appearances over the past two seasons, capable of carrying a lineup when hot.

He also struck out more than 30% of the time and did not have a defensive fit, having played all three outfield spots and each of second base, third base and shortstop in the majors without rating well anywhere. The Cubs used him mostly at designated hitter that year and had suggested they would get him first base run in 2024 — not a great fit for a plus runner with top-of-the-scale arm strength, yet Morel had not shown himself capable of handling more important positions on a regular basis. How teams projected him defensively could affect both whether he was traded and what kind of return the Cubs could receive: as a first baseman or DH he was a fringe regular with a statistical profile that would paint him as a lumbering slugger rather than a dynamic athlete, but if another team felt he could hold his own at second base or in center field he became far more desirable.

The Cubs had no urgency to trade him, since Morel was controllable through 2028 and would not reach arbitration for at least another season. Yet president of baseball operations Jed Hoyer had alluded to the possibility of a deal, saying the previous week that "another team might be able to put him [at second base]," where the Cubs had Nico Hoerner.
