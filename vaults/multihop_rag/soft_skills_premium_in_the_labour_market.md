# The Soft Skills Premium Employers Actually Pay For

"Soft skills" — also called character skills or behavioural skills — are the capabilities beyond technical proficiency that Adam Grant says employers put a premium on: communication, teamwork, leadership. "Parents may well push their kids in directions that seem to be really practical, but don't know what employers actually value," he says. The term originated in the 1960s with US Army psychologists tasked with developing proficiency in soldiers beyond the literal "hard skills" of working with weapons and machines, and these skills are becoming increasingly important in a world where technology may replace other jobs.

The boundary case that makes the premium matter is the impossibility of long-range career planning. Grant recounts chatting with a "very influential person in tech" who told him it was impossible to plan a career more than a couple of years ahead — when that person graduated from university, the likes of Mark Zuckerberg and Elon Musk hadn't yet finished school. "We couldn't predict what kinds of companies were going to exist, and we didn't know what kinds of jobs were going to exist."
