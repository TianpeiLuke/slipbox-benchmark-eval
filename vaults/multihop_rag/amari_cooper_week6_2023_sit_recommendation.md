# Amari Cooper As A Week 6 2023 Sit Against The 49ers

CBS Sports listed Amari Cooper under "Sit Him" for Week 6 of the 2023 season against San Francisco: the claim is that anyone starting Cooper should have lower expectations and basically pray to the fantasy gods for garbage production or ludicrous target volume, and the warrant is that Cleveland is down to a practice-squad quarterback whose career record with receivers is poor, against the defense that allows the fewest yards per catch to wideouts in the league.

The quarterback grounds are the heart of it. With rookie Dorian Thompson-Robinson at quarterback in the previous game, Cooper saw six targets and caught one for 16 yards, with half the targets uncatchable; in his previous three games with Deshaun Watson he had at least seven targets in each and at least 16 PPR points in the last two. Watson has not practised with the team since, and P.J. Walker was named the starter despite being on the practice squad, taking reps this week in preparation for the 49ers. Walker was cut by the Bears after the preseason before linking up with Cleveland; he has five career passing touchdowns in 15 games and seven starts, has had multiple passing scores in one NFL game, and has produced only two 100-yard receiver games in his career, both by D.J. Moore in 2020 and 2022, with two other 70-yard games, by Curtis Samuel in 2020 and Terrace Marshall in 2022. The one good thing the column can extract is that Walker has thrown to wide receivers on 71.2% of his career throws. Comparing the past four seasons of Jacoby Brissett and Walker, Brissett had a considerably higher completion rate (63% to 57.5%) with a lower interception rate (1.7% to 4.8%) and lower off-target rate (10.3% to 18.9%), while Walker's average depth of target was slightly higher (8.8 to 8.3), both sitting right around 6.5 yards per attempt. Cooper's own backup-quarterback baseline is a genuine counterweight: in effectively 30 career games without Dak Prescott, Derek Carr or Deshaun Watson on the field he has 13 touchdowns, seven of them from Brissett, and seven 100-yard games, four with Brissett, and more importantly he cleared at least 15 PPR points in 13 of those 30 games, six with Brissett — 43% of the time.

The matchup is the closing ground. No team allows fewer yards per catch to wideouts than the 49ers at 9.7, and they are thrown at a ton by offenses that have to play from behind; only three receivers have topped 15 PPR points against them — Puka Nacua on 20 targets, Marquise Brown on 10 targets and Michael Wilson on two touchdowns — and only one other, Tutu Atwell on nine targets, has managed more than 12. The verdict is that it's hard to trust Cooper for a reliable stat line, with a lot of downside for a single-digit-point week, making him at best a low-end No. 3 receiver, behind Tyler Lockett, Chris Godwin or Drake London.
