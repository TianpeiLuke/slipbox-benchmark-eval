# The DIY Sleep Hacks Insomniacs Report Trying

The Independent's October 2023 report on insomniacs catalogues the remedies people actually use: lavender oil, eating kiwis, masturbation — the days of counting sheep long gone. Jennifer Aniston's top tip for battling insomnia is to "sleep with my phone at least five feet away from me," while Martha Stewart recommends focusing on your breathing; in her 1973 feminist novel Fear of Flying, Erica Jong wrote that sometimes the only way to overcome sleeplessness is to pretend you don't care about nodding off, after which "sleep became piqued, like a rejected lover, and crept up to try to seduce you." The article notes that the internet is littered with tips promising sound sleep, that not all of them are effective and none are foolproof, and that kiwis contain serotonin and antioxidants, which is why they are thought to have sleep-promoting benefits.

Two case histories illustrate the trial-and-error. Social media consultant and mother of two Amy Fieldhouse-Downes began suffering from insomnia after the birth of her second son in 2019 and believes it was rooted in anxiety: "If you Google how to go to sleep, I've probably tried everything on there. Going to bed earlier, going to bed later, meditations that would get cut off by an advert, lavender products, sleep cream, eye masks, Sominex sleep tablets. And I even used it as an excuse to have a few glasses of wine." Only specialised earplugs finally let her rest. Long-time insomniac and video producer Preshita has tried "from drinking chamomile tea to counting backwards from 1,000," and found that showering before bed and keeping her bedroom clutter-free were most effective: "Someone recommended masturbating, and that was another thing that worked."
