# Week 14 Standard-League Running Backs To Start

Sporting News kept Christian McCaffrey at No. 1 in its Week 14 standard-league running back rankings and argued for a top tier built around workload and matchup, publishing on Dec. 7, 2023 with only one week standing between then and the fantasy football playoffs.

The framing is urgency: it was make-or-break time in fantasy land, so every decision could be the difference between an optimal finish and a womp-womp ending, and the rankings were meant to guide managers to the studs, sleepers, busts and injuries of note going into the most important week of the regular season. The 2023 season's rollercoaster rolled on through Week 13 with yet another fresh batch of injuries, which the rankings promised to navigate.

McCaffrey (vs. Seahawks) retained No. 1 especially because he was playing at home against a Seattle team he had just roughed up for 139 yards and two touchdowns on Thanksgiving. Sporting News stuck with Austin Ekeler (vs. Broncos) even though he had managed just 2.99 yards per carry since returning from injury eight games earlier, with the explicit condition that if Denver's abysmal run defense did not cure what ailed him, Ekeler would be demoted for the fantasy playoffs. Travis Etienne Jr. (@ Browns) drew a tough matchup on paper, but the Rams' Kyren Williams (@ Ravens) might beg to differ — Williams put 112 total yards and a touchdown on Cleveland in Week 13 — and with Etienne's usage rate and the Jaguars hitting their stride offensively, Sporting News was confident in ranking the third-year back No. 3.

Two sets of teammates made the top 10: Detroit's David Montgomery and Jahmyr Gibbs (@ Bears) and Miami's Raheem Mostert and De'Von Achane (vs. Titans). Both duos had such solid workloads in high-flying, run-first offenses — and such juicy matchups — that Sporting News could not imagine any of them finishing outside the elite eight; Montgomery and Gibbs combined for 193 yards and two touchdowns against Chicago in Week 11, and Tennessee had just surrendered 31 points to Indy, so Miami would be dominating time of possession and running early and often against Will Levis and company. Breece Hall (vs. Texans) was backed because Houston had surrendered 111.8 scrimmage yards per game to running backs that season and DeMeco Ryans' unit had allowed 14 total rushing touchdowns on the year; Sporting News liked Hall's upside even more than dual-purpose backs Alvin Kamara (vs. Panthers) and Saquon Barkley (vs. Packers), but only just, with Carolina and Green Bay both sitting in the bottom 10 in the NFL in standard fantasy points allowed to running backs.
