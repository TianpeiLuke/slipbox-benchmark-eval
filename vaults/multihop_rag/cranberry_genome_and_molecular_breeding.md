# The Cranberry Genome and Molecular Breeding

The cranberry is a diploid — each cell contains one set of chromosomes from the maternal parent and one from the paternal parent — with 24 chromosomes and a genome size less than one-tenth that of the human genome, which makes it comparatively simple to breed.

Insights like these help scientists better understand where potentially valuable genes might be located in the cranberry genome, and diploid crops tend to have fewer genes associated with a single trait, which makes breeding them to emphasize that trait much simpler. Researchers have also described the genetics of the cultivated cranberry's wild relative, the "small cranberry" (Vaccinium oxycoccos); comparing the two can help determine where the cultivated cranberry's agronomically valuable traits reside in its genome, and where some of the small cranberry's cold hardiness might come from. Researchers are developing molecular markers — tools to determine where certain genes or sequences of interest reside within a genome — to help determine the best combinations of genes from different varieties of cranberry that can enhance desired traits, for example to make the fruits larger, more firm or redder in colour.
