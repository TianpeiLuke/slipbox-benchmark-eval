# Castlevania: Nocturne Surpasses The Original Netflix Series

The Verge argued that Castlevania: Nocturne steps its game up considerably on its predecessor, using the franchise's iconic lore to challenge your understanding of what it truly means to fight for freedom, equality, and fraternity rather than settling and coasting on classic Castlevania vibes.

The grounds start with the reasons for doubt. Netflix's first Castlevania animated series excelled by balancing quiet, beautifully performed moments of human drama with explosive and artful action set pieces dazzling enough to evoke playing Konami's classic side-scroller for the first time, but its regular issues with pacing and with effectively cultivating characters from season to season made another entry a dubious prospect — this one a spinoff from executive producer Clive Bradley and co-directors Sam Deats and Adam Deats. Nocturne both looks and feels a lot like its predecessor in its focus on a tortured scion waging war against machiavellian, bloodsucking ghouls who fancy themselves undead gods walking among mortal livestock. The warrant for the favourable verdict is that the new show does something the first did not: it puts the lore to thematic work instead of trading on atmosphere.
