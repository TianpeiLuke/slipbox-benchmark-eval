# The Escalation Of Alameda's Negative Balance

Alameda Research first ran a negative balance on FTX in late 2019, and the amount it was allowed to go negative crept upward in steps, each one justified after the fact — the model of the fraud that Gary Wang's and Caroline Ellison's testimony described was escalation rather than a single decision. As The Verge's reporter summarised Wang's testimony: first it was okay to dip into FTX's profits, then it was okay to take more, then even more; first it was a little line of credit just to provide liquidity, then a bigger one, then an effectively limitless one.

The steps ran as follows. Because Alameda and FTX shared an open-plan office, Wang heard a trader ask Bankman-Fried whether it was okay to keep withdrawing money from the negative account, and Bankman-Fried said it was okay as long as the amount stayed lower than FTX's total trading revenue; he was CEO of both companies at the time. Around 2019 or 2020, Wang checked the database and found Alameda was negative by about $200 million, more than the $150 million FTX had made in revenue — which had to mean Alameda was taking customer money. Wang was surprised and discussed it with Bankman-Fried, who told him he just needed to take into account the value of FTT, the token the two of them had launched; Wang trusted his judgment and did not pursue it. Later Alameda's balance was more negative than FTT and the trading revenue combined, and Wang had further conversations with Bankman-Fried about it. Wang testified he knew at the time that Alameda was using FTX customers' funds and that he knew it was wrong.

The same gradual accommodation shows in Ellison's account. Choking back tears, she testified: "When I started working at Alameda, I don't think I would have believed you if you told me I would be sending false balance sheets to our lenders or taking customer money, but over time, it was something I became more comfortable with."
