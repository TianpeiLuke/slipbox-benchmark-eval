# Shortened Solar Cycles During the Maunder Minimum

A pre-print paper published in June 2023 suggests that solar cycles during the Maunder Minimum — a period of reduced solar activity between 1645 and 1715 — likely lasted only eight years, much shorter than the roughly 11 years a cycle takes today.

The sun currently takes around 11 years to complete one solar cycle, during which it transitions from solar minimum to solar maximum and back again. The researchers reached the shorter figure by studying ancient texts unearthed in Korea, which contain detailed accounts of auroral displays during the Maunder Minimum. Scientists have not been able to properly explain why the solar cycle shortened during this period.
