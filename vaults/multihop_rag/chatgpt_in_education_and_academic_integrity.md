# ChatGPT In Education And Academic Integrity

New research from Stanford University shows that the popularization of chatbots like ChatGPT has not caused an increase in cheating across U.S. high schools: in a survey of more than 40 U.S. high schools, researchers found that cheating rates were similar across the board that year.

That finding runs against the reception ChatGPT met in schools. ChatGPT has been criticized by educators for encouraging cheating, resulting in bans in certain school districts, and has been controversial in the classroom because of plagiarism. OpenAI has responded on two fronts: COO Brad Lightcap revealed at a San Francisco conference that the company would likely create a team to identify ways AI and ChatGPT can be used in education, and OpenAI released a guide for teachers using ChatGPT in the classroom, offering a selection of ways to put the chatbot to work and seeking to rehabilitate the system's image in education.
