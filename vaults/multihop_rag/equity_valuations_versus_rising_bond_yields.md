# Equity Valuations Must Be Judged Against Rising Bond Yields

Equity valuations do not function in a silo, so with bond yields at multi-decade highs investors may have to assess current valuations not just against the last ten years' valuations but against prior periods as well. The ground is that investors have many options, including fixed deposits, bonds and real estate, and when other assets get more attractive one must assess the scope for valuation change in equities; as of 14 October 2023, interest rates and bond yields in many developed markets, including the US, were at their highest levels since 2007.

The warrant runs through the earnings yield an equity offers relative to a risk-free alternative. Near-zero interest rates and low bond yields made stocks with an earnings yield of a mere 1-2 per cent attractive for a foreign portfolio investor: stocks such as Asian Paints, Nestle India and ABB India traded at PEs of 65, 83 and 85 times respectively at the time of writing, implying earnings yields of 1.5 per cent for Asian Paints and 1.2 per cent for Nestle and ABB. Such earnings yields were fine for FPIs to hold when compared with zero interest income in their domestic market, but with US bond yields offering 5 per cent these may not be so attractive even when considering growth prospects. While Indian interest rates were always higher, investors in equities benefited from global investors' hunger for yields, and that advantage is diminishing today. The article states explicitly that this is not a recommendation on the stocks but pointers on factors to consider in making the relative valuation process more foolproof.
