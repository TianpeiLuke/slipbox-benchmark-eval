# Metroid Prime Remastered

Metroid Prime Remastered, developed by Retro Studios for Nintendo Switch, is ranked 19 on Polygon's best video games of 2023 list of 2023-10-31. Polygon's claim is that few games from 2002 hold up as well as Metroid Prime, and the remastered version — surprise-dropped during February's Nintendo Direct — proves Samus Aran's first-person adventure is still worth experiencing, whether for the first time or, in the critic's case, the fourth.

Retro Studios' take on one of sci-fi's most famous intergalactic bounty hunters controversially took her out of the 2D puzzle-platformer realm that made her famous, although Metroid Fusion also came out in 2002 as a gift for the 2D Metroid purists — which critic M. Myers suggests may also be why Fusion joined Nintendo Switch Online's catalog shortly after Prime Remastered was released. By placing the player inside Samus' helmet, Metroid Prime recontextualized the bounty hunter's relationship with the hostile planets around her.

Wearing Samus' suit, aggressive alien lifeforms could now get right in the player's face, forcing dodging, strafing, and rolling in morph ball form using all three dimensions; no longer would players sit back and watch as Samus dipped her toe into a pool of lava, since in first-person, as molten fire spread over the visor, the pressure to find that Varia Suit upgrade was real. Perhaps most importantly, from behind the visor players gained the ability to scan enemies and environment, collecting and translating logs from the long-dead Chozo aliens who once inhabited these now-hostile places. Myers calls the world harsh and unrelenting, with save points at times quite far from one another, but worth buckling down and pushing through the pain points to discover its secrets.
