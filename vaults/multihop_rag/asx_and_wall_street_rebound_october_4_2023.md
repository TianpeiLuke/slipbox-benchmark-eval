# The ASX And Wall Street Rebound Of 4 October 2023

IG Australia market analyst Tony Sycamore said the ASX received some relief from a rally on Wall Street following the release of cooler-than-expected US economic data which helped cap US bond yields. "Much has been written about the unrelenting rise in long-term US interest rates threatening prospects of a soft landing and raising doubts around equity market valuations," he said. "This was stopped in its tracks overnight following the release of a considerably weaker employment, and ISM services purchasing managers index data topped off by a 5.6 per cent fall in the price of crude oil to below $85 per barrel." Sycamore said the property sector and the interest rate sensitive technology sector reclaimed some lost ground while banks advanced on the back of the pullback in yields, but that iron ore miners were shackled by concerns around the Chinese economy as the market braced for China's consumer purchasing index report the following week, which was expected to show inflation remaining morbid.

The local rebound came after Wall Street rose on relief from relaxing bond yields and falling oil prices. The S&P 500 climbed 0.8 per cent to claw back more than half its sharp tumble from a day earlier, which had sent it to a four-month low; the Dow Jones rose 0.4 per cent, a day after erasing the last of its gains for the year so far; and the Nasdaq composite led the market with a gain of 1.4 per cent. Stocks have struggled under the weight of soaring Treasury yields in the bond market, because high yields undercut stock prices by pulling investment dollars away from stocks and into bonds, and also crimp corporate profits by making borrowing more expensive.
