# Prime Day 2023 Amazon Echo, Kindle And Fire Deals

WIRED's Prime Big Deal Days roundup covers Amazon's own devices and store promotions: an Echo Dot (5th Gen) bundle that includes a free smart plug, Echo Show 8, Echo Studio, the Kindle Oasis, Kindle Scribe, and the Fire Max 11, alongside Amazon-brand and gift card offers.

On the store offers: a discount applies automatically at checkout once you meet a $40 order threshold on select Amazon-branded products, which range from home essentials like paper towels and batteries to snacks, office supplies, and over-the-counter medicines — an easy way to stock up on frequently used items cheaply. Gift card deals are only worthwhile if you'd be spending the money anyway, but with brands like Doordash, Instacart and Fandango featured, chances are there's a worthy discount; each card has a unique coupon code on its product page to enter at checkout. Amazon also has private-label clearance up to 55 percent off across 10 pages of random cheap items — D-cell batteries for $5, compostable plates for $8, giant crayons for $9, "a truly hideous fanny pack" for $12. On devices: the Echo Dot (5th Gen) is one of WIRED's favorite Alexa speakers, and this deal is especially nice because it comes with a free smart plug that typically sells for about $20 — not the same model, but a similar Kasa plug is the top pick in WIRED's Best Smart Plugs guide. Other Echo Show devices are on sale, but the Echo Show 8 is WIRED's favorite; it comes with a free trial of Alexa Together, an Amazon service aiming to replicate a caregiver's tasks that usually costs $20 per month, so set a reminder to cancel. The Echo Studio is the best-sounding Alexa speaker though pretty expensive, and this price matches a low seen just once before, with more powerful sound overall and changes especially noticeable on the low end. Among e-readers, the other Kindles are more affordable so there's no practical reason to spend on the Oasis unless you love physical page-turn buttons — and WIRED thinks it's probably due for an update soon. The Kindle Scribe (8/10, WIRED Recommends) is the first Amazon e-reader that lets you write on its enormous 10.2-inch screen like a regular notebook, though writing in the margins of books requires sticky notes, which is frustrating given the price (marking up books is better on the Kobo Elipsa). The Fire Max 11 (5/10, WIRED Review) is Amazon's biggest, nicest, and most overpriced Fire tablet, made much more palatable by this deal: the display and new fingerprint sensor are nice, as is using the keyboard without Bluetooth, but Fire OS leaves much to be desired, like a decent app store.
