# Keep Labs' Repositioning From Cannabis To Medicine

Keep Labs stopped presenting its product as a cannabis device after CES took back an innovation award over exactly that framing: look at the website now and cannabis is not mentioned, and the Keep is marketed with the catch-all term "medicine." The observation comes from TechCrunch's 4 October 2023 conversation with co-founder, president and chairman Philip Wilkins about the company's journey from a preorder success story to finding an enterprise offering by partnering with Canadian healthcare providers — the writer's second conversation with him, the first having been back in 2019.

The triggering event dates to that first period. Keep Labs won an innovation award at CES 2020, and the award was later rescinded because the device was marketed as a cannabis storage device. The governing condition at the time was a policy inconsistency: in 2019, going into 2020, the trade group hosting CES had a strict but convoluted stance on cannabis products, and somehow Keep Labs won an award anyway. Eventually someone at the CTIA noticed Keep Labs' marketing and pulled the prize. This set off a firestorm, and eventually led the CTIA to soften its stance on cannabis-adjacent products — after all, cannabis is legal in Nevada, where CES is held.

The repositioning is presented as a change of vocabulary rather than of mission. The experience has had a lasting effect on Keep Labs, but "Cannabis is still there," Wilkins said, noting that the company is still on the same mission of harm reduction. "Cannabis was a great way to start a conversation because everyone wants to talk about cannabis, but no one wants to talk about getting high."
