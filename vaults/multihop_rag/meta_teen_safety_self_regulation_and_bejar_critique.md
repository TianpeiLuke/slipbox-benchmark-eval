# Arturo Bejar's Critique Of Meta's Teen Safety Self-Regulation

In the absence of a legislative decision, Meta began regulating itself, adding new defaults and features that would restrict teens' access to content on its apps, protect their privacy and limit ad targeting to teens, as well as introducing parental controls. The premise under attack is that this self-regulation was sufficient.

In the weeks before November 15, 2023, a second Meta whistleblower, Arturo Bejar, came forward to express concerns that Instagram's approach to protecting teens was not working to keep them safe from sexual predators and unwanted sexual advances and harassment. Bejar had been hired as a consultant on the matter and eventually blew the whistle on Meta after seeing issues remain unresolved following years of work.

His argument essentially was that the improvements and safety features Meta was implementing were not enough and not effective — so the attack is on efficacy rather than on whether Meta acted at all, and its evidentiary weight comes from his having worked on the problem from inside for years.
