# Regulatory Investigation

## Definition

In the corpus, a regulatory investigation is the government-agency-led inquiry a regulator such as the European Commission, Australia's OAIC, or the FAA/EASA/UK CAA opens into whether a company has breached the specific rules that agency administers.

The corpus distinguishes this from a lower-intensity step where a regulator is merely "monitoring" a situation, and from a private or internal review, such as a corporate board's own governance committee investigating its own affairs.

## Context

The corpus shows the pattern recurring across very different regulatory regimes and jurisdictions: the European Commission opening a Digital Services Act (DSA) proceeding against X over content moderation, transparency, and deceptive design; the same Commission running an in-depth antitrust probe into Amazon's proposed acquisition of iRobot; Australia's Office of the Australian Information Commissioner (OAIC) "monitoring" TikTok's handling of personal information and considering a formal privacy investigation into its tracking pixel after an Australian senator called for one; the US Federal Aviation Administration and European Aviation Safety Agency investigating a counterfeit aircraft-parts scandal, with the UK Civil Aviation Authority supporting that investigation; and a US Virgin Islands attorney general pursuing a civil enforcement action against Jeffrey Epstein's estate under her territory's own regulatory and civil-recovery powers.

## Key Characteristics

- **Agency-initiated, not self-initiated** — the trigger is a regulator's own scrutiny (or a complaint routed to it), as when the EU Commission decided to open formal DSA proceedings against X "on the basis of the preliminary investigation conducted so far," not X's own choice to review itself.
- **Preceded by lower-intensity information-gathering** — regulators typically issue formal requests for information or warnings before escalating; the Commission sent X an "urgent" request for information in October 2023 months before opening the formal DSA proceeding in December.
- **Bounded by the regulator's specific legal mandate** — the EU's iRobot probe is scoped to competition-law foreclosure concerns, the DSA probe to content-moderation/transparency articles, and the OAIC's scrutiny to Australia's Privacy Act, each agency confined to the statute it administers.
- **Carries formal sanction powers** — confirmed DSA breaches can draw fines up to 6% of global annual turnover and interim blocking measures; aviation operators remove unauthorized parts "in accordance with the recommendations issued by the regulatory agencies"; the US Virgin Islands attorney general's civil enforcement extracted settlements from the Epstein estate, which returned $80 million in tax incentives and established a victims' compensation fund.
- **Distinct from private/internal inquiries** — the corpus separately shows a non-regulatory investigation, Rogers Communications' own corporate governance committee investigating the 2021 board fight among the Rogers siblings, which lacks the statutory enforcement mandate that defines a regulatory investigation.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, a regulatory agency is generally defined as a government authority that exercises jurisdiction over an area of activity and, when it reasonably suspects a regulated entity is not complying with its obligations, may monitor performance and investigate compliance, publishing its findings (Wikipedia, "Regulatory agency," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Regulatory_agency).
