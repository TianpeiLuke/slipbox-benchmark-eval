# Steam Deck OLED Battery Life

Valve says the Steam Deck OLED battery lasts 30 to 50 percent longer than the original's depending on how you play, with an official claim of "three to 12 hours," and Engadget's November 2023 testing measured gains at the top of or above that range.

The original Steam Deck's short battery life was one of its downsides, maxing out at eight hours and only under ideal conditions. The OLED pairs a more-efficient processor with a 25 percent larger battery — 50Whr capacity against the original's 40Whr — and charges faster, going from 20 percent to 80 percent in 45 minutes by Valve's claim. Engadget tested with Hades, its reviewer's most-played game on the original: although Hades does not support HDR it pops on the OLED, with animations looking more fluid than on the LCD version, and the OLED lasted four hours and 23 minutes before dying, against two hours and 42 minutes for the original at the same settings of 800p with a 60 fps cap and similar brightness — a 62 percent improvement that surpasses Valve's own estimates. Engadget flags the confound: batteries degrade, and its LCD Steam Deck dated from the device's launch in early 2022, an 18-month-old unit at review time, which explains why its gains were more pronounced. A harsher stress test playing Elden Ring with everything maxed out gave the OLED two hours and 20 minutes, about 40 percent longer than the original. Polygon separately resolved the obvious ambiguity in Valve's 30-50% figure by asking whether the comparison was against a Steam Deck running original firmware, which had poor battery life, or the latest firmware; Valve's answer was the latter, so existing owners should expect the big improvements over their current experience. Polygon's conclusion is that the OLED screen makes for a fancier Steam Deck, but it is the battery life — plus tweaks throughout the design to carve out more of it — that lets the handheld truly deliver on its promise of a portable gaming PC.
