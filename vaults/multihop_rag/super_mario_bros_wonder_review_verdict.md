# Super Mario Bros. Wonder: The Verdict

The review's verdict is that Super Mario Bros. Wonder is a marvelous game: its art and animations have a level of polish practically unmatched by rival titles, and the addition of Wonder Seeds subverts your expectations and keeps this 40-year franchise feeling new and innovative.

The grounds are a three-part answer to the problem the review poses — Mario has been stomping Goombas, smashing blocks and running from left to right for damn near 40 years, so how is Nintendo supposed to keep its classic side scroller feeling fresh? First, you shift the location to a brand new kingdom; next, you introduce a bunch of new enemies and power-ups; but what really pushes things over the top is the introduction of a new mechanic that turns everything you think you know about Mario games on its head. What you get is a game that dazzles and delights while living up to its name in a variety of ways.

The warrant is comparative: in a lot of ways Wonder reminds the reviewer of his favorite 2D Mario game, Super Mario World. The one reservation is about the wait — he wishes it had not taken more than 10 years for Nintendo to create a proper sequel to its last mainline platformer — but as he concludes, if that is what it takes to get a game like this, he is happy to sit and wonder what is next for Mario.
