# SpO2 Sensors Are Not Essential To The Apple Watch

Against the assumption that losing blood oxygen sensing would gut the Apple Watch, The Verge's wearables reviewer argued that removing the SpO2 sensors wouldn't be the end of the world for Apple in the short term — betting that the average Apple Watch user forgets the blood oxygen features even exist.

The premise attacked is the feature's importance. Apple's blood oxygen features are mostly for wellness and sleep tracking, and are not that useful beyond determining a nightly baseline; the watch periodically takes SpO2 readings in the background, or the user can actively take a spot check, but Apple itself doesn't make wide use of these metrics — unlike the way the watch uses its EKG capabilities to detect atrial fibrillation. On consumer wearables generally, SpO2 features have no medical use cases; they are essentially for "entertainment" purposes, and The Verge has repeatedly reported that they should never be used as any sort of replacement for traditional pulse oximeters. The reviewer conceded one boundary case: if rumors that Apple is working on sleep apnea detection are correct, losing the sensors would make developing that feature harder. But such a feature would require a lengthy FDA clearance process, which makes more advanced health features a medium- to long-term concern — and it's very possible Apple will have a workaround by the time they are ready for consumers.
