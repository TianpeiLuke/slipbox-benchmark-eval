# Visual Intelligence Versus Human Sources

Technological sophistication and massive investment in intelligence means have greatly increased the IDF's visual intelligence gathering capabilities, according to former IDF Intelligence Directorate head General (ret.) Aharon Ze'evi-Farkash — but visual collection and human sources play different roles, and only the latter supplies interpretation.

"I'm sure it doesn't come at the expense of human sources, but as time goes by, gathering visual intelligence is easier than human sources and they bring better results," Ze'evi-Farkash said. "It's hard to bring in a Palestinian 'Ashraf Marwan,' the agent who passed the information to Israel before the Egyptian attack in 1973. It's a problem because the human source is essential to provide an interpretation of the rest of the information that's received, and to make decisions. Golda knew who Marwan was and expected to hear what he would say about the Egyptian deployment in the south." Two further gaps sit alongside that structural point: according to "The New York Times," a year before the attack the IDF stopped monitoring Hamas's radio communications, and the US had put stock in Israeli intelligence regarding Hamas in recent years and had not monitored it independently.
