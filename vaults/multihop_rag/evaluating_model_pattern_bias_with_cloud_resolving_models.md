# Evaluating Model Pattern Bias with Cloud-Resolving Models

The article argues that the advent of global coupled storm- and cloud-resolving models — which currently can simulate only a few months to years at a time — presents an exciting opportunity for evaluating model pattern bias, while acknowledging that their short runs make evaluation itself an open problem.

The most pressing question, on its account, is whether climate model simulations will be as far off from observations in the future as they have been relative to recent past conditions, and if so, how model biases in surface warming trends will be reflected in radiative feedbacks and global mean warming rates; it must also be quantified whether coupled models compensate for their bias in the surface warming pattern and radiative feedbacks through erroneous ocean heat uptake rates or aerosol forcings. For the new high-resolution models the difficulty is methodological: how to evaluate surface warming patterns and radiative feedbacks in these simulations and compare them meaningfully to observations and coarser-resolution models, knowing that even several decades are not enough to robustly detect a forced trend in some regions. And most pressing for the communities relying on coupled models' climate change projections is the question of the extent to which remaining carbon budgets with respect to global warming targets (Zhou et al., 2021), rates of near-term future warming, and detection and attribution efforts are contingent on the pattern effect.
