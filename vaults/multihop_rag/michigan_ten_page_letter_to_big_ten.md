# Michigan's Ten-Page Letter To The Big Ten

Michigan's 10-page letter to the Big Ten attacked the premise that the conference could properly discipline Harbaugh or the university before the NCAA investigation was complete. Posted in full on X by Yahoo Sports' Dan Wetzel on Nov. 9, 2023, the letter argued that the Big Ten would be acting too hastily in suspending Harbaugh or levying any punishment before a full NCAA investigation had concluded, and warned commissioner Tony Petitti against "premature" disciplinary action.

The due-process objection was procedural. Michigan said it had not had the chance to review all the evidence and respond, which oversteps the due process allowed to the university, and that Petitti should not have unilateral authority to punish violations without first a completed investigation and then a response from the Compliance and Reinstatement Committee, the body that would recommend penalties for rules violations.

The evidentiary objection attacked the factual basis. Michigan said it was aware of nothing supporting a claim that athletics personnel at Michigan committed the alleged violations, and that the rule on electronic recordings of games is limited to field equipment used by the institution for the game it is currently playing. It said it had not seen much evidence but had only heard a summary of second- and third-hand information.

The materiality objection attacked the significance of any conduct that did occur. Given the use of sign decoding by other universities, the letter said, there was little reason to believe Stalions' efforts proved meaningful, and it pointed to the teams' average margin of victory since Stalions was suspended. The information obtained was "at most a reflection of information that teams can and easily do obtain through public and permissible sources," making it "highly dubious" that Stalions' observations would have a "material effect on the integrity of the competition." The letter further alleged that in-person scouting and sign-stealing are commonplace across the NCAA — "The conference should act cautiously when setting precedent given the reality that in-person scouting, collusion among opponents, and other questionable practices may well be far more prevalent than believed" — and, per Yahoo Sports, enclosed "two attached documents of Michigan's offensive and defensive signs allegedly stolen and shared by other Big Ten teams."

Michigan submitted this response to the Big Ten's notice of discipline as a step toward resolution. ESPN reported on Wednesday that no conference ruling was expected until Thursday because the Big Ten planned to "take time to absorb" what Michigan had to say, and ESPN's Adam Rittenberg reported on Nov. 8, 2023 that the school had sent its response and that Petitti now had to decide how to proceed with potential discipline.
