# Renewed Global Investor Interest in Japanese Equities

Global investors' interest in Japan was sharply heightened in late 2023, but many funds found themselves short of the expertise to act on it. Tokyo conference guests were told by various experts that the stars do appear aligned in Japan's favour, both on its own merits and in comparison with what is happening around it — yet because so many global funds have diverted expertise away from Japan in recent years, they are concerned that they need to know more, as a matter of urgency.

"There is heightened interest in Japan after what has been, for many, a long period of not being focused on that country," says Paul Hitchens, head of the Japan research department at Jefferies, which hosted a major investor conference in Hong Kong. He names distinctive catalysts: a recent push by the Tokyo Stock Exchange for companies to improve their capital efficiency, and Warren Buffett's high-profile decision to invest heavily in five of Japan's largest trading houses. "Is there some healthy scepticism? Yes. However, global investment firms know that they need to invest and build their offerings in Japan to meet the growing appetite, and that they need to pay attention to an equity market at a 33-year high."

The market backdrop reinforced the urgency: Topix, Japan's main stock index, had risen more than 25 per cent in 2023 to the date of this Financial Times report, outperforming almost all the world's developed market indices and, as some conference attendees admit, creating a "fear of missing out" on a rally that may have significantly further to go.
