# Point Spread Bet Definition

A point spread bet is a wager on the difference in score between two teams (or players) in a specific game or event, in which bookmakers assign the favored team a point spread or handicap intended to equalize the odds against the less-favored team. To place one, a bettor selects either the favored team or the underdog and predicts whether that side will surpass or fall short of the given spread; the odds on both sides are set to try to even out the money wagered on each.

The discriminating features are the two symbols and what they oblige each side to do. The "(-)" symbol denotes the favorite — the team expected to win, typically assigned a negative point value indicating the number of points by which it must win for a bet on it to succeed; the favorite has points deducted. The "(+)" symbol denotes the underdog — the team expected to lose, typically assigned a positive point value indicating the number of points it may lose by and still win the bet; the underdog has points added. Covering the spread means a team has surpassed the expectations the spread set and so won the bet: for the favorite, winning by more than the specified number of points; for the underdog, either winning outright or losing by fewer points than the spread. If the underdog wins the contest outright, it is also the spread winner.

The term "against the spread" carries two senses that mark a boundary case. Most generally, betting against the spread simply means placing a point spread bet at all — betting the underdog to win outright or lose by fewer points than specified, or betting the favorite to win by a margin greater than specified. In a secondary definition that distinguishes betting "with the spread" from betting "against the spread," the latter term means specifically that you are betting on the underdog.
