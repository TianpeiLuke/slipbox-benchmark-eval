# Israel's Startup Funding Decline Before The War

Startup investment in Israel was already far below the prior year before the fighting began: as TechCrunch's Anna Heim reported in July, a provisional tally of $3.2 billion for the first half of the year meant funding activity in Israel had dropped by 73% compared with the same period in 2022, according to IVC data. Other countries and regions reported similar declines in their private-market capital flows that year, so the fall was not unique to Israel.

The decline had a political prelude. The tech industry led opposition to the government's bid for judicial reform and, even before the fighting started, it was voting with its feet, with some companies and investors already refusing to do business in the country with the prospect of the reforms getting pushed through. That effort was in turn brought to a halt, because people on the two sides of that debate were then standing together for what they saw as the significantly bigger fight and threat.

By the sixth day of the war, many people were being called up to the reserves or volunteering to help in other ways, and with the tech industry skewing younger it was estimated that anywhere between 10% and 30% of all of Israel's tech employees were getting mobilized. More than 500 VCs had pledged their support of those efforts as well; the signatories wrote, "In the spirit of peace and unity, we encourage the global venture community to support and engage with Israeli startups, entrepreneurs, and investors as they navigate through these challenging times."

TechCrunch spoke to a number of startup founders, PR people who work with startups, and investors to understand the on-the-ground situation for Israeli entrepreneurs, and the predominant response was that the businesses have to continue to operate as best they can, to help ensure the economic viability of the country.
