# Michigan 30, Ohio State 24 In The 2023 Game

No. 3 Michigan beat No. 2 Ohio State 30-24 at Michigan Stadium in Ann Arbor on Saturday, Nov. 25, 2023, taking The Game for a third straight year and moving to 12-0 while the Buckeyes fell to 11-1. Cornerback Will Johnson set the tone by intercepting Ohio State quarterback Kyle McCord with 7:32 left in the first quarter, setting the Wolverines up at the 7-yard line; Blake Corum slammed into the line of scrimmage three times before a fourth-and-1, heard acting coach Sherrone Moore scream "Smash," and punched in a one-yard touchdown for a 7-0 lead — his 21st touchdown run of the season — after tight end A.J. Barner told the huddle "It's time to play the game." Michigan never played from behind.

The turning point came through an injury. Michigan lost senior All-American guard Zak Zinter to a broken tibia and fibula with 2:19 left in the third quarter of a tie game; J.J. McCarthy heard Zinter scream and saw tackle Karsen Barnhart holding Zinter's foot in the air, and Zinter was carted off in what could have swung the game the other way given his value on the interior line. "It was a sight that I wouldn't wish upon anyone to see," McCarthy said. "At that moment, seeing the look in everyone's eyes, seeing them rally together, it was spiritual, honestly. It was a different drive that hit on everybody." On the very next play Corum broke through the second level of the Buckeyes' defense for a 22-yard go-ahead touchdown and flashed Zinter's number "65" with his hands. Corum, who had missed the previous year's 45-23 win over the Buckeyes with a knee injury, said: "Zak's my guy. He's another guy that came back for unfinished business. That's why he came back. Seeing him go down and hurt, it was very emotional seeing what he was going through. We came together, and we knew we had to do it for him. The very next play, 'Boom.'"

The margin came from decisions and answers. Michigan played to win the game and Ohio State, in Sporting News' judgment, did not — the Buckeyes settled for a 52-yard field goal late in the first half that Jayden Fielding missed wide left. When Ohio State tied it 17-17 with 6:08 left in the third quarter on an up-tempo drive featuring eight straight run plays and a TreVeyon Henderson touchdown, Michigan answered, scoring on every second-half drive with three field goals and a touchdown. Ohio State had one minute to score at the end, but Michigan safety Rod Moore, a Clayton, Ohio, product, made the game-sealing interception in yet another recurring theme from the 1990s. Moore also out-coached Ohio State's Ryan Day on the day.
