# June 2022: Alameda's Reckoning And Lender Repayment

By 2022 Alameda's negative balance on FTX had become a cause for concern for Caroline Ellison, Nishad Singh, and Gary Wang, who built a spreadsheet to work out how negative Alameda actually was; the version shown in court ran to several pages of attempts to reconcile, and Wang figured Alameda owed FTX about $11 billion at a time when FTX's revenue was about $1.5 billion. Ellison, Singh, Wang, and Bankman-Fried then met in the Bahamas office, where Alameda's lenders wanted their money back and Bankman-Fried directed the group to pay the lenders back using FTX customer funds.

Bankman-Fried considered shutting Alameda down and even wrote a long memo about it, but Wang testified that in a now-deleted Signal discussion of that memo he told Bankman-Fried and Singh they could not shut Alameda down because they could not repay its debt. Another empty Signal chat, with its setting to delete messages after a week, was shown to the court; its members were Ellison, Singh, Wang, and Bankman-Fried.

Bankman-Fried's own account from the stand ran differently. He testified that in June 2022 he heard about the account called "fiat@ftx" tracking how much money Alameda owed to FTX, that he did not know what it was and did not bother to find out because he was busy, and that this was when he directed Ellison to repay Alameda's lenders because he thought Alameda was good for it. He also gave BlockFi and Voyager, two crypto lenders, some capital infusions for good measure.
