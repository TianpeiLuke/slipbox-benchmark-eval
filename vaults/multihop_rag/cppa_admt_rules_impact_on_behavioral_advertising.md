# Why The CPPA's ADMT Rules Would Bite On Behavioral Advertising

AI-based profiling could fall in scope of the planned rules, per the draft the CPPA presented, and TechCrunch's claim is that this would have big implications for US adtech giants like Meta, which has a business model that hinges on tracking and profiling users to target them with ads. The claim carries an explicit scope condition: it assumes this provision survives the consultation process and makes it into the hard-baked rules.

The grounds are two features of the draft. Such firms could be required to offer California residents the ability to deny their commercial surveillance, with the proposed law stating businesses must provide consumers with the ability to opt out of their data being processed for behavioral advertising. And the current draft further stipulates that behavioral advertising use-cases cannot make use of a number of exemptions to the opt-out right that may apply in other scenarios — such as if ADMT is being used for security or fraud prevention purposes. The warrant is that an opt-out right with no available exemption is one a tracking-based business cannot route around.

The more expansive list of proposed thresholds would clearly make the law bite down harder on adtech giants and Big AI. But, it being California, TechCrunch notes the CCPA can probably expect a lot of pushback from local giants like Meta and OpenAI, to name two.
