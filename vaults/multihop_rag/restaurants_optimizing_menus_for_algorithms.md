# Restaurants Optimizing Menus For Algorithms

We are in an era of dining where one viral video online could make or break a restaurant, so business owners must be strategic about getting their operation in front of potential customers — and the optimization now reaches the food itself. As New York Magazine reported, the allure of a social media hit is shaping the very food being served: gooier eggs, more obscene cheese, sauces exploding and gushing across a plate. If the algorithm and diner-slash-viewers want messier, spectacle-driven dishes, many restaurants are complying.

Thai Food Near Me has optimized its food in its own way. The owners have tweaked their menu and modification options on delivery apps like Uber Eats or DoorDash according to the common written requests they get on online orders. It is a savvy business decision, co-owner Robert Sampson says, and a way to get and keep loyal customers — what is required for a business to survive when diners have endless options at their fingertips. "At first we were a little too proud of our Thai food. We wanted to make it how we think you should have it. Well, that's not actually a smart idea. It's the other way around," Sampson says. "We now want to make it however you most want it." The restaurant also keeps a "secret menu" with speciality dishes.

The stakes of getting this right are visible in the location's history: Thai Food Near Me is at least the fourth Thai restaurant to occupy the same address, according to defunct delivery and review websites the reporter dug up, and Jirapraphanan was told they are the fifth. The restaurant is still in its early days, and good restaurants shutter for reasons that are no fault of their own — but Sampson and Jirapraphanan are content with their choice of name.
