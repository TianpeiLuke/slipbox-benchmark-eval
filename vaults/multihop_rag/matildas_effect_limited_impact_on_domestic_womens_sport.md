# The 'Matildas Effect' And Its Limited Impact On Domestic Women's Sport

The Guardian argued on 26 October 2023 that the popularity of the Matildas' run to the Women's World Cup semi-finals has had only a limited impact on support for domestic women's sport, because the early spike did not persist.

The grounds come from the A-League Women, which launched that month with a first round highlighted by a crowd of more than 11,000 in Sydney — but more modest attendances in subsequent games, including two sub-2,000 crowds in Perth, follow it. The warrant is that a durable effect would show up beyond the opening round; a large first-round crowd followed by small ones is consistent with novelty rather than converted support.

Nicole Livingstone, the AFL's general manager of women's football, made the same point from the league side, saying the "green and gold fever" associated with the Women's World Cup was a one-off and that the AFL has plans in place for long-term growth: "We'll continue to keep building the base and keep building the foundations and have it not just strong at year seven, season eight, but strong for generations to come."
