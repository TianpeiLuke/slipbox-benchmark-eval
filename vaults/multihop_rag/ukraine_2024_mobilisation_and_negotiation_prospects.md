# Ukraine 2024 Mobilisation and Negotiation Prospects

Ukraine's Defence Ministry said in the week before 25 December 2023 that its main goal in 2024 is to boost its domestic defence industry in the face of uncertain future supplies from its Western allies. It has also changed conscription laws, foreseeing the need to bolster its forces, which are dwarfed in size by Russia's but are more highly trained and equipped. Ukrainian President Volodymyr Zelenskyy said that week that the military had asked for up to 500,000 additional conscripts, but that he needed to hear "more arguments" to support the sensitive and costly proposal.

On talks, CNBC reported that with both Ukraine and Russia investing heavily in the war it is unlikely there will be any negotiations to end the conflict or agree a cease-fire, because defence analysts argue that neither side would want to enter negotiations unless in a position of strength and able to dictate terms.

The condition that could change this is political. "In the case of a Republican winning the presidential election next year, especially if that's Donald Trump, who seems to be the front-runner, and [if] funding is decreased substantially, then there will be increased pressure on Ukraine to negotiate," Mario Bikarski, a Europe and Russia analyst at the Economist Intelligence Unit, told CNBC.
