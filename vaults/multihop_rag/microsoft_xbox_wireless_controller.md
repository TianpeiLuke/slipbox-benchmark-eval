# Microsoft Xbox Wireless Controller

On the Polygon team the Xbox controller got the highest number of endorsements of any controller, a result the outlet attributes to its comfort and value and to Microsoft having released enough colors to satisfy varying tastes in design. It usually costs around $50 depending on the color, and it just works with most PC games — regardless of the Windows PC game client, there are no extra steps for setting it up.

The controller is a refined version of Microsoft's Xbox One wireless model, with added texture around its grips plus a capture button in the middle. It connects to Xbox consoles and to PCs that have Bluetooth, and one universally positive change from the older version is that its wired connector port is the more ubiquitous USB-C instead of Micro USB. It also has a 3.5mm headphone jack for private listening, though that jack only works when the controller is wired into your PC, not wirelessly. Staff writer Nicole Carpenter is quoted saying "The Xbox wireless controller just works for me — it fits right in my hands, the texture feels just right, and it's not too heavy." Beyond PC compatibility it has native compatibility on Steam Deck plus recent versions of Android and iOS, so it connects easily over Bluetooth to a phone or tablet; a growing number of mobile games support it, and it works like a charm with the Xbox Game Pass game streaming app. The biggest downside may not bother you: it requires two AA batteries, though battery life is around the 40-hour range depending on usage, and for those who would rather not swap batteries Polygon recommends a $44.99 8BitDo dual controller charging dock that includes two rechargeable battery packs, enough for two controllers.
