# YouTube's AI Music Incubator And Its Rightsholder Pledges

During its period of discussions with the majors, YouTube made a series of announcements aimed at the biggest issue for any rightsholder regarding AI-driven voice replication of artists: their ability to police it.

In August, YouTube and Universal Music Group jointly announced that the video platform was launching an "AI Music Incubator" — a program by which new tools and innovations would be developed at YouTube in close conjunction with artists and the music business; to progress the incubator, YouTube said it was working behind the scenes with a number of UMG-affiliated artists. Also in August, YouTube publicly committed to three principles or pledges behind its development of music-based generative AI tools. These pledges promised "appropriate protections… for music partners who decide to participate" in such tools, and promised "invest[ment] in the AI-powered technology" that, among other things, would help YouTube "protect our community of viewers, creators, artists and songwriters… [from] trademark and copyright abuse" — all of which seemed to hint at a desire to build a "Content ID"-style system on YouTube to control and police content made by cloning artists' voices. Then, earlier in November, YouTube announced it was developing a specific system for music partners allowing them to request the removal of content on its platform that "mimics an artist's unique singing or rapping voice."
