# Eylon Levy's 2013 Oxford Debate With George Galloway

In 2013, while an undergraduate at the University of Oxford, Eylon Levy was conducting a debate with British MP George Galloway about Galloway's remarks on Israel. Levy describes Galloway as known for being a notorious Israel hater who arrived an hour late, gave his speech and sat down; when Levy spoke and in the middle of his speech said "we", referring to the State of Israel, Galloway stood up and asked "what do you mean we? Are you Israeli?", and when Levy answered yes, said "I don't do debates with Israelis. Goodbye" and left. It was filmed, all the UK media reported on it the next morning, and it even made it into a cartoon in The Times.

The background is that Levy was born and raised in England and attended a private school in North London in an area with a large Jewish population, always taking an interest in news and current affairs and, as a student, active in the world of debating including tournaments; he says it would not be an exaggeration to say he would sit in the bath reading The Economist, that arguing and opposing incorrect positions has always been one of his great hobbies, and that he grew up with a strong awareness of the importance of the State of Israel and its historical role, so he has often had to explain Israel over the years. Asked whether he encountered antisemitism in his UK childhood, he says he did not personally experience it but was exposed to it during debates, and every time there was a round of fighting he found himself defending Israel's position. The 2013 episode reverberates for him now: he looks at what is happening on campuses around the world and tears his hair out, because he was there 10 years ago trying to warn about the connection between antisemitism and anti-Zionism, how deep-rooted the hatred against Israel is, and how many Jews are harmed by this blind hatred.
