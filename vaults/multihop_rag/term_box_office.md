# Box Office

## Definition

Box office, as used throughout the corpus, is the reported revenue figure for a film's ticket sales, cited either as a single weekend's opening or debut number or as a cumulative running gross toward a domestic or global total.
Beyoncé's "Renaissance" is described as having "topped the box office" with "a $21 million debut" from North American ticket sales on its opening weekend (doc_0173), and "Taylor Swift: The Eras Tour" is tracked the same way, launching with "$100 million" in advance sales (doc_0419) and later "surpass[ing] $250m at the global box office" (doc_0046).

## Context

The corpus reports box office in two recurring ways: as a snapshot of a single weekend's ranking (who "topped the box office" that weekend) and as a running cumulative gross that signals a film's overall commercial success or failure.
Concert films sit alongside conventional theatrical releases in this reporting: "Renaissance" and "The Eras Tour" are both measured in the same box-office terms as scripted films like "The Batman" (doc_0081) or Scorsese's "Killers of the Flower Moon" (doc_0192), and both were distributed through a direct deal with AMC Theatres rather than a traditional studio (doc_0419).
2023 industry-wide box office is also used as a barometer of the theatrical exhibition business: analyst Dergarabedian expected the two concert films to help lift the North American box office "to more than $9 billion in 2023," up from "$7.4 billion" in 2022 (doc_0419), and the "Barbenheimer" pairing of Barbie and Oppenheimer is credited with dominating "the 2023 box office and the cultural discussion" (doc_0094).
The term also appears in retrospective judgments of a film's commercial fate: Scorsese and De Niro's "The King of Comedy" (1983) is described as having been "a flop at the box office" despite positive reviews (doc_0193), while Tobe Hooper's "The Texas Chain Saw Massacre" (1974) "made over $30 million at the box office on a budget of around $140,000" (doc_0563), illustrating the term's use for both underperformance and outsized return relative to cost.

## Key Characteristics

- **Reported as a debut and as a cumulative total** — outlets cite both a single weekend's opening number (e.g., "$21 million debut," doc_0173) and a running gross accumulated over a film's release (e.g., "surpassed $250m," doc_0046).
- **Ranking-based** — weekend box office is reported competitively, with films described as "topping" or "ruling" the box office relative to other new releases that same weekend (doc_0173).
- **Applies to concert films alongside scripted releases** — the corpus tracks Beyoncé's and Taylor Swift's concert films using the same box-office vocabulary and comparisons applied to traditional Hollywood films (doc_0173, doc_0419, doc_0081, doc_0192).
- **Used as both a success and failure signal** — the same term describes a "flop" (doc_0193) and a low-budget film's outsized return (doc_0563), as well as a blockbuster's records (doc_0046, doc_0419).
- **Aggregated into industry-wide totals** — individual films' box office performance feeds into annual North American or global box-office totals used to gauge the health of theatrical exhibition (doc_0419, doc_0094).

## Background (external)

The corpus uses "box office" as industry shorthand for a film's ticket-sales revenue without ever defining the term itself.
Outside the corpus, a "box office" (or "ticket office") is literally a place where tickets are sold to the public for admission to an event; by extension, the term is used, especially in the film industry, as a metonym for the amount of business a production earns, and total movie ticket sales have been termed "box office" since at least 1904 (Wikipedia, *Box office*, accessed 2026-09-02).
