# United's Squad Incoherence And Old Trafford's Decline

Barney Ronay argues that Manchester United's problem on the night of their Champions League exit was not spirit but coherence: the players stood up and died rather than lying down, but they looked like what they are — a muddled creation, stitched together out of parts and off-cuts.

The grounds are what was visible in the stadium and on the pitch. As the seconds ticked down at the end of a season-ender that felt like a gentle, even quite tender, act of sporting euthanasia, the only noise inside Old Trafford was Bayern Munich fans singing an impressively sustained version of Football's Coming Home, then Is This A Library? — to which Ronay answers no, it is more of a museum — before chanting "Auf Wiedersehen" at departing home supporters who had done pretty well to stick it out. In the second half United's back five was André Onana, Aaron Wan-Bissaka, Jonny Evans, Raphaël Varane and Diogo Dalot, an entirely random collection of players signed at various times by Ten Hag, José Mourinho, Ole Gunnar Solskjær and Alex Ferguson. By the end United had five academy players on the pitch with ages ranging between 17 and 35 — in outline evidence of internal good health, in reality another symptom of a machine that wants to function properly but has for so long been made to judder along at half-speed. The warrant is that the decay is institutional, not motivational, and it extends to the fabric of the place: when VAR happens at Old Trafford there is not even a VAR screen to say "VAR Check." The 1990s were a hell of a decade — they still are around there.
