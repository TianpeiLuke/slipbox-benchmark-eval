# Executive Order

## Definition

In the corpus, an executive order (EO) is the unilateral instrument a head of state or head of government uses to create a new administrative body, program, or restriction without waiting on a legislature — Biden uses it for AI regulation and an investment ban, and Philippine Presidents Marcos, Jr. and Marcos each use it to organize a fund or stand up a new committee — and a senior US administration official describes it as carrying "the force of law" "like all executive orders," even as commentators counter that "what the president can dictate without legislative support is limited" (doc_0313, doc_0098).

## Context

The corpus documents executive orders from two different presidencies and eras. In the US, President Biden used the instrument twice: to establish AI safety and security standards in October 2023 (doc_0098, doc_0313), and to bar US investment in three China-based strategic sectors — AI, quantum computing, and semiconductors — in August 2023 (doc_0181). In the Philippines, President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. signed Executive Order No. 28 in May 2023 to create the Inter-Agency Committee on Inflation and Market Outlook (doc_0410), while decades earlier President Ferdinand Marcos issued Executive Order 156 in 1968 to organize the Fund for Assistance to Private Education (doc_0463). Across both countries, the mechanism is used for the same purpose: standing up a new administrative body, program, or restriction by executive fiat rather than waiting on a legislature.

## Key Characteristics

- **Force of law without legislative passage** — a senior US administration official described the AI EO as having "the force of law" "like all executive orders," despite never going through Congress (doc_0313).
- **Bounded by the limits of executive authority** — commentators on the Biden AI EO stressed that "what the president can dictate without legislative support is limited," so an EO functions as a stopgap propping up voluntary industry practices rather than a substitute for statute (doc_0098).
- **Can create new administrative machinery** — the AI EO stood up an "AI council" chaired by the White House Deputy Chief of Staff (doc_0313); Executive Order No. 28 formed the Inter-Agency Committee on Inflation and Market Outlook (doc_0410); Executive Order 156 organized the Fund for Assistance to Private Education, part of the Special Fund for Education (doc_0463).
- **Applies across policy domains** — the corpus shows EOs used for technology regulation and reporting requirements (doc_0313), restricting outbound investment in China-based AI, quantum computing, and semiconductor sectors (doc_0181), macroeconomic inflation management (doc_0410), and education financing (doc_0463).
- **Not confined to one country or era** — the instrument appears in US executive practice in 2023 and in Philippine executive practice in both 1968 and 2023, showing it as a generic feature of presidential systems rather than a US-specific device.

## Background (external)

The U.S. National Archives defines executive orders as "official documents, numbered consecutively, through which the President of the United States manages the operations of the Federal Government," and notes that their text is published in the Federal Register when signed (National Archives, "About Executive Orders," accessed 2026-09-02, https://www.archives.gov/federal-register/executive-orders/about.html).

This general definition is US-specific; the corpus's own Philippine examples (doc_0410, doc_0463) show the same instrument used by a different head of state outside that federal framework.
