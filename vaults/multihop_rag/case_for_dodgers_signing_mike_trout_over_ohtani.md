# The Case For The Dodgers Signing Mike Trout Over Ohtani

Essentially Sports argued on 18 November 2023 that despite Mike Trout's injury-prone history, the more logical option for the Dodgers would be to choose Trout over Shohei Ohtani: the claim rests on the premise that the team will not be able to afford both, and the warrant is that Trout is cheaper and usable in between injuries while Ohtani's first year would be spent as a hitter at the risk of further injury and more lost pitching time.

The record grounds are given first. Trout may have been absent for most of the 2023 season, but his career records boast the story of a legendary hitter who has given more than his salt's worth to his current team: 1,624 hits and 940 RBIs with an 85.2 WAR and a .301 batting average across a 12-year career. The countervailing fact is his injury streak starting in 2022, when he injured his hands, left groin, back and foot five times in the season, followed by two hand injuries in two consecutive months in 2023.

The comparative grounds close it. Trout comes with a cheaper price tag of $35.45 million compared with Ohtani's projected $48.63 million, and the Dodgers could use his All-Star form in between injuries if he still keeps getting them in 2024. With Ohtani, the Dodgers would be wasting a year letting him play at-bat, with a high chance of getting him further injured and missing more pitching time. The article poses the trade-off as a rhetorical question: isn't it better to have a consistently wounded soldier than risk losing an overperforming one in a game of chances?
