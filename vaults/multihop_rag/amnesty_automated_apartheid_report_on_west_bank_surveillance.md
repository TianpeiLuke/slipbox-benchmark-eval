# Amnesty's Automated Apartheid Report On West Bank Surveillance

The presence of Hikvision cameras in the West Bank was first revealed in Amnesty International's May 2023 report, which documented facial-recognition and surveillance-camera technology that the human rights organization concluded is being used to reinforce Israel's occupation of the West Bank in an act of "digital repression."

Amnesty surveyed two West Bank neighborhoods, East Jerusalem and Hebron, where Israeli settlers have set up homes in violation of international law, and found that facial-recognition systems "are providing the Israeli authorities with powerful new tools for curbing freedom of movement … adding further layers of technological sophistication to the system of apartheid that Israel is imposing on Palestinians." The device count is specific: the report identified dozens of Hikvision devices in the old city in East Jerusalem, nearly 40 of which were found on security infrastructure and illegal settlements in the Silwan neighborhood. Those devices were "operated by Israeli police and by private settlers" and were "mounted to military infrastructures and residential areas in and around the old city of East Jerusalem, in particular Damascus Gate, the Armenian quarter, the Muslim quarter, and Silwan."

The Guardian's stated reason for revisiting the report in November 2023 is that it is newly relevant in the weeks after the 7 October Hamas attacks, which the Israeli government said killed 1,400 people: since then, settlers have escalated their violence against West Bank Palestinians and the Israeli army has conducted raids in West Bank cities and refugee camps.
