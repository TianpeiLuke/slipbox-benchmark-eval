# Northern Lights Viewing Sites in Europe

For those willing to plan a holiday getaway across the pond, three European sites stand out for the Aurora Borealis: Tromsø in Norway, Abisko in Swedish Lapland, and the Jökulsárlón Glacier Lagoon in Iceland. The qualifying features are the same as elsewhere — position within the aurora oval, clear skies, and minimal light pollution.

Tromsø, Norway, is widely considered one of the top places to see the Aurora Borealis, and looks exactly like the landscape most people picture around the Northern Lights. It is the third largest city north of the Arctic Circle and "lies just within the Northern Lights Oval, the region above Earth's geomagnetic North Pole, where aurora displays are most likely to occur," according to Space.com. The stunning display can be seen there from September to April, and many travel sites and guides offer aurora chases.

Sweden has become a top place to view the dancing lights in the Northern Hemisphere, with the Lapland region in particular being the most popular. "Not only is Abisko, Swedish Lapland, within the aurora oval but the surrounding mountains also keep the skies clear and there's barely any light pollution," Swedish Lapland shared online. In Abisko you can find the Aurora Sky Station, which has an observation tower and experts available to answer any questions about the display, the Space Tourism Guide shared.

One of the most unique places to see the display is the Jökulsárlón Glacier Lagoon in Iceland. "Jokulsarlon's secluded location ensures minimal light pollution, providing the perfect setting to witness the stunning colors of the Aurora Borealis," said Arctic Adventures. "The best time to observe the Northern Lights at Jokulsarlon is during the late evening and early night hours when the darkness enhances their visibility," the website also notes — time of year and time of day being two very important factors when choosing to see the Northern Lights in Iceland.
