# Jon Evans Recommends "The Steerswoman"

Jon Evans recommends "The Steerswoman," by Rosemary Kirstein, on the ground that it is his go-to example of a book that turns out to be far deeper, richer and more thought-provoking than it originally seems. Evans is known as a former TechCrunch columnist and from his engineering and CTO roles, and is also a novelist; most of his books are technothrillers painting travelers caught in tricky situations in exotic locations, while "Exadelic" is different — and not just because it is his first novel in over a decade — in being set in Silicon Valley, where a tech exec gets threatened by a rogue AI.

His grounds start with the bait-and-switch of the opening. Read just the first few chapters, he says, and you will think you "know" you are reading a clichéd fantasy novel, albeit better-written and more feminist than most: two adventurer women meet in a medieval-ish tavern, in a land plagued by dragons, and commence a quest to discover the source of mysterious — some say magical — gems. A reader might be a bit surprised by the asides in which they begin to discuss math and physics, but still take it for a fairly forgettable genre fantasy story.

That reading, Evans says, is very, very wrong. He apologises for the spoiler but says it is impossible to write about the series without either doing so or being maddeningly elliptic: these books are some of the least fantastic, most meticulously thought-out science fiction you will ever read. More than that, they are a meditation on science itself, on how and why we acquire knowledge, on the repercussions of that knowledge being hoarded rather than shared, and on the inevitable(?) inequities, oppressions and breakdowns between communities that result. They are also a whole lot of fun — not just an ongoing puzzle to be solved but a series of genuinely thrilling, funny and wrenching adventures, populated by great characters, to experience from the edge of your seat. Read the first, he advises, and if you like it, lucky you: the sequels are even better.
