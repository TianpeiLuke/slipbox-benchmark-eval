# Miles Sanders As A Week 5 2023 Sit At Detroit

CBS Sports listed Miles Sanders under "Sit Him" for Week 5 of the 2023 season at Detroit: the claim is that starting Sanders means hoping for a few long plays, perhaps through the air, plus a touchdown, and the warrant is that the Lions' run defense should keep him grounded while his own playing time is slipping away.

The grounds combine a collapsing role with collapsing efficiency. In Week 4 Sanders' playing time hit a season-low 43% of snaps as he continued losing third-down work to Chuba Hubbard; that has not crushed his reception totals, though his only game with more than four catches came with Andy Dalton under centre, and he played just 31% of Carolina's second-half snaps, a big change from Weeks 2 and 3 when his second-half snaps were much closer to Hubbard's. His yards per carry has tumbled like a bowling ball downstairs — 4.0 against the Falcons, 3.1 against the Saints, 2.7 against the Seahawks, 1.5 against the Vikings — and while those were tough matchups, the column says that doesn't make it a good look. Unsurprisingly he has two runs of 12-plus yards through four games and neither went more than 15 yards; the blame is shared, since Carolina's offensive line barely gave him any daylight last week or for much of the year, and defenses aren't yet scared of getting beaten by Bryce Young's arm. The breakdown lists Carolina at Detroit, the Lions favoured by 10, an over/under of 45, an opponent ranked 5th versus running backs, 9.9 projected points, a 25th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 158 rushing yards, 15 receptions, 81 receiving yards, one touchdown and 10.7 fantasy points per game.

The opponent evidence is the other half. Detroit is a tough matchup, giving up 2.9 yards per rush to running backs on the season, a top-three figure, as are its yards before contact allowed (0.49) and runs of 10-plus yards allowed (three); its 2.38 yards after contact per rush allowed is only fifth-best. Only Kenneth Walker, on the strength of two touchdowns, has managed more than five non-PPR and nine PPR points against these Lions. The column pre-empts the obvious historical objection: Detroit was strong against the run last year too when it went into Carolina and got absolutely pummelled by a Panthers run game that included Chuba Hubbard, and coach Dan Campbell accepted responsibility for that loss, saying the Lions "were not emotionally ready to go" — they should be this time, and besides, it is a different coaching staff in Carolina and clearly a different run game. The verdict prefers Jahmyr Gibbs, Dameon Pierce and Najee Harris to Sanders.
