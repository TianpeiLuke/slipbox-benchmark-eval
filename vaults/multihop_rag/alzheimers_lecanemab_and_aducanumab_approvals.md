# Alzheimer's: The Lecanemab And Aducanumab Approvals

Leqembi, lecanemab, developed by Eisai in collaboration with Biogen, was the first and only approved drug for Alzheimer's, approved in July 2023, coming after several failed attempts and one controversial false start in recent years. Alzheimer's disease is characterised by amyloid beta deposits on brain cells leading to shrinking and loss of cognitive abilities, and despite a clear marker of amyloid plaques and a target area to work on, pharma companies failed to address plaque removal or to show a statistical improvement in cognitive functioning after plaque removal until this approval; Pfizer, Eli Lilly, Merck and many others faced failure in the space, and the amyloid theory itself was in question.

Biogen's aducanumab had a peculiar start: the company itself stopped development in 2019 but surprisingly pursued a high-dose study into 2021, after which the FDA gave the high-dose version an accelerated approval that faced sharp criticism, with three advisers resigning over the approval of a drug that could have cost $56,000 per annum to administer. With a "traditional" approval for the Eisai-partnered lecanemab, compared with the accelerated approval for aducanumab, Biogen has the first approved treatment, putting aducanumab on the backburner. On the critical marker of cognitive function improvement, lecanemab delivers lower degeneration compared to placebo: the treatment reduced clinical decline by 27 per cent at 18 months on clinical dementia scoring and delivered a 37 per cent improvement compared to placebo on the daily living scale. The article's verdict is that the development is satisfactory but a lot more progress remains to be made on the Alzheimer's treatment curve.
