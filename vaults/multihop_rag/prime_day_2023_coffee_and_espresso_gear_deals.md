# Prime Day 2023 Coffee And Espresso Gear Deals

WIRED's coffee picks are the Breville Impress, the Breville Barista Touch, a generic $8 milk frother, a gooseneck electric kettle, and the AeroPress.

What WIRED loves about the Breville Impress (6/10, WIRED Review) is that beginners can make good to very good espresso right out of the box, since Breville has automated some of the trickier elements of espresso-making — the downside being a good bit of variation from shot to shot. The Barista Touch is a coffee shop in a machine, with a built-in burr grinder, a hot water spout, and a digital display to control brews; tap the Latte button and it cranks out a latte, even foaming the milk, with results not as good as by hand but a nice option if you're feeling lazy. Of the milk frothers, dozens are available on Amazon from dozens of spammy companies selling more or less the same device, none outstanding, but the writer bought one a few years ago and it's still going, useful for frothing milk or mixing matcha tea — and at $8, little is lost if it doesn't last. On kettles: nothing beats the speed and convenience of an electric kettle for heating water to a precise temperature, and stovetop kettles can't compete on any criteria; pour-over coffee, which many WIRED staffers heartily recommend, needs a gooseneck for a precise pour, and the nearly identical EKG+ (8/10, WIRED Recommends) has a smooth pour and holds its temperature for up to an hour before auto shut-off, though its 2.5-foot cable could stand to be longer. WIRED's Gear Team goes nuts for coffee, and the ingenious AeroPress is one of its favorite portable coffee makers — a damn fine cup, incredibly easy to use and clean, making up to 10 fluid ounces at a time, about a diner mug's worth.
