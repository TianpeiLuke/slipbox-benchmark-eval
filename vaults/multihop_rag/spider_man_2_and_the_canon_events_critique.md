# Spider-Man 2 and the Canon Events Critique

Polygon's claim on 2023-10-16 is that while Marvel's Spider-Man 2 does not push against established Spider-Man story as hard as the critic wanted, where it does go is still satisfyingly slant — and that this matters, because if we are going to retell a story we ought to retell it anew: canons were made to be broken, and Spider-Man 2 swings in that direction.

The standard the critic sets is high, and named: they were hungry for the game to treat the established story like Angela Carter repurposing "Little Red Riding Hood" to her whims, like Michael Cunningham plumbing "Rumpelstiltskin" for a story about the desire to have a child, or like Helen Oyeyemi and her body of work — or, in mass media terms, like Star Wars: Visions, which gives creative leeway to a variety of animators and storytellers to play with the very basics of what makes Star Wars Star Wars.

The grounds for the qualified verdict are that wrinkles to critical characters and plot points keep even seasoned Spider-fans on their toes, and that the game plays like a dream: smart additions to combat enhance the power fantasy, with each Spider-Man having a full arsenal of special moves and gadgets that complement one another, making even the most basic encounter feel like a choreographed ballet of kicks, thwips, and zaps. It is beautifully rendered, taking full advantage of the newest PlayStation, with textures on each unlockable suit so detailed you would swear they were real and ray-traced window after ray-traced window in a loving rendition of New York — if you can get past the Frankenstein-ification of the boroughs.

The critic also registers what the game gestures at but cannot develop: hints of greater thematic aspirations appear, including questions about mass incarceration, recidivism, and reintegration into society for formerly incarcerated people, and worries about the surveillance state and what we give up when we engage with modern technology. These appear only fleetingly, because there is not enough time to elaborate on any single one in the unrelenting march of set-piece after glorious set-piece — but the critic is glad they are there, and with a sequel teed up by the game's final act is eager to see where Insomniac goes next.
