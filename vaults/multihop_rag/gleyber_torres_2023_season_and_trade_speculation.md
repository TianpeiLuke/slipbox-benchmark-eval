# Gleyber Torres: The Trade Case Against Him, And Why His Own Numbers Rebut It

Essentially Sports argues on 2023-10-10 that the Yankees should not include Gleyber Torres in their 2024 reset, because his worth as a player outweighs the money his trade would save. The case for trading him: although it was a decent season for the second baseman, the lack of consistency brought him under scrutiny, and because he is in arbitration with the Yankees his trade chances rise — the team won't want to lose the opportunity to cash in some money from a trade. The context is a franchise in turmoil: the Yankees had been in the news since the league stage ended, first over the possibility of a third-party audit that was later shunted out, then over comments from Aaron Judge that drew attention to the workings of the team, conflicts and divisions between the core four, and now over assumptions of changes with the idea of cutting down the $300 million budget for the next season. Two specific reasons drive the interest in moving him: mlbtraderumors.com suggests Torres is expected to earn $15.3 million next year, so his trade could save the Yankees some money, and the Miami Marlins have been interested in him for a long time, giving the Pinstripes a good deal.

The rebuttal is the data. The 26-year-old avoided arbitration ahead of the 2023 season and signed a $9.95 million contract — a brilliant move given the requirements of the Bronx Bombers — and Torres fulfilled it for most of the season, finishing on a high with 90 runs, 25 home runs and 68 RBIs at an average of 0.273 and an OPS of 0.800. He also finished at the top of the batting charts for the team alongside captain Aaron Judge, clinching the most hits and recording an OBP of 0.347, and finished second in the fielding department behind the team's Gold Glove prospect Anthony Volpe, making 80 double plays with 563 chances created at a fielding percentage of 0.975. That performance was crucial in a season filled with injuries on both fronts. The conclusion: nothing worked well for the Yankees this season — their worst finish in seven years even with all the resources — and while the shift Brian Cashman is pressing encompasses alterations in both offensive and defensive personnel, they should refrain from including Torres because of his worth as a player; the $15 million expectation may sound huge, but it is not a big ask for the multibillion-dollar Yankees, and the 2009 champions can easily fulfill this request without constraining their spending.
