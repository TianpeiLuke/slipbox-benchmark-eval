# CFPB Consumer Redress Ordered From The Big Four Banks

At a congressional hearing on Wednesday reported by Fortune on 2023-12-06, Sen. Robert Menendez established that the Consumer Financial Protection Bureau had ordered the four largest US retail banks to return nearly $4 billion to consumers over the past dozen years: $360 million from JPMorgan Chase, $819 million from Bank of America, $1 billion from Citigroup, and "over $2 billion" from Wells Fargo.

Menendez put the question to four retail bank CEOs in turn — JPMorgan Chase's Jamie Dimon, Bank of America's Brian Moynihan, Citigroup's Jane Fraser, and Wells Fargo's Charles Scharff — asking each how much his or her firm had "returned to customers in the form of redresses and payments at the direction of CFPB?" None of the executives was able to answer. The alleged violations of federal law behind those payments included charging illegal junk fees and opening fake customer accounts. "It's amazing you don't know the number because they're not small in nature," Menendez said. "It's over $4 billion returned to hardworking consumers in the past dozen years."

The senator's purpose in the questioning was rhetorical as much as factual: he was attempting to show that, contrary to what the bank CEOs argued, new financial regulations do benefit everyday customers.
