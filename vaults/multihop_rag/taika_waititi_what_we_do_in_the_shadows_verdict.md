# Taika Waititi: Vampire Verdict

Polygon's verdict on Taika Waititi, best known in this line for What We Do in the Shadows (2014, 2019-present), is the guide's most severe: Dracula.

The piece first waves away two objections — What We Do in the Shadows is not a work of prose fiction and Waititi is not an author — with the shrug that who is to say what constitutes true literature anyway. It then gives the work's significance: co-created with fellow New Zealander Jemaine Clement, with the two writing and directing the original 2014 movie, it is surely the most culturally pervasive piece of vampire-themed fiction to reach mass audiences over the past few years, and it marks the necessary next step in the inexorable movement of vampiric lore from creeping terror to sexy camp to outright comedy with only the faintest twist of terror to remind us of its origins — two centuries after its English-language birth, the vampire genre has at last been metaphorically defanged. The grounds for the verdict are Waititi's public trajectory: a director and writer who started out charmingly likable, beloved by audiences and critics, was happily invited into our homes, rapidly wore out his welcome, and almost before we knew it curdled into an odorous pestilence we could not seem to be rid of — and when you lose the Chris Hemsworth demographic, you know you are in trouble. Yet audiences keep coming back year after year, drawn by a force they are powerless to explain; when it comes to Our Flag Means Death, we are all Renfields. And since nothing could serve the vampire cause better than tricking us into thinking the threat had passed, the conclusion is that we have not just found a vampire but their king.
