# The DOJ Theory That Google's Default Deals Foreclose Search Rivals

The Justice Department's case against Google is focused on the deals Google makes — with Apple, but also with Samsung and Mozilla and many others — to ensure it is the default search engine on practically every platform.

The grounds the DOJ pressed through Eddy Cue's testimony were framed as a single question: whether Apple picked Google because it is the most lucrative choice or the best product. That question was a key part of Cue's testimony and, in The Verge's account, a key part of the DOJ's entire case. The warrant is the inference joining the two: if the defaults are bought rather than earned, then the network of default agreements, not product quality, is what keeps Google in place across platforms.
