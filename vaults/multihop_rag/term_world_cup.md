# World Cup

## Definition

In the corpus, "World Cup" names a sport's top international championship, but its shape differs by sport: the ICC's men's ODI Cricket World Cup and World Rugby's Rugby World Cup (RWC) each crown a winner via a national-team final, FIFA's football World Cup is preceded by confederation qualifying such as CONMEBOL's, and Mikaela Shiffrin's Alpine skiing World Cup is tracked in the corpus as a tally of individual "World Cup wins" building toward an "overall World Cup" title rather than a single match.

## Context

In the corpus the term spans multiple sports and formats.
The ICC organises the Cricket World Cup "every four years," a men's ODI tournament whose 1975 edition (hosted by England, contested by eight teams including a composite East Africa/Sri Lanka side) established the pattern of a single-host, knockout-culminating event; the 2023 edition ran in India from October 5 to November 19, and its final pitted "two-time World Champions" India against "five-time World Champions" Australia.
World Rugby's Rugby World Cup (RWC) crowned the Springboks in the corpus's 2023 final — a 12-11 win over the All Blacks in France, reported under the headline "claim record FOURTH RWC"; the cited passage covers that single match and its aftermath, not the tournament's periodicity or hosting structure.
FIFA's World Cup operates on a longer cycle with a distinct qualification stage: national confederations such as CONMEBOL run "World Cup qualifying" matches (e.g., Argentina vs Paraguay for the 2026 tournament) years ahead of the finals themselves.
By contrast, the Alpine skiing World Cup that Mikaela Shiffrin dominates is not one tournament: the corpus counts her record in terms of individual "World Cup wins" and "five overall World Cup victories," a cumulative tally rather than a knockout final (see Background for how that circuit is structured).

## Key Characteristics

- **Governing-body run** — the corpus explicitly credits the ICC with organising the Cricket World Cup (doc_0409) and refers to the football World Cup as a "FIFA tournament" (doc_0105), with confederations such as CONMEBOL organising qualifying (doc_0105).
- **Periodic, at least for cricket** — only the Cricket World Cup's cadence is explicitly quoted in the corpus as "every four years" (doc_0409); the cited Rugby World Cup passage does not state a periodicity for the RWC.
- **National-team contest culminating in a final** — the cricket and rugby World Cups in the corpus each end in a single final: India vs Australia at Ahmedabad in 2023 (doc_0246), and the Springboks' 12-11 win over the All Blacks in France (doc_0022).
- **Separate qualification phase** — for football, "World Cup qualifying" is a distinct, multi-year stage (run per confederation, e.g. CONMEBOL) that precedes the finals tournament itself (doc_0105).
- **Not always a single event** — in individual sports such as Alpine skiing, the corpus describes "World Cup" wins accumulating toward an "overall World Cup" title for Mikaela Shiffrin (doc_0585) rather than a single match; the underlying season-long, points-based structure is documented externally (see Background).

## Background (external)

The FIS Alpine Ski World Cup that doc_0585 references is, per Wikipedia, a season-long circuit of races (roughly January to mid-March) rather than a single tournament.
Points are awarded to the top 30 finishers in each race (100 for first place, sliding to 1 for 30th, a system in place since the 1991-92 season), and the skier with the most cumulative points at season's end wins the overall title, symbolised by the "big crystal globe," with separate discipline titles awarded via smaller globes.
Source: Wikipedia, "FIS Alpine Ski World Cup," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/FIS_Alpine_Ski_World_Cup, accessed 2026-09-02.
