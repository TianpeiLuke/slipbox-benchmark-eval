# Whether A VPN Is Worth It

Whether VPNs are worth it depends on how often you would actually use one for the standard use cases: if you travel a lot and rely on public WiFi or hotspots, are looking to browse outside your home country, or want to keep your traffic hidden from your ISP, then investing in a VPN will be useful — but even the best VPN services often slow down your internet connection speed, so they may not be ideal all the time.

The stronger claim is that a VPN should not be your main cybersecurity tool. The grounds are that VPN use can provide a false sense of security, leaving you vulnerable to attack, and that choosing just any VPN may leave you less secure than simply relying on your ISP — because the VPN could be based in a country with weaker data privacy regulation, be obligated to hand information over to law enforcement, or be linked to weak user data protection policies.

For users in professions like activism or journalism who want to really strengthen their internet security, Crandall suggests options like the Tor browser may be a worthwhile alternative: Tor is free, and while it is less user-friendly, it is built for anonymity and privacy.
