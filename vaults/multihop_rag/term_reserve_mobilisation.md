# Reserve Mobilisation

## Definition

In the corpus's Israeli context, reserve mobilisation ("mobilizing reserves," being "called up to reserve duty") is treated as a distinct, weighty escalation step separate from readying the standing army: Major General (Res.) Giora Eiland, former head of the IDF Planning Division, contrasts a commander's routine authority to put troops on alert with the much higher-level decision needed "to mobilize reserves or convene a cabinet," implying reserve call-up sits near the top of a state's response ladder.

## Context

The same source recounts that ahead of the 1973 Yom Kippur War, Israeli leadership gathered intelligence pointing to an Egyptian-Syrian attack but "decided not to mobilize reserves because the cost was high," choosing instead to under-prepare because they judged war unlikely — a decision the analyst frames as the direct historical analogue of the intelligence failure preceding the October 7, 2023 attack. Economist Leo Leiderman's account of the Israel-Gaza war's fiscal impact treats reserve mobilisation as a concrete budget line: he names "payment for the reserve duty days of those mobilized" as a direct wartime expense alongside procurement of equipment, ammunition and advanced systems. TechCrunch's reporting on Israeli tech firms shows the same mobilisation from the workforce side: founders and executives describe 10-30% of tech employees being called to reserve duty in the days after October 7, 2023, straining business operations even as companies tried to keep running.

## Key Characteristics

- **A discrete escalation decision, not an automatic response** — ordering reserve mobilisation is distinguished from routine troop readiness and is described as requiring a decision comparable in weight to convening the cabinet.
- **Weighed against cost** — the Yom Kippur War account shows the decision not to mobilise reserves being made explicitly because "the cost was high," i.e., the cost of mobilisation itself was a factor leaders weighed against the probability of attack.
- **A recurring fiscal cost channel in wartime budgeting** — Leiderman itemises paid reserve duty days as one of several direct expenses (alongside equipment and ammunition procurement) that raise wartime defence spending.
- **A visible labour-market and business shock** — because reservists are drawn from the civilian workforce, mobilisation shows up immediately as workforce loss for employers; Israeli tech firms reported 10-30% of employees called up within the war's first week.

## Background (external)

Outside this corpus, "mobilization" is generally defined as the act of assembling and readying military troops and supplies for war, a category that includes calling up reservists to active duty (Wikipedia, "Mobilization," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobilization).
