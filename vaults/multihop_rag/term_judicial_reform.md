# Judicial Reform (Israel, 2023)

## Definition

Judicial reform, in the corpus, is the Israeli government's contested 2023 "bid for judicial reform" — a domestic political fight the corpus treats as a settled point of reference (a fault line the country was already living through before the Hamas attacks of October 7) rather than something it explains from first principles. What the reform actually proposed is set out only in the Background (external) section below, not in the cited corpus documents.

## Context

The corpus surfaces the reform twice, each time as backdrop to a different story rather than as its subject. In the Eylon Levy profile, the reform is the fault line of Levy's own pre-war life: he "was also active in the protests against the government's judicial reform," and Globes pressed him on the shift — "one day you are at a demonstration against the government and the next day you are its spokesperson" (doc_0413). In the TechCrunch piece on Israel's startup ecosystem, the reform is the fault line of the tech sector's pre-war life: "the tech industry led opposition to the government's bid for judicial reform," with companies and investors "already refusing to do business in the country with the prospect of the reforms getting pushed through," a boycott effort that "has been brought to a halt" once the war made the two sides of that debate "stand together for what they see as the significantly bigger fight and threat" (doc_0448).

## Key Characteristics

- **Government-initiated, not judiciary-initiated** — doc_0448 describes it as the government's "bid" for reform; both docs frame it as opposed from outside government (doc_0413, doc_0448).
- **Provoked street protests** — Levy is identified by his participation in "the protests against the reform," which he describes as "no secret" (doc_0413).
- **Provoked tech-sector opposition** — the tech industry "led opposition" to the reform bid, with some companies and investors already refusing to do business in the country over it, before the war began (doc_0448).
- **Superseded, not resolved, by the war** — doc_0448 frames the reform fight as merely "brought to a halt" by October 7, with opposing sides now "standing together" against what they see as a bigger threat, not as a dispute that was settled; doc_0413 echoes this, with Levy describing pre-war disputes as something Israeli society "pulled our hair out over" that now look small against the war.

## Background (external)

The corpus names the "judicial reform" and its protest and boycott fallout but does not describe what the reform itself proposed. Per Wikipedia, the reform package introduced by Justice Minister Yariv Levin and MK Simcha Rothman in January 2023 proposed giving the government majority control over the committee that selects judges, curbing the Supreme Court's power to strike down legislation (including Basic Laws), letting the Knesset override certain court rulings with a 61-seat majority, making government legal advisors' opinions non-binding, and eliminating "unreasonableness" as a ground for judicial review of administrative decisions — changes aimed at reducing judicial checks on the government and the Knesset (Wikipedia, *2023 Israeli judicial reform*, accessed 2026-09-02). Mass protests began almost immediately after the plan's introduction in January 2023, well before the events covered in the corpus.
