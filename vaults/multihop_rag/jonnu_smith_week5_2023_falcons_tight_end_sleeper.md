# Jonnu Smith As A Week 5 2023 Sneaky Sleeper At Tight End

CBS Sports listed Jonnu Smith as a "Sneaky Sleeper" for Week 5 of the 2023 season against Houston: the claim is that Smith is doing what Kyle Pitts is supposed to do — get open and make plays after the catch — and the warrant is that Houston's heavy zone coverage leaves open the short middle of the field, which is exactly where Smith operates.

The role grounds compare the two Atlanta tight ends directly. Week 4 was the second time in three weeks Smith outproduced Pitts, and the first time it was so lopsided; the difference in snaps played and routes run is marginal, with Pitts doing more of both, but Smith is taking advantage of shorter routes with a quarterback who doesn't like to challenge downfield much. Smith averaged more yards per catch not only in Week 4 but also in Week 2, and was behind Pitts by less than a yard in Week 3 when both had at least eight targets. On film Smith is running shorter routes and getting open faster than Pitts, which has earned him targets from Desmond Ridder, who seemed to have better timing with Smith; Smith is not drawing tight coverage like Pitts, mostly because he stays closer to the line of scrimmage, and after the catch he has been a handful, breaking three tackles in Week 4 alone and picking up extra yards — not fast, but faster than Pitts, and for at least Week 4 and Week 2 that changed the calculus for how Atlanta divvied up targets. He certainly hasn't done anything to lose his role.

The matchup grounds are Houston's tight end problem. The Texans have had a bundle of troubles with tight ends, giving up an 84% catch rate and 6.76 yards after catch per reception to the position, both bottom-six in the league; that includes allowing an 89% catch rate on 18 total targets to Colts and Jaguars tight ends in Weeks 2 and 3, for 9.9 yards per catch and 7.2 yards after catch per reception. Those numbers were a byproduct of the amount of zone coverage Houston played against both teams, leaving the short middle of the field open, and Houston has played the third-most zone coverage of any team this year and should do so again. The breakdown lists Atlanta at home versus Houston, the Falcons favoured by 2, an over/under of 41.5, an opponent ranked 18th versus tight ends, 5.2 projected points, a 24th tight end ranking, and year-to-date totals of 15 receptions on 20 targets for 179 yards, no touchdowns and 8.2 fantasy points per game. The recommendation is bounded twice: the hope depends on the game staying competitive, as most Falcons games tend to be, and while the author doesn't mind chancing it with Smith as a streaming tight end, he doesn't have the conviction to start him over Pitts.
