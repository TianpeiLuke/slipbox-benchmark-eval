# The Cyberstalker's Twitter Campaign Against Asian Academics

When Janani Umamaheswar looked up the Twitter account her former colleague had flagged in March 2022, she found thousands of tweets posted under S.'s name — most of them racist, sexual, vulgar, and violent, and little of the ranting coherent. The user tweeted at all hours, sometimes nearly a hundred times a day, and the tweets focused solely on three people: Umamaheswar, her husband Alex Sinha, and the Vassar professor Catherine Tan who had alerted them. The account had started posting in October of 2021 — seven months of tweets before they knew it existed. It featured an image of a white woman's face that Umamaheswar recognized as the student she once taught.

The targeting spread by association rather than contact. Sinha had never met or taught S., had never been employed on the same campus as his wife, and by then they lived over six hours away from her; Tan had likewise never taught or met S. and did not know her personally, but had published papers with Umamaheswar, and the account's author had linked Tan back to her, likely through that academic work and their benign social media interactions. Anyone Umamaheswar collaborated with professionally or even interacted with online had become a potential target. The tweets frequently denigrated all three for being Asian, using racial slurs against Umamaheswar and Sinha, alleging Sinha was abusive to his wife, and including lines such as "Live in hell bitch" and "I have super detailed deaths I like to think about them experiencing."

The threats escalated over time and across accounts. Sinha decided he needed to monitor the account closely, screenshot everything, and head off any potential danger; Umamaheswar did not want to keep looking. The graphic nature and racist sentiments sent a wave of fear and anger through Sinha, coming in a year when a white man had murdered six Asian women in three Atlanta-area spas and anti-Asian hate crimes had increased by over 300 percent. "There was this moment of 'wow, this has been happening all this time?'" Sinha said. "We moved to another state. We've been living our lives. We've been raising our kids." The tweets kept coming almost every week except during periods when S. was suspended by Twitter before restarting under a new account, reaching at least 40,000 tweets and counting by Sinha's estimate. They included racist slurs casting Sinha as a "dirty Indian hacker" and a terrorist; a tweet from April 16th, 2022 calling for someone to "get rid of" him; a post a month later pairing an image of a grisly murder in Game of Thrones with a farewell to Alex; and "I would like for these professors to die." As Sinha put it: "She calls for people to murder us. She says that she wants me raped. She would pay money to watch us bleed to death." Other tweets described wanting the three of them hanging off a cliff so she could step on their hands and watch them fall, and ended: "I want them to suffer." While Sinha captured the images, Umamaheswar began writing letters to her current administration and to Vassar on behalf of Tan, alerting them to her history with S.
