# Disney Fans' Conflicting Nostalgic Demands

This passage answers the implicit objection that Disney is simply giving fans what they ask for, by attacking the premise that fan demand for the past is a coherent target at all.

Hardcore Disney fans are not always keen on new things: they cry out for a return to their favorites, to the memory of watching a Disney movie for the first time. But what that means varies wildly — demanding that animated Disney movies have proper villains again, asking for more musicals or fewer of them, lamenting the loss of traditional 2D animation, specifically missing the animated musical sequences of the Disney Renaissance, or even yearning for a return to the animal-focused movies of the 1970s. Whatever that Disney draw is, it is different for every single person, so because fans have so many separate, individualized desires it is impossible for the company to meet every one. The deeper attack is on what the demand actually expresses: more often than not, even when people cry out for the good old days, what they are really missing is the feeling of discovering something new — that first viewing of a movie that will become a lasting favorite. Trying to check off all the items on a checklist that does not even describe what people want does not make good movies; it barely makes tolerable ones.
