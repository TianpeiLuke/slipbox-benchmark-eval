# Tech Layoffs In November 2023

November 2023 saw broad tech job cuts across gaming, media, mobility, crypto and enterprise software, with Amazon cutting in at least three separate divisions and OpenSea laying off about half of its staff.

Early in the month, Splunk said on November 1 that it would cut around 500 employees ahead of its planned acquisition by Cisco, CEO Gary Steele told employees; Informatica announced in its Q3 results on November 1 that it was laying off 10% of its global workforce. On November 2 the esports brand 100 Thieves said it was reducing its workforce by 20% and spinning out its game studio and its energy drink brand Juvee, CEO John Robinson announced, and OpenSpace conducted a round of layoffs announced by CEO Jeevan Kalanithi, with the number of affected employees not yet reported. On November 3 Niu confirmed to TechCrunch a round of layoffs affecting about 10% of its workforce, citing "fierce competition"; Faire laid off about 250 people as part of restructuring, the wholesale marketplace having raised a $416 million extension at a $12.6 billion valuation the previous year; Starz said it was laying off more than 10% of its staff ahead of spinning out from Lionsgate, with CEO Jeffrey Hirsch announcing that the network and streaming app would also exit Australia and the U.K.; and OpenSea confirmed it had laid off about 50% of its staff, with CEO Devin Finzer posting on X that the NFT marketplace was now focused on "OpenSea 2.0." Ava Labs cut 12% of its workforce, CEO Emin Gün Sirer confirmed on November 6, and Beyond Meat announced cuts on November 6 affecting around 19% of its non-production employees, saying it would also review its entire global operations for cost-cutting opportunities. Nextdoor revealed in its Q3 earnings that it was reducing its workforce by 25%. Ubisoft Montreal confirmed on November 7 that it would cut 98 roles in business administration and IT, with 124 positions eliminated overall across Canada including reductions in its global IT team and its VFX studio Hybride.

On November 8, Virgin Galactic told staff in a memo that it was cutting costs and reducing staff, with the number of employees impacted unknown; Google confirmed it had eliminated "a small number of roles" in its consumer support staff, Google Users & Products; Amazon confirmed it had started cutting workers in its music streaming division in Latin America, North America and Europe; and Snap laid off fewer than 20 project managers, with CNBC reporting that Snap's vice president of engineering, Nima Khajehnouri, would also be leaving. On November 9 Cruise started laying off contingent workers who supported driverless operations, after the company recalled 950 robotaxis and lost key commercial permits to operate in California, and Pico, ByteDance's Oculus challenger, cut a "few hundred" roles, holding an internal meeting announcing it was halting its expansion that week. Carta was reportedly cutting more of its workforce in what would be the unicorn startup's third layoff round that year, with the number of positions unknown. Amazon cut "just over 180" roles in its gaming division, VP Christoph Hartmann wrote to employees on November 13. Chewy laid off more than 200 employees on November 14, with cuts hitting HR, recruiting, data and business intelligence roles plus directors and higher managers. Sega reportedly planned to "phase out" 80 temporary workers by 2024 and now faces an unfair labor practice complaint, with workers alleging the layoffs are retaliation for unionizing. Paystack laid off 33 employees in Europe and Dubai on November 16 as it cut down on operations outside Africa. Beamery reportedly cut 25% of its workforce amid a larger restructuring and cost-cutting effort. FreshBooks laid off 6% of its workforce on November 17 as it shut down operations in Raleigh, North Carolina, with president Mark Girvan and CEO Don Epperson also leaving the company. Amazon was reportedly laying off "several hundred" employees in its Alexa division and its freshly launched Artificial General Intelligence team.

Late in the month, Jodo reportedly cut around 100 roles across engineering, customer success, data and sales on November 20, and C3.ai reportedly cut jobs across departments on November 20 citing a need to reduce costs, with people familiar telling Bloomberg that several impacted employees received just one month of severance. Alerzo cut 100 workers on November 21, reportedly due to increases in automation, after cutting 15% of its workforce earlier that year and 5% in 2022. Anar shut down operations and returned capital to investors, CEO Nishank Jain announced on November 23, and Tulip laid off 25 employees, CEO Ali Asaria announced November 24. Veev closed up shop according to multiple reports, after reaching unicorn status in 2022. On November 27 the EV battery startup ONE cut its workforce by around 25% citing "market conditions," and ByteDance started mass layoffs in its gaming department, Nuverse, after two years of tepid performance, with the number of affected employees unknown. Multiverse said it would lay off nearly a third of its U.S. staff, the second round of layoffs at the U.K.-based unicorn in just over a month. Dataminr began laying off about 20% of its staff on November 28, citing "the recent rapid advancements of our AI platform" according to a memo from founder and CEO Ted Bailey, and Tier Mobility cut roughly 22% of its staff, CEO and co-founder Lawrence Leuschner announced November 28. Unity laid off 265 workers on November 29 after eliminating its Wētā Digital division, acquired in 2021. On November 30 Loco cut its workforce by about 36%, announced by co-founders Anirudh Pandita and Ashwin Suresh at a town hall; the A-Rod and Marc Lore-founded sports betting startup Mojo, which had previously raised $100 million, told staff it was laying off 20%; Domo made reductions to 7% of staff plus reductions in its contract workforce, CEO Joshua G. James announced on the Q3 earnings call; and Zepz cut 30 roles after the fintech unicorn eliminated 26% of its workforce earlier that year.
