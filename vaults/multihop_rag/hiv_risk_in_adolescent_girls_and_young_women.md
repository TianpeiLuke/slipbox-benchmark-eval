# HIV Risk in Adolescent Girls and Young Women

Nearly 1 in 6 new HIV cases in 2022 were in girls and women ages 15 to 24, many of whom are in sub-Saharan Africa, which makes better reach into this group — especially with prevention and testing — a requirement for hitting the 95-95-95 target.

Once diagnosed and started on ART, women's viral suppression rates are "high and so are survival benefits," Quarraisha Abdool Karim said: among all diagnosed women over 15, 82% had access to ART and 76% were virally suppressed in 2022. But starting ART first requires being tested for HIV, and testing rates remain low in hard-hit regions, particularly among teens. Many of the hardest-hit regions lack prevention programs for young women, and the few existing programs often miss girls who are not in school. Girls facing a lack of education, poverty and food insecurity have an especially high risk of HIV, as do girls with older male partners; intimate-partner violence and sexual coercion often mean they cannot control when they are exposed to HIV. In some countries HIV services require parental consent, which can further reduce girls' access to prevention and treatment. Improving girls' access to discreet prevention services as well as sex education — both in and out of school — will be key to reducing their HIV rates, and cabotegravir, which is "stunningly effective against vaginal acquisition of HIV," could be a powerful tool for HIV prevention in women, Dr. Raphael Landovitz said.
