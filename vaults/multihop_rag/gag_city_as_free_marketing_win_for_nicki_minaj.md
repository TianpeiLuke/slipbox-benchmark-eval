# Gag City As A Free Marketing Win For Nicki Minaj

TechCrunch's writer argues that whatever its provenance, Gag City is a win for Nicki Minaj. Is it an authentic stan-led campaign to build hype, or a plant to game engagement for both the album and the brands? What is clear is that the viral moment is a win for Minaj, manufactured or not — it is free promotion, and as a lifelong Barb who spent her adolescence running a stan account for "Pink Friday," the writer counts it as one.

The reason the free promotion matters so much here is Minaj's history with brands. She is an artist who has been embroiled in controversy throughout her career, from posting bad takes about COVID vaccines to defending her husband Kenneth Petty, a convicted sex offender; she may be a brilliant artist, but her problematic history makes her far from the family-friendly public figure that brands are more likely to endorse. With Gag City, Minaj has brands doing all of her marketing for her.

The final ground is a contrast with the prevailing playbook. "Pink Friday 2" is an artistic marvel in itself, the writer says, but the free promotion it has received as a viral meme is particularly astounding: artists have spent the last few years trying to drum up engagement by making their songs trend on TikTok, which audiences have started to resist, whereas Gag City does not bank on being the viral song of the summer to drive streaming numbers — the bit is removed enough for non-stans to enjoy it, while still revolving around the album it is promoting.
