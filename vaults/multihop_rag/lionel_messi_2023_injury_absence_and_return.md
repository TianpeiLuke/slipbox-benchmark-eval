# Messi's Four-Game Injury Absence And His 35-Minute Return

Lionel Messi missed four straight Inter Miami games with injury before returning off the bench for 35 minutes against FC Cincinnati on Oct. 7, 2023. He was left out entirely for the Oct. 4 trip to the Chicago Fire: 35 minutes before kickoff the lineups showed him not part of the squad, with Josef Martinez up front as Leonardo Campana was out with a knock. An hour before kickoff, Michelle Kaufman of the Miami Herald reported Messi "did not arrive with the team" to Soldier Field — a strong indication rather than outright confirmation that he would not play. Two hours out, Sporting News noted Gerardo Martino had played his cards extremely close to his chest each week about Messi's injury: asked about Jordi Alba's return, Martino said a timeline was in place, but asked about Messi's he refused to acknowledge whether one existed, saying "I understand your desire to know the exact timelines, but if I had the exact timelines I would share them. I just don't have them." Sporting News judged his involvement unlikely given recent matches but thought he could be more ready over the weekend against FC Cincinnati. Meanwhile Chicago was still feeling Messi fever, with Messi shirt vendors outside Soldier Field and the Fire media team joking that they had "the GOAT in the building."

Against FC Cincinnati he was available. Two hours before kickoff, well-connected Argentine journalist Gaston Edul had reported the night before that Inter Miami were likely to have Messi back in some capacity — "the idea is for Leo Messi to get minutes when Inter Miami plays FC Cincinnati" — which sounded like bench availability, alongside reporting that Messi was battling "fatigue" and a "scar tissue issue." Lineups 45 minutes out confirmed him on the bench, with a photo of his jersey hanging in his locker taken as a good indication he would return. Thirty minutes out, his availability was called a welcome sight for a club whose backs were against the wall and which had to win to stay alive. He entered in the 55th minute, replacing Tomas Aviles in a very attacking change with around 35 minutes to save Inter Miami's season, alongside Jean Mota for David Ruiz. By the time he stepped on the pitch, Inter Miami's early dominance had come and gone; Messi played a part but was unable to fully change the tide of the game.

Five days later, ahead of Argentina's World Cup qualifier, Sporting News noted Messi had been dealing with fitness concerns for weeks and had missed significant time for his club, but was on the national team roster and expected to play a part. According to a report from Argentina he would not start but was likely to play much or all of the second half. With Inter Miami's playoff run officially over, Argentina could feel better about deploying him for a full 90 minutes despite his recent fitness issues, knowing his club season was nearly over and months of rest lay ahead; he had missed the previous match against Bolivia and four Inter Miami games after that, then returned for a half-hour in the club's final fixture before the international break.
