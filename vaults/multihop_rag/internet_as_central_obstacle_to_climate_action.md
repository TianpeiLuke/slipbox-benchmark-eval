# The Internet As A Central Obstacle To Climate Action

Buck's claim is that the three forces of rising authoritarianism, climate change and tech platform domination do not lead where you would expect: the political economy of online media has boxed us into a social landscape wherein both the political consensus and the infrastructure we need for the energy transition are impossible to build, making the current configuration of the Internet a key obstacle to climate action.

The grounds are several mechanisms, of which the promotion of false information about climate change is only one. There is general political polarization, which inhibits the coalitions we need to build to realize clean energy and also creates paralyzing infighting within the climate movement about strategies — infighting the platforms benefit from. There is networked opposition to the infrastructure we need for the energy transition. There is constant distraction from the climate crisis in the form of the churning scandals of the day, in an attention economy where all topics compete for mental energy. And there is the drain of time and attention spent on these platforms rather than in real-world actions.

The warrant is that climate strategy is not merely informed by the media ecology but constituted within it, so a media system that prevents coalition-building prevents the transition itself. The essay narrows to how the contemporary media ecology interferes with climate strategy and infrastructure in particular, which requires a closer look at the concept of energy democracy as generally understood by the climate movement and its tenets: renewable, small-scale systems, and community control. The bitter irony of the current moment is that it is not just rising authoritarianism blocking us from good futures — it is also our narrow and warped conceptions of democracy that are trapping us.
