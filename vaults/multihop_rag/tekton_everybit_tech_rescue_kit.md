# Tekton Everybit Tech Rescue Kit

The Tekton Everybit Tech Rescue Kit was $22, down from $29 — 24 percent off at Amazon. It is a 46-piece screwdriver kit that can help you repair practically any tech device out there, and it is managing editor Alex Cranz's staff favorite.

Cranz owns at least two of them and frequently buys them for friends and family. For an average price of $35 you get a screwdriver with nearly every bit you would need for most gadgets — including the weird ones for Apple products — plus a plastic and a metal spudger, tweezers, and a suction cup. She has replaced batteries in iPhones with this kit, built entire PCs with it, swapped out backplates on Steam Decks and housings of Joy-Con controllers with one, and even used it to repair her eyeglasses.

One of the best parts is that it all goes in a single case that can be tossed in a computer bag or purse or left in a desk drawer at the office. But the main reason she loves it is the selection and quality of the bits: too often precision screwdrivers have super soft bits that strip the first time you use them with a screw tightened by a machine, and since most gadgets have at least one too-tight screw, she went through quite a few cheap screwdriver kits before settling on this one. While she is slowly building out a high-quality selection of precision screwdrivers, most people do not have that luxury or necessity — and, she adds, you feel like kind of a badass when someone asks you to help fix a gadget and you just pull this kit out of your bag in the middle of Starbucks and get to work.
