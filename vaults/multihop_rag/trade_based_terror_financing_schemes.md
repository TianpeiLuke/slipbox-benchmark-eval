# Trade-Based Terror Financing Schemes

Another way to transfer money is trade — transferring goods, or inflating their value — a channel Israeli authorities have interdicted repeatedly at ports and crossings.

Two seizures illustrate the method. Only days before the November 2023 interview, containers of goods worth hundreds of thousands of shekels were seized at Ashdod Port, destined for Hamas merchants in Nablus and other cities, with the proceeds intended to finance Hamas operations. In August 2021, 23 tons of chocolate bars were seized in a joint operation by the NBCTF, AMAN and the Tax Authority at the Nitzana Border Crossing between Israel and Egypt; it was suspected the snack bars, destined for Gaza, were purchased with terror financing funds, with the proceeds from their sales earmarked for Hamas. A third affair combined trade and hawala: Hamas funds originating in Iran were transferred in cash to money changers in Turkey for transfer to Gaza, while Hamed al-Khachari, a Gazan money changer who worked for Hamas and was assassinated by Israel in 2019, located Gazan merchants who imported goods from Turkish companies and owed payment on them; they paid cash to Khachari, who transferred the money to Hamas in Gaza, while the money changers in Turkey received funds from Hamas abroad that were used to pay the Turkish companies.
