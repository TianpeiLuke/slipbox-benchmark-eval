# The Brussels Airport Bombing Superbug Case

A woman who survived the 2016 Brussels Airport bombing was left with a drug-resistant Klebsiella pneumoniae infection that refused to be vanquished despite years of antibiotic treatment, and doctors finally rid her of the superbug only by combining antibiotics with a new, experimental treatment.

The bacteria may have entered her flesh along with shrapnel from the bomb detonated at the airport, or the microbes may have hitched a ride on the surgical instruments used to treat her wounds. Klebsiella pneumoniae is a bacterial strain often picked up by surgery patients in hospitals. Having survived a terrorist attack, she was, in the article's framing, held hostage by the infection that followed.
