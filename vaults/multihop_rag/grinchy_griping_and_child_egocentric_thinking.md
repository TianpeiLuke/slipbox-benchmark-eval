# "Grinchy Griping" and Child Egocentric Thinking

Hannah Keeley's model of why parents should not vent about holiday stress in front of their children has two components that process the same event differently. The "mom brain" is wired to process problems rapidly and move on to solutions. The "child brain", however, is wired to process a problem internally and take on the responsibility.

The relation between the two produces the mismatch: children up to the age of 9 process information egocentrically, which means that if there is a problem, they see themselves as the cause. So the same episode that a parent experiences as temporary venting is received by the child as attribution of blame: "Knowing this, make sure you don't do any 'grinchy griping' out loud to the children. You may see it as temporary venting, but they see it as, 'Mom is upset and I'm probably the reason.'"

The scope condition matters — the egocentric processing Keeley describes holds for children up to the age of 9. Within that range, the prescription is that parents should avoid "processing their frustration around the holidays audibly around the children", and should instead stay calm, find a reason to be grateful and vocalise it, even when frustrations accumulate.
