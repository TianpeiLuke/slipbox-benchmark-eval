# Creative Industries AI Concerns

Artists across different mediums hold the same concerns about AI but do not know it, and that is why organizing across disciplines rather than within one is the source of leverage — the argument Fight for the Future Campaigns and Communications director Lia Holland made to TechCrunch on 2023-10-06. The warrant is stated plainly: "when artists of different mediums move together they have a lot more power."

The grounds are the mutual invisibility Holland reports between fields. "It's funny because if you've talked to musicians who have these concerns, they say, 'Well, authors have been very quiet.' If you talk to others about these concerns, they say, 'Well, musicians and photographers don't seem to care at all,'" she said. "So part of it also is that the different creative fields, when it comes to this sort of work, are a little bit siloed." Hence the design of the day of action: "That was another intent with our launching this effort with the day of action, to try to illustrate how these are common concerns that are shared across artistic mediums. And to create an organizing point."

The argument is deliberately not anti-AI. The campaign targets potential corporate abuse of AI technology, but it's realistic about the ways that musicians and some other creatives could benefit on an individual level from automating parts of their work; the goal is that AI tools "become ways for individual humans to make more money, work less, and compete with the corporations that exploit them." Holland extends this to musicians specifically: "musicians are perhaps more familiar with the idea of AI . . . Musicians in general are more familiar with things like music production software, and AI tools like MIDI drum loops . . . so I think that there is a certain amount of more progressive learning from them, when it comes to technology, and its ability to make their music better."

The complication Holland's framing has to absorb is that the interests within a single industry already conflict. Musicians are nervous about industry giants copyrighting AI music and cutting them out of the process; major record labels are worried about AI models training on their catalogues and stealing a slice of their considerable pie; and Spotify erased thousands of AI-crafted songs from its platform but also recently globally launched an AI-powered DJ that curates music for listeners while talking to them in a synthetic voice.
