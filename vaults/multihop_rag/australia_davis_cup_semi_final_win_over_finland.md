# Australia's Davis Cup Semi-Final Win Over Finland

Australia beat Finland 2-0 in the 2023 Davis Cup semi-final on the Friday (Saturday AEDT), with Alexei Popyrin defeating Otto Virtanen 7-6 (7-5), 6-2 and Alex de Minaur sealing the tie by beating Emil Ruusuvuori 6-4, 6-3, meaning the crack doubles team of Max Purcell and Matt Ebden was not even needed, The Age reported on 24 November 2023.

De Minaur's win was his 11th victory in his last 13 cup singles matches, and he was adamant about the team's standing: "No one really wants to play us." He had been the hero on the Wednesday, pulling out an extraordinary comeback win over Jiri Lehecka with Australia on the verge of defeat in their quarter-final with the Czech Republic, but was happy to pass the laurels to Popyrin, a late squad replacement asked to take on the troublesome "second singles" role by Lleyton Hewitt. "Alexei hasn't played for Australia in a very long time [two years], had a massive match today against a very high-quality opponent and dug super deep," said de Minaur. Popyrin, who defied jangling nerves, made only 11 unforced errors to his erratic opponent's 25 and prevailed in just under an hour and a half. "It was probably the biggest win of my career," he admitted. "To win a match that means so much to us, it's an honour and something I'll never forget. Nothing compares to this. I thought I was ready but when I came out and heard the two sets of fans chanting so hard, but it was nerves that I've never experienced before." The pressure had been on, with Thanasi Kokkinakis — the injured teammate he replaced — Purcell and Jordan Thompson having all lost opening singles ties in that year's competition, and Popyrin struggled against a big-hitting 22-year-old going for broke; but after repelling a set point in the opening stanza, his solidity helped silence a noisy contingent of Finnish fans hoping to cheer their underdogs to a first-ever final. De Minaur, cheered on by his excited mum, then overcame the loss of the first two games without needing similar heroics to Wednesday's.

The win set up Australia's 49th appearance in a Davis Cup final, the 28-time champions seeking a 29th title and a first win in the World Cup of men's tennis in 20 years, against either a Serbian side led by world No.1 Novak Djokovic or an Italian team inspired by Jannik Sinner. "We're a very solid squad, a lot of depth in every single position, and if we can get ourselves to a deciding doubles, we've got an unbelievable pairing as well," de Minaur said. "So we singles guys have just got do our best to try and get a win and let the doubles guys do the job. Hopefully we can go one better this year," he added, recalling the 2-0 defeat by Canada in 2022.
