# Meta Response To Suppression Claims

Meta's rebuttal attacks the premise that any pattern of reduced reach for Palestine-related content shows deliberate suppression: the company says the reduced reach came from globally scoped technical bugs and from errors in good-faith enforcement, not from the subject matter of the posts. As reported by TechCrunch on 19 October 2023, Meta said it is closely monitoring its platforms for violations and may inadvertently flag certain content, but that it never intends to "suppress a particular community or point of view."

The scope condition Meta sets around the whole dispute is its terrorist-organization rule: content praising or supporting Hamas — which governs Gaza and is designated as a terrorist organization by the United States and the European Union — is expressly forbidden on Meta's platforms. This is stated in the aftermath of Hamas' attack on Israelis and Israel's retaliatory airstrikes and total blockade cutting access to electricity, water and vital supplies, which have devastated Gaza.

Meta communications director Andy Stone declined to comment on the record and pointed TechCrunch to Meta's newsroom post about the conflict, updated on Oct. 18 with a statement addressing the shadowbanning claims. "Our policies are designed to keep people safe on our apps while giving everyone a voice," it said. "We apply these policies equally around the world and there is no truth to the suggestion that we are deliberately suppressing voice … We can make errors and that is why there is an appeals process for people to tell us when they think we have made the wrong decision, so we can look into it."

The specific counter-explanations are two bugs, each claimed to be global and therefore content-neutral. Meta blamed technical errors for the removal of posts about Palestine during the 2021 conflict, and on the recent shadowbanning claims a company representative pointed TechCrunch to a Threads post by Stone: "We identified a bug impacting all Stories that re-shared Reels and Feed posts, meaning they weren't showing up properly in people's Stories tray, leading to significantly reduced reach. This bug affected accounts equally around the globe and had nothing to do with the subject matter of the content — and we fixed it as quickly as possible." Stone separately directed TechCrunch to the updated newsroom post, which said Meta identified and fixed a "bug" that prevented users from going live: "This was also a global issue that was fixed within a few hours. We understand people rely on these tools and we're sorry to anyone who felt the impact of these issues."

The rebuttal's reach is limited in ways the reporting records. It is unclear whether the bug affected accounts posting content unrelated to Gaza, while Instagram users have posted screenshots showing Stories about Palestine receiving significantly fewer views than other Stories posted the same day, and allege their view counts recovered when they posted content unrelated to the conflict. Meta's representative did not address the other accusations of censorship beyond Story visibility and did not respond to TechCrunch's follow-up questions; Meta initially did not respond to follow-up requests for comment. Asked about comments containing the Palestinian flag, Stone again pointed to the updated newsroom post, which did not directly address the issue, and he declined to comment on allegations of Meta disproportionately flagging Arabic news content, directing TechCrunch to the same post, which did not directly address those allegations either.
