# Guerilla Humour: Vincent Namatjira's Portrait Practice

"Guerilla humour" is how fellow artist and collaborator Tony Albert describes Vincent Namatjira's portrait practice, in which colourfully idiosyncratic portraits both skewer and honour their subjects — sometimes at the same time — as reported by The Guardian on 16 October 2023.

Its discriminating features are self-insertion and tonal doubleness. Namatjira often paints himself into the picture: in one scene he might be seen on a palace balcony gatecrashing a royal family photo op; in the next he is in an Acca Dacca T-shirt, patting a dingo and waving the Aboriginal flag alongside Cook and Queen Elizabeth II. The practice has two poles. Many of his portraits pay affectionate tribute to his heroes, including his artist father-in-law Kunmanara (Jimmy) Pompey, Archie Roach, Nicky Winmar and Eddie Koiki Mabo; his 2018 series Legends features land rights campaigner Eddie Mabo, football player Nicky Winmar, songwriter Archie Roach and artist Gordon Bennett. At the other pole he takes aim at the rich, white and privileged with status-puncturing irreverence — his 2016 series Prime Ministers depicts the seven Australian leaders of his lifetime mugging for the viewer with grey eyes and white teeth, and 2018's Vincent & Donald (Happy Birthday) has Namatjira and Trump sharing a birthday cake (both men were born on 14 June) alongside a bag of McDonald's and a red hat reading "Make APY Great Again".

The boundary case is where the humour turns ambiguous: pointing to the cake knife his painted self holds between a planet Earth-themed chocolate cake and the then US president's abdomen, Vincent says only, "See, humour." He often describes his art as a weapon, and despite moments of levity there is a keen edge of subversion informed by his years inside the systems these powerful figures preside over.
