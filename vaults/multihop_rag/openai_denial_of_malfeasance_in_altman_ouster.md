# OpenAI Denial Of Malfeasance In Altman Ouster

OpenAI's own management pushed back against the inference that Sam Altman had been fired for wrongdoing, stating definitively that the board's decision was not a response to malfeasance and framing it instead as a communication breakdown.

The rebuttal came in an internal memo obtained by Axios and sent on the Saturday morning after the firing by OpenAI COO Brad Lightcap. Lightcap said the previous day's announcement "took [the management team] by surprise" and that management had had "multiple conversations with the board to try to better understand the reasons and process behind their decision," with discussions still ongoing as of Saturday morning. "We can say definitively that the board's decision was not made in response to malfeasance or anything related to our financial, business, safety, or security/privacy practices," Lightcap added. "This was a breakdown in communication between Sam and the board … We still share your concerns about how the process has been handled, are working to resolve the situation, and will provide updates as we're able."

The premise this attacks is the natural reading of the board's stated grounds — that a CEO removed for not being "consistently candid" must have done something wrong in the company's financial, business, safety or security and privacy conduct. Lightcap's memo concedes the communication problem while denying the conduct problem, and it also concedes that management itself did not understand the board's reasoning at the time.
