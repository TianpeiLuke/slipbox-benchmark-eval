# The UNAIDS 95-95-95 Targets

The "95-95-95" target, set by UNAIDS for 2025, means that 95% of people with HIV know their status, 95% of those diagnosed take HIV drugs, and 95% of those treated are "virally suppressed" — that is, the drugs keep them from spreading the infection via sex. Achieving all three translates to around 86% of people with HIV being virally suppressed.

The targets were created in response to a specific projection. By 2014 there was strong consensus that existing drugs could end the AIDS epidemic, but UNAIDS cautioned that they were not being rolled out fast enough to head off rebounds in infection; models at that time predicted that if treatment and prevention services did not reach more people over time, the number of people with HIV would balloon to 41.5 million by 2030. UNAIDS therefore set ambitious targets to scale up the global HIV response, projecting that hitting them would prevent 28 million new HIV infections and at least 21 million AIDS-related deaths between 2015 and 2030. Other 2025 targets aim to ensure that 95% of people at risk of HIV have access to prevention and that PrEP be made available to at least 10 million at-risk people. Progress is behind schedule: in 2022, only 76% of the total 39 million people with HIV worldwide were taking ART, and 71% were virally suppressed, according to the latest UNAIDS report.
