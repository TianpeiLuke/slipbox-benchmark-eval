# Preparing Both Partner And Family Before A Holiday Introduction

Relationship experts told The Independent that the preparation for introducing a partner to your family at the holidays happens in a set order, and before anyone travels. The precondition is clarity between the couple: Saba Lurie, a licensed marriage and family therapist at Take Root Therapy in California, says there is no right or wrong time for the introduction, but the intentions behind it must be clear — you should already have discussed where the relationship is headed. Step one is then to brief your partner on the purpose: "I think you should tell the person why you're bringing them home for the holidays, so you're on the same page about the intentions and what's motivating the decision to include," Lurie said. Step two is to brief them on the family they are walking into: "Sometimes it can be helpful for them to know about the dynamics that they're stepping into and how they can best support you. Frankly, they should be aware if there is tension or if there is stress within your family."

Step three runs the other way. Rachel Zar, a licensed marriage and family therapist at Spark Chicago Therapy, says the family needs advance notice too: "If there's something about your partner that could be surprising to your family, or that's a notable difference from who someone you've brought home before, tell your relatives that. Let's set your family and partner up for success." Step four is to say how you want your family to include your partner — for example by incorporating your partner's family traditions into your own celebration. The limit on the procedure is oversharing: Zar advises against telling too much before Christmas, since you still want your family and partner to connect in person rather than hearing about each other through the grapevine. Zar also sets the context for why the preparation matters — every family is different, but problems arise mainly because we put too much pressure on having a joyful holiday: "I think anytime that emotions are heightened, with the holidays certainly being one of those times, our expectations are really high. We're expecting it to be this joyful, happy, perfect occasion. So, we sometimes forget that the holidays come with a lot of stress."
