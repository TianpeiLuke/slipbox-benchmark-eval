# Opening Arguments in the SBF Trial

The prosecution's case is that [Sam Bankman-Fried](sam_bankman_fried.md) knowingly stole customer funds for personal enrichment. Prosecutor Thane Rehn told the court, "This man stole billions of dollars from thousands of people. He defrauded sophisticated investors and lenders, and he emptied the accounts of ordinary customers, too. He bought himself wealth, power and influence." The prosecution described an empire "built on lies" and showed photographs of Bankman-Fried alongside wealthy and powerful figures to establish the scale of his influence before the collapse.

The defense's case is that the collapse reflected incapacity rather than intent. Defense attorney Mark Cohen said, "Sam and his colleagues were building the plane as they were flying it," describing a team simultaneously running [FTX](ftx.md), building its systems, handling hacking threats, managing customer credit risk and managing hundreds of employees.

The two accounts share the facts of the collapse and differ on intent, which is what the fraud counts turn on.
