# Protecting Populations Most Exposed to Extreme Heat

Public health measures and mitigation strategies are needed to protect the citizens at greatest risk from heat, especially as rising temperatures become more common — and studies of existing exposure disparities are the way to identify who those citizens are.

"Studies like this, which seek to understand more deeply the existing disparities in heat exposure, are important for identifying which communities are at greatest risk of suffering environmental changes related to climate change, such as extreme heat that keeps getting worse," said Neelima Tummala. Lara Whitely Binder, climate preparedness programme manager for King County, Washington, who was not involved in the research, located the cause: "Areas with fewer street trees, less access to green spaces — that is where you start to see the legacy of disinvestment and institutional racism that has come into play." Because the climate is changing and advancing, cities must from now on prepare for hotter summers and more frequent deadly heat waves by various means, both indoors and outdoors. "We not only need to better understand where it is hot, but also what the socioeconomic factors are for the inhabitants of those areas," Whitely Binder said. "We can begin to analyse what is in the heat islands and use that to start informing the policy decisions we are going to make."
