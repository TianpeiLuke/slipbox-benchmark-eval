# CBS Sports' Matchup-First Method For Weekly Fantasy Lineup Decisions

CBS Sports' weekly "Lineup Decisions" columns for the 2023 season set out a repeatable method: fantasy lineup calls should be driven by weekly matchups rather than by the order in which players were drafted, so the manager checks who each of their players faces and makes sure the right players are in the lineup and the wrong ones are out.

The steps, as the columns describe them, run in this order. First, set aside draft position — you drafted your team with certain hopes and intentions, but weekly lineup decisions shouldn't be determined by the order you picked your players in. Second, check the opponent for each player on the roster. Third, because it is too early in the season to be absolutely sure which matchups will be easy and which will be tough, make educated guesses using four inputs: healthy personnel, defensive schemes, track records, and key details of offenses — the things we know help minimize the impact of the things we don't know, which should lead to better decisions. Fourth, work through every game on the slate and highlight only the players who aren't obvious starts and sits. Fifth, look beyond the current week to matchups dictated by the remaining schedule: the author updates projected strength-of-schedule rankings every week at SportsLine, broken down by position in the Week 6 edition, covering the upcoming four weeks, the playoff stretch and the entire season, along with buy-low, sell-high and other trade advice.

The procedure's scope is explicitly limited. It does not apply to obvious lineup calls — as the columns put it, you don't need to be told to start Justin Jefferson — and its matchup judgements are hedged because early-season certainty is not available. The stated payoff is comfort: you should feel more comfortable starting or sitting players based on the information given, and feeling comfortable with your fantasy lineup before the games start is the best feeling in the world.
