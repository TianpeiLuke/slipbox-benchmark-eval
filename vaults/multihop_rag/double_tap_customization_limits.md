# Double Tap's Customization Limits

Against the framing of double tap as a magical feature that "just works," The Verge's reviewer argued that it does not take long to run into its limitations — and that the root of them is Apple's decision to let the watch guess your intent instead of letting you configure it. When it performs as intended it feels a bit like the watch can read her mind, it works with her index finger and the rest of them as well, and it feels less gimmicky than she expected; but customizability is currently limited to music playback and the smart stack, the only two instances where you can choose what double tap does.

Media controls illustrate the ambiguity. Should a double tap pause or play your music, or should it skip to the next track? For someone using a smartwatch to control playback on smart speakers the former makes more sense; for a runner whose playlist serves up the wrong song, the latter is more useful — the reviewer set hers to skip. The lack of choice is also apparent in third-party apps: her email app lets her double-tap to start a quick reply using Siri, but even in 2023 she is not dictating her emails, while apps like Spotify and Pocket Casts do not work with double tap at all, and most third-party apps that do work with it only let you dismiss notifications. Navigation is another gap: watchOS 10 redesigned apps like Fitness and Weather to be more glanceable, dividing information into digestible chunks so there is less scrolling, but she often wants to use double tap to navigate menus instead of the digital crown and cannot. You can scroll through the new widget Smart Stack, but selecting a widget to open it still requires the other hand; you can customize double tap to select, but it only ever selects the top widget, so you have to trust that Apple's algorithms will surface the right one. Multitasking falls short too: to pause a timer after navigating away from that app she has to use her other hand or Siri, or wait for the timer to go off and use double tap to end it. A double tap glyph indicates when the gesture can and cannot control an action.

Apple's answer to the obvious remedy is caution. "While customization can be a really powerful tool, oftentimes customization is where a lot more complexity can come into the process," says David Clark. The reviewer conceded the point — not everyone has the patience to set up dozens of Focus modes or tinker with Shortcuts, and if you want to keep things simple you inevitably have to narrow the focus.
