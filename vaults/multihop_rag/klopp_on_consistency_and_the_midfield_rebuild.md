# Klopp On Consistency And The Midfield Rebuild

What Jürgen Klopp was seeking most of all from Liverpool in October 2023 was consistency throughout the 90-plus minutes of a Premier League game, and he argued that some instability was inevitable given that summer's midfield rebuild — a problem that could only be solved step by step, with no shortcut.

The grounds are the pattern of performances. Consistency was achieved in the commanding 3-0 defeat of an in-form Aston Villa early the previous month, a performance that carried echoes of Liverpool at their dominant best under Klopp, but other highlights, even in victory, had been interspersed with lapses. Klopp accepted the rebuild as a factor while adding two others: "Yes it is [a factor] but we've also changed between games quite a lot because of the amount of games and we've been interrupted by international breaks twice now. If it is a more settled team it doesn't make that much of a difference because we are much more used to each other and that is not the case yet. As a group we have to become consistent but you do it step by step."

The warrant is a precedent from his own tenure — that these traits are learned over time rather than installed. "It is not too long ago that we had the problem of performing on a really high level until we didn't perform at all any more; we were 1-0 or 2-0 up in a game and all of a sudden we came under pressure. Crazy. We had to learn to control the game. It is all the things that happen with time — there is no shortcut to that. With the signs we showed so far I am absolutely fine but I don't know the final destination, I can't even see that yet, but it is not a problem because other teams have similar problems. It is about how quick we can make the steps and that is what we are working on every day but the problem now is how could we work since Brighton [where Liverpool drew 2-2]? We had three, with a goalie, first-team players in training until Wednesday. Tomorrow we have to play with what we did so far but in general you have to gain stability bit by bit."
