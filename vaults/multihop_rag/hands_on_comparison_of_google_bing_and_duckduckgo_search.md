# A Hands-On Comparison Of Google, Bing And DuckDuckGo Search

Testing Google against Bing and DuckDuckGo, The Age's writer found Google performed best on the sample query while DuckDuckGo performed less well and was harder to read, and Bing's generative-AI answers were not really there yet in practice.

On the query "kids BMX bike green," Google did pretty well, DuckDuckGo ignored the green part, and Bing Chat offered a comparison of a few options that were all too expensive; asked "any under $200?", it replied with more options that were all over $300. Bing has overhauled its layout over the past year to be very graphic and augmented by generative-AI explanations from its chatbot, and the writer liked the idea of asking follow-up questions in searches this way. The test also reminded the writer how much data Google collects — not just searches but anything said to a smart speaker and any search result or ad clicked — which will count in DuckDuckGo's favour for some people, even though that data collection is also why Google, across search, mobile widgets and other services, personalises more effectively. A final observation: the writer uses the Google Search bar as much for returning to familiar sites as for searching for information, so switching providers could be temporarily annoying simply because a new one wouldn't remember all the places they had been.
