# The Fediverse Definition And The Email Analogy

In a sentence, the fediverse is an interconnected set of apps that can all read and write the same content — and the reason unbundling social media suddenly feels possible is that the fediverse has emerged.

The analogy The Verge uses to make the definition concrete is email: you don't have to compose, organize, and read your messages all in the same app, and two people don't have to use the same tools to communicate. Email apps can have different interfaces, different privacy policies, even wildly different ideas about what email is for; every app knows what an email address is, and every email address can talk to every other. The point is that email is just data, and lots of apps can understand and manipulate it. The concrete instances given: when Meta's Adam Mosseri posts on Threads and you see it on Mastodon, that's the fediverse at work; if you post on Mastodon and someone sees it in their Pixelfed feed, that's fediverse too; and commenting on a Flipboard post so it shows up as a reply in the Mastodon feed someone checks through the Mammoth app is "pure fediverse, baby."
