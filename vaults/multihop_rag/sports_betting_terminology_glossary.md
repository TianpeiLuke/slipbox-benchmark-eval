# Sports Betting Terminology Glossary

CBS Sports set out the top Kentucky betting terms for new bettors on 13 October 2023, defining each bet type by what the bettor is actually wagering on and, in several cases, by a worked Kentucky example.

Against the spread means that instead of betting on an exact winner, the widely popular spread bet levels the playing field by letting you wager on how many points a team will win by: in basketball, if Kentucky is the 7.5-point favorite against Louisville then Kentucky covers by winning by 8 or more, while Louisville as the 7.5-point underdog covers by winning outright or losing by 7 or fewer. Money line is an elevated outright bet with odds adjusted to reflect a favored team and an underdog: if Kentucky is a -200 favorite against Florida you'd need to bet $200 to return $100, while Florida at +200 would return $200 on a $100 bet. An outright bet is the easiest wager to place and is perfect for horse racing, one of Kentucky's biggest sporting events — bettors choose one athlete, or thoroughbred, to win an entire event. An Over/Under has the sportsbook post an estimated total of both teams' final scores, with the bettor wagering on whether the actual final will be higher or lower: if the Over/Under for a Kentucky-Louisville basketball game is 125.5, you wager on the final total being Over 126 or Under 125. A player prop projects a player's stat at the end of a game and asks you to wager over or under that projection, with popular examples being how many touchdown passes a quarterback throws or how many three-pointers a basketball player hits; Kentucky is one of the 18 states that allows prop bets on collegiate athletes.

The remaining terms describe the market's participants and mechanics. The underdog is the team deemed less likely to win based on the odds, and a bet on one typically yields a larger return than a bet on the favorite; the favorite is deemed more likely to win, and bettors often pay a premium for backing one though they more often win outright. Consensus odds are generally defined as an average of all available betting markets, since different sportsbooks may price markets variably based on the action they've taken or their own interpretation of information. Futures markets encompass almost anything outside single-game bets — total regular-season wins, championship odds, MVP odds and more. A parlay requires two or more bets within a single wager, all of which must win: wagering $100 on a three-leg parlay at 6 to 1 odds could win $600 if all three legs win. Juice, also known as the vig or vigorish, is effectively the tax oddsmakers charge for handling a wager — if a book judges a game a virtual coinflip it may price both options at -110 to give itself a 10% rake on all the action. A push is a bet where neither the bettor nor the sportsbook wins: if an over/under for total points is set at 44 and both teams combine for exactly 44, the result is a push and the principal is returned. Live or in-game betting allows bets on several different markets in real time — in basketball, on the outcome of a quarter, individual statistics and live-updated spreads and totals.
