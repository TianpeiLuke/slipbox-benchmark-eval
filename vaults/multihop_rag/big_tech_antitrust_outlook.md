# What A Ruling Against Google Would Mean For Big Tech

A ruling against Google would not stop at Google: the remedy would likely resemble the Microsoft settlement and the outcome would ripple through the other pending Big Tech cases, because enforcers are using this trial to demonstrate that antitrust law can still take on the largest and most powerful companies in the world.

The grounds for the first half of that claim are the available remedies. If the judge rules against Google, the outcome could look a lot like the Microsoft deal, in which the computer company was required to change its behavior and share its APIs with third-party developers, and was also banned from making anticompetitive and exclusive deals with computer manufacturers. Google might likewise end up having to turn over all or most of the data it has collected to other search engines so they can improve their products and attract more users — a remedy scaled to the disparity the DOJ has cited, that Google gets 16 times more data than Bing does everyday.

The grounds for the ripple effect are the cases already queued behind this one. The FTC sued Amazon in September for using anticompetitive and unfair strategies to illegally maintain its monopoly power. The DOJ has been investigating Apple for years over the company's policy for third-party apps on its devices and whether it unfairly favors its own products. And there is an ongoing case between the FTC and Facebook, in which the agency calls on Facebook to sell Instagram and WhatsApp.

The warrant joining grounds to claim is enforcers' own stake in the result: they will want to show that antitrust law is still relevant and can successfully take on the biggest, most powerful companies in the world, so a win here sets the template the neighbouring cases are argued against. The scope condition is that nothing was settled as of this report — the case is expected to continue for several weeks, bringing to a head one of the biggest fights in tech antitrust since the U.S. took Microsoft to trial in the 1990s.
