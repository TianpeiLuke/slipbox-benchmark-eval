# Steam Deck OLED HDR Display

The OLED panel in the new Steam Deck is lighter, thinner, and more power efficient than its LCD predecessor, as well as bigger and faster. Though the device keeps the same shape as the original, smaller screen bezels allow a slightly larger display: 7.4 inches against 7 inches.

The panel has a wider color gamut — 110% DCI-P3 — and the inky blacks OLED enthusiasts value. Its 90 Hz refresh rate is described as perfect for frame doubling from 45 frames per second, which in plainer terms means gameplay looks smoother. Polygon notes the comparison and its caveat: 90 Hz falls short of the Asus ROG Ally's 120 Hz, but the higher the frame rate the faster the battery drain, assuming you can even spot the difference between the two. The reviewer's own use case was Sega's Like a Dragon Gaiden: The Man Who Erased His Name, played on high settings at 60 fps while watching football and Christmas movies with family; its night sequences flaunted the OLED's dark blacks and its abundant neon signage showcased the HDR. The most welcome feat only registered later: on the original Steam Deck he had always kept the handheld plugged into an outlet when playing AAA games, whereas with the OLED model a couple of hours passed before he realised he had forgotten to connect the charger.
