# The New Bacterium Will Not Grow In The Lab, So Antibiotic Choice Rests On Its Structure

Needle and his colleagues have not been able to grow the new bacteria in the lab, and in the absence of a culture Needle suggested that the antibiotic doxycycline may be effective against it. The suggestion is inferred from the organism's structure rather than measured.

The failure to culture matters because of what it displaces. Normally, to determine what antibiotics might work best against a particular type of bacteria, labs grow the bugs in a petri dish and then try to kill them with various medications. That standard susceptibility route is closed here, since the new bacteria will not grow.

Nevertheless, the organism's structure offers some clues about which medications might be the best choice to fight it, Needle said, and on that basis he suggested doxycycline may be effective. The scope conditions are worth keeping: this is Needle's suggestion, reported on 2023-11-21, resting on structural clues rather than on any petri-dish test of the drug against the organism.
