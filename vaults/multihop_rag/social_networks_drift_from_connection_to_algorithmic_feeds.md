# Social Networks' Drift From Connection To Algorithmic Feeds

The claim is that social networks stopped being social. At some point they were no longer about connecting with your closest friends, keeping up with long-distance family members and feeling a special connection with people you love; TikTok, Instagram, Snapchat, Facebook, YouTube and X (formerly Twitter) are all slowly evolving to become the same infinite scrollable feed of algorithm-optimized short videos from top performers. TechCrunch's writer notes he had argued in a 2018 article that 2018 was "the year social networks were no longer social," and that while he is not sure 2018 was the turning point, the premise still holds up well.

The structural ground Mozilla offers is ownership and revenue: social networks are often run by large corporations — like Meta, Snap and Google — where advertisers pay the bills, which has created a world where modern social networks are profit-driven and consumer needs are not always at the forefront. The warrant is that when advertisers pay, the feed optimizes for them rather than for connection.

Mozilla dates the inflection point: this disconnect between what people want and what today's networks offer hit an inflection point in 2022, when billionaire Elon Musk bought Twitter, leading to a surge of interest in alternatives to centralized social networking including apps like Mastodon and Bluesky. Under Musk, Twitter — now called X — has prioritized "free speech" managed by crowdsourced-based moderation, and its policies have resulted in increased hate speech and toxic content on the platform, some studies have shown.
