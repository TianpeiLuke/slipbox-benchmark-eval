# Valor VC's Applied AI Thesis

Lisa Calhoun, founding managing partner at Valor VC, argues that the right startup posture is applied AI rather than specialization in one layer of the LLM stack. Asked in TechCrunch's 13 October 2023 investor survey whether startups will build striated solutions for individual layers of the LLM stack — models, pre-training solutions, and fine-tuning tools — or pursue a more vertical approach, she answered that while there are startups specializing in parts of the stack (like Pinecone), Valor's focus is on applied AI, which the firm defines as AI that is solving a customer problem.

The grounds are three portfolio-style examples of that definition in practice. Saile.ai uses AI to generate closeable leads for the Fortune 500. Funding U uses its own trained dataset to create a more useful credit risk score. Allelica uses AI on treatment solutions applied to individual DNA to find the best medical treatment for a person personally in a given situation. The warrant joining examples to thesis is that each is identified by the customer problem it solves rather than by the stack layer it occupies.

On whether incumbent tooling curtails the space startups can compete in, Calhoun's answer is conditional rather than dismissive: tools like Datadog can only help the acceptance of AI tools if they succeed in monitoring AI performance bottlenecks, and that in and of itself is probably still largely unexplored territory that will see a lot of change and maturing in the next few years. One key aspect there might be cost monitoring as well, since companies like OpenAI charge largely "by the token," which is a very different metric than most cloud computing.
