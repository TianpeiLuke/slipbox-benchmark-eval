# Bankman-Fried's Conduct On Direct Examination

On October 28th, 2023, before the cross-examination had even begun, Judge Lewis Kaplan had already instructed Sam Bankman-Fried to answer the question he was being asked by his own lawyer — which The Verge's reporter called honestly kind of incredible, a man torpedoing his own credibility on direct testimony. The count of "Objection, narrative!" to Bankman-Fried's answers, followed by "Sustained," stood at three; the main thing clear so far, she wrote, was that he really loves the sound of his own voice, and that sometimes when he says "we," he only means himself.

The scolding was a pattern across two days. The day before, during an evidentiary hearing, Bankman-Fried had been repeatedly scolded by Kaplan for not answering prosecutor Danielle Sassoon's questions on cross-examination; on this day he was scolded for not answering his own counsel Mark Cohen's questions on direct, and he occasionally interrupted Cohen with "yes" and "yup." Much of the morning went on vocabulary — explaining "Amazon Web Services" and "database" was a bit too detail-oriented — and when Bankman-Fried tried to define "market manipulation," Kaplan told the jury that he, the judge, was the final authority on that.

In fairness, the reporter added, he was clearer and much easier to understand than at the evidentiary hearing the day before, with a minimum of word salad; she could not tell whether he was more relaxed or simply more rehearsed, and said she would be watching to see whether he became much less coherent once Sassoon got him on cross. The jury watched all of it intently.
