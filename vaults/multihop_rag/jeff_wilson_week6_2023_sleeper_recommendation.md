# Jeff Wilson As A Week 6 2023 Sneaky Sleeper Against Carolina

CBS Sports listed Jeff Wilson as a "Sneaky Sleeper" for Week 6 of the 2023 season against Carolina, conditional on his being active: the claim is that once it is clear Wilson will play, the matchup combined with Miami's multiple-back approach should put him in position for good opportunities, and the warrant is that his injury was to his abdomen rather than his legs, so there may be little limit on his workload even though a heavy touch count is unlikely.

The availability grounds are specific and dated. Wilson started practising this week after being sidelined with finger and abdomen injuries since late August, and his fresh legs return just in time, because rookie sensation De'Von Achane hit injured reserve this week with a knee injury. The depth chart behind him is thin: Salvon Ahmed was a healthy scratch the previous week and figures to be no better than a special-teams player, and the precedent supports that — in the seven games last year when Raheem Mostert, Wilson and Ahmed were all active for Miami, Ahmed played 18 offensive and 25 special-teams snaps while Wilson played 213 offensive and 10 special-teams snaps. Wilson was also efficient in his eight games with Miami last year, averaging 4.7 yards per carry with three touchdowns and just over 60 total yards per game, a figure that rises to 78.2 total yards per game once two appearances under 20 snaps are removed. The breakdown lists Miami at home versus Carolina, the Dolphins favoured by 13.5, an over/under of 48.5, an opponent ranked 31st versus running backs, seven projected points, no running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of zero across the board.

The usage and matchup grounds complete the case. Miami isn't quite the bastion of rushing some headlines suggest — just over league average in rush rate at 43.7% and above average in running back runs per game at 22.6, with all of it weighted heavily by the 50-point Week 3 win over Denver when the backs ran 42 times, and a 7.6-yard per carry average owing mostly to Achane's breakout start. Mike McDaniel prefers to use two running backs from game to game, and Miami's past three games show a relatively close carry split between two backs: 18 and 13 in Week 3, 8 and 7 in Week 4, 11 and 10 in Week 5. Carolina ranks bottom-five against running backs in rushing yards per carry allowed (5.2), rushing touchdowns allowed, explosive run rate allowed (9.8%) and yards before contact per rush allowed (2.26); every back with at least 10 carries against the Panthers this season has posted at least nine non-PPR points, with at least one such back every game and multiple backs clearing 10 non-PPR points in two of five games. The author would take a chance on Wilson over Samaje Perine, Jerick McKinnon and Antonio Gibson.
