# Burrow's End: Dimension 20's Stoat Season And The Specificity Rule

Burrow's End is the next season of Dimension 20, run by Aabria Iyengar with Brennan Lee Mulligan playing in it, and its premise is stoats. "Why stoats?! They're a marvelous animal," Mulligan says, explaining that Aabria is taking the cast to "this wonderful Secret of NIMH, Watership Down — there's a certain subgenre."

The season illustrates Dimension 20's governing design principle: "we find in Dimension 20 that we have our most success when we get specific. It's not just fantasy, it's Game of Thrones meets Candyland. It's not just a mystery, it's noir, or it's Agatha Christie — which is actually two of the Side Quest seasons we've done, one of which Sam was a player in. It's about getting specific with the comparisons that you're reaching out to and where you're drawing inspiration from."

The tonal boundary is what makes the "cute little critters" genre work. Within that quite broad, fantastical storytelling genre sits a subgenre of perhaps the darkest and most grim stories ever told: "something about the juxtaposition of the cute little bunnies, and a story about death, displacement, horror... Lovecraft wishes that he could have the eldritch horror of rabbits trying to describe a road and the cars that drive on it." Sam Reich puts the intent bluntly: "We want to take our audience and expose them to deep trauma. And the way that you do that is by luring them into a sense of comfort and security." Mulligan adds the mental mathematics of the season — in nature documentaries little stoats and bunnies get absolutely eviscerated by falcons and you think "Tough break, buddy" and move on, "But we've given them names and voices. And now we'll see how glib they are!" Reich's closing line: "David Attenborough couldn't do this!"
