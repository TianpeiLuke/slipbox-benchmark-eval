# Justin Fields As A Week 5 2023 Bust Candidate At Washington

CBS Sports flagged Justin Fields as a "Bust Candidate" for Week 5 of the 2023 season at Washington: the claim is that Fields is being started only for his passing, and the warrant is that a short-week trip behind the league's worst pass protection, with his designed rushing all but gone, removes the floor that usually protects his fantasy score.

The grounds separate the opponent from the circumstances. Washington itself is not that big a step-up in competition for Fields — but it is for his offensive line. The Commanders have a slightly below-average pass-rush pressure rate, with only one game all year above 30% (52.7% at Denver, where Russell Wilson still had a great game even discounting a Hail Mary touchdown catch), ranking seventh in sacks with 13 and 13th in pressures with 53, both around league average. Chicago, by contrast, ranks league-worst in pressure rate allowed at 47.4% of snaps — nearly one in every two dropbacks — and bringing back guard Teven Jenkins might help a little, but the unit is still struggling. The deep ball is the clearest upside: Washington ranks bottom-five in attempts, yards per catch and touchdowns allowed on throws of 15-plus air yards over the past three weeks, an area Fields can exploit, since he is tied for the most touchdowns on 15-plus air-yard throws this season with four and sits around league average in completion rate, attempts per game and expected points added per dropback. The weekly breakdown lists Chicago at Washington, the Commanders favoured by 6, an over/under of 44.5, an opponent ranked 23rd versus quarterbacks, 17.3 projected points, a 10th quarterback ranking, and year-to-date totals of 861 passing yards, 134 rushing yards, eight touchdowns, five interceptions and 20.4 fantasy points per game.

The bust risk rests on how Fields' Week 4 breakout was produced and on his vanished rushing. He posted his first-ever game with four passing touchdowns and over 300 yards against a Broncos defense devoid of a serious pass rush or pass-coverage skills through three quarters — a defense that had allowed season-best fantasy numbers to each of the past three quarterbacks it faced and was a terrific matchup for anybody. Once Denver generated heavy pressure in the fourth quarter Fields finished 5 of 11 for 50 yards, no touchdowns and two turnovers. On film he looked terrific for three quarters firing downfield from clean pockets, with an off-target rate of literally 0.0% and plays where he manipulated defenders with the threat of running before throwing over their heads, including Cole Kmet's short touchdown; by the fourth he was pressured, throwing off his back foot, throwing with too much velocity or out of sync with his target, and his off-target rate rose to 9.1%, closer to his 13.6% average over the first three games. The rushing collapse is the other prop removed: after seven designed runs in Week 3 he had just two designed runs in Week 4 for two total yards, and on the season 28 rushes for 134 yards and a score. Last season Washington got a season-best 57.1% pressure rate on Fields and blitzed him on 40.5% of dropbacks, and he still cleared nearly 20 fantasy points on one passing touchdown and one interception because he ran for 88 yards — the very cushion the column doubts he will have now. The verdict is comparative rather than absolute: Anthony Richardson and Jordan Love have more potential this week, Russell Wilson, Trevor Lawrence, Dak Prescott and Jared Goff do not, and you just might not have a better option than Fields.
