# Google's Project Hug Developer Payoff Program

Project Hug, as Tim Sweeney described it from the trial evidence, was a Google program that offered payoffs to developers so they would not pursue their own app stores or exclusive distribution — the same corrupt deal structure, in his account, that Google had tried on Epic when it wanted to pay Epic off to launch Fortnite on Google Play and not launch Epic's own store.

Its defining feature is scale and its trigger is Epic's refusal: as soon as Epic thwarted Google's effort, Sweeney said, Google went around to 27 different developers and offered each one a payoff to undermine any effort Epic made to get their games onto the Epic store exclusively. He named Activision, Riot and Supercell as developers that had direct distribution plans they were planning on and that Google paid not to pursue. Sweeney called these "just direct blatant violations of anti-competition law" and said it was crazy that a company of Google's scale would do that. He noted that as an Epic employee he had not been able to see Google's internal documents until the trial started — the lawyers could, but he saw almost all the key elements in the case at the same time the jury did — and that Project Hug was one of two things that stood out big time.
