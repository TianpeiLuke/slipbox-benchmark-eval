# The Clock Change And Road Accident Risk

The Royal Society for the Prevention of Accidents (RoSPA) has called for the abolition of Daylight Saving Time on road-safety grounds, arguing that turning the clocks back increases the risk of road accidents because more people end up driving during the darker winter months. Its proposed remedy is to adopt British Summer Time permanently: "RoSPA is in favour of this proposal, and is calling for the government to adopt British Summer Time (GMT+1) all year. This would mean road users will no longer experience the sudden onset of darkness during their autumn commutes, potentially saving many lives."

The quantitative grounds come from RAC Foundation figures cited in support of abolishing DST: road traffic collisions increase by 19 per cent in the fortnight after the clocks go back one hour from British Summer Time, and reduce by 11 per cent when the clocks go forward.
