# Twitch Competitive Pressure

Twitch's product leadership rejects the reading that its recent policy reversals were forced by rival platforms, arguing instead that they followed from listening to its own community: "I wouldn't call it out of competitive pressures. I would call it out of focus on the community," a Twitch executive told TechCrunch on 2023-10-26 about Partner Plus, simulcasting and the stream-embed changes discussed in the keynote.

The premise being contested is real and was put to the executive directly. The discord among Twitch's community comes at a time when other streaming platforms like YouTube or Kick are starting to take off, and enticing streamers with better revenue splits; the common complaint is that bigger streamers are going to be making money wherever they are, but smaller streamers who have less of an audience and don't qualify for Partner Plus will get a better deal at YouTube or Kick than at Twitch. The reversals also run against long-standing Twitch practice — for a long time there was a sort of exclusivity with Twitch, with exclusive deals, and simultaneous streaming on other platforms was forbidden.

The grounds offered for the community explanation are the CEO's own position and the product organization's mandate. The executive credits "what Dan has done as the CEO to get out and engage with streamers, hear their concerns, and really influence our priorities as a company," which he attributes to Dan being both an active streamer and extremely engaged within the community, and credits him with making "some big, hard decisions" in a short time as CEO. More broadly, for the product leaders — including Jeremy Forrester, VP of Community Product — "building the product is continuing to innovate and ensure that we are absolutely a unique service. Whether that's features, like we're working on Stories, whether that's monetization — it's all in service of ensuring that livestreaming creators can be more successful on Twitch."

The warrant joining grounds to claim is that the three changes are legible as answers to specific community feedback rather than to rival offers: "these are three things that I think are very much an acknowledgement of the feedback from the community."
