# Music Catalog Acquisition Market Slowdown 2023

Robert Kyncl's assessment in September 2023 was that the hot music rights acquisitions space is "likely slowing down a bit" — and that this is fine for a buyer like Warner Music Group, because it makes acquisition multiples come down.

The backdrop given is that the second half of 2022 saw a notable slowdown in music rights acquisitions, which some blamed on higher interest rates and the reduced liquidity that came with them. There had been something of a rebound in 2023, but in Kyncl's view the slowdown persisted. He explained the earlier boom as a one-off shift in seller behaviour: "I think what happened was there was an opening where artists and songwriters were suddenly open to selling catalogs, which they were not open to before. And they just created this incredible tidal wave. And these things happen in waves, ebb and flow. So I think it likely is slowing down a little bit, but that's okay. Because it makes the multiples come down a little bit more."

Asked whether that means Warner Music can buy more if multiples are low enough, Kyncl said: "Of course. We are in the business of buying catalogs, obviously. We have the publishing catalog of David Bowie, for instance, which we bought a few years ago. So yeah, we are in that business. We are in the business of administering [rights], which means collecting revenue from thousands of platforms around the world; very complicated matters. But we're also in the business of ownership."
