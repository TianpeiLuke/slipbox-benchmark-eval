# Kevin Federline's 2022 Comments And The Spears Camp's Rebuttal

Britney Spears and her camp rejected Kevin Federline's 2022 claim that their teenage sons were distancing themselves from her because of her social media posts, attacking both the causal premise and Federline's standing to make it public. In an August 2022 interview with ITV, Federline said the couple's sons found it "tough" seeing their mother sharing nude photos online — she had been posting such images while embracing her newfound freedom after the end of her 13-year conservatorship — adding: "I can't imagine how it feels to be a teenager having to go to high school." More than a decade after the marriage ended, the pair were still having ups and downs co-parenting their two teenage sons, Sean Preston and Jayden.

Spears attacked the causal claim in a statement on her Instagram Stories, denying that Instagram was the reason: "It saddens me to hear that my ex husband has decided to discuss the relationship between me and my children … As we all know, raising teenage boys is never easy for anyone. It concerns me the fact that the reason is based on my Instagram … It was LONG before Instagram … I gave them everything … Only one word: HURTFUL." Sam Asghari attacked the factual premise and Federline's motive, accusing him of using their sons to "vilify" his wife and claiming her ex-husband's "gravy train will end soon, which probably explains the timing of these hurtful statements"; he wrote on Instagram that "there is no validity to his statement regarding the kids distancing themselves and it is irresponsible to make that statement publicly." Spears' attorney Mathew Rosengart shifted the attack to Federline's own conduct, saying in a statement obtained by Variety that by posting private videos of Spears sternly speaking with her children without her consent he had created "legal issues" for himself: "Whether he realises it or not, Mr Federline has not only violated the privacy and dignity of the mother of his children, he has undermined his own children, whose privacy he should protect. Putting aside his ITV interview, Mr Federline's ill-advised decision to post an old video of his 11 and 12-year-old children was cruel, bottom of the barrel stuff. It was abhorrent." Rosengart added that "in addition to demeaning himself and violating societal norms, he has now also created various legal issues for himself, including, but not limited to, implicating cyber-harassment and cyber-bullying statutes, among other things."
