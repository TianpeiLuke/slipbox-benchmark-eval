# Danny Elfman's Composing Career and Oingo Boingo

Danny Elfman is the legendary composer behind projects like Batman, Spider-Man, The Simpsons, and countless Tim Burton films — known to most movie buffs as Burton's bespectacled, goth-adjacent music pal — who in 2022 took the desert stage at Coachella shirtless and ready to rip. Those who witnessed the spectacle were blown away; "Nobody else even came close to bringing that level of chaotic pageantry," wrote Vulture at the time, while images of the roaring composer quickly went viral. Polygon told the story on 2023-10-13.

The rock turn may not have surprised those clicked into the alternative scene of the late 1970s and '80s. Before establishing his macabre staccato identity in Burton's Pee-wee's Big Adventure, Beetlejuice, and Edward Scissorhands, Elfman was a member of the new wave band Oingo Boingo, which hit its hardest with the 1985 single "Weird Science," featured in the film of the same name. Founded by Danny's brother Richard, the band steadily toured and cut records through the early '90s, and members still tour today. When Elfman left the band to pivot to composing he also left behind his live music career — for so long that many of his younger superhero-movie-watching, Hot Topic-shopping devotees never knew his lineage. Even in the Oingo Boingo days, Polygon notes, he was not going as hard as he did at Coachella.

Elfman did not peak with Nightmare in 1993. His four Oscar nominations — for Good Will Hunting, Men in Black, Big Fish, and Milk — were all awarded in the years that followed, and his work has extended far beyond film: in 2005 he premiered the highly baroque Serenada Schizophrana, his first work composed specifically for concert orchestra; in 2008 he wrote the music for the American Ballet Theatre's Rabbit and Rogue ballet; and in 2011 he composed for Cirque Du Soleil's $100 million Iris show. But it is bops like "This Is Halloween" and "Jack's Obsession" that will be engraved on his future tombstone, and he has never been ashamed to play the hits — unlike Disney, which had no clue how to release The Nightmare Before Christmas to an audience weaned on The Little Mermaid and Beauty and the Beast, but has since incorporated its parts into everything from theme parks to 3D re-releases to the Disney Dreamlight Valley sim. Unlike his film score work, Nightmare gives him the chance to take the stage. Polygon concludes there are few musical wanderers like Elfman, capable of swinging between genres and mediums and venues, and that his time performing as Jack Skellington has everything to do with that elasticity: he is now unified as a six-packed weirdo rock star with floppy red hair and body tattoos head-banging to the theme from The Simpsons.
