# War Duration and Scenarios Driving Israeli Growth Damage

Leo Leiderman's model of the war's economic damage has two drivers: duration and breadth. "First of all, the duration and character of the war will have a decisive impact on the depth of damage to the economy. Many studies, as well as past experience, indicate that the longer the war lasts, the broader will be the impact on the economy. It is also known that this connection between the length of the fighting and the economic damage might not be linear. And of course, the more the war widens to more fronts, the greater will be the economic damage."

He declined to quantify the effect. Asked on 12 October 2023 for a forecast of the consequences for growth, he said it was too early to make a quantitative estimate of the effects on the economy's growth in 2024 and 2025. Israel has a great deal of information about loss of growth and product in past episodes — the Yom Kippur War, the Lebanon War, two intifadas, various military operations, and the Covid pandemic — but in his view this is a completely different episode: "This is a new situation that has no precedent as far as the damage to the economy is concerned."

The range over which the model runs is therefore a spectrum of scenarios rather than a point estimate: "from a short war on the southern front only, to the nightmare scenario of a prolonged war with additional sectors involved." He noted the interview was taking place only on the fifth day of the war, and expressed the hope that the picture would start to become clearer.
