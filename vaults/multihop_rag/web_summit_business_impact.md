# Web Summit Business Impact

Damage has been done to Cosgrave and the Web Summit brand in the interim, TechCrunch reported on 16 October 2023, with cancellations spreading from Israeli tech figures to prominent US investors and the organisation itself confirming it was in conversations about attendance. Josh Kopelman, the founder of First Round Capital, publicly doubted Cosgrave's conciliatory statement: "Hard to take this statement at face value — given all the tweets @paddycosgrave has been liking over the last few days. I saved several of them on the attached google doc (so we have a record when the @WebSummit PR team asks him to delete them)," he said. Kopelman separately suggested in a tweet that Cosgrave is in the pocket of Doha and Qatar, a country that many believe is connected to the financing of Hamas. That was enough to push Garry Tan, the head of Y Combinator, over the edge, too: "I refuse to appear at Web Summit and am canceling my appearance," he said. "I condemn Hamas and pray for peace for the Israeli and Palestinian people."

Whether the withdrawals would extend from the individuals to the companies they back was left unresolved at the time of reporting. TechCrunch reached out to Tan and Kopelman to ask if they are advising portfolio companies and partners at their firms against also attending; Tan declined to comment, and Kopelman had yet to respond.

Web Summit provided a statement on the cancellations, saying that the organization is talking to "a number of people about their attendance at Web Summit" but is not in a position to discuss exacts and individuals. "We understand that it is an incredibly sensitive and painful time during this utter tragedy of war. We want to reiterate our devastation for the loss of innocent life in Israel and Gaza. We strongly condemn the horrific attacks by Hamas on Israelis. Web Summit's mission is to connect people and ideas changing the world from all around the globe. The more voices we have from around the world, the more we can help change the world for the better," the spokesperson added. "We are saddened to hear that some Israelis in the tech community will no longer be attending Web Summit. We regret any hurt caused and extend our deepest sympathies to everyone who has lost loved ones. We hope for peaceful reconciliation."

On headline attendance the organisation gave figures close to the prior year: the spokesperson said that last year's event attracted about 71,000 people and this year it's on track to "max out" at 70,000. Separately, a public page in Notion titled "techcondemingterror" was tracking the growing response, and by then included press clippings, comments from a number of leaders in Israel's technology industry, and comments.
