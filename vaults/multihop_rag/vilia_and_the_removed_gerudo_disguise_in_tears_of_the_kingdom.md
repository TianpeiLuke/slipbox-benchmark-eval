# Vilia and the Removed Gerudo Disguise in Tears of the Kingdom

Vilia is one of a handful of named characters missing from Tears of the Kingdom, with no mention of her memory, and the armor she gifted Link — once for sale in Gerudo Town's market — is also conspicuously absent, one of a few pieces of clothing not to return. Polygon reported this on 2023-10-19.

Instead, the game's main quest takes players into a derelict Gerudo Town where Link must again infiltrate their sanctum, now an underground bunker, but this time he is welcomed as a man amongst the sheltering women. Moving through the settlement, Link is greeted with suspicion and novelty by sheltered children who have not seen men before, and he is treated as a presumed threat for his proximity. Gerudo Town previously rejected essentialist logics like the idea that men are inherently dangerous to women — which undergirds real-world transphobia — but here it simply invokes such beliefs in much less humorous tones than it did even in Vilia's quest.

Outside town, a Hylian man mentions to Link that he is looking for a black market armor shop that sells voe, Gerudo for "man," armor. Polygon argues the change transforms queer materials into something more like cultural tchotchkes, further denying the queer subtext of trans masculinity and furthering the sense of Orientalism pervading the Gerudo.

Tears of the Kingdom does introduce new armor sets that can play with expression, but none are explicitly feminine. Most are Zonai in origin, a fictional ancient culture borrowing heavily from pan-Mesoamerican and Egyptian imagery, so it is hard to read their skirts and body paint as feminine when these were features of masculine presentation in those cultures. There is the one-off Cece's Hat, designed by a Hylian in Hateno, but it lacks a matching set of top and bottoms — so nothing really replaces the gender nonconformity the vai armor introduced in Breath of the Wild.
