# Nostalgia Works Best As A Side Ingredient

Polygon's qualification is that nostalgia is not a bad thing, nor entirely at odds with Walt Disney — but that it is a potent ingredient which works best in the Disney recipe for success on the side, in celebratory shorts, theme-park parades, and collectible merchandise, rather than as the main offering.

Two concessions set it up. Even in his quest for innovation, Walt was a nostalgic guy who idealized "the good old days," to the point of modelling the entrances of his theme parks after how his hometown looked at the turn of the 20th century. And the writer disclaims any immunity herself: she loves Disney, owns multiple Disney-themed plushies, teared up watching Once Upon a Studio, and launched an entire yearlong content package to celebrate Disney. The structural claim is about ordering: for most of the past century Walt Disney Studios was a movie studio first and a storefront, streaming service, and theme-park empire second, and it feels as though that balance has shifted over the last decade. The conclusion is conditional — if Disney wants to last another century and make things that actually spark new nostalgia down the line, it should go back to embracing fresh, different ideas.
