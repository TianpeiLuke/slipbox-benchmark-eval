# ID's Emergent Social Gameplay

ID's social layer works by circulation: as you browse around the app looking at what is new on your friends' profile pages, you may want to steal something for your own wall, and ID lets you add content from other profiles to your sticker library so that you can either add it to your own profile or put it on someone else's profile. Provenance is preserved — you can see who originally created a sticker as it moves around from one wall to another.

The behaviors that result were not designed in. Having used the app for a little more than a week, TechCrunch's writer could already see trends spreading around the small community of beta users: some users have put nice shelves so they can neatly categorize everything that matters to them, and one user created a guestbook section on her profile, writing "if you're dropping by, please leave a note here."

The design model Amo is working from comes from games: a few video games rely heavily on the player's creativity to have fun, such as Minecraft or recent Zelda games, where you can create your own fortress or build your own vehicle. That is also the main concept behind ID — Amo gives you the creative tools and a limitless Figma-like canvas, and it is up to the community to figure out what they want to do with it. The best part, per TechCrunch, is that it does not look like any other social app out there.
