# Ed Ternan's Case Against Suing Snap

Not every bereaved parent supports the litigation. Ed Ternan, whose 21-year-old son Charlie died in April 2020 after taking a counterfeit Percocet he bought on Snapchat, sees suing the platform as counterproductive: "It feels like looking backwards," he said.

His objection targets the premise that responsibility can be located in the platform alone. Ternan, who did not join the suit, explains that losing his son — an energetic and fun-loving young man who was weeks away from graduating from UC Santa Cruz — forced him to come to terms with the several factors that came together to cause Charlie's death: the app, the dealer, the friends Charlie bought drugs alongside, and himself as a father. "What was my role as a parent?" Ternan asked.

The conclusion he reached is one that looks beyond blame. With his wife, Ternan now works full time on initiatives to educate parents on the dangers of fentanyl, and he has even worked with Snap to promote awareness campaigns and find ways to make the app safer. "It's been a very successful partnership," Ternan said.
