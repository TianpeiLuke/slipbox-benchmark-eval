# MacBook Pro Display, Camera And Audio Systems

Both new MacBook Pro models are built around Apple's Liquid Retina XDR display: the 14-inch has 3024 x 1964 pixels and the 16-inch 3456 x 2234 pixels, both coming out to a pixel density of 254 ppi, against the 15-inch Air's 224 ppi.

The 16-inch system's display is largely unchanged from the 2021 model, with 1000 nit sustained brightness and 1600 nit peak with HDR content; for SDR content, however, brightness has been bumped up from a 500 to a 600 nit maximum. On the camera, Apple finally started bumping its webcams from 720p to 1080p back in April 2021, a long-awaited refresh for a company that puts so much emphasis on its mobile imaging, and the new hardware was coupled with improvements brought on by the M1's on-board digital signal processing. The camera hardware has remained the same since, but updates to the chips have further tweaked picture quality; as it stands the webcam is totally fine, and the addition of portrait and studio lighting modes further improves it, though portrait mode still has some trouble with edges — the reviewer wears over-ear headphones while podcasting and it has not quite figured out what to do with the gap between the band and his head. Apple's biggest enemy here is itself: Continuity Camera, which lets a mounted iPhone take over webcam duties, is a game changer for many and there is really no comparing the two.

The on-board speaker and microphone systems remain mostly unchanged from the 2021 M1 Pro. There are six speakers, with two large grilles on either side of the keyboard, and the system supports both Spatial Audio and Dolby Atmos; it is not an exceptional speaker system, but it gets the job done. The Pro also has a three-mic array with directional beamforming.
