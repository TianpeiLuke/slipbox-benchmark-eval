# Trump Tower Fifth Avenue Penthouse Valuation Dispute

Among the allegations in the New York case was that Trump claimed his Trump Tower apartment in Manhattan — a three-storey penthouse replete with gold-plated fixtures — was nearly three times its actual size, and valued the property at $327 million; no apartment in New York City has ever sold for close to that amount, Attorney General Letitia James said. The Age's comparison in November 2023 listed Trump's 2021 valuation at $US131.3 million and Bloomberg's 2023 valuation at $US40 million, with no current New York estimate available.

The size inflation is documented over a specific window: between 2011 and 2016, Trump valued the apartment on the basis of it being 30,000 square feet (2,787 square metres), almost triple its actual measurement of 10,996 square feet, and because of this New York State arrived at a valuation about a third of Trump's in those years. Space inflation was not the only issue: between 2014 and 2015 Trump increased the apartment's value to $US327 million from $US200 million, which the prosecution argues was to mask a drop in the value of another of his properties. "A discrepancy of this order of magnitude, by a real estate developer sizing up his own living space of decades, can only be considered fraud," Judge Arthur Engoron wrote in his September ruling.

Bloomberg values the property using recent Trump Tower sales and those of comparable Manhattan penthouses. While some splashy residences in much newer Billionaires Row buildings have listed for well in excess of $US100 million, they typically boast higher ceilings, more up-to-date finishes, contemporary layouts, outdoor space and better views. A better comparison, the report says, would be a 9,450-square-foot penthouse in Olympic Tower, five blocks south on Fifth Avenue, on and off the market for $US35 million since 2016; or Carl Icahn's 14,000-square-foot penthouse at Museum Tower on West 53rd Street, listed for $US35 million in 2019 before he attempted to split and sell it as two apartments, both eventually delisted.
