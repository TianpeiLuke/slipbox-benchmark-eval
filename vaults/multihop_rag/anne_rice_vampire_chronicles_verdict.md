# Anne Rice: Vampire Verdict

Polygon's verdict on Anne Rice, author of The Vampire Chronicles series (1976-2018), is: not a vampire.

The case for suspecting her is her place in the genre. Ever since the Victorian era, vampires in fiction have stood in for the sexual urges, temptations, and repressed fears burbling below the surface of a repressive society, but until the late 20th century those messages stayed buried as subtext — charged little winks to audiences that could never actually be stated out loud by any character on the page. It took until the 1970s for the themes to take center stage, and, Polygon says, not coincidentally it took a woman to do it: when Interview with the Vampire reached bookshelves in 1976 it was a watershed, the first time a mainstream book had prominently featured vampires that were gay, campy, and unapologetically fabulous. The acquitting evidence is her religious history. Rice had a famously ambivalent relationship with the Catholicism of her youth — first turning away from it in favor of sexy gothic monster books, then embracing it in the early 2000s to write a series of Christ-themed religious fiction, then rejecting the Church once more over its conservative social positions, writing on her Facebook page at the time that "It's simply impossible for me to 'belong' to this quarrelsome, hostile, disputatious, and deservedly infamous group." Since religious flirtation is canonically one of the few things a vampire cannot tolerate even in small doses, the jury is no longer out.
