# FTX Collapse Mechanism

The collapse of [FTX](ftx.md) in November 2022 followed a disclosure-then-run sequence.

CoinDesk published a copy of [Alameda Research](alameda_research.md)'s balance sheet showing $14.6 billion in assets against $8 billion in liabilities as of June 30, 2022. The problem was the composition: Alameda's largest asset was $3.66 billion of "unlocked FTT" plus $2.16 billion of "FTT collateral" — FTT being the token behind FTX itself. The $5.82 billion in FTT that Alameda claimed was 193% higher than FTT's total market capitalisation of roughly $3 billion, meaning the balance sheet purported to hold more FTT than existed.

Around the same time Binance, the world's largest crypto exchange and an equity holder in FTX from 2019 to 2021, began withdrawing its remaining $2.1 billion equivalent in BUSD and FTT. This triggered a bank run on FTX. FTX and Alameda filed for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in the United States in mid-November 2022.

The mechanism depends on the affiliate holding the exchange's own token as its principal asset, so that a loss of confidence devalues the collateral and the exchange together.
