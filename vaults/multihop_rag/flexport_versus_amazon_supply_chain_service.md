# Flexport Versus Amazon In End-To-End Supply Chain Services

On Sept. 12, almost a week after Clark was fired, Flexport executives convened in Seattle to launch an end-to-end supply chain service allowing sellers to move products from factories to customers' doorsteps through integrations with major online marketplaces — and earlier that same day, just up the street, Amazon had unveiled a strikingly similar service in front of approximately 2,200 attendees at its annual Accelerate seller conference.

The project was spearheaded by Parisa Sadrzadeh, an executive vice president at Flexport whom Clark had poached from Amazon's logistics unit. Flexport had planned a booth at Accelerate but was told it could not be an exhibitor, which some staffers suspected was due to the competing supply chain products; Amazon said Flexport had discussed securing exhibit space months earlier but did not meet all the requirements to participate, and that its launch was not mentioned in those conversations. Flexport's own event was underwhelming: in a conference room about 50 people watched Sadrzadeh debut the service and then introduce Petersen, who spoke for roughly 20 minutes, according to Burak Yolga, co-founder of a digital freight forwarding company who attended. "Flexport announced pretty much the same thing that Amazon announced," Yolga said, adding that he left after about half an hour. The company had paid rapper Nelly $150,000 to perform at the event, but in the days beforehand Petersen opted to squash the performance because the optics were bad after his post about rescinding job offers — and Flexport still paid the artist. Beyond the public relations fallout from the Clark saga and any legal wrangling to follow, Flexport faces staffing turnover and a growing threat from Clark's former employer: it recently ousted Wagers as CFO, lost its human resources chief, and more layoffs are expected soon after the company cut 20% of its staff in January.
