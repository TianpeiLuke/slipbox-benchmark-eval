# Egypt's Refusal To Host Palestinian Refugees

President Abdel-Fattah El-Sisi has rejected any suggestion that Egypt host Gazan refugees, even as the same Western governments now court him for help in alleviating the pressure on Gaza, suggesting instead that Israel take in Palestinians in its Negev desert: "They can transport Palestinians there until Israel implements its announced plan to destroy Hamas," he said on Wednesday, 2023-10-18. The core political ground is that "there is little to no constituency for going against decades of policy on not allowing Palestinian displacement," said Mirette Mabrouk of the Middle East Institute, while the inducements offered "might wind up becoming a political liability, especially in an election year."

Three further grounds support the refusal. Egypt already hosts some 9 million refugees and other migrants from countries including Syria, Sudan, Yemen and Libya. Opening a route for Palestinians may also present a new security risk in the Sinai peninsula, where the army has only just managed to get a handle on its fight with Islamist militants. And Jordan has also refused to accept more Palestinians, making it unlikely Egypt would become a regional exception and take in refugees many suspect may never be let back into Gaza — which would risk being seen in the Arab world as facilitating another mass displacement and betraying the Palestinian cause all Arab countries vocally espouse.

The warrant tying these grounds to the policy is that the domestic political cost outranks the economic one. While Egypt could absorb a certain number of people, the domestic political consequences would be "huge," said the Washington Institute's Robert Satloff: "The political leadership considers this a red line not to be crossed and would prefer to face worsening financial distress than accept a significant number of refugees."
