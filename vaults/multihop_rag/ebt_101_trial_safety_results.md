# EBT-101 Trial Safety Results

The initial safety results from the EBT-101 trial are promising because no adverse outcomes had been observed to date, according to Thomas Hope, a professor of cell and developmental biology at Northwestern University who was not involved in the work and whose lab studies the mechanisms behind HIV infections.

Hope told Live Science in an email that the trial "is an important step forward in the development of this technology to treat human disease and infection, including HIV," but added that "more time is needed to be sure because of the way genetic off-target mutations can take years to manifest into complications." Off-target effects of CRISPR refer to cases where the CRISPR molecule alters DNA at sites other than those targeted; these unintended snips have long been a worry for researchers designing CRISPR treatments, so they will be something to watch for with EBT-101, especially since the treatment targets multiple sites in the genome. Experts also told Live Science that even though the ongoing trial hints the therapy has a positive safety profile, it is still unknown whether one dose can effectively target latent HIV cells and whether it can control HIV in humans. "It is crucial to approach this with caution," Elena Herrera-Carrillo said. "While optimism about being on the right track is justified, it's important to recognize the substantial work that still needs to be done."
