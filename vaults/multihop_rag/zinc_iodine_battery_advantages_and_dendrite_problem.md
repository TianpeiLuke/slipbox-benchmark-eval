# Zinc-Iodine Battery Advantages and the Dendrite Problem

Rechargeable zinc-iodine batteries are one potential substitute for lithium-ion batteries, with the advantages of being safer, costing less, and being more friendly to the environment than lithium alternatives.

They are not perfect, however. Composed of a negative iodine electrode and a positive zinc electrode, these batteries suffer from lower energy output compared with commercial non-aqueous lithium batteries, and from the growth of zinc dendrites on the anodes — projections of material that build on electrodes and short batteries when they cross from one electrode to another — as well as corrosion of the zinc anode, which together result in reduced efficiency and even short-circuiting.
