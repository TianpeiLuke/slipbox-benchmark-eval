# Bengals 24, Bills 18 On Sunday Night Football

Cincinnati beat Buffalo 24-18 on Sunday Night Football on Nov. 5, 2023, by quarters of Bills 7-0-3-8 and Bengals 14-7-0-3, winning a fourth straight game after jumping to a 21-7 first-half lead and letting the defense limit the Bills to 11 points the rest of the way. Sporting News framed it as proof the Bengals were back: Cincinnati had started 1-3, including a pair of blowout losses in which it scored just three points against the Browns and Titans, and it had seemed Joe Burrow's calf injury would hold back the team in what appeared to be one of its best chances to finally win an elusive Super Bowl — but since the loss to Tennessee it had been the Bengals of old.

Fans expected an offensive showdown between two premier quarterbacks, and the first three drives suggested one. The Bengals scored an opening-drive touchdown in just nine plays, matched quickly by a Bills scoring drive of seven plays and 3:15, and Cincinnati re-took the lead on a 5:28 drive to close the first quarter. From there, "Mad Scientist" Lou Anarumo's defense kept Josh Allen and the Bills off balance for most of the rest of the game: their next four drives went punt, interception, punt, end of half. Burrow and the Bengals hit a few bumps in Buffalo territory but scored on their final drive of the half for a two-score halftime lead.

Buffalo picked up a field goal on the first drive of the second half and held Cincinnati to three points after the break, but it was not until 3:32 remained that Allen got the Bills back into the end zone, with a 17-yard strike to Stefon Diggs and a two-point conversion to make it a six-point game. Cincinnati then trusted No. 9 to put it away, starting the drive with a 32-yard deep shot to Tyler Boyd into Buffalo territory before Joe Mixon ran three times to pick up the final first down and let Burrow kneel it out.

In the highly anticipated rematch, Burrow again came out better than Allen in both the win column and the stat sheet: Burrow completed 31 of 44 passes for 348 yards with two touchdowns, while Allen went 26 for 38 for 258 yards with one passing touchdown and an interception, adding eight rushes for 44 yards and another score. With the win the Bengals moved into the final wild-card spot behind the Steelers and Browns, while the Bills were bumped to the outside of the AFC playoff race.
