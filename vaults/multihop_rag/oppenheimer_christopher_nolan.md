# Oppenheimer (Christopher Nolan)

Oppenheimer is Christopher Nolan's biopic of theoretical physicist J. Robert Oppenheimer, starring Cillian Murphy, Robert Downey Jr., and Florence Pugh, and Polygon's No. 1 movie of 2023 in its list of 2023-10-07; it was in theaters and available for digital rental/purchase before streaming on Peacock.

Polygon's framing is that there may not be much left to say about the United States' criminal, moral compromise in dropping the atomic bomb or the obviously tortured psychology of the bomb's creator, but there is plenty left to feel. Cross-cutting through time at lightning speed and smashing together facts in ways its source material, the exhaustive biography American Prometheus, cannot in bound form, Nolan's action-movie sensibilities split the very atoms of his subject to understand not the what but the how and why.

The film bounces from the early days of a daydreaming scientist to the congressional hearings of his eventual political confidant to Oppenheimer's time at Los Alamos, his $2 billion built-from-the-ground-up research base, littering the drama with factual detail ripped straight from the book while ditching the Bohemian Rhapsody school of explanation that would handwave away complicated mathematics and legalese. As in everything from The Dark Knight to Dunkirk, stakes do the talking: Oppenheimer must end the war, while wrestling with turbulent family life, the burial scrutiny of a blacklist-giddy government that wants names of his Communist pals, and the heartbreaking fact that the Jewish people, his people, are under attack — but it all comes back to the bomb, with a ticking clock Nolan takes full advantage of.

Critic MP describes the result as part heist movie, part courtroom drama, part dreamscape, at constant crescendo thanks to a kinetic camera, Ludwig Göransson's humming score, and what might be the most stacked cast in movie history. Every IMAX-sized close-up of Cillian Murphy reveals layers to Oppenheimer that are easily assumed; Robert Downey Jr. takes the right lessons from Tony Stark to imbue political adversary Lewis Strauss with swagger; Emily Blunt, Florence Pugh, Josh Hartnett, Rami Malek, Kenneth Branagh, and many more all deliver, and yet there is still room for The Santa Clause's David Krumholtz to be the MVP. The film has a magnitude worthy of the Trinity tests, but most admirable is that it never fetishizes the accomplishment of the bomb — the end will leave a person absolutely furious, as it must.
