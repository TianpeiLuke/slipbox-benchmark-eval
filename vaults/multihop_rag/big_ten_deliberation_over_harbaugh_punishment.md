# The Big Ten's Deliberation Over Harbaugh's Punishment

In the week before the Nov. 10, 2023 ruling, the Big Ten weighed a "range of possibilities" for punishing Jim Harbaugh, with a suspension the most-discussed option and a significant fine also under consideration. ESPN's Adam Rittenberg reported on Nov. 9 that public reprimands and fines are common penalties under the Big Ten sportsmanship policy and that a range of outcomes was being considered.

The timetable slipped as the conference absorbed Michigan's written case. Pete Thamel reported on Nov. 9 that the Big Ten was reviewing the lengthy written responses from both Michigan and Harbaugh with no indication of any timetable on a decision, while Rittenberg had said a decision was expected by Thursday. Thamel had reported on Nov. 8 that the Big Ten expected Michigan's formal response to the notice of discipline by the end of the day Wednesday and that no ruling should be expected that day, since the conference would take time to absorb it. Heather Dinich reported on Nov. 9 that commissioner Tony Petitti would not be in Dallas for that Thursday's College Football Playoff meeting, which fuelled further speculation that punishment was near.

The NCAA's findings did not implicate the head coach. Yahoo Sports reported on Monday that the NCAA had shared its investigation findings with the Big Ten and that the findings "do not connect the in-person scouting and recording of opponents' sidelines to Harbaugh." The conference nonetheless considered a suspension and could hand down punishments before the end of the week; Yahoo said a multi-game suspension was on the table and that Michigan was fully expected to fight a ban. It would have been Harbaugh's second suspension of the season, after Michigan self-imposed a three-game ban on him to start the year amid a separate NCAA investigation that found he lied to investigators.

Momentum built through consultation and public pressure. Pete Thamel said on ESPN's "College GameDay" that "Over the past few days, Petitti has spoken with the athletic directors, he's spoken with the presidents, he's spoken with the coaches. And the next steps appear that there will be some type of punishment for Michigan. The most likely scenario here is some type of suspension for Jim Harbaugh." Earlier in the week it was reported that coaches were pressing the Big Ten to punish Michigan, with Purdue's Ryan Walters publicly calling for action and saying the allegations were no longer simply allegations.
