# The USMNT Back Line's Errors Against Germany

The Sporting News argues that the story of the United States' 3-1 defeat to Germany was defensive error rather than attacking failure, and grades the back four accordingly: Joe Scally 6, Chris Richards 4, Tim Ream 5 and Sergino Dest 4.

Scally was tidy in possession and offered very little in attack, largely by design since the U.S. did not need him on the right with Tim Weah cooking; he defended okay and was not guilty of any big mistakes, which is more than most of the other U.S. defenders can say — the whole back line has some soul searching to do, but others were far more culpable. Richards, by contrast, had a statistically good match — missing with just one of his 42 attempted passes and never beaten by a German attacker — yet the grounds for his low mark are that his lack of volume speaks volumes: he was off the pace for much of his 65 minutes and failed to get involved as his teammates were beaten repeatedly on the dribble, with positioning letting him down when he should have been more involved with the U.S. under siege in such an open match.

Ream was often left on an island by Richards, and the 36-year-old just could not cope with the waves of German attackers. The third German goal was emblematic of the lack of chemistry between the two centre-backs: Richards stepped up to guard a free Niklas Fullkrug who got by Sergino Dest, and Ream was unaware, leaving Leroy Sane free on goal — the two just didn't have it, and Ream was powerless to pick up the slack.

Dest draws the sharpest verdict because the same match contained his best and worst work. He was lively up front, combining with Christian Pulisic, working well on the ball, getting Mats Hummels booked on a counter-attack and delivering a few very quality crosses; if it were possible to give him two different scores, one for each half of the game, the Sporting News would. But he was also guilty of some of the game's worst defensive errors — completely non-existent as Sane blazed by him on the first Germany goal, and nearly in trouble again right before halftime by holding onto the ball too long after an interception rather than clearing it. Filling in for Antonee Robinson at left-back rather than his natural right, he was targeted by Germany on multiple occasions to great profit, and though he had a few good moments guarding Sane in the second half he was a top culprit for Germany's second goal by playing Fullkrug onside. Had the U.S. profited more from his attacking presence the narrative might be different, but with the story of the match focused on defensive errors, Dest is a guilty party.
