# Uber's Recording Features And Driver Screening

Recording inside an Uber is currently something drivers opt into and control, not something the platform mandates, and driver screening is run by third-party vendors rather than by fingerprint check — the two procedures survivors are asking Uber to change, per TechCrunch on 2023-10-13. Many drivers install their own dash cams to record trips, usually as backup evidence for insurance claims or to defend themselves against unfair deactivations from Uber's platform. Uber is also piloting a new video recording feature for drivers that allows them to record video on their smartphones — but in those cases, the driver decides what and when to record, and when to share that data.

The case for making cameras mandatory is deterrence: "These are predatory, opportunistic drivers taking advantage of vulnerable women, so if they're on film, they likely wouldn't commit the crime," attorney Rachel Abrams told TechCrunch. Uber didn't respond to TechCrunch's request for more information about why it hasn't mandated the use of cameras in ride-hail vehicles, but as with any surveillance, there are privacy issues, and the legality of mandating cameras also varies across local and state laws — so the procedure cannot simply be imposed uniformly. Abrams has her own theory as to why Uber has been slow to implement security cameras: "Cost isn't the issue. It's that it would deter drivers because a lot of drivers don't want cameras. And so if they don't have drivers, they don't make money."

Screening runs through outside vendors. Uber uses third-party companies like Checkr and Appriss to do background checks, which Sergio Avedian, senior contributor at The Rideshare Guy, says "are at best watered down and not guaranteed of bad apples from falling through the cracks." The survivors in the joined lawsuit allege that Uber's "fast and shallow background checks" are substandard and designed to make it as easy as possible for drivers to sign up quickly.

The change the lawsuit asks for is an added screening step: it calls on Uber to also include fingerprinting, which would run prospective drivers through FBI databases. Kevin Conway, managing partner at Peiffer Wolf Carr Kane Conway & Wise, frames the current arrangement as chosen rather than inherited: "This is a very intentional and deliberate decision as evidenced by Uber's active lobbying and resistance against municipalities and regulatory bodies implementing any kind of biometric fingerprinting requirements for drivers."
