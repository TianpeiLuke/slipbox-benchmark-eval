# Blank Space As Satire Of The "Psycho Serial Dater" Caricature

Blank Space is described by the BBC as pretty much the perfect Taylor Swift song: it takes every horrible rumour that was being spread about her love life and amplifies it with satirical relish. The defining move is appropriation of a media caricature. "In the last couple of years the media have had a wonderful fixation on painting me as the psycho serial dater girl," she told the Grammy Museum. "Every article was like, 'Here's Taylor Swift standing near some guy. WATCH OUT GUY!'"

Her account of writing it makes the mechanism explicit — a first reaction of dismay giving way to opportunism. "My first reaction was to be like, 'That's a bummer. This isn't fun for me'. But my second reaction was, 'Hey, that's actually a really interesting character they're writing about: She jet-sets around the world collecting men - and she can get any of them, but she's so clingy that they leave and she cries in her marble bathtub surrounded by pearls'. I was like, 'I can use this'."

Lyrically it contains some of her best lines ("darling, I'm a nightmare dressed like a daydream") and shows a delicious attention to detail, with a pen click signifying every new entry in her little black book. The song also produced a famous mishearing — the "lonely Starbucks lovers" line — which Chicago indie band Hard Femme turned into a real song.
