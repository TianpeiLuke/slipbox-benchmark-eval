# The Kansas City Chiefs' Declining Explosive Passing, 2023

The New York Times argued on 16 October 2023 that the Chiefs' pass offense will become only slightly more explosive as Patrick Mahomes gets reps with his receivers, with no huge shift expected, because their scoring decline reflects plummeting explosive-play rates rather than a temporary slump.

The grounds are historical comparisons within the Mahomes era. The Chiefs have never scored less per game on offense through six games or a full season since Mahomes became their quarterback, yet they have also never enjoyed larger average point differentials (9.8 per game), because their defense is so much better. They are averaging 9.5 plays per touchdown drive when starting at least 70 yards from the opponent's goal line, per TruMedia; that figure was 7.8 in Mahomes' first year as the starter and has climbed every season since — a testament to the Chiefs' adaptability, but also a reflection of falling explosive-play rates.

An executive offered the counterweight, arguing the Chiefs win anyway because of who their best players are: "Yeah, it looks harder for them on offense until Mahomes finds (Travis) Kelce on every major third down, every major two-minute play, every major touchdown. Kansas City has the luxury of their best players being their toughest players. Chris Jones is a very tough player. Kelce is a very tough player. Mahomes is a very tough player. You wonder why they win the games? Because the games are won by the toughest teams most of the time."
