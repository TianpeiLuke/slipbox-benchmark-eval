# Snap's Moderation Response To Drug Dealer Accounts

Snap's defence against the charge that it does nothing about dealers is that it moderates: after the Guardian's story published, a spokesperson said Snap works to "block search results for drug-related terms" and redirects at-risk users to resources on the dangers of fentanyl. "We have great empathy for families who have suffered unimaginable losses. At Snap, we are working hard to stop dealers from abusing our platform," the spokesperson said. Snap did not respond to the Guardian's request for comment prior to the story's release.

Parents in the suit contest that claim on the record of what actually happened when they reported dealers. Perla Mendoza, whose son Daniel (Elijah) Figueroa died after buying fentanyl-laced pills from a dealer on Snapchat, found that Snap did little to prevent illegal drug sales in the weeks and months after his death: even after she created her own account and found her son's dealer posting images with hundreds of pills, her reports to the help center went unanswered, and it took eight months for them to flag his account. "It was really disheartening," she said.

Hanh Badger's objection goes further, attacking the sufficiency of account removal as a remedy at all. "They'll just create another," she said, emphasising the need for structural reforms that clearly warn young users of dealers' predatory practices, or that make it so drugs cannot be peddled to kids on the platform in the first place. "Snapchat perpetuated this crisis," she said.
