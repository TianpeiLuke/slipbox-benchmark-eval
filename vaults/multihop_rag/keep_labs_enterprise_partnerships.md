# Keep Labs' Enterprise Turn And The Pharmacist Co-Lead

Keep Labs brought in Jeff Wandzura — a trained pharmacist who had previously sold two digital health companies — because the company judged that it could not do medication compliance without clinical knowledge on the team, and Wilkins credits that hire with driving the company's enterprise success. Wilkins said he felt Wandzura's skills were complementary to his own skill set, and that it was the right thing to do for the company: "Honestly, it's one of the best decisions I've made throughout my career as Jeff grounded the company, has driven our enterprise success, and become a close friend." TechCrunch reported the account on 4 October 2023.

The reasoning given for the decision, in the words TechCrunch attributes to Wandzura, starts with the pandemic and ends with the preorder customers. "As you can imagine, the pandemic brought about a lot of uncertainty, and we lost some good people," he told TechCrunch. "After I started speaking to preorder customers to understand why they bought Keep, it was clear that I needed a partner from the pharma/pharmacy world. The world of medication compliance is complex, and we needed the clinical knowledge on the team. I wasn't comfortable. And two, I've always felt a strong fiduciary duty to my investors to make decisions in the company's best interest, which meant checking my ego at the door."

Two conditions frame the claim. The first is that the stated justification is domain competence plus fiduciary duty, not growth strategy: the complexity of medication compliance is what made the outside clinical hire necessary, and the willingness to hand over ground is framed as an obligation to investors. The second is that the enterprise result is asserted by the co-founder in an interview — "has driven our enterprise success" — rather than quantified here.
