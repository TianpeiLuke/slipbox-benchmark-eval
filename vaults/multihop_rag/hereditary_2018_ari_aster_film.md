# Hereditary: A Family Drama That Becomes Terrifying

Hereditary, directed by Ari Aster and starring Toni Collette, Alex Wolff and Milly Shapiro, is available on Max. Polygon's argument is that it is a victim of its own success: the poster child for the misguided term "elevated horror" and the subject of more than a few memes, particularly around telephone poles, the thing that often gets lost is that it is actually really good — and damn scary too.

The movie follows Annie Graham, a difficult mother of two who has just lost her own mother. During the funeral service Annie notices quite a few people are there to mourn the mother she thought had no friends, and she eventually learns this group of old people all belonged to the same bizarre semi-cult her mother did — and that's where the witchy stuff starts. From there everything descends into a complicated mishmash of tightly coiled family drama, supernatural plotting and years-old resentments, and the reviewer asks who is to say which is scarier here, the verbal immolation or the literal one.

Even if you have seen it, the reviewer says, you probably owe it a rewatch: you remember it's good but not just how great it really is. Hereditary is elegantly creepy right up until the point that it becomes terrifying, and you can't really ask any more from a horror movie than that.
