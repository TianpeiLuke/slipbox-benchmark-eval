# Point Spread Variants By Team Sport

Spread betting is popular across sports but takes slightly different forms in each: football and basketball use a point spread, baseball calls it the "run line," hockey the "puck line," and soccer the "goal line." In every case the mechanism is the same — the favorite must win by more than the posted margin, and the underdog wins the bet by winning outright or losing by less than it.

In a hypothetical NFL spread with the Patriots as -5.5 favorites over the Steelers, a bet on the Patriots needs them to win by 6 or more points, while a bet on the Steelers wins if they win outright or lose by 5 or fewer. In a hypothetical NBA spread with the Warriors as -9 favorites over the Raptors, a Warriors bet needs a win by 10 or more points and a Raptors bet wins on an outright win or a loss by 8 or fewer; at Warriors 110, Raptors 104, Raptors bets win because they lost by only 6, whereas a 115-104 Warriors win covers the spread by 11 points.

In a hypothetical MLB matchup between the Yankees and Red Sox with the Yankees at -1.5 on the run line — baseball's point spread — a Yankees bet requires a win by 2 or more runs, and Red Sox bets win on a victory or a 1-run loss; Yankees 5, Red Sox 4 means Red Sox bets win, while a 6-3 Yankees win covers the run line with a 3-run margin. At NHL betting sites the "puck line" is akin to the point spread: with the Canadiens as -1.5 favorites over the Blackhawks, the Canadiens need to win by 2 or more goals, and Blackhawks bets win on an outright win or a one-goal loss; Canadiens 3, Blackhawks 2 means Blackhawks bets win because the one-goal victory does not cover 1.5, while a 4-1 Canadiens win covers by a 3-goal margin. In a hypothetical MLS game between LA Galaxy and Atlanta United with LA Galaxy at -1 on the goal line, the Galaxy need to win by 2 or more goals and Atlanta bets win on an outright win or a single-goal loss; LA Galaxy 2, Atlanta United 1 means Atlanta bets win, while a 3-1 Galaxy win covers with a 2-goal margin.
