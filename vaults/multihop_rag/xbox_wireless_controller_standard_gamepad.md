# The Xbox Wireless Controller

The standard Xbox Wireless Controller is the best option for most Xbox gamers. Its specifications: connectivity via Xbox wireless, Bluetooth and wired; USB-C connector; no mappable rear buttons; no software customization; powered by AA batteries or an add-on rechargeable. It has a dedicated share button for saving clips and screenshots, and a USB-C port for charging Microsoft's play-and-charge rechargeable battery. Listed at $45, down from $65 — 31 percent off — at Target.

The case for it is accumulated refinement. It may lack some of the extra features of premium options, but this controller is the distillation of four generations and numerous first-party controllers before it, and it shows. It is also the only affordable wireless Xbox controller out there, thanks to Microsoft's proprietary protocol. Some may hate that it ships with AA batteries instead of a built-in rechargeable cell, but that also makes it flexible — allowing rechargeable AAs or a battery pack, like this one from Hori — and user-replaceable batteries mean you are not stuck with a controller that does not hold its charge after years of use. It also works as a wired accessory with a USB-C cable. Microsoft even color-matches the light-up Xbox button to the controller.

The hardware is excellent for the controller's $60 asking price. The sticks, buttons, triggers, and the sunken dish-shaped D-pad all feel impeccably tight, with the D-pad having a satisfying clickiness. Hardcore fighting game fans may be better served by an arcade stick or a controller geared toward that genre — one with an impeccable D-pad like Hori's Horipad Pro — but the Xbox Wireless Controller is an exceptional jack-of-all-trades.

What it really lacks compared to pricier options are extra, customizable buttons and software tuning for things like stick sensitivity, though Microsoft does offer cosmetic customization through Xbox Design Lab, ranging from $69.99 to $99.94 depending on the configuration.
