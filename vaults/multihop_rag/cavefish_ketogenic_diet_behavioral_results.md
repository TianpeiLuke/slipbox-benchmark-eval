# Cavefish Ketogenic Diet Behavioral Results

After a month on the ketogenic diet, the cavefish began to act like the more social surface fish: they would follow each other in groups and ceased going round in circles.

In the experiment, cavefish were fed the same ketogenic milk provided to human patients, albeit with a few modifications for fish consumption, and their behaviour was monitored; as a comparison, a type of A. mexicanus that lives in rivers rather than caves was also tested. The surface fish do not display the same autism-like behaviours as their cave-dwelling relatives — in the presence of other surface fish, individuals begin to follow each other and swim together, something rarely seen in cavefish, Yoshizawa said, and the surface fish also do not do the repetitive behaviour of swimming in circles. Some other behaviours, such as attention to a specific task and sleeping, were unaffected, but overall the results were promising and, according to Yoshizawa, suggest dopamine could be key to how the diet affects behaviour: "We suspect that due to this behavior variation, probably the target of the ketogenic diet is the dopaminergic system," he said. The challenge now is teasing out all the complex factors that may be involved. The reference is Motoko Iwashita, et al., "Metabolic shift toward ketosis in asocial cavefish increases social-like affinity," BMC Biology (2023), DOI: 10.1186/s12915-023-01725-9.
