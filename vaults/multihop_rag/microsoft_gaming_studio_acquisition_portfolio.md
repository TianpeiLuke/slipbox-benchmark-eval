# Microsoft's Gaming Studio Acquisition Portfolio

The Activision deal expands an already massive gaming empire: Microsoft now owns more than 20 different gaming studios altogether, built through a slew of acquisitions stretching back two decades.

Two of those purchases anchor the history. Gamers who grew up with the N64 will recall Microsoft's shock purchase of GoldenEye 007 developer Rare for $375m in 2002, a studio that until then had specialised in Nintendo games. Modern players might be more familiar with Microsoft buying Minecraft developer Mojang for $2.5bn in 2014, which means it owns the best-selling game of all time. The BBC frames these as savvy acquisitions used to try to muster in gaming the same dominance Microsoft has had in computing.

World of Warcraft now comes under the Microsoft umbrella, and the BBC lists more big names about to fall under it: Tony Hawk's Pro Skater, World of Warcraft, Diablo, Overwatch and Candy Crush Saga — a selection from what it calls a long list.
