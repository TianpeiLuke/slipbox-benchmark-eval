# Spider-Man Embodies Ordinary Struggle

Polygon argues that Spider-Man represents the basic human needs Thanksgiving is really about, even though very few readers have to balance photojournalist work, a fraught dating life, and pummeling Dr. Octopus.

The grounds are the character's circumstances. Peter Parker is often the most financially strapped among his Avengers buddies and frequently the loneliest too, and those are recognizable traits among many young people, even those without radioactive-spider powers: when you are growing up and trying to figure out the world, it is easy to feel lost and isolated. The seasonal hinge is that feelings of loneliness spike during the holidays, so there is a good chance a reader will feel even more like Peter Parker when late November rolls around. From his first comic book appearance, Spider-Man has been a reminder that life is hard and complicated and that being a superhero does not preclude anyone from experiencing ordinary frustrations, setbacks, and confusion. The conclusion the article draws is that however his Thanksgiving escapades turn out, they remind readers and viewers that the holiday is about the hope of mutual connection, support, and nurturing — even if the girl of your dreams is out of reach and the Green Goblin is on your case, a table, some friends, and Aunt May's turkey might make everything better for a little while.
