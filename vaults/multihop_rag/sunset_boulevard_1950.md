# Sunset Boulevard (1950)

Billy Wilder's Sunset Boulevard opens with one of the all-time great openings — has-been writer Joe Gillis, played by William Holden, wearily narrating over a shot of the police examining his floating corpse in a Los Angeles swimming pool — and Polygon's Tasha Robinson calls it a stunner from start to finish. It streams on Paramount Plus, is free with a library card on Hoopla or Kanopy, and is rentable on Amazon, Vudu, and YouTube.

What sets it apart within the genre is that it is not a crime procedural like so many classic noirs. It is about Joe, out of work and fleeing the men trying to repossess his car, trying to find shelter with an aging movie star by promising to write her a comeback vehicle, and getting drawn into a drama he did not expect — one that is exceptionally well acted and scripted. Robinson describes it as dark stuff about Hollywood dreams and how they die, with Gloria Swanson at her most captivating and frightening.
