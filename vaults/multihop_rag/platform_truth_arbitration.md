# Why Platforms Cannot Arbitrate Truth

Asking platforms like Facebook, Twitter, or Reddit to be in charge of verifying truth and accuracy is a difficult task, Alexis Ohanian argued in remarks reported 31 October 2023 — and the best system for moderation he has seen so far is not a platform verdict at all but Twitter's Community Notes. His ground is scale and its consequences: there are more people online today than early tech founders may have imagined, and though he is glad that access to the technology has broadened, that has come with many complications. "I don't like the idea that the internet I'm so nostalgic for was actually an ivory tower," he said, but on the flip side, we are all exposed to more people, in real-time, than we could have ever fathomed. The warrant is that this exposure puts people at odds with one another and with everyone's different versions of the truth — even if their truth is believing in something as far-fetched as the earth being flat, an example he noted has a following on Reddit. If the population that must be adjudicated holds irreconcilable versions of the truth, the job of certifying one of them is difficult for whichever operator is handed it.

Ohanian did not exempt the newer players from the problem. He called out TikTok as "an arm for the Chinese government" that's really good at "feeding us what we want, when we want it" — in other words, not always a fountain of truth, either.

The constructive half of the argument is a specific endorsement. If anything, Ohanian credits Twitter (now called X) with coming up with the best system for moderation so far. "Community Notes, I personally think is a good — it's the best measure I've seen yet. And frankly, I wish we had thought of it first," he said. The endorsement is bounded: he still called out the notion that a platform, like X, can be a town square.

Underlying the whole position is an admission about what he failed to foresee as a founder. "To… think Oh my god, I'm gonna be so successful, it could have a tremendous impact on democracy would take a level of delusion that even I, as a first-time CEO, just couldn't have," he noted. "Was that a blind spot? Absolutely," he admitted.
