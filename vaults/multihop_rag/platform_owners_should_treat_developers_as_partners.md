# Platform Owners Should Treat Developers As Partners

Tim Sweeney argued that any tech company, Apple and Google included, would be much better off in the long term if it viewed developers as awesome partners and did everything it could to support and empower them rather than get in their way financially — and said the opposite behavior at Apple and Google is what has disappointed him most.

His grounds are a comparison and a diagnosis. The comparison is Microsoft: even at the peak of the antitrust trial against Microsoft, he said, Microsoft was awesome to developers — always respectful, giving developers a great deal and treating them as partners — so that even as Microsoft was crushing corporate competitors, the developer experience was excellent. The Verge appended an editor's note that Netscape might feel differently. The diagnosis is that Google and Apple both treat developers as adversaries, trying to attack developer revenue streams and prevent them from competing with the platforms' own products, and have built massive self-preferencing schemes around excluding developers and disadvantaging third parties — which Sweeney calls very shortsighted. The warrant is that a platform's own long-term position improves when its developers thrive, and Sweeney offers Epic's own conduct as the demonstration: this has been Epic's philosophy with Unreal Engine and the Epic Games Store, where Epic wants to be "a cool partner that helps other companies succeed the way we do." He suspected such a philosophy change at Apple and Google may only come with a generational change in the companies' management.
