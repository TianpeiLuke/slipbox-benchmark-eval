# EU AI Act Phased Entry Into Force Timeline

The AI Act's compliance demands enter into force in phases after the law is adopted: six months until rules on prohibited use cases kick in, 12 months for transparency and governance requirements, and 24 months for all other requirements — so the full force of the EU's AI Act may not be felt until 2026.

Several steps stand before that clock starts. The final text must be compiled — the task facing the bloc over the coming weeks after the marathon 38-hour talks that ended on Friday night — and translated into Member States' languages, then affirmed by final votes in the parliament and Council, after which there is a short period before the text of the law is published in the EU's Official Journal and another before it comes into force. The consolidated text was expected to emerge in January or February, and it would be worth scrutinizing since there could still be devils lurking in the detail even though the EU's executive seemed keen to put key details out there in spite of there being no final text yet. Despite the EU visibly patting itself on the back over securing a deal on world-first AI rules, the lawmaking process was not quite at the end of the road; but given how much division and disagreement there had been over how, or even whether, to regulate AI, the biggest obstacles were dismantled by the political deal and the path to passing the Act in the coming months looked clear.

Within the phased approach, 24 months are allowed before the high-risk rules will apply for GPAIs, while the list of strictly prohibited use-cases applies sooner — just six months after the law enters into force. That could potentially mean bans on certain "unacceptable risk" uses of AI, such as social scoring or Clearview AI-style selfie scraping for facial recognition databases, get up and running in the second half of 2024, assuming no last-minute opposition to the regulation springs up within the Council or Parliament.
