# Chemosynthesis and the Search for Life Beyond Earth

Deep sea exploration has shown that the range of conditions under which life can thrive is far greater than imagined, Jon Copley argues, which is why it informs the search for life outside our solar system.

The first ground is chemosynthesis — life powered by a form of chemical energy instead of sunlight, as in photosynthesis. The idea that chemosynthesis could sustain whole populations of animal species was considered impossible until the discovery of hydrothermal vents and other similar habitats. The second ground is light: deep sea vents also glow very faintly, too faintly for the human eye to see but bright enough that microbes can use it as an energy source. Again, that expands our notion of what is possible in the cosmos, because you do not necessarily have to be that close to a bright star, potentially, to sustain life.
