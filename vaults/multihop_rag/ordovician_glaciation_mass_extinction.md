# The Ordovician Glaciation Mass Extinction

A major glacial event at the end of the Ordovician, around 450 million years ago, killed off about half of all existing genuses — the first of the widely recognized global mass extinction events.

The trigger was geochemical: chemical weathering outpaced the volcanic emissions of gas and atmospheric CO2 levels dropped. The resulting cooling caused a buildup in ice mass on the large South Pole-centered supercontinent of Gondwana, sea levels dropped, and much of the coastal habitat that had been home to primitive mollusks and crustaceans disappeared. Some of the creatures scraped by, but roughly half of genuses perished. Much as one can only wonder what knowledge was lost in the ransacking of the Library of Alexandria, Mann writes, one can also ponder what sort of magnificent creatures born of the Cambrian explosion were lost — and this would hardly be the last such event.
