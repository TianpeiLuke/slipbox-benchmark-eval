# NASCAR Media Deal Streaming Diversification Rationale

Sports Business Journal's Adam Stern, speaking on the Dirty Mo Live podcast and quoted by Essentially Sports on Dec. 1, 2023, explained the structure behind NASCAR's media rights deal as the interaction of three pressures: the money available from incumbent partners, the decline of cable, and the demand from teams for more revenue.

Stern said the sanctioning body would have loved to stay with Fox Sports and NBC, but since that would most likely have resulted in less money, NASCAR opted to explore other options. He framed media rights revenue as a key role in sports using MLS as the comparison: "You look at MLS. MLS takes a big deal with Apple, almost all of their games behind the Apple TV's paywall. That was the money they could get and they had to deliver for their industry. Their industry is expected to be a growing business and one of the most sporting ways to be a growing business in sports is to have growing media rights revenue." He added a further factor specific to distribution: the cable universe has been declining, with a drop of a couple of percentage points of subscribers every quarter, which is very concerning — one of the reasons NASCAR did not want to gamble solely on cable users, and with Amazon Prime it had the leisure to explore the streaming arena and glimpse how the future could look.

The relation holds jointly, not separately: "So partially, they [NASCAR] did it to spread their eggs in multiple baskets but also part out of it was to get more money and to make sure they get more money in the industry at the time when their teams are saying we need more money, we are not profitable currently." Stern concluded that NASCAR was more or less left with no choice: "NASCAR is in a tough situation because they had to deliver more money as well and clearly if they went this route, that's a good indication that's because they had to go this route to deliver more money for the industry."
