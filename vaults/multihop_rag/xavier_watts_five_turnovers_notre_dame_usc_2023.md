# Xavier Watts Fuels Notre Dame's Five-Turnover Rout Of USC

Notre Dame safety Xavier Watts was the decisive figure in No. 21 Notre Dame's 48-20 home win over No. 10 USC on Saturday, Oct. 14, 2023: he intercepted Caleb Williams twice, forced a fumble and returned another fumble for a touchdown, while Williams — the reigning Heisman Trophy winner — turned in what the Sporting News called arguably the worst game of his career. The Fighting Irish rode those crucial turnovers and a relentless pass rush to the overwhelming result.

The sequence began four plays into the game, when Williams over-threw Lake McRee and Watts picked the pass off, returning it 38 yards to USC's 12 and setting up a quick three-play Notre Dame scoring drive. Midway through the third quarter, with Notre Dame leading 10-3, Williams threw another pick to Watts, who was stopped at the USC 2; Audric Estime finished the job with the first of his two touchdowns to make the score 17-3. Williams threw a third interception on the following drive — the first time in his career he had thrown three picks in a single game — and Notre Dame again cashed the turnover in for seven points. Late in the fourth quarter, with USC trying to stay in the game, Watts recovered a fumble after a reception by Mario Williams and raced it into the end zone; with his team trailing 48-20, Lincoln Riley pulled the USC starters, and the backup running back then fumbled on USC's final drive, forced once more by Watts, letting Notre Dame run out the clock.

Coach Marcus Freeman framed the performance as a team effort after the game: "I think the entire team was hungry. We had a sour taste in our mouth. But the reality is that I've told those guys just compete against [Williams]. He's gonna make plays, he's a great football player, but be relentless in terms of your effort, your attitude, how you attack that guy and to come up big with five turnovers is really impressive." Watts himself deflected credit: "Just thank you to all my teammates. None of that would have happened without them. All the pressure they brought, the good coverage down the field." The game featured other marquee names — Caleb Williams, Sam Hartman, Zachariah Branch and Audric Estime — but Watts was the one who played the hero.
