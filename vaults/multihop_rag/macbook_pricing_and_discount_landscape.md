# MacBook Pricing And Discount Landscape

Apple sells MacBooks equipped with its own M-series chips in a wide range of sizes and price points, starting with the 13-inch MacBook Air from 2020 at $999 and going up to the latest 16-inch MacBook Pro starting at $2,499 — and, as The Verge put it on October 26th, 2023, finding a deal on a current Mac with an M1, M2, or even the new M3 chip, as well as the higher-end M3 Pro and M3 Max, is actually not that difficult.

The discriminating feature of Mac discounting is that it is episodic rather than continuous: Macs may not experience perpetual discounts, but it is not uncommon to see various current models discounted by as much as $400. The alternative route to savings is buying refurbished directly from Apple, which avoids waiting for the changing winds of deals; Apple's refurbished store provides a one-year warranty on all products and generally offers discounts of up to 15 to 20 percent off the price of a new unit.

One boundary case is explicitly excluded. The Verge does not recommend buying any older Intel-based Macs: retailers may try to tempt buyers with very low prices on leftover new or refurbished stock, but there is a reason nobody wants them anymore — they are completely outclassed by all the newer models, unless you have some super-niche Windows Boot Camp needs, and even then you should consider which Windows apps you really need and whether they work with Parallels.
