# Jim Jordan's Third-Ballot Defeat And Removal As Nominee

Republicans dropped Rep. Jim Jordan on Friday, 2023-10-20, as their nominee for House speaker, making the decision during a closed-door session after the hard-edged ally of Donald Trump failed badly on a third ballot for the gavel. In the floor vote that morning Jordan lost 25 Republican colleagues — worse than he had fared earlier in the week, and far from the majority needed — as they rejected his hardline approach. Friday's vote was 194 for Jordan, his lowest tally yet, and 210 for Democratic leader Hakeem Jeffries, with two absences on each side. He had lost rather than gained votes despite hours spent trying to win over holdouts, no improvement from the 20 and then 22 Republicans he lost in early rounds that week.

Ahead of the vote Jordan showed no signs of stepping aside, insisting at a Capitol press conference: "The American people are hungry for change." Afterward he told colleagues behind closed doors, "I tried my best," according to one of his opponents, Rep. Don Bacon, R-Neb. Publicly he said simply of his colleagues, "We put the question to them, they made a different decision," and added that House Republicans now need to come together and "figure out who our speaker is going to be."

The evening before, after a late Thursday meeting at which Jordan sought to hear detractors out and shore up support, Rep. Carlos Gimenez, R-Fla., said "He doesn't have the votes to be speaker." The holdouts want "nothing" from Jordan, Gimenez said, adding that some lawmakers in the meeting simply called on him to drop out of the race. Up to the closed-door session, Jordan was refusing to step aside, appearing determined to wait out his foes even as his path was all but collapsing.
