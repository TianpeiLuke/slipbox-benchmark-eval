# Ohanian's Critique of Social Media and the Creator Economy

Reddit co-founder Alexis Ohanian, speaking at an event at the University of Virginia in an interview with Kara Swisher published 31 October 2023, argued that social media as currently built brings out the "worst parts of our nature in many ways" — and that the creator economy is a large part of why. His grounds are behavioural: everyone has "been so trained and conditioned now to just get the likes and retweets, get the upvotes…I wish I could be more optimistic about it, but we're seeing it play out before our eyes." He added a second ground, the race for primacy, worrying about the desire for people to be first and win on whatever leaderboard a platform features, which then leads to a situation where misinformation is able to spread. The warrant joining the two is that platform scoring mechanics are not incidental decoration but the thing shaping what users do, so the resulting misinformation is a product of the design rather than a failure of individual users.

Ohanian paired that critique with a supporting claim about human wiring, posted the same day: "Humans evolved trusting the faces & voices we knew around the campfire. The idea of believing something bc of experts/institutions you've never met is actually new to our species. I visited @UVADemocracy + chatted with @karaswisher + @dkroy about what's next for social media."

Despite the negative reading, Ohanian — also the founder of venture fund Seven Seven Six — ultimately described himself as a "techno-optimist," an apparent nod to a16z General Partner Marc Andreessen's recent diatribe where he claimed technology could solve all our problems. "Guilty as charged," Ohanian said. "I do think that pendulum will swing back. I think even swifter than we realize." He theorised that the war in the Middle East may even be the tipping point, as it is now "abundantly clear" that we need something better to figure out the world than the platforms we have today.

His own record is offered as partial evidence that the pendulum can move. He notably resigned from Reddit's board in 2020 over differences of opinion on moderation and wanting to crack down on communities about violence and hate, a moment where he seemed to be reckoning with what his platform had wrought; at the time he asked for his board seat to be replaced by a Black board member, and the company agreed, appointing its first Black board member with Y Combinator partner Michael Seibel. Reddit also later banned several toxic communities, including r/donaldtrup following violence at the U.S. Capitol, and Ohanian says the site has "gotten demonstrably better" since, as well as better as a business.
