# High Limit Racing's Purse Escalation Mechanism

High Limit Racing works, in Kyle Larson's account, as a pressure mechanism: by paying large purses at its own events, the series pushes competing weekend shows to raise theirs. "I was hoping this would add pressure to the weekend shows to raise their purses as well so I think we've seen a big step in that this year and I think it's gonna continue forward so, I would say so far it's been successful," Larson told Dale Earnhardt Jr at the Lernerville Speedway.

The money moves through several parties. The series helps out both Sweet and Larson financially, given the fan following it has garnered, but it is also quite profitable for the drivers and the teams: the winning driver — in this case David Gravel — wins $50,000, and with crowds so big, it also helps the teams with merchandise sales.

The mechanism extends to the venues as well. Events like these help keep smaller race tracks across the country in good shape: if a track is not used or taken care of for an extended period, it will start to wear out and eventually become completely unsuitable for any kind of racing unless it is taken apart and rebuilt.
