# ChatGPT Legal And Privacy Controversies

ChatGPT has generated a run of legal and privacy controversies: an Australian mayor publicly announced he may sue OpenAI for defamation over ChatGPT's false claims that he had served time in prison for bribery, which would be the first defamation lawsuit against the text-generating service, and there have been other cases of ChatGPT accusing individuals of false crimes.

The libel exposure follows from how the models work: they do not know or care whether something is true, only that it looks true — a problem when the output is used for homework, and potentially libel when it accuses someone of a crime they did not commit. How troubling statements produced by ChatGPT will be handled was expected to play out over the following months as tech and legal experts tackled the fastest moving target in the industry. As of the article, there were no lawsuits specifically targeting ChatGPT, but OpenAI is involved in at least one lawsuit with implications for AI systems trained on publicly available data, which would touch on ChatGPT. There are also plagiarism issues: text-generating AI models like ChatGPT have a tendency to regurgitate content from their training data. Copyright in output sits with the user — the person who requested the input from ChatGPT is the copyright owner.

On privacy, OpenAI has said that individuals in "certain jurisdictions" such as the EU can object to the processing of their personal information by its AI models by filling out a form — the "OpenAI Personal Data Removal Request" — which includes the ability to request deletion of AI-generated references about them, although OpenAI notes it may not grant every request since it must balance privacy requests against freedom of expression "in accordance with applicable laws." In its privacy policy OpenAI makes a passing acknowledgement of the objection requirements attached to relying on "legitimate interest," pointing users to instructions on how to opt out of use of their information to train its models. ChatGPT chats are not public, but OpenAI disclosed a bug, since fixed, that exposed the titles of some users' conversations to other people on the service.

Other controversies span platforms and schools. Discord integrated OpenAI's technology into its bot Clyde, and two users tricked Clyde into providing instructions for making methamphetamine and napalm. CNET found itself in controversy after Futurism reported the publication was publishing articles under a mysterious byline completely generated by AI, and Red Ventures, the private equity company that owns CNET, was accused of using ChatGPT for SEO farming even where the information was incorrect. Several major school systems and colleges, including New York City Public Schools, have banned ChatGPT from their networks and devices, claiming the AI impedes the learning process by promoting plagiarism and misinformation — a claim not every educator agrees with.
