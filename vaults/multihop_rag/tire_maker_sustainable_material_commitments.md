# Tire Makers' Sustainable Material Commitments

Virtually every tire manufacturer is making at least token efforts to increase the usage of sustainable materials like guayule. Yokohama is using motorsports to develop its Advan A005 tire, which debuted at last year's Pikes Peak International Hill Climb and contains 33 percent sustainable materials including biomass-derived rubber; Michelin shod Porsche's prototype electric GT4 e-Performance racer with tires said to be 53 percent renewable, made with materials like strap steel and lemon peels.

That may sound like marketing plays — and it partly is — but motorsports have historically proven an ideal development opportunity in the automotive industry: track tires are pushed to their limits, resulting in better products for the road, in time at least.

Many manufacturers have set self-imposed deadlines for a shift to renewable sourcing. Dunlop is promising 40 percent of its tires will be renewable by the end of this decade and 100 percent by 2050. Bridgestone's goal is also 2050 but it is introducing models now with an increased focus on sustainability, like the Turanza EV, made of 50 percent renewable and recycled materials.
