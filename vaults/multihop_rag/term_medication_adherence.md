# Medication Adherence

## Definition

In the corpus, medication adherence is the term Keep Labs uses for whether patients are actually taking their medication, and it is the specific outcome the company's enterprise offering is built to track and improve.

The company pairs it with harm reduction as the device's two founding goals — the founders call the Keep "a solution to harm reduction and medication management" — and later describes its enterprise pivot as "focused on medication adherence along with harm reduction."

## Context

In the corpus the term surfaces through Keep Labs, maker of a lockable smart storage container for medicine: the founders describe their device as more than a secure box, calling it "a solution to harm reduction and medication management," and the company's enterprise pivot is explicitly "focused on medication adherence along with harm reduction." Jeff Wandzura, a trained pharmacist whom co-founder Philip Wilkins brought on to lead the company, is quoted saying "the world of medication compliance is complex, and we needed the clinical knowledge on the team." The concept sits between the device's raw tracking mechanism (detecting whether pills were removed from a bottle) and the enterprise business built on top of that data (aggregating it for healthcare partners).

## Key Characteristics

- **Behavioral outcome, not a device feature** — adherence describes whether the patient took the medication, which the Keep infers indirectly by weighing bottles on a scale and logging whether they came back lighter.
- **Distinct from harm reduction** — the founders repeatedly pair the two ("harm reduction and medication management") but treat them as separate goals: harm reduction is about safe storage (e.g., keeping medicine away from children or curbing misuse), while adherence is about the tracked act of taking the medication.
- **Basis for an enterprise data product** — Keep Labs monetizes adherence tracking by providing "de-identified aggregate data to understand how many patients in the population are adhering [to their medications] and how many need intervention," sold to enterprise partners for a subscription fee.
- **Requires clinical expertise to operationalize** — Wandzura's arrival as a trained pharmacist was driven by the judgment that "the world of medication compliance is complex" and that the company needed clinical knowledge on the team.
- **Deployed through healthcare partnerships** — Keep Labs joined the McKesson Digital Health Network in Canada to supply real-time data on whether patients are taking their medication, and partnered with Savvy Cooperative to place devices with patients living with chronic conditions.

## Background (external)

The corpus itself does not offer a clinical definition of the term, only its usage; the general definition below is supplied for context and is not corpus-sourced.

Outside this corpus, the general medical concept is usually called "patient adherence": Wikipedia's article on the topic (titled "Patient adherence," with "Medication adherence" redirecting to it) states that "patient adherence describes the degree to which a person correctly follows medical advice," a definition the article traces to the World Health Organization's 2003 report *Adherence to long-term therapies: evidence for action*. (Source: Wikipedia, "Patient adherence," https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Patient_adherence, accessed 2026-09-02.)
