# Why Israeli Office Rents Have Not Fallen

Asked why rents are not falling given huge supply and a fall in demand, the market's practitioners give two answers. Guy Amosi says prices have fallen by about 10% at most, and that those cutting rents are the ones who bought buildings in purchase groups like BSR City, where many individuals bought as a profitable investment; once construction of the building is finished and payments such as management fees, mortgage and property taxes drop, some owners think of selling, and in those places a drop in prices may be seen.

Or Ben Zvi Klein argues prices will not go down because people have learned lessons from the past: everyone was burned by Covid, and a property owner who gave a discount at the beginning of Covid realised it by the end, when the office opposite the one leased at a discount of NIS 100 per square meter was being rented out for NIS 140 per square meter. Property owners are not under pressure because they rented most of their space during the peak periods of 2021-2022; those who may be under pressure are companies trying to sublet their properties, where attractive prices are visible for fully furnished space. He qualifies the claim with a condition: Israelis have been living for years with fighting in the background, and if the war continues people may want to break a contract; but as the economy returns to work with kindergartens and schools opening there is a general feeling of an economy returning to normal alongside the war, and it is possible to start going to work. He draws the Covid parallel to its conclusion, that there was talk about whether people would return to work and in the end everyone returned to the office, and says he is optimistic about the office market in future.
