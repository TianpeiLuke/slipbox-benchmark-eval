# Tears of the Kingdom Forecloses Queer Expression

Polygon's claim, published 2023-10-19, is that Tears of the Kingdom is damning because it denies space for imagination: its narrative is not just a perpetuation of Hyrule's status quo but introduces new constraints that deny queer subtext, and even expression, where once there was room — changes teased out by returning to the fulcrum of gender discourse in Breath of the Wild, Gerudo Town. The writer describes stepping into Hyrule Field again and feeling much more constrained.

The grounds are the ambient dialogue. What pervades the land in place of calamity is not the evil of Ganon but of gender deviance: characters often make jokes emasculating Link. At one stable a Lurelin refugee laughs at the notion that "a skinny little guy" might attempt to rescue his village, while a Gerudo pilgrim jokes with Link that he could not possibly be her destined lover. More than one Gerudo remarks on his short stature, and others reject the notion he may be the famed hero of time because he is so short. The Great Fairy Mija, however fond of him, describes Link as "a slim little lad" in what is framed as a backhanded compliment. These comments envelop Hyrule with gendered expectations, much like gendered beauty standards and social norms do in our own world.

The warrant is a claim about what made the earlier game's openness possible: queer joy was only accepted in the empty, unstructured wilds of a Hyrule beholden to calamity, and in a world filled by side quests and the new settlements of a monarchy reborn, there is no need for a hero who wears a skirt like a girl. Polygon's conclusion is that Tears of the Kingdom presents a Hyrule where the Triforce rests not in the trinity of the spiritual aspects of Power, Courage, and Wisdom, but instead in the gender binary.
