# Tech Layoffs In March 2023

March 2023 was one of the heaviest months of the 2023 tech layoff wave, headlined by Accenture's plan to cut 19,000 jobs announced on March 23, Meta's confirmation on March 14 that it would cut 10,000 people, and Amazon's second big round of 9,000 job cuts announced on March 20.

The month opened on March 1 with Thoughtworks laying off about 4% of its global workforce, approximately 500 employees; Alphabet's Waymo issuing a second round of layoffs that year, which combined with the January cuts meant the self-driving technology company had let go 8%, or 209 employees, of its workforce; and Cerebral letting go 15% of its workforce, roughly 285 employees. On March 6 Atlassian announced it was laying off about 500 employees, 5% of its total workforce; SiriusXM said it had laid off 475 employees, 8% of its total workforce; and the Nigerian B2B e-commerce platform Alerzo confirmed it had laid off 15% of its full-time workforce, leaving about 800 employees at the startup, which had a headcount of more than 2,000 before a first round of layoffs in September 2022. In the week of March 10 more Salesforce employees learned they had been laid off; Salesforce first announced it was laying off 10% of the workforce in January but some employees did not know until February, and the company confirmed these later layoffs were part of the same 10%. Y Combinator announced on March 13 that its cuts would affect 20% of staff, 17 team members. On March 14 CEO Mark Zuckerberg confirmed rumors that Meta would cut 10,000 people from its workforce and around 5,000 open roles it had yet to fill. Klaviyo announced on March 15 that it had laid off 140 staff across all teams, and Course Hero announced on March 16 that it had cut 15% of staff, 42 people.

The second half of the month was busier still. On March 20 Amazon announced another round of substantial layoffs, with 9,000 people set to lose their jobs — TechCrunch heard around 10% of that total came from AWS — and as part of the round Amazon shut down DPReview; Twitch announced it would lay off 400 employees; and Livespace announced it had laid off at least 100 employees, about 2% of its workforce. On March 22 Indeed announced it would lay off 2,200 employees, 15% of its staff, and Roofstock announced it had laid off 27% of its staff, approximately 100 employees. Accenture announced on March 23 that it planned to cut 19,000 jobs, 2.5% of its workforce. On March 24 Bloomberg reported that more layoffs could be on the way at Salesforce, quoting chief operating officer Brian Millham, who indicated the company could add to the ongoing job cutting at the CRM leader and in tech generally; if the layoffs happened they would come on top of the 10% cut in January. On March 27 Disney revealed in an internal memo that there would be three rounds of layoffs, the first beginning that week, impacting approximately 7,000 employees as announced in February, and Microsoft laid off 559 workers from its Bellevue and Redmond operations; as part of its earlier announcement to lay off 10,000 people, Microsoft had also laid off an entire team dedicated to guiding AI innovation that leads to ethical, responsible and sustainable outcomes. On March 28 GitHub announced it had eliminated over 100 jobs in the South Asian market, laying off virtually its entire engineering team in India, which a GitHub spokesperson told TechCrunch was part of the streamlining effort disclosed in February to cut roughly 10% of its workforce by the end of Q1 2023; Lucid announced it was laying off 1,300 employees, 18% of its workforce, to be completed by the end of Q2 2023. Shift Technologies announced on March 29 that it had laid off 30% of its workforce in Q1 2023. On March 30 Unacademy announced it had laid off more than 350 roles, 12% of its workforce, just over four months after cutting about 350 roles in November, and Roku announced it was letting go of about 200 employees, 6% of its workforce, having laid off 200 U.S. employees back in November. On March 31 Netflix confirmed a "handful of layoffs" including two longtime executives, with the exact number unclear; Netflix was scheduled to report Q1 2023 results on April 18.
