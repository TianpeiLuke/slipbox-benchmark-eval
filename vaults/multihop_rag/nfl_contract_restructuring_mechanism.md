# How NFL Contract Restructuring Works

When NFL teams restructure contracts, they typically take a player's base salary — which is accounted for in a one-year duration — and turn it into some sort of signing bonus.

The mechanism works because of proration: that bonus is allowed to be prorated over the duration of the contract, so it spreads what would be a high, one-year cost out into a moderate cost over several years, lowering the current-year cap hit.

The boundary condition is which contracts are worth restructuring. Typically, players who have their contracts restructured have high cap hits and high base salaries, which is why an analysis of a team's restructure options starts by looking at its highest-paid players for the year in question — as the Sporting News does with the 49ers' 2024 roster.
