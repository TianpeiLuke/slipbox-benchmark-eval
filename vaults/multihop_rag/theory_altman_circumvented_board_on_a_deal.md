# Theory: Altman Circumvented The Board On A Deal

The prevailing theory floating around on November 18, 2023, based on the board's language and the way these giant tech companies work, is that Altman went around the board on a deal. "Not consistently candid" is a very diplomatic way of saying Altman lied.

The proposition: Altman — and potentially OpenAI President Greg Brockman, who stepped down as chairman simultaneously, then resigned — wanted to make a bold move that he knew the board would not like. It is not uncommon for these deals to be hammered out quietly in smoke- or vape-filled rooms and then presented as a fait accompli, but if it was controversial enough and the board found out about these maneuvers, it could be fuel for an ouster. The open question TechCrunch poses is what kind of deal would be big and dangerous enough for a summary dismissal of the CEO and famous face of the company, given the man was onstage two weeks earlier.

Microsoft is the natural candidate: few would be shocked if Microsoft, which is deeply embedded in OpenAI as an investor and customer, is a factor. Could Altman have been working with — or against — OpenAI's patron in secret? If Altman wanted to kill the golden goose by going independent, that might have activated the board's fiduciary or otherwise statutory duty; if he was negotiating some other deal, like an acquisition or deeper and more exclusive integration, it could also have caused the board to bristle, either at the idea itself or at being excluded. What would falsify it is weak: if Microsoft was as shocked as everyone else, as one report has it, it could hardly be the kind of high-stakes conspiracy some seem to be hoping for — but one must assume Microsoft would say that either way, since even if they had been working with Altman on a secret plan they could truthfully say they were surprised by his firing, and they "remain committed to our partnership."
