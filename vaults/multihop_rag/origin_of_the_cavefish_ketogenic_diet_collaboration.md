# Origin of the Cavefish Ketogenic Diet Collaboration

Believing that cavefish might be useful for the study of autism, Masato Yoshizawa approached his colleague Ryan Lee, a physician working with autism patients at Shriners Hospital for Children Honolulu: "When I showed him the video of the cavefish he immediately said, 'yes, that is very similar to what I observe in my patients'," Yoshizawa recounted.

Lee, who has experimented with ketogenic diets to improve autism symptoms with some success, suggested they try it with the fish. In humans, not all patients receiving the ketogenic diet improve, but some do; if it worked with the fish, the researchers might then be able to figure out how the diet affects behaviour and improve the effectiveness of the treatment for humans. Yoshizawa was again sceptical: "I doubted it at first," he said. "I didn't think it was going to work, but it did." The article carries a correction dated November 2, 2023: Ryan Lee was originally described as working at the University of Hawai'i Mānoa, which was incorrect and has been corrected to Shriners Hospital for Children Honolulu.
