# The EU's Urgent Warning Letter To X

The European Union fired a public warning at Elon Musk-owned X (formerly Twitter) for failing to tackle illegal content circulating on the platform in the wake of Saturday's deadly attacks on Israel by Hamas terrorists based in the Gaza Strip — an "urgent" letter from internal market commissioner Thierry Breton that TechCrunch described as a very swift test of the bloc's newly updated content moderation rulebook. Breton shared the letter publicly, tweeting on October 10, 2023: "Following the terrorist attacks by Hamas against 🇮🇱, we have indications of X/Twitter being used to disseminate illegal content & disinformation in the EU. Urgent letter to @elonmusk on #DSA obligations". TechCrunch's later account places the letter on the Wednesday.

The letter's substance was twofold. Breton said the bloc had seen "indications" from "qualified sources" that X was being used to disseminate illegal content and disinformation in the EU following Saturday's attacks, then reminded X of the DSA's "very precise obligations" on content moderation: "When you receive notices of illegal content in the EU, you must be timely, diligent and objective in taking action and removing the relevant content when warranted." Separately, the European Commission raised concerns about the spread of disinformation on X related to the terrorist attacks and their aftermath.

On the disinformation side Breton was specific about what had been reported and what he wanted done: "Public media and civil society organisations widely report instances of fake and manipulated images and facts circulating on your platform in the EU, such as repurposed old images of unrelated armed conflicts or military footage that actually originated from video games. This appears to be manifestly false or misleading information," he wrote. "I therefore invite you to urgently ensure that your systems are effective, and report on the crisis measures taken to my team."

TechCrunch's own characterisation of the platform at that moment was blunter than the letter's: rather than tackling the problem, it wrote, X "appears to be turning into an engine of disinformation" — one that had demonstrably enabled the very swift amplification of a smorgasbord of toxic fakes around the Israel-Hamas war, falsehoods that might be trying to manipulate opinion around the conflict, or otherwise exploit horrific events to drive engagement (clickbait) or for even more cynical and potentially harmful ends.
