# What Israeli Leaders Claimed The Gaza Barrier Would Do

At the time of the fence's completion, Israeli leaders argued that the barrier had already proved itself and would provide security to the region — claims that, as the article notes, rested on a threat model built around tunnels and ladders.

Prime Minister Benjamin Netanyahu, then in opposition, referred mainly to the below-ground aspect: "Any time Hamas terrorists have tried to penetrate the border settlements through the tunnels, they were terminated underground. The below-ground barrier surrounding the Gaza Strip has already saved many lives." Then-Minister of Defense Benny Gantz said, "The barrier, which is a superior initial technological and inventive project that sets an iron wall, sensors and concrete between it and the residents of the south. It provides a sense of personal security that will allow this beautiful region to continue to grow." The reference scenario behind that confidence is identified by Yehoshua Kalisky, senior researcher at Tel Aviv University's Institute for National Security Studies: "The concept underlying the construction of an advanced technology fence relates to the Second Lebanon War, when Hezbollah surmounted a high fence with a ladder. This was the reference scenario. There was no thought that they would use aircraft here to cross it or that they would come with a heavy machinery, and simply breach it."
