# The Case Against Supersized Electric Vehicles

TechCrunch argues that the death of several would-be antidotes to the supersized and high-priced EV trend in 2023 is bad news for everyone — even for a reader who would pick a beefy Cybertruck over something resembling a teeny Kei car.

The grounds are physical and environmental. Cars in general are no substitute for more efficient modes of transportation, including trains and bikes, but the last thing the climate and most budgets need is another tank-like truck or SUV: large, heavy electric vehicles demand more materials and energy than smaller EVs, which means more avoidable greenhouse gas emissions during and after production. Despite these downsides, automakers build big because big sells.

The warrant joining the two is that market resistance to "bigger is better" keeps failing. Numerous vehicles from startups and some legacy automakers challenge that notion via compact designs and smaller price tags, yet little typically comes of their resistance — as observed in 2023 with several ill-fated EVs including the ElectraMeccanica Solo, Sono Sion and Mazda MX-30. The article surveys the small and lower-priced EVs that did not make it: the flops, the failures to launch, the u-turns and holdouts of North America's size- and range-obsessed EV market.
