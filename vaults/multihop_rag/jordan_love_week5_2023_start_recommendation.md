# Jordan Love As A Week 5 2023 Start Against The Raiders

CBS Sports listed Jordan Love under "Start Him" for Week 5 of the 2023 season against Las Vegas: the claim is that Love has upside to get back to the 25-plus fantasy points he posted in Weeks 1-3, and the warrant is that the Raiders' track record against quarterbacks plus the return of Green Bay's injured personnel restore the conditions under which Love produced.

The grounds are personnel and opponent behaviour. Aaron Jones and Christian Watson return without snap limits, and guard Elgton Jenkins is back too. The Raiders deserve credit for trying — over the previous two weeks they led the NFL in pass-rush pressure rate and ranked 10th in blitz rate — but playing the weaker offensive lines of Pittsburgh and the Los Angeles Chargers helped boost those numbers, and quarterbacks still found success at 13.9 yards per catch and 7.07 yards after catch per reception over those two games, both bottom-three in the league. Both quarterbacks Las Vegas faced in Weeks 3 and 4 posted at least 22 fantasy points: Kenny Pickett, and Justin Herbert without his second-best receiver and without his top running back. The column's hunch is that the Raiders will keep blitzing and play a lot of gutsy man coverage behind it, having leaned man in two of their past three games and moved to heavy zone only against Denver in Week 1. Love has been a mixed bag against man — a terrible 39.4% completion rate but an outstanding 12.1% touchdown rate — and more man plus more blitzing could produce more rushing from Love, which he has shown he is willing to do.

The counterweight is his Week 4 loss to Detroit, which the column works through rather than ignores. The Lions' pass rush was too much for Love and the Packers line in the first half, Detroit played a lot of early zone and Love still threw into the teeth of it; he calmed down in the second half, took more of what the defense gave him and got into a groove before a bad read on a miscommunication with Romeo Doubs produced his second interception. Even so he set season bests in completion rate (63.9%) and off-target rate (11.1%) while averaging a hefty 9.5 average depth of target, his second-lowest of the year but still above league average. On film the recurring problems were being late on throws — something the author had flagged in preseason — and passes sailing high because he put too much of his body into them, which combined with his penchant for attacking deep explains the bad game; the redeeming trait was that Love was pass-rush ignorant, refusing to let Detroit's pressure scare him even as it overwhelmed him for early sacks. The verdict ranks Love ahead of Trevor Lawrence, Joe Burrow and Jared Goff.
