# Rhamondre Stevenson's Fantasy Outlook In Weeks 5 And 6 Of 2023

CBS Sports listed Rhamondre Stevenson under "Sit Him" in both Week 5 and Week 6 of the 2023 season, and hardened the call in the second week: the claim is that Stevenson's workload no longer converts into production, and the warrant is that his before- and after-contact efficiency is at or near the bottom of the league, so neither volume nor a soft matchup is enough on its own.

The Week 5 case, published 4 October 2023 ahead of a home game with New Orleans, concedes that the workload is nice but expects Stevenson once again not to do much with it. The breakdown lists New England favoured by 1, an over/under of 40, an opponent ranked 8th versus running backs, 13.2 projected points, a 26th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 164 rushing yards, 12 receptions, 87 receiving yards, one touchdown and 10.8 fantasy points per game. On film the numbers don't match the talent — Stevenson is a capable runner with great vision, balance and power plus good speed — but he struggled on lateral runs and his line failed him plenty on north-south runs, while game script limited him to one fourth-quarter touch. He has at least 16 touches in every game, which is good, but has yet to total 100 yards and has been under 70 yards three times. The Patriots' front five troubles show up in his 0.98 yards before contact per rush, 29th of 44 qualifying backs, and his 1.75 yards after contact per rush, dead last among those 44. He has one explosive carry of 12 or more yards all year — 12 yards, against Miami — against 21 explosive runs and a 10% explosive run rate in 2022, eighth-best. In the passing game he averages 4.0 targets and catches 75% of them but gets just 7.3 yards per catch with one explosive catch of 16-plus yards, a 32-yarder against the Eagles in Week 1, with no reception longer than seven yards since. The Saints are sixth-best in rushing average allowed to backs (3.6) and top-10 in basically every run-defense metric including just nine missed tackles on the year; only Derrick Henry has exceeded 10 fantasy points against them, PPR or not, with 13 PPR points in Week 1, and no one has run for a touchdown on them. Against backs through the air they are roughly league average except in yards after catch per reception (8.53), inflated by a massive Henry catch-and-run in Week 1 — since Week 2 that figure is 5.94, sixth-best in the span, with zero explosive pass plays allowed to backs. Stevenson is still called a decent start ahead of Najee Harris, Khalil Herbert and Jahmyr Gibbs, but the author would rather chance a lineup on upside plays like Jaleel McLaughlin, if Javonte Williams sits out, or Alexander Mattison.

The Week 6 case, published 12 October 2023 ahead of a road game at Las Vegas, refuses to recommend him at all until there is proof of him playing well, since a favourable road matchup alone isn't enough. The breakdown lists the Raiders favoured by 3, an over/under of 41.5, an opponent ranked 20th versus running backs, 12 projected points, a 27th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 188 rushing yards, 12 receptions, 87 receiving yards, one touchdown and 9.1 fantasy points per game. Bill Belichick spoke of "starting all over" after the loss to the Saints; the column notes that while some might say dumping Ezekiel Elliott and giving Stevenson more work would make sense, Elliott actually has a higher rushing average and better before- and after-contact rates, so New England may instead turn to another back hoping to find anything that takes pressure off a depleted passing game. Week 5 was the first time Stevenson did not have 16 or more touches: eight carries, no receptions, a season-low 50% snap share, though he handled 10 of 16 third- and fourth-down snaps; he started and played the first two series, Elliott started the next two, they rotated all game, and Elliott remained the back of choice in the two-minute offense at the end of the first half. His efficiency was unchanged — 0.97 yards before contact per rush, 30th of 41 qualifiers, and 1.79 yards after contact, dead last among them — with the same single explosive carry and the same single explosive catch. The Patriots have run one play inside the opponents' five-yard line all season, which explains his lack of touchdowns. The Raiders rank bottom-10 versus backs in yards per carry allowed (4.3) and yards after contact per rush allowed (3.04), but are top-10 in rush attempts by backs faced (24.4 per game), have given up just three rushing touchdowns and allow an explosive run on 4.9% of snaps, tenth-best, and only James Cook has scored more than 15 PPR points against them this season. The author would rather start Jerome Ford, Zack Moss and D'Onta Foreman.
