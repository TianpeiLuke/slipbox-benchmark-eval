# Alameda's $65 Billion Line Of Credit At FTX

Alameda Research held a line of credit at FTX that ultimately reached $65 billion, an effectively unlimited borrowing facility unmatched by anyone else on the exchange and carrying no requirement to post collateral. Caroline Ellison testified that there was no contract and no written terms, and that the credit line was not visible to FTX's auditors; when she raised that with Bankman-Fried, he told her not to worry about it.

The defining features are its size, its informality, and its exclusivity. Gary Wang, who set the line up, testified that it did not start that big: Bankman-Fried asked him to increase it a few times because Alameda kept running into its limits — first "a few hundred million," then a billion, then even that was not enough. It was not clear how Wang arrived at $65 billion specifically, but he said he had discussed the number with Bankman-Fried, and that other customers did not have the same privileges. Ellison said the credit was drawn in increments of $100,000 to $10 million at a time and was used primarily for trading: the effectively unlimited funds "allowed us to make profitable trades we couldn't have made otherwise."

The boundary case is the source of the capital and Bankman-Fried's disclaimed knowledge of it. Ellison testified that Alameda and FTX were initially "very integrated," run by the same team from the same office, and that when Alameda was scrounging for funds Bankman-Fried told her FTX would be a good source of capital. On the stand, Bankman-Fried denied he knew about the effectively infinite line of credit Alameda received from FTX — an argument The Verge's reporter found peculiar, summarising the take-away as that the CEO of a financial company simply did not pay attention to finances.
