# VPN Use Cases And Prerequisites

Before considering a VPN, make sure your online security is up to date in other ways: complex passwords, multifactor authentication methods and locked-down data sharing preferences. Even then, you probably do not need to be using a VPN all the time. "If you're just worried about somebody sitting there passively and looking at your data then a VPN is great," Jed Crandall, an associate professor at Arizona State University, told Engadget.

The traditional use case is protecting your internet traffic on untrusted networks. If you use public WiFi networks a lot — like while working at a coffee shop — a VPN can give you private internet access, hiding what you do from the internet service provider so the owner of the WiFi or hackers trying to get into the system cannot see the identity of your computer or your browsing history. They are also helpful for hiding information from other people on your ISP if you do not want members of your household to know what you are up to online.

The non-textbook use case is geoblocking. VPNs can mask your location, so even if you are based in the United States they can make it appear as if you are browsing abroad and unblock access — useful for reaching shows only available on streaming services like Netflix, Hulu or Amazon Prime in other countries, for watching Canadian Netflix from the US, or for playing online games with people located all over the globe.
