# Golf Ball Rollback Effect On LIV Golf

Essentially Sports laid out on Dec. 3, 2023 how the rollback's non-binding design interacts with LIV Golf's business model: because the R&A and USGA have not made it binding on any governing bodies to implement the rules, opting out is the easiest path for the breakaway league.

Two relations drive that. First, power hitters such as Bryson DeChambeau would be affected in more ways than one otherwise. Second, a league that thrives on thrills and excitement would lose its unique selling point if the ball suddenly did not travel 300 yards on most occasions — and as of that year, 28 of 48 LIV golfers averaged over 300 yards driving distance, with Dustin Johnson and DeChambeau both in the top five. The complication that limits how far this holds is manufacturing: the rollback policy will render most balls totally "unplayable," and the PGA Tour has contradicted itself several times in the past few years, so it would not be surprising if Monahan & Co. eventually agreed with the policy change later. That leaves the open question of how viable it would be for manufacturers to produce two types of balls, one for the majors and the other for LIV Golf — which throws the world of golf into uncharted territory.
