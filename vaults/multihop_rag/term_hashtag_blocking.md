# Hashtag Blocking

Hashtag blocking is a content moderation technique in which a platform renders certain terms non-searchable, so that posts tagged with a blocked hashtag no longer surface in hashtag search results.

## Context

In the corpus the term appears in a TechCrunch report (2023-10-13) covering Meta's blog post responding to EU regulators' content-moderation warning over the Israel-Hamas war, where Meta lists hashtag blocking alongside other visibility-reduction measures — demoting potentially violating or borderline content in recommendations and reducing the visibility of potentially offensive comments — as one of the "other steps" it took to respond to the conflict. Meta applied hashtag blocking "to render certain terms related to the conflict non-searchable on its platforms," but its blog post did not specify which hashtags were being blocked.

## Key Characteristics

- **Search suppression, not removal** — the technique makes tagged terms non-searchable rather than deleting the underlying posts, distinguishing it from content takedowns.
- **Undisclosed scope** — Meta declined to specify which hashtags it was blocking in relation to the Israel-Hamas war, so the mechanism's actual coverage was opaque even in Meta's own disclosure.
- **Paired with other visibility levers** — the corpus presents it as one of a set of ranking/search measures Meta deployed together, alongside reduced recommendation of borderline content and reduced visibility of offensive comments.
- **Fallible in prior use** — Meta has previously misapplied a comparable hashtag block: during the 2021 Hamas-Israel conflict, it blocked the hashtag for Al-Aqsa Mosque, "mistaken for a terrorist organization." Following a third-party report on that conflict's moderation, Meta committed to policy changes such as updating its "keywords associated with dangerous organizations."
