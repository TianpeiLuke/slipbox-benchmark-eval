# European Tech Valuation Pressure And PE Dealmaking

The Verdane-Meltwater deal underscores two themes in European tech and VC: tech companies continue to see huge pressure on their valuations, and investors are structuring deals to de-risk as private equity fills the gap left by shrinking venture funding.

On valuations, the evidence is Meltwater's own numbers: its market cap of just under $600 million is actually less than the company raised over the years when it was a privately held startup — over $700 million per PitchBook data — and less than half of its valuation when it went public in December 2020 at over $1 billion. On dealmaking, the market is particularly tight in Europe: VC firm Atomico's annual deep dive into the European funding landscape, put together with a number of third-party research firms and participation from others in the ecosystem, found that funding in 2023 halved to just $43 billion, and private equity firms are making a much bigger appearance in deals to make up some of the drop from VC.

The warrant linking those conditions to the deal's unusual shape is de-risking. It is notable that Verdane opted to invest in Fountain Venture rather than directly in Meltwater: that gives Verdane not only the stake in Meltwater but also a stake in Jobylon and whatever else Fountain and Lyseggen find interesting, which in turn de-leverages a focus on just one business. Verdane itself has only recently started to spread its wings to invest in startups across all of Europe and beyond, so tying up with a partner to help direct it is a very de-risked approach to take while trying to be more ambitious.
