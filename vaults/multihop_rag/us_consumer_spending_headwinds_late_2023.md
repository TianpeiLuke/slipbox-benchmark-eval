# Headwinds To US Consumer Spending In Late 2023

US consumer spending growth was cooling under three named pressures — higher energy prices, student loan debt repayments, and real disposable incomes on a declining trajectory since June. "It came to a boil during a hot summer, and the temperature is really starting to come down," said Brian Jacobsen, chief economist at Annex Wealth Management, adding that the combination "doesn't bode well."

Each channel is specified. Oil prices have jumped to their highest level in more than a year, pressuring the economy by raising fuel costs for everyone: a barrel of US crude sank 92¢ on Friday to settle at $US90.79, but it is still up sharply from $US70 in June, and Brent crude, the international standard, also weakened. The resumption of US student-loan repayments may funnel more dollars away from the consumer spending that has helped keep the economy afloat. The data calendar that would test this was still ahead — the latest monthly update on the US jobs market was due the following week, with a couple of important reports on inflation coming the week after, and postponements of such reports could complicate things for the Fed, which has insisted it will make upcoming decisions on interest rates based on what incoming data say about the economy; the Fed's next meeting on rates ends on November 1. Not every reading pointed the same way: on Wall Street, Nike jumped 6.7 per cent after reporting better profit for the latest quarter than analysts expected, with strength overseas helping it make up for some declines in North America.
