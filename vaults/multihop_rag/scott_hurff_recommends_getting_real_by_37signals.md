# Scott Hurff Recommends "Getting Real"

Scott Hurff recommends "Getting Real: The Smarter, Faster, Easier Way to Build a Successful Web Application," by Jason Fried, David Heinemeier Hansson and Matthew Linderman, calling it an absolute treasure from 2006 that is still teaching him things. As a writer, Hurff does not always write about product design, but when he does he is able to share his perspective as someone who is also a product maker and designer — an intersection that nurtured his own book, "Designing Products People Love: How Great Designers Create Successful Products."

His grounds are the book's originality and its voice. It was the first time anyone building in modern tech put together a practical, straightforward, all-encompassing guide for conceiving, building, shipping and marketing web apps, and Fried and Heinemeier Hansson's words are efficient when they need to be, detailed when it matters, and packed full of nuance that is both unique and endearing. Hurff suspects the book feels like it started as an internal series of posts or a handbook to help scale 37signals beyond the core team, recalling that Basecamp launched in 2004 and had quickly found success.

The warrant he leans on is that the method was proven on its authors' own product: whatever the format, what lends "Getting Real" authenticity is the fact that 37signals used these exact techniques to ship Basecamp and find its customer base. To this day he has never read anything like it — one of those books you can finish in an afternoon but that stays with you far longer, morphing into a reference book for the times when you need some extra perspective.
