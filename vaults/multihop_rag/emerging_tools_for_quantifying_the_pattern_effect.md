# Emerging Tools for Quantifying the Pattern Effect

Promising new tools and observations are beginning to emerge for quantifying the pattern effect: targeted model experiments and intercomparisons, extended global satellite observations of clouds and radiation, Argo floats sampling deep-ocean heat uptake, estimates of radiative imbalances before the year 2000, and new observational constraints on clouds' sensitivity to environmental controlling factors.

These developments encourage an optimistic outlook on the ability to quantify the pattern effect and its implications over the next couple of years. Working to solve these problems at the intersection of different disciplines and with input from different research communities will likely benefit related research into, for example, the sensitivity of tropical cyclones and future rainfall changes in the southwestern United States or in South America to surface warming patterns.
