# Global HIV Burden in 2022

HIV remained a global public health threat in 2022, with 1.3 million new infections and around half that many deaths that year, while UNAIDS put the number of people living with HIV globally at approximately 39 million and AIDS-related deaths at about 630,000 for the same year.

An HIV diagnosis has not been a death sentence for years, thanks to powerful medications, but the burden persists despite that progress. While new HIV infections have dropped steadily since their peak in 1995, the pool of people who are HIV-positive has only grown as people live longer with the disease. People with HIV must consistently take medications to prevent the virus from becoming transmissible again or progressing to AIDS (acquired immunodeficiency syndrome), so new infections could actually rebound fast if the world does not dramatically ramp up the number of people being regularly treated, tested and protected from new HIV infections. There is no vaccine or easily accessible cure for HIV, although a handful of people have been effectively cured through intensive stem-cell transplants.
