# Suspiria (2018, Luca Guadagnino)

Luca Guadagnino's 2018 Suspiria did the impossible, in Polygon's judgment: it created a contemporary equal to Dario Argento's 1977 horror masterpiece. It was the Oct. 21 pick in the 2023-10-10 Halloween Countdown, listed on Prime Video and Freevee.

Polygon's account of the original is that it is the sort of film even the best directors would face insurmountable odds of matching with a remake: the story of an American ballerina who moves to Germany to attend a prestigious ballet company only to be greeted by a series of murders and a supernatural mystery is a vessel for Argento's craft, featuring some of his boldest visuals and some truly nauseating body horror.

How Guadagnino managed it, per critic CP, was casting: Tilda Swinton in multiple roles and Mia Goth to do the Mia Goth thing; cinematography by Sayombhu Mukdeeprom, known for Guadagnino's own Call Me By Your Name but also Apichatpong Weerasethakul's seminal indie films Uncle Boonmee Who Can Recall His Past Lives and Memoria; and a score by Thom Yorke. Where other directors would have obsessively recreated a source text, rebooting it into something unrecognizable, Guadagnino's film is an homage — the sort of movie you imagine Argento would make with the budgets and creative freedom afforded by tech companies trying to buy cultural capital. It is more political, more grotesque, and the ending is just more. The critic's recommendation is to enjoy the 2018 and original Suspiria together, which has been a challenge because the original is surprisingly difficult to watch, especially for streaming viewers — but that month the Criterion Collection made one of the most batshit double features possible.
