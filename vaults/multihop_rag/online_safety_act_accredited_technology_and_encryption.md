# The Accredited Technology Clause And The Encryption Objection

WhatsApp, other encrypted messaging services, and digital rights groups reject the assumption that the Online Safety Act's CSAM-detection clause can be satisfied without breaking encryption: the section in question allows Ofcom to require online platforms to use so-called "accredited technology" to detect CSAM, and they say this scanning would require breaking apps' encryption systems and invading user privacy.

The objection is aimed at territory Ofcom has not yet reached. Today's consultation covers some of the act's least contentious ground, like reducing the spread of content that was already illegal in the UK. As Ofcom releases future updates it will have to take on touchier subjects, like content that is legal but harmful for children, underage access to pornography, and protections for women and girls — and, perhaps most controversially, it will need to interpret the section that critics have claimed could fundamentally undermine end-to-end encryption in messaging apps.

The matter is unresolved. Whitehead says Ofcom plans to consult on this next year, leaving the act's full impact on encrypted messaging uncertain.
