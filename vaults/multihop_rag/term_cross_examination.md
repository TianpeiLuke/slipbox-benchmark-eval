# Cross-Examination

## Definition

Cross-examination is the questioning of a witness by the opposing side's attorney after that witness has given testimony on direct examination, and it is the stage at which a witness "doesn't get to say, 'I'm done'" — once a witness takes the stand, they must answer the other side's questions rather than simply walking out.

## Context

In the corpus the term structures the SBF/FTX fraud trial's witness-examination sequence: witnesses who took the stand — including Adam Yedidia, Matt Huang, Caroline Ellison, and finally Sam Bankman-Fried himself — gave direct testimony for the side that called them, then faced cross-examination from opposing counsel, and in several cases a brief redirect examination afterward.

Gary Wang also testified in the trial, but the cited corpus passages describe only his direct examination by prosecutor Nicolas Roos, not a cross-examination of him.

Former federal prosecutor Josh Naftalis explained the mechanic ahead of Bankman-Fried's own testimony: taking the stand is a "Hail Mary" precisely because cross-examination cannot be escaped once begun. Bankman-Fried's decision to testify was widely judged to have backfired because it opened him to Danielle Sassoon's cross-examination, described by one reporter as the nastiest cross she had ever watched. Judge Lewis Kaplan repeatedly intervened during both cross- and direct examination of Bankman-Fried, at one point scolding him for not answering Sassoon's questions on cross and, the next day, for not answering his own counsel Mark Cohen's questions on direct.

## Key Characteristics

- **Follows direct examination** — cross-examination is conducted by the opposing attorney only after the calling side has finished its own questioning (direct examination) of a witness.
- **Adversarial and probing** — cross-examining counsel tests a witness's reliability, as when Sassoon used Bankman-Fried's own cross to establish that his direct-examination account was "absurd," or as Christian Everdell's cross of Adam Yedidia focused on Yedidia's compensation and responsibility for the code that misdirected customer funds.
- **Can be followed by redirect** — after cross-examination, the original calling attorney may conduct a brief redirect examination, as Bankman-Fried's own lawyers did after Sassoon's cross of him, and as Sassoon did after Cohen's cross of Ellison.
- **Quality varies and is publicly assessed** — reporters explicitly graded cross-examinations as effective or ineffective; Cohen's cross-examination of Ellison was called disorganized and was said to have "absolutely biffed" the opportunity, boring the jury, while Sassoon's cross-examination of Bankman-Fried was described by one reporter as the nastiest cross-examination she had ever watched.
- **A witness cannot opt out** — once a witness takes the stand, cross-examination is compulsory; a testifying defendant such as Bankman-Fried cannot end the process early if he dislikes the direction of the questions.
