# The Last Seduction (1994)

John Dahl's The Last Seduction is one of the most gleefully cruel, shockingly dirty neo-noirs to ever claim the name, and Polygon's Tasha Robinson says it feels all the more subversive because the femme fatale is the protagonist and there is no hero worth the label. It streams on Peacock, free with ads on Tubi, Vudu, Roku, FreeVee, and Plex, and is rentable at Amazon, Vudu, and Apple.

Bridget Gregory, played by Linda Fiorentino, schemes with her patsy husband Clay, played by Bill Pullman, to get her hands on a pile of illicit cash — then runs off with it and leaves him behind to take the fall, only to wind up stranded in a small town with time to kill and a second patsy to play with. Robinson warns that it gets particularly ugly by the end and calls it a dark, dark story, but finds a gleeful joy in the tightness of the narrative and in Bridget's sociopathic skill at coming up with a manipulation for every situation.
