# Gatekeeper Platforms Will Keep Drawing Antitrust Suits Over Their Fees

Patrick McGahan, a partner at Scott+Scott who focuses on antitrust litigation, argued to TechCrunch that platform companies operating as gatekeepers in their market — PayPal among them — will continue to be the subject of antitrust litigation as a result of the significant fees they charge.

His grounds were that the underlying conflict has not been resolved. The case, he said, illustrates that "the tensions between merchants and the payment system providers are not over, and that litigation regarding this key cost faced by merchants is likely to continue for some time." The warrant joining the grounds to the claim is that fees are the key cost merchants face in the payment system, so as long as gatekeepers set them, merchants have a standing reason to sue.

McGahan also anticipated the gatekeeper's rebuttal, and it turns on where the fees originate: "PayPal's fees are, however, driven by the costs imposed upon them by the dominant card schemes, Visa, Mastercard and American Express. So, we can expect PayPal will respond to this suit by arguing that it is as much of a price taker as the merchants themselves, and that the terms imposed upon it by the card schemes drive some of its anti-steering rules." PayPal did not respond to requests for comment. The item was reported by Mary Ann.
