# Philippine 2024 Budget Deficit and Debt Trajectory

The Philippine national budget for 2024 is P5.768 trillion, 9.5% higher than the 2023 budget of P5.268 trillion — an increase of P499.6 billion — and observers note that this first budget fully prepared by the current administration accounts for 21.7% of gross domestic product. Supported mainly by P3.5 trillion in tax revenues, based on 2023 tax revenue projections, it implies a budget deficit of some P2.268 trillion to be funded by additional borrowings, which could increase government debt from P14.24 trillion as of July to P16+ trillion by the end of 2024.

At the Congressional budget hearing, Finance Secretary Benjamin Diokno optimistically provided updates on the government's fiscal and revenue collection performances and presented sources of funding for the national budget. He told the Philippine News Agency on 10 August that tax revenue is projected to increase from P3.5 trillion in 2023 to P6.5 trillion in 2028, while non-tax revenues are expected to decline from P191 billion in 2023 to P184 billion in 2028. Diokno said the Finance department aims to gradually reduce net financing, or new debt, from P1.42 trillion in 2022 to P1.22 trillion in 2024, which will help bring the country's debt-to-GDP ratio to almost below 60% by 2025.

Against that optimism, President Ferdinand Marcos, Jr. told members of the US-ASEAN Business Council who visited Malacañang, in remarks reported by ABS-CBN on 9 August 2023: "We worry about our debt-to-GDP ratio in the Philippines, as it stands at about 63% and that's a little high for us and it is not ideal."
