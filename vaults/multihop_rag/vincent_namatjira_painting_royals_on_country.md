# Vincent Namatjira On Painting Royals On Country

Vincent Namatjira argues that painting royals on country strips them of power: "When I paint the queen on country or the king on country, it's like taking away their power, putting us level with the rest of the world," he told The Guardian on 16 October 2023.

His grounds are the contrast with official portraiture. Among prime ministers, royals and chief executives, painted portraits typically convey the power of their position — officially commissioned for palaces, parliaments or boardrooms, with artists granted rare access to their famous sitters. Vincent's portraits, painted in his studio at Iwantja Arts in Indulkana, are by contrast gleefully unofficial and defiant, so the setting itself refuses the sitter's authority.

He pushes the claim further: "And also, here in Australia, Indigenous people should be more powerful than the royal people." The warrant is a recasting of dynastic equivalence — "To me, I see myself as a royal," he adds, treating his great-grandfather's 1954 meeting and handshake with Queen Elizabeth II as an equalising moment between two very different dynasties. A work of his from 2017 is titled Captain Cook with the Queen and Me.
