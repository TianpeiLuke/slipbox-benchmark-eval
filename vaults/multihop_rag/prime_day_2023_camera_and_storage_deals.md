# Prime Day 2023 Camera And Storage Deals

WIRED's camera and storage picks span the GoPro Hero10 Black at $249, the Canon R5, Sony's A7 III, SD and CFexpress cards, the SanDisk portable SSD, Samsung's T7 Shield, and a small photo printer at $78 (other colors $99), with the larger Canon SELPHY CP1500 also on sale for $99.

GoPro recently dropped the Hero10 Black (8/10, WIRED Recommends) to $249, making it one of the cheapest action cameras on the market — two generations old but still very capable — and this deal adds an extra battery, case, small tripod, and other accessories. The Canon R5, an upgrade over WIRED's Canon top pick in its mirrorless camera guide, features a 45-megapixel full-frame CMOS sensor, staggeringly fast autofocus, excellent subject tracking in continuous AF mode, and dual memory card slots; it isn't cheap, and the deal is body only, with lenses sold separately. Sony's A7 III has been superseded by the A7 IV but remains very nice, its 24.2-megapixel sensor offering fantastic dynamic range and decently fast phase-detection autofocus. The writer swears by the SD cards, having used them for seven years without issue, fast enough for every camera tested for WIRED and untroubled by years of being tossed around camera bags; on CFexpress, they remain puzzled as to why the cards are so dang expensive, but if your camera uses them — as most modern, high-end video cameras do — this is about as good a deal as WIRED has seen. The SanDisk is WIRED's favorite speedy portable SSD, lightweight with an IP22-rated enclosure for life on the go and used for weekly backups for almost two years without issue, though Ars Technica and other users have noted extremely high failure rates mainly with the 2- and 4-TB versions, which a SanDisk firmware update seems to fix. Samsung's T7 external SSDs are among the WIRED gear team's favorites — lightning fast, reliable, and less demanding than hard drives — with the T7 Shield adding a rubberized exterior against drops and impacts plus IP65 water and dust resistance, a USB-C cable, palm-sized form, and no need for external power; product reviewer Matt Jancer has used several T7s and T7 Shields for three years without a hiccup, and other capacities are also on sale. Several WIRED staffers love the little printer that outputs perfectly sized, small-but-not-too-small, Polaroid-like images: technically $150, it has been hovering at $129 lately, so $78 is still a solid deal.
