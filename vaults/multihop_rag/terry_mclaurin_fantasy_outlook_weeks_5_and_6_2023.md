# Terry McLaurin's Fantasy Outlook In Weeks 5 And 6 Of 2023

CBS Sports listed Terry McLaurin under "Start Him" in Week 5 of the 2023 season and then under "Sit Him" in Week 6, and the reversal turns on two things changing: the Commanders' targets diffused among three receivers, and the opponent shifted from a depleted Bears secondary to an ascending Falcons one in a low-total game.

The Week 5 case, published 4 October 2023 ahead of a home game with Chicago, holds that Washington may wish to win with a big dose of the run game — Brian Robinson is called a must-start — but that if the Bears put up any offense at all, McLaurin will be in position to put up nice numbers. Over the past three weeks he had led all Commanders wide receivers in targets and all Commanders players in receptions; he is the No. 1 guy, and his big Week 4 line of 8-86-0 plus an offensive fumble recovery for a touchdown would have been even bigger had Sam Howell placed a deep ball better on a play McLaurin was open for. Chicago was expected to play again without top outside cornerback Jaylon Johnson and safety Eddie Jackson — absences that had not led to a bonanza for Denver's receivers, since Russell Wilson spread the ball around — with rookies Tyrique Stevenson and Terell Smith lining up across from McLaurin most; if he remains the target leader he has the best shot at a big game. The pattern the column leans on: every top-target-earning receiver who plays mostly outside, like McLaurin, has found at least 10 PPR points in every game against the Bears, including Rashee Rice in Week 3 (seven targets, 5-59-0), Courtland Sutton in Week 4 (five targets, 3-27-1), Romeo Doubs in Week 1 (five targets, 4-26-2) and Mike Evans in Week 2 (eight targets, 6-171-1), those receivers combining to catch 18 of 25 targets, 72%, against Chicago. The breakdown lists Washington at home versus Chicago, the Commanders favoured by 6, an over/under of 44.5, an opponent ranked 19th versus wide receivers, 15.2 projected points, a 21st receiver ranking, and year-to-date totals of 21 receptions on 26 targets for 212 yards, one touchdown and 12.1 fantasy points per game. The verdict makes him a low-end WR2 worth the nod over George Pickens, Zay Flowers, Drake London and Jahan Dotson.

The Week 6 case, published 12 October 2023 ahead of a game at Atlanta, reverses it. McLaurin posted good numbers in only two games — Washington's 35-33 Week 2 win over Denver and its 34-31 overtime loss to Philadelphia in Week 4 — and this matchup carries an over/under of 42.5, tied for fourth-lowest of the week; adding the ascending secondary Atlanta seems to have and the Commanders' diffusion of targets makes him at best a low-end flex. The breakdown lists the Falcons favoured by 2.5, an opponent ranked 6th versus wide receivers, 12.1 projected points, a 37th receiver ranking, and year-to-date totals of 25 receptions on 31 targets for 261 yards, one touchdown and 11.4 fantasy points per game. In Week 5, even in a favourable game script where Washington trailed by three scores before halftime, McLaurin saw only five targets against Chicago's heavy zone coverage. On film, of the 10 third-down routes he ran, he drew extra coverage on four, got open on two where Howell threw elsewhere, and was a decoy on Curtis Samuel's touchdown; Howell was getting rid of the ball quickly and short as the Bears generated pressure, and by the end McLaurin's body language seemed dispirited. Washington throws to its wideouts on 58.7% of targets, below league average though not as low as Atlanta's 36.4%, and worse, McLaurin has just one more target (31) than Jahan Dotson (30), who has three more than Curtis Samuel (27) — all nearly even after five games, each having led in targets at least once. Atlanta is expected to play a heavy dose of zone, if only because Washington has struggled to be explosive against zone this year, and the Falcons gave a full dose of snaps last week to outside cornerback Jeff Okudah, not flawless but another sizable body against stronger perimeter receivers, so between Okudah and A.J. Terrell there isn't a matchup that's great for McLaurin. His own splits confirm the cap: an impressive 91.3% catch rate against zone but just 9.3 yards per catch on a sub-9.0 average depth of target, with a target share below 20% regardless of coverage. The verdict would rather roll the dice on Drake London, Tyler Lockett or Garrett Wilson.
