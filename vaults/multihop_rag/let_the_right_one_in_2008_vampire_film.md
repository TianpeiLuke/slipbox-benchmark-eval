# Let The Right One In: A Kaleidoscopic Swedish Vampire Friendship

Let the Right One In, directed by Tomas Alfredson and starring Kåre Hedebrant, Lina Leandersson and Per Ragnar, is available on Hulu, Prime Video, Roku Channel, Crackle, Hoopla and Kanopy. A 12-year-old Swedish boy finds a friend in a vampire who looks roughly his age but is actually an old vampire permanently trapped in the body of a young child.

The defining quality Polygon's reviewer identifies is that the film is kaleidoscopic, each viewing revealing something different than the last. He offers his own readings as evidence: the first time he saw it, as a pessimistic college student, he read the central relationship as a warning about the parasitic nature of love; after college, the children's bond reminded him of the impermanence of youth and why growing up is a mixed blessing; this past year he was far more focused on the girl's relationship with her caretaker, an older man who sacrifices everything for her existence.

The film was adapted from John Ajvide Lindqvist's 2004 novel of the same name, which inspired not just this Swedish film but a 2010 American adaptation, a comic-book prequel, and two stage plays. The plays have their own legacy — one was adapted by the National Theater of Scotland and eventually had a run at St. Ann's Warehouse in 2015 — which leads the reviewer to say he is recommending the book just as much as the film.
