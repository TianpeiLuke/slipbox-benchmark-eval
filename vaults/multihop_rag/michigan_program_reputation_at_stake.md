# Michigan's Program Reputation At Stake

Sporting News argued that the sign-stealing affair puts Michigan's reputation, not just its season, on the line — and that the villain role the Wolverines have been cast in only works while they keep winning. Michigan was 10-0 and on track to meet No. 1 Ohio State in another blockbuster in college football's best rivalry, and on the field the Wolverines remained on track for a third straight Big Ten championship and a shot at the College Football Playoff. But, the piece insisted, this is about more than football.

The open questions were institutional. Would more penalties come from the Big Ten and the NCAA over the in-person scouting scandal that had not left the college football consciousness that month? Would the Harbaugh-Petitti battle lead to an unsettling end for Michigan? President Santa Ono and athletic director Warde Manuel had voiced support for Harbaugh, but that support had been about due process rather than any admission of wrongdoing.

The fragility is the point. The affair thrust the Wolverines — a talented team led by J.J. McCarthy, Blake Corum and a defense allowing 7.5 points per game — into a villain role complete with visits from Ric Flair, and that only works when you are winning; one loss and it could all unravel, with the Terps and Buckeyes still on the schedule. Asked whether a national championship would outweigh the stain on the program's reputation, Sporting News concluded that the answer is almost always yes for the fans, but that the program might feel a different long-term result.
