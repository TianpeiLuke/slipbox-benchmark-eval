# The Epoch Times' Marketing Blitz And Subscription Revenue Growth

The Epoch Times' aggressive online and real-life marketing campaigns paid off in subscription revenue: the group reported $76 million in subscription revenue in 2021, compared with nearly $7 million in 2019.

Undeterred by the Facebook advertising ban, the outlet pivoted to video, specifically YouTube, spending millions on internet-infamous ads featuring Roman Balmakov — a former Epoch Times delivery person who now hosts the online show Facts Matter — in which he rails against the mainstream media, communism and the persecution of Trump. It also revisited the tried-and-true strategy the original anti-communism newspaper had been built on, free physical copies: instead of boxes on street corners, this time it printed and distributed unsolicited special editions from California to the Carolinas to the U.K., and in Philadelphia alone in 2020 the company mailed 280,000 free copies of its newspaper "to increase subscription," according to tax documents.

The targeting behind that mailing is described by a former employee of a regional Epoch Times operation, who asked not to be identified because he feared retribution: to send papers to the most likely customers, they bought lists of addresses from data brokers, specifically for conservatives aged 60 and over. Many of the new subscribers are seniors, according to an employee's account shared at a Falun Gong conference. Not all of the new customers were satisfied, however — hundreds of online complaints suggest otherwise, one reading "I've had the terrible misfortune of being subscribed to the Epoch Times without my consent," and another "I want to stop receiving Epoch Times emails. That is all I want."
