# Bank of Israel Interest Rate Policy During the War

Asked what the Bank of Israel should do at its interest rate decision on 23 October 2023, Leo Leiderman argued for holding: "As for cutting the interest rate, it's too early to consider that. The current interest rate is at the right level for the state of the economy, and together with the policy on exchange rates, it contributes to ensuring financial stability."

His first ground is that the inflation effect of the war is indeterminate. It was still too early to tell how the war would affect the rate of inflation in the short and medium term: just as there are new inflationary factors, such as the depreciation of the shekel, there will also be factors working in the opposite direction, given the expected decline in private consumption, tourism and other items.

His second ground extends his fiscal reasoning to monetary policy: as with fiscal policy, a country at war is allowed to deviate temporarily from the set inflation target. He judged that at the moment there was no surge in inflation expectations, and that the investment community is aware of the existence of index-linked assets and the exchange rate that can assist in hedging inflation risk. The warrant tying this to a hold rather than a cut is that financial stability, not inflation, is the binding objective while the shock is unresolved.
