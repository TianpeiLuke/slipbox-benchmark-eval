# Digital Twins as Virtual Sensors in Complex Conditions

The second trend is the use of digital twins as virtual sensors: they approximate data that otherwise cannot be obtained via physical sensors, often because physical sensors are impractical, costly or hazardous to employ. Companies build digital twins to model hardware and calculate data based on other conditions, which allows the collection of data from complex equipment and also enables operators to track equipment performance and predict maintenance and downtimes.

The conditions under which this pays off are illustrated by large motor applications, such as room-sized motors that pump high volumes of gas, oil or other chemicals. Operators need to monitor motor temperatures, especially if the motors are high-powered and repeatedly started, because a motor that is too hot during a restart could suffer serious component damage. Using a direct, physical sensor within a motor is often impractical, so operators must work from an assumed cooling time plus an additional safety buffer — meaning motors can be down longer than necessary, impacting efficiency and revenue.

Siemens developed a prototype virtual sensor based on a digital twin to address this. It offers a simulation of how a physical sensor would operate if it were possible to install it within a motor. Using AR headsets, operators can see a simulation of the motor and its interior with a demonstrator superimposed over it, and can read the motor's temperature.
