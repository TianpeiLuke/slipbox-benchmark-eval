# Technology Innovation Must Solve A Business Problem

A technology innovation must solve a business problem, such as improving operational efficiency, growing existing revenue streams, or generating new ones. The problem space may be customer-facing — how can we deliver a new feature? — or internal-facing — how can we make a process more efficient? — but either way the problem is the paramount concern, and the specific technologies or innovations used to solve it are often less critical. Losing perspective of the business needs makes the activities too academic, or a paid hobby.

The warrant is illustrated with an ordinary domestic analogy: hanging a picture. The hole size, bracket, or tools used are immaterial as long as the picture is on the wall and straight. The specifics of the process and technologies are only important as they pertain to how well they solve the problem, the costs to do so, and the overall end user experience.
