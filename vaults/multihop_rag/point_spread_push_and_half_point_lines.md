# Point Spread Push And Half-Point Lines

A "push" in point spread betting is the situation where the final result of an event exactly matches the set point spread, producing neither a win nor a loss for bettors; the wagers are typically refunded, so neither the bettor nor the sportsbook wins or loses money on that bet. Sporting News illustrates it with an NFL game in which the Dallas Cowboys are favored by 7 points against the Philadelphia Eagles: if the Cowboys win by exactly 7, bettors on either team experience a push and their wagers are refunded, because the Cowboys neither exceeded nor fell short of the 7-point spread.

Sportsbooks often employ half-points in their lines precisely to avoid this outcome, since they do not want to return bets. Had the example spread been Cowboys -7.5, the game could not possibly have ended in a push; half-point lines such as 7.5 or 6.5 remove the exact-match case entirely.

A tie in the literal matchup is a distinct boundary case from a push, and many spread bets still have winners and losers when one occurs. If the Cleveland Browns are -1.5 favorites over the Baltimore Ravens and the NFL matchup ends 21-21 after overtime, the Ravens win that spread bet, because the margin of victory of 0 was narrower than 1.5.
