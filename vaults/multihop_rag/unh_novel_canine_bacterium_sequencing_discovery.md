# UNH Sequencing Discovery Of A Previously Unknown Canine Germ

Researchers at the University of New Hampshire's Veterinary Diagnosis Laboratory and the Hubbard Center for Genome Studies told NBC News they have identified a pathogen that might be what is making pets sick, a development that might help shed light on the illness, which has affected a variety of dog breeds. Through genetic sequencing they say they have discovered a previously unknown germ.

The sequencing covered two sample sets. The first was an initial group of 30 dogs from New Hampshire who were infected last year; the second was an additional 40 dogs from Rhode Island and Massachusetts who got sick this year. The report of the finding was published on 2023-11-21.

The bacterium was discovered after a painstaking search. "After initial sequencing showed there were no known viral, bacterial or fungal pathogens, time consuming and dogged work by graduate student Lawrence Gordon showed that 21 of the initial 30 samples from New Hampshire had some genetic material from one atypical bacterial species," Needle said. In other words, the positive signal came only after the standard panel of known pathogens had come back empty, and it appeared in 21 of the 30 New Hampshire samples.

An important condition attaches to the result: the UNH team is sharing its findings prior to publishing a research article, hoping they will give veterinarians some information as they deal with other respiratory syndrome outbreaks, Needle said. The finding is therefore a pre-publication disclosure, not a peer-reviewed result.
