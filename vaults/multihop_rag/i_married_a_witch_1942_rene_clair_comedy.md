# I Married A Witch (1942): René Clair's Romantic Comedy

I Married a Witch is a 1942 black-and-white romantic comedy directed by René Clair, running 1h 16m, starring Fredric March, Veronica Lake and Robert Benchley. Clair, who made his name in early French silent and sound cinema, spent a few years making movies in the US during World War II, and this is Polygon's exemplary pick from that period. Lake plays a witch who hopes to take revenge on the descendant of the man who imprisoned her by making him fall in love with her.

The plot's mechanism is a curse: when two witches — Jennifer (Lake) and her father Daniel (Cecil Kellaway) — are burned at the stake by Puritans in colonial Salem, they curse the man who denounced them, dooming him and his descendants (all played by Fredric March) to be unhappy in love and always to marry "the wrong woman." Jennifer and Daniel wake 270 years later and she pursues her target, Wallace Wooley, the latest descendant and a leading candidate for governor, whose wedding to the daughter of his top political supporter is the next day. Polygon singles out the costumes by the legendary Edith Head, the charming practical effects (the witches are represented by wisps of smoke before inhabiting bodies), and the gags — including a "popped maize" vendor during the "intermission" of the witches' execution — calling it a breezy 77 minutes of Classic Hollywood delight. It streams on Max and Criterion Channel, or is available for digital rental or purchase on Amazon and Apple TV.
