# Speculation About Link's Gender Around Breath of the Wild

Players have been looking for gender in this generation's iteration of Link since before Breath of the Wild released, Polygon reported on 2023-10-19: donning his tunic's now iconic champion blue, Link's androgyny in the game's very first teaser trailer back in 2014 stirred mainstream audiences to scrutinize the player character's presumed gender, and as soon as that came into question so did the character's entire identity — the idea of a female Hero of Time simmered, and if the character shown was not Link, then maybe it was a playable Zelda.

Nintendo eventually addressed the speculation. Zelda producer Eiji Aonuma said nothing so revolutionary as a woman was in the works, explaining that changing Link's gender would "mess with the balance of the Triforce," while focusing instead on Zelda as a playable female character would leave Link with nothing to do: "If we have princess Zelda as the main character who fights," Aonuma asked, "then what is Link going to do?"

The gender trouble continued closer to release, as a series of leaked screenshots revealed Link's encounter with the character Vilia outside of Gerudo Town, the all-female city in Hyrule's desert. The screenshots suggested a transphobic punchline about crossdressing, and the game delivered on the joke, with the quest being a necessary step to completing the game's true ending. But when the game was released weeks later, players went on to find queer expression and even joy in the wilds of Hyrule: a crossdressing Link was genuinely embraced among artists, cosplayers, and authors of fan fiction, and queer expressions were captured in poetry, art, and prose. Many players celebrated Breath of the Wild for the space it made to find queer expression in Hyrule, its story providing just enough possibility space for players to explore in ways that did not conform to its developers' intent.
