# Michigan Drops Its Suit And Accepts The Three-Game Ban

Michigan and Jim Harbaugh dropped their litigation against the Big Ten on Thursday and accepted the three-game suspension, so Harbaugh was not on Michigan's sideline that week against Maryland or the following week against Ohio State to end the regular season. The joint resolution closed both the lawsuit and the conference's investigation.

The statement set out the terms: "This morning, the University, Coach Harbaugh, and the Big Ten resolved their pending litigation. The Conference agreed to close its investigation, and the University and Coach Harbaugh agreed to accept the three-game suspension. Coach Harbaugh, with the University's support, decided to accept this sanction to return the focus to our student-athletes and their performance on the field. The Conference has confirmed that it is not aware of any information suggesting Coach Harbaugh's involvement in the allegations. The University continues to cooperate fully with the NCAA's investigation." It continued: "The Big Ten Conference's commitment to student-athletes, sportsmanship and the commissioner's duty to protect the integrity of competition will never waver. Today's decision by the University of Michigan to withdraw its legal challenge against the conference's November 10th notice of disciplinary action is indicative of the high standards and values that the conference and the university seek to uphold, the University of Michigan is a valued member of the Big Ten Conference and the conference will continue to work cooperatively with the university and the NCAA during this process."

Before the withdrawal, Harbaugh held his usual weekly news conference on Monday but mostly avoided the only topic on anyone's mind. He initially told reporters he would speak at the scheduled Friday hearing, then backtracked and sounded uncommitted, pending legal advice. Nicole Auerbach reported on Nov. 13, 2023 that Harbaugh said he was looking for "due process," mentioning a civics class he took 40 years ago — "You're innocent until proven guilty. I'd like that opportunity." Austin Meek reported the same day that Harbaugh clarified he was not sure whether he would testify Friday: "That's not my dance floor. I've watched a lot of shows. I've watched Judge Judy a lot."
