# Video Game Release

## Definition

A video game release is the point at which a title becomes available to the public to buy or play, and the terms attached to that moment — which platforms it launches on, whether it is exclusive or day-one on a subscription service, and how its price behaves over time — are as much a part of "release" as the launch date itself.

## Context

The corpus discusses release terms most directly around the Microsoft-Activision Blizzard deal: Bethesda's Starfield launched in September 2023 exclusively on PC and Xbox and was playable on Xbox Game Pass from day one, the pattern Microsoft is expected to repeat with future Call of Duty titles once Activision Blizzard's catalog moves to Game Pass. This is presented as a departure from the prior arrangement, in which Sony typically held early access to Call of Duty ahead of general release, including early access to the game, testing, and bonus features. Separately, Black Friday 2023 shopping coverage shows how release timing shapes a game's retail position after launch: a newly released PS5 title, Marvel's Spider-Man 2, was added to the $500 PS5 bundle for no extra cost without any qualification about the deal's value, while Super Smash Bros. Ultimate, released back in 2018 and still retailing for $50-$60 on its own, was also added free to a $350 Nintendo Switch OLED bundle but drew the caveat that its age made it "hard to call...an amazing deal."

## Key Characteristics

- **Day-one platform and service availability** — a release can be tied to specific hardware (Starfield: PC and Xbox only) and can be simultaneously playable on a subscription service like Xbox Game Pass "on the first day they release," rather than requiring a separate purchase.
- **Exclusivity windows precede or follow release** — before the Microsoft-Activision deal, Sony's arrangement with Activision gave it early access to Call of Duty ahead of the general public, plus early testing and bonus features; UK regulators required a 15-year carve-out (via Ubisoft's cloud-streaming rights) to prevent Activision Blizzard titles from becoming Xbox-exclusive after release.
- **Release age shapes how a free pack-in game is framed** — a title's distance from its release date affects how retailers' holiday bundles are perceived: the newly released, critically acclaimed Spider-Man 2 was added free to the full-price $500 PS5 bundle with no caveat, while the 2018-released Smash Bros. Ultimate — still worth $50-$60 on its own — was called out as making its $350 Switch OLED bundle "hard to call...an amazing deal" precisely because the pack-in game was five years old.
- **Distribution model (buy vs. access) is a release-time choice** — Microsoft's habit of putting blockbuster titles on Game Pass at release leaves buyers choosing between purchasing the game outright or paying a monthly fee to access it from day one.
