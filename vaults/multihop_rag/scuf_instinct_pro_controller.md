# Scuf Instinct Pro Controller

The Scuf Instinct Pro occupies a weird spot: it is the only wireless option from a third party, but by far the most expensive Xbox controller around, starting at $199.99. The one tested clocked in at $263.91 with a special faceplate and added color accents — more than the Xbox Series S occasionally sells for, which is an entire console. Specifications: Xbox wireless, Bluetooth and wired connectivity; USB-C connector; four mappable rear buttons; no software customization; AA batteries or add-on rechargeable power.

If you are okay spending that kind of money you are in for a treat, because the Instinct Pro is arguably better than Microsoft's Elite in some very specific ways — primarily its comfort and its implementation of rear buttons and hair triggers. Its rear buttons are unique and the reviewer actually prefers them: instead of paddles there are four rocker-like buttons built into the controller's body that you push toward or away from your hand with a middle finger. It still requires training your brain a bit, but he found them more intuitive.

The other big feature is its hair triggers, which have the quickest response of all the controllers tested. Flick a switch on the back and the left and right triggers feel like clicking a mouse — incredibly satisfying. The hair triggers, rear buttons, and excellent textured grip material on the underside make the controller a joy to use, and the magnetic faceplate and sticks are easy to pop off and swap out.

The caveats are cost and omissions. The Instinct Pro feels like Scuf essentially hot-rodded a stock Xbox controller, but like any boutique item it comes with a massive cost; it still uses AA batteries and lacks software customization. As much as the reviewer enjoys using it, he would only advise getting one if you are super passionate about its very specific qualities, or you absolutely love the colorful accents and magnetically swappable faceplates on Scuf's configurator.
