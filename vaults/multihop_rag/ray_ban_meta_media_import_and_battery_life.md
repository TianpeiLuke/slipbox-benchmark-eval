# Ray-Ban Meta: Media Import And Battery Life

Importing photos and videos from the Ray-Ban Meta glasses to a phone is easy, even on the go: the glasses have their own local Wi-Fi network, and your phone just needs to connect to it with the Meta View app open. It can be a little slow with a lot of footage — The Verge's reviewer imported 143 videos and photos in about five minutes, after a few tries to establish a stable connection — and much faster, maybe two or three minutes, for 15 or fewer items. One potential con is that videos max out at one minute; Meta says that is to optimize storage and importing, which the reviewer called a fair tradeoff, noting the glasses are clearly meant for social content since all photos and videos are vertical with no landscape option, and you generally do not watch more than a minute-long clip on Reels, TikTok, or YouTube Shorts anyway.

Battery life is the constraint for heavy users. Light use gets five, maybe six hours on a single charge; taking a ton of video and photos brings that down to three or four hours. Using the glasses as headphones on a 70-minute commute drained the battery by about 18 to 20 percent. The reviewer doubted this would trouble casual users, since the glasses are still a functional pair of glasses when the battery is dead, but said power users who want to get through a whole day of meetings using them as headphone replacements may find it annoying.

Several factors mitigate this. The charging case is a lot slimmer than the previous version and easy to stash in a fanny pack, purse, or backpack; it charges via USB-C and has a new indicator LED button that turns orange while charging and green when everything is full — working for the case itself when empty and for the glasses when they are inside. Charging is quick: she went from 15 percent after three hours of heavy use to 100 percent in less than an hour. Over a whole week, aside from the initial charge, she never had to plug in the case — though you do end up charging the frames multiple times a day, because where else would you put them when they are not in use.
