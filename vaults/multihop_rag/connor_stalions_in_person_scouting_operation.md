# Connor Stalions And The In-Person Scouting Operation

Connor Stalions was the Michigan football analyst at the center of the NCAA's sign-stealing investigation, accused of buying tickets to scout future opponents in person — a practice prohibited by NCAA rules. According to ESPN.com, Stalions allegedly purchased tickets to more than 35 games involving Big Ten and potential College Football Playoff opponents. Michigan suspended him when news of his alleged involvement first broke, and he resigned on Nov. 3, 2023; the university confirmed the resignation in a statement saying only that "Connor Stalions resigned his position with Michigan Athletics this afternoon. We are unable to comment further regarding this personnel matter."

A second strand of the investigation concerns whether Stalions appeared in Chippewas gear on Central Michigan's sideline during the team's opener against Michigan State on Sept. 1. Photos surfaced of a person resembling Stalions on that sideline, and Central Michigan had not confirmed who the person was, adding another layer to a story that supplied endless meme and conspiracy material on social media. Through his lawyer, Stalions said he hoped to avoid creating more distractions for Michigan and denied that any Wolverines staffer "told anyone to break any rules."

Sporting News framed Stalions' eventual place in the story as still undetermined: he might be the fall guy who saved Michigan from further wrath from the Big Ten and the NCAA, or the reason the Wolverines' national championship season unraveled. Either way, the outlet judged that he would be one of those controversial names never forgotten, in the company of Harvey Updyke and Nevin Shapiro.
