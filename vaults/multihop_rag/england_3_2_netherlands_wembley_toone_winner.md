# England 3-2 Netherlands At Wembley: Toone's Winner

Ella Toone's injury-time goal at 9.34 p.m. on a briskly cold Wembley night earned England three points against the Netherlands and kept their faintly glowing Olympic dream alive.

Throughout the Nations League campaign this had been an England team fighting its own sense of entropy, still giving everything and still trying the same plans and processes in the hope that eventually something would click — and it finally did. The goal breathed life into a team and a cycle, turning a moment that had felt like it was drawing to its natural conclusion into a rousing encore for a cast preparing for its final curtain; new challenges would come soon enough with European Championship qualifiers in the spring, but for now this iteration of the Lionesses still had tricks up its sleeve, talent and creativity to burn, and a fire that could scorch any team on the planet. They rallied spectacularly to salvage a match that looked cooked after 35 minutes, running on fumes and adrenaline, sustained by the sizzle of the Wembley crowd, the cold air on their skin and the flickering candle of qualification for Paris — an energy not really glimpsed since they reassembled that autumn, barely a month on from the trauma of Sydney, still a little dazed and concussed. Beth Mead came on for Chloe Kelly at half-time, Georgia Stanway moved further forward and made herself a menace in the penalty area, and the old buddies Toone and Alessia Russo teamed up off the bench; the Dutch retreated further and further into Wembley's wide open spaces, giving Lauren James the time and territory to dictate. James had a hand in all three goals, and as Toone ghosted in at the far post to provide the final flourish, Wembley screeched out one last call to arms.
