# Amazon's Industrial Innovation Fund And Agility

The Industrial Innovation Fund, from which Agility was announced around April as one of the first recipients, is described by Tye Brady as an exploration vehicle rather than a procurement pipeline: "The Innovation Fund is really about exploring what's possible out there. It's about understanding practical real-world examples as well." Its defining boundary is that funding does not imply deployment — "I'm not necessary saying that if we fund something, it's going to be inside our processes. It's very early stages" — and Amazon also has other companies in the fund it learns from, with the option to make a larger investment if it wants.

Brady's stated interest in Agility is in walking robots and the ability to move on different terrains, and equally in what works and frankly what doesn't work about it: "The humanoid form is really interesting. I don't know if it's a good thing or a bad thing. We're experimentalists at heart. We're gonna figure that out. We're going to do a pilot and see how that works out." Asked what "very early stage" means, he defined it as learning about function and utility — what's possible, what's hype, what's reality, whether this would scale — and gave the scale constraint that makes the bar high: it can't work 99% of the time, because a 1% defect rate is a huge number inside any of Amazon's buildings. On whether Amazon buys or leases the test robots, he said there's no one-size-fits-all and it is done case-by-case; Amazon declined to comment further on the arrangement.
