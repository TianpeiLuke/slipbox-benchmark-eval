# Novo Nordisk's Response To The GLP-1 Gastroparesis Findings

Novo Nordisk's rebuttal attacks two premises at once: that the gastrointestinal risks are unlabeled and unknown, and that the study's window supports conclusions about its current weight-management products.

In a statement at the time, a spokesperson for Novo Nordisk told FOX Business that gastrointestinal events "are well-known side effects of the GLP-1 class" and "are mild to moderate in severity and of short duration." The company provided a further statement to Fox News Digital on Friday, Oct. 6.

On labeling, the company said: "At Novo Nordisk, patient safety is a top priority. We work closely with the U.S. Food and Drug Administration to continuously monitor the safety profile of our medicines. The FDA-approved product labeling for Novo Nordisk's GLP-1RA medicines indicated for use in weight management (Saxenda and Wegovy) includes information about their potential side effects, including pancreatitis, acute gallbladder disease, ileus and delayed gastric emptying." It went on: "Similar information is included in the product labeling for our GLP-1RA medicines indicated for the treatment of type 2 diabetes (Ozempic, Rybelsus and Victoza)." The company added that it "stands behind the safety and efficacy of all of our GLP-1RA medicines when used consistent with the product labeling and approved indications."

On the study itself, Novo Nordisk said, "With respect to the study, as the authors acknowledge, the study has limitations, including potential confounding by indication and by other factors." The company added a timing objection: "It is also important to note that the study analyzed data collected during the period between 2006 and 2020. During this time, Wegovy was not on the market; Saxenda was first approved in December 2014. In addition, Victoza was FDA-approved in January 2010 and Ozempic was FDA-approved in December 2017."
