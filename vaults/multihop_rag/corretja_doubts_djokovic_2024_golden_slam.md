# Corretja Doubts A Djokovic Golden Slam In 2024

Alex Corretja questioned Novak Djokovic's durability ahead of a Golden Slam attempt in 2024: the claim is that a Golden Slam would be "a little bit surprising," and the warrant is that even with an improving game, ageing slows recovery while a hungry pack of younger rivals waits for a slip up.

The scope of the task is what makes the doubt concrete. The Paris Olympics adds an extra high-profile tournament to the four majors in 2024, so the Golden Slam requires winning all five — and the report notes it appears a big leap for Djokovic, considering he is yet to claim a Grand Slam of all four majors in one year, having pulled up just short three times. Corretja's stated reasoning: "Because even if he improves his game so much, he's getting older. It's normal that he is getting older and the recovery is not the same." He is careful to bound the prediction: "I'm sure that he's going to keep on winning Slams, but it's going to be tougher and tougher and he knows that."

The supporting evidence is the 2023 record on both sides. Djokovic ended 2023 as world No. 1 for an eighth time, yet faced several defeats by younger rivals, including Lorenzo Musetti at the Monte-Carlo Masters, Holger Rune at the Rome Masters, Carlos Alcaraz in the Wimbledon final and Jannik Sinner in both the ATP Finals group stage and the Davis Cup. Corretja read the last of those as useful: "In a way, I feel the match that he lost against [Jannik Sinner] in the Davis Cup might be good for him. It's a wake-up call to say, 'Okay, I'm doing unbelievably, but I can't relax for one second because some of the guys behind me are going to push me so much. They're very dangerous and they know they can beat me now.'" The report adds that there are doubts over the fitness of Rafael Nadal, who was set to return in Brisbane ahead of the Australian Open.
