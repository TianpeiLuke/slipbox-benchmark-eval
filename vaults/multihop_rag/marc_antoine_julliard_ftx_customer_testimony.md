# Marc-Antoine Julliard's Testimony As FTX's First Witness-Victim

The prosecution's first witness at Sam Bankman-Fried's trial was not a blockbuster name or a former lieutenant-turned-cooperator like Caroline Ellison but a victim: Marc-Antoine Julliard, a Paris-born cocoa trader who lives in London and whose money got stuck on FTX. Julliard testified that on Nov. 8, 2022, in the crypto exchange's final few days, he tried to pull out almost $100,000 in cash and crypto, and that he was never able to. The withdrawal screenshots shown in court were of attempts to move $20,000 USD and about 4 Bitcoin, worth about $20,000 at the time — roughly $100,000 in total, inaccessible.

Julliard explained why he had chosen FTX in 2021 when he decided to invest in crypto, trading currencies like Bitcoin and Dogecoin there. He trusted FTX because Bankman-Fried came across as a leading figure of the industry; because the sheer volume of users mattered to him, and FTX was at the time among the top three biggest exchanges; and because major VC firms had invested. "They don't commit hundreds of millions without doing due diligence, checking the books, the accountancy of the firm, going through several compliance process[es], so that was a vote of confidence for me," he said. He also noted FTX's glossy ads — featuring Gisele Bündchen, for instance — which suggested a very high budget, and reasoned that it wouldn't make sense to spend that much money unless FTX had very strong financials. He opened an account, transferred in both regular money and cryptocurrency, and used the exchange to execute his plan of buying Bitcoin to sell back in five to ten years at higher prices.

In November 2022 things went bad. Julliard followed Bankman-Fried on Twitter and read aloud in court the "FTX is fine. Assets are fine" tweets, along with "FTX has enough to cover all client holdings. We don't invest client assets" and a few others, which gave him the impression that his money was there and that any problem might be technical — anti-spam measures — or regulatory. By the time he tried to get his money out on November 8th, it was too late. Cross-examining a customer whose money is gone is described as a thankless task, but Mark Cohen tried anyway, noting that Julliard was a licensed commodities broker who was trading in crypto because he didn't have to disclose it, that he knew crypto was new and risky, and that he didn't review the terms of service agreement he had assented to when making his FTX account. The next witness called was Bankman-Fried's former college and FTX roommate, Adam Yedidia.
