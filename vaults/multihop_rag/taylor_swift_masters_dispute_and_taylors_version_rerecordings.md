# The Masters Dispute And The "Taylor's Version" Re-Recordings

When music mogul Scooter Braun acquired Taylor Swift's longtime label, Big Machine Records, in 2019, he also gained the rights to the master recordings of Swift's first six studio albums; in November 2020 Braun sold those masters to an investment fund in a deal believed to be worth over $300 million. Swift responded by re-recording her catalogue, and by the end of 2023 had released four "Taylor's Version" albums: Fearless (Taylor's Version) in April 2021, Red (Taylor's Version) in November 2021, Speak Now (Taylor's Version) in July 2023, and 1989 (Taylor's Version) in October 2023.

The Independent suggests it was that decision to re-record the catalogue that catapulted Swift into her 2023 success. Asked by Time about the success of her tour, Swift traced it back to two ordeals: "It's not lost on me that the two great catalysts for this happening were two horrendous things that happened to me. The first was getting cancelled within an inch of my life and sanity. The second was having my life's work taken away from me by someone who hates me."
