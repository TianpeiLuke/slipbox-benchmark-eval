# M (1931)

Fritz Lang's M is one of the most chilling crime dramas ever put to film, built on what Polygon's Tasha Robinson calls an unbeatable premise: when a child murderer starts haunting the streets of Berlin and the police cannot find him, the local crime bosses join forces to bring him down. It streams on Max and The Criterion Channel, free with ads on Plex, free with a library card on Kanopy, and is rentable on Amazon, Vudu, and Apple.

Robinson describes it as something like a proto version of The Wire, jumping back and forth from the police perspective to the criminals' side of things and humanizing people on both sides — a procedural with several unusual turns, built around a particularly memorable Peter Lorre performance. It is also absolutely packed with breathtaking shots and exceptional use of light and dark, and she rates the final manhunt sequence a classic all-timer.
