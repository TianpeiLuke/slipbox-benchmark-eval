# MXene Ink, Films and Deployment Formats

To make freshly prepared MXene powder workable, scientists add it to water to create an ink, says Anupma Thakur, a materials scientist at Purdue University in West Lafayette, Indiana; as ink, MXenes can simply be painted onto desired surfaces such as fabric, plastic or glass, and if a thick enough coat of MXene paint is peeled carefully off a surface it can even become a free-standing sheet.

Each fleck of the dark-coloured powder contains many MXene sheets, which under a microscope look like loose sheets of paper. When toughened with polymers, MXene films can durably separate CO2 from exhaust gas, and for carbon capture Per Persson imagines fitting MXene filters atop chimneys where the exhaust from burned materials is rich in CO2. To absorb CO2 from the air elsewhere, Thakur suggests putting stacks of MXene screens out in the open, though this method would be less efficient at capturing CO2 since average levels of CO2 in the air far from pollution sources are quite low. Mihri Ozkan, an engineer at the University of California, Riverside, is lead author of the new study showing that certain MXenes can act like carbon-dioxide sponges.
