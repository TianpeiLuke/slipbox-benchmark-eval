# Live-Service Games' Dependence on Content Creators

At this point live-service games need content creators like Twitch streamers to stay solvent, Polygon reported on 2023-10-19: according to the anonymous developer speaking to the outlet, streamers help old games transform into new and visible moments, and developers need live-service games to stay in the conversation because the base experience can worsen without a persistent player base.

The dependency is not only commercial. It is "not even from a cost perspective," the developer says, though obviously more players make profits more viable. "But [if] we don't have 20,000 players, then matchmaking times are terrible. Or connections will be pretty bad because we can't mobilize players together in a single match. There's all these cascading effects."

The relation runs both ways, and taxes the other side too. While a Twitch streamer's livelihood depends on having a steady stream of content, the system grinds them down: Tenney's goodbye to his fans was crushing because the path that led him there was fueled by lost youth and a dream. Over time the promise of a dream job — get paid to play games — became corporatized, and contracts turned things ugly; Tenney said he started feeling trapped and did not like how much his decisions started being motivated by money, and he is hardly alone now that Twitch has "grown up." As he put it, "There [are] these enormous benefits for some people, but there's also just, like, a lot of it just really grinds you down on both sides of the table," referring to game developers and Twitch streamers.
