# Jordan Addison As A Week 5 2023 Sit Against Kansas City

CBS Sports listed Jordan Addison under "Sit Him" for Week 5 of the 2023 season against Kansas City: the claim is that rolling with Addison remains a risk even though the Vikings are almost guaranteed to throw a ton, and the warrant is that he is not the definitive No. 2 target and the Chiefs are specifically strong against receivers of his type.

The volume side of the argument actually favours Addison, and the column grants it: you can bank on the Vikings reversing course against Kansas City, since two of the past three offenses to face the Chiefs wound up throwing on at least 71% of their snaps — the exception being Chicago, so badly blown out by halftime that they ran on 55% of second-half snaps just to get out of Arrowhead. What undercuts it is his role. Week 4 was a total disaster: in a game script that actually gave Minnesota a chance to run the ball, Kirk Cousins barely threw and Addison went catchless on one target, with a major reduction in snaps to 58%, against a season-low of 56% in Week 1. With the Vikings able to run, the offense used heavier formations — backup tight end Josh Oliver played about as many snaps as in Week 1 and third tight end Johnny Mundt saw a season-high 17% of snaps — and Week 4 was the first time all year Minnesota didn't throw on at least 69% of its snaps. The week also affirmed that Addison may actually be the No. 3 receiver behind Justin Jefferson and K.J. Osborn, though even with the donut he holds a five-catch lead on Osborn for the season with 68 more yards.

The opponent evidence closes it. The Chiefs have been stout against receivers all season, ranking top-five in catch rate allowed (53.4%) and yards after catch per reception allowed (2.96) and sixth-best in yards per catch given up (11.1); even excluding the Bears game they have yielded a 56.8% catch rate, 2.93 yards after catch per reception and a 10.9 receiving average to the Lions, Jaguars and Jets. Drilling further, they are great against outside receivers like Addison in catch rate (45.8%) and yards after catch per reception (2.86), but have allowed 15.0 yards per catch specifically to outside receivers — the only saving grace Addison has. The breakdown lists Minnesota at home versus Kansas City, the Chiefs favoured by 4, an over/under of 52.5, an opponent ranked 10th versus wide receivers, 9.8 projected points, a 34th receiver ranking, and year-to-date totals of 13 receptions on 20 targets for 185 yards, two touchdowns and 10.9 fantasy points per game. The verdict makes him a flex at best, to be sat for Romeo Doubs, Gabe Davis and Christian Watson.
