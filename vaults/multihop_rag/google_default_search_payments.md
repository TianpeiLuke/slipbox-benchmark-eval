# Google Paid $26.3 Billion In 2021 For Default Search Placement

Google paid $26.3 billion in 2021 to multiple browsers, phones and platforms — from companies including Apple, Samsung and Mozilla — to be everyone's default search engine. The figure was revealed by Google's search head Prabhakar Raghavan when he testified in court on October 28, according to The Verge.

About $18 billion of that total went directly to Apple, according to a New York Times report published earlier in October 2023, and Google has had its deal with Apple in place since 2003 — so the payment is the current year of a two-decade arrangement, not a one-off. Questioned on the amount of money Google spends to get first pick of search engines, Pichai said the decision was made with the consumer in mind: Google pays big bucks to be everywhere so that it can take in all the data and be the best search engine across different companies' devices, he said, according to The Verge.

The trial also produced evidence that Google understood the value of defaults very early on. U.S. Justice Department lawyer Meagan Bellshaw showed Pichai a 2007 email from a Google product strategy meeting containing data showing that when people changed their browser homepage to Google, they did 15% more Google searches, and that when they switched away, their Google searches dropped 27%. The email summarizing that meeting, sent to Pichai as well as other Google leaders, read: "Nitin argues that focusing on homepage market share is one of the most effective things we can do to make gains in search market share," according to The Verge.
