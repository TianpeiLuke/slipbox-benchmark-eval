# US Existing-Home Sales Fell To A 13-Year Low In September 2023

Existing-home sales dropped a stunning 15% in September 2023 on a year-over-year basis, to a seasonally adjusted annual rate of 3.96 million transactions, according to the National Association of Realtors — the lowest figure in 13 years, since 2010, when the world economy and particularly the U.S. housing market were struggling to pull out of the Great Financial Crisis. After a long, hot summer that saw mortgage rates creep ever higher, October brought an early winter for the housing market.

The contributing factors named for the continuous decline in home transactions are surging mortgage rates, which hit 8% in the week of 2023-10-20, a record in the 21st century; low inventory levels; and home prices that refuse to stop rising. In other words, there aren't enough homes to buy, money isn't cheap anymore, and the ones for sale are too rich for most homebuyers' blood. Existing-home sales prices topped $306,000, a 5% increase since the year began, according to the Case-Shiller U.S. National Home Price Index. With transactions at their lowest level in 13 years coupled with century-high mortgage rates of 8%, many real estate experts and economists aren't hopeful that affordability constraints will let up soon.
