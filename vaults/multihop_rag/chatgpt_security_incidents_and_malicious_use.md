# ChatGPT Security Incidents And Malicious Use

ChatGPT has been both a target and a tool of attack: it leaked training data under a researcher probe, went down under a DDoS attack, and has been used by cybercriminals to write phishing emails and to lure victims into installing malware.

On data leakage, a test led by researchers at Google DeepMind found there is a significant amount of privately identifiable information in OpenAI's LLMs. The test involved asking ChatGPT to repeat the word "poem" forever, among other words, which over time led the chatbot to churn out private information such as email addresses and phone numbers. Following that experiment, OpenAI flagged asking ChatGPT to repeat specific words "forever" as a violation of its terms of service. On availability, OpenAI confirmed that a DDoS attack was behind outages affecting ChatGPT and its developer tools; ChatGPT experienced sporadic outages for about 24 hours, leaving users unable to log into or use the service.

On offensive use, a report by SlashNext found a 1,265% increase in malicious phishing emails since Q4 of 2022, alleging that AI tools like ChatGPT are being prominently used by cybercriminals to write compelling and sophisticated phishing emails. Meta said in a report on May 3 that malware posing as ChatGPT was on the rise across its platforms, and that since March 2023 its security teams had uncovered 10 malware families using ChatGPT and similar themes to deliver malicious software to users' devices. "In one case, we've seen threat actors create malicious browser extensions available in official web stores that claim to offer ChatGPT-based tools," said Meta security engineers Duc H. Nguyen and Ryan Victory in a blog post. "They would then promote these malicious extensions on social media and through sponsored search results to trick people into downloading malware."
