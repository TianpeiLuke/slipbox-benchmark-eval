# Works Created Principally Through AI Should Not Be Copyrightable

The NMPA's first policy demand is that works created principally through AI should not be copyrightable: simply giving a prompt to an AI music generator — "compose a power ballad about falling in love" — should not be enough to create a copyrightable work.

The grounds are incentive-based: "Policies surrounding copyrightability of AI-generated content will either serve to incentivize continued investment and effort into human creativity or to disincentivize it," the submission states. The warrant is stated as a principle about what the law must never allow: "The law should never be such that human creators stand to gain more from repeatedly clicking a button to generate massive amounts of AI-produced materials than from putting their hearts, souls, experiences, skills, talents, and emotions into expressive works of art." The remedy the NMPA proposes is that the law treat a musical work created entirely by AI the same way it treats works in the public domain — not copyrightable, and free for anyone to use as they see fit, whether for commercial or other purposes.
