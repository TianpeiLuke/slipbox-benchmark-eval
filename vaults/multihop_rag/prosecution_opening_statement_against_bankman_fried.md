# The Prosecution's Opening Statement Against Bankman-Fried

Thane Rehn, a prosecutor for the government, opened the case against Sam Bankman-Fried with the line "One year ago, it looked like the defendant was on the top of the world," and then argued that this appearance was false: "all of that, all of it, was built on lies," he declared to the jury, adding "Behind the curtains, he was not what he appeared to be." Rehn described a defendant who, as former CEO of FTX, oversaw a supposedly thriving crypto exchange, jetted between international locales, hobnobbed with celebrities like Tom Brady and Larry David, and repeatedly emphasized to customers that their money was safe and secure.

What followed was a roughly 30-minute story that repeatedly emphasized how Bankman-Fried allegedly stole customer funds to facilitate his jet-setting lifestyle, donate millions to political candidates, and finance risky bets. Rehn also directly contested the media framing of the defendant: he was not a crypto "boy" genius, as so many in the media — Fortune included — have written, but a "man" who "stole billions of dollars from thousands of victims." Rehn closed the characterization with a promise to the jury: "You will see the full picture."
