# Twitch Partner Plus Program

Partner Plus reached its current form through a fixed sequence of steps, each one a reaction to the step before it, as Twitch's Chief Monetization Officer Mike Minton described to TechCrunch on 2023-10-26. First, two years earlier, Twitch withdrew the discretionary deals: "we said we're no longer gonna do these deals that weren't objective in terms of eligibility criteria. We ended that program." Second, having removed them, Twitch had to replace the aspiration they created — the withdrawal "affected a relatively small number of creators, but what it really impacted was the community going, 'I don't have anything to work for.' And that led to us recently announcing Partner Plus." Third, Twitch answered the objection that the bar was too high: "There was some feedback saying, 'Hey, we're going to need a lot of subs,' and we reacted to it by making it eligible by sub points so that your higher price point subscriptions cost more, and that was actually received quite positively."

The precondition for the whole sequence is objective eligibility criteria — that is the property Twitch said the discontinued deals lacked, and it is what the numeric threshold and then the points system supply. The threshold is explicitly not fixed: "The simple answer is now, 350 was the criteria we established, right? And that number is arbitrary and could easily come down."

The program is scoped, and Minton is explicit about where it is not aimed: "our target and our goal in this program still remains the same. We're looking to benefit the creators, where livestreaming is a meaningful part of what they're doing as a creator. That allows us to focus on those streamers that are really at a point where it will matter most for them in terms of keeping them as a livestreaming creator." Minton also framed the current state as provisional: "Both Dan and I have said in different forms, that this is the beginning. It's not the end and we're going to continue to work to provide access to higher rev shares as we can over time, to more streamers."

The surrounding conditions matter to how the sequence played out. CEO Dan Clancy, who took over earlier that year, has prioritized engaging with Twitch streamers and involving their perspectives in policy changes going forward; the company's receptiveness to community feedback has marginally lessened animosity from its streamers, and community sentiment toward Twitch at that year's TwitchCon in Las Vegas was significantly more positive than it was the year before. New features and policy changes announced during the convention's keynote address — including granting streamers more control over ad breaks and allowing simulcasting to other livestreaming platforms — also appeased the Twitch community.
