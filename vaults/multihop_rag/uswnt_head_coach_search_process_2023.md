# The USWNT Head Coach Search Process In 2023

U.S. Soccer's 2023 head coach search ran through multiple rounds of evaluation, with the list of candidates becoming smaller each time, as described by The New York Times on 15 November 2023.

The stages are given in order. The first pass was driven purely by data, which was then whittled down to a double-digit list Matt Crocker was considering as of September, and then a final shortlist, which also included Tony Gustavsson, head coach of Australia; multiple sources confirmed both Hayes and Gustavsson flew to the U.S. for interviews. Initial contact with Hayes was made a couple of months before the announcement, early in the search, with all three top-level U.S. Soccer executives — Crocker, president Cindy Parlow Cone and CEO J.T. Batson — involved in those talks. A U.S. Soccer statement describes the interview process as involving "psychometrics and abstract reasoning tests, in-depth discussions of strategy, coaching philosophy and the current player pool, as well as evaluation on the reactions to pressure, culture-building and interactions with players and staff"; USMNT coach Gregg Berhalter went through a similar process, including an abstract reasoning test, when he was re-hired by Crocker earlier in 2023. Crocker said in September that the final interviews would include lengthy technical and tactical assessments as well as questions to determine cultural fit.

Two further details are recorded. The federation had also checked in on the availability of Sarina Wiegman, according to one source briefed on the situation, despite clear messaging from both the English FA and Wiegman herself in August: "I'm staying out of it. I've heard it (from the press officer) but no, I'm with England and I'm really happy with England, and I have a contract until 2025," Wiegman said, and a representative from her camp declined to comment. And on timing: part of the surprise around the hire and the six-month runway is down to U.S. Soccer's own messaging, since Crocker had said in a September meeting with U.S. reporters that he hoped to have a new head coach in place by December; he and the federation stayed fairly consistent on their desired start date since the role opened in August, but that became one of the major concessions made in selecting Hayes.
