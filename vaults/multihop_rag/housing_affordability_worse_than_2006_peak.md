# Housing Affordability Is Worse Now Than At The 2006 Peak

Roger Ashworth, a managing director at Goldman Sachs, argued in a credit strategy research paper released in mid-October 2023 that although some economic factors are stronger today than during the Global Financial Crisis, housing affordability is undeniably worse: "While housing and more generally consumer fundamentals are in a much stronger position today, affordability for the incremental buyer is worse than it was at the peak in 2006 before the crash." The claim is deliberately narrow — it is about the incremental buyer's affordability, not about overall fundamentals, which he grants are stronger.

The proximate causes cited are the same constraints that have held all year. "As has been the case throughout this year, limited inventory and low housing affordability continue to hamper home sales," NAR chief economist Lawrence Yun said in a statement, adding a policy conclusion: "The Federal Reserve simply cannot keep raising interest rates in light of softening inflation and weakening job gains." The Fed had raised rates several times in 2023, with Chair Jerome Powell arming his "higher for longer" stance.
