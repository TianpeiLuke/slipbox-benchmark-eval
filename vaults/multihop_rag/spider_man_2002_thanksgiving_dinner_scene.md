# The 2002 Spider-Man Thanksgiving Dinner Scene

The Thanksgiving scene in the 2002 Spider-Man film descends rapidly into chaos: Norman Osborn ominously sticks his fingers into Aunt May's sweet-potato casserole, figures out Spider-Man's secret identity, and delivers a grossly sexist diatribe to his son Harry, leaving Harry and Mary Jane at odds with each other, and Aunt May and Peter clearly wind up with a ton of leftovers after everyone else storms out.

Polygon's reading focuses on the opening of the scene rather than its collapse. For Peter, who has just lost his Uncle Ben and is facing the initial trials of being Spider-Man, there is a nice moment of personal respite as he walks into a room where he knows he is loved, bearing an offering of cranberry sauce. Everyone in that room is trying to hold things together: Mary Jane wants to impress Norman, Harry wants to impress Norman and Mary Jane, Peter loves Mary Jane but does not dare hurt Harry, and Aunt May is finishing a turkey in an attempt to care for all of them. The whole movie, the piece adds, is an exercise in heart-on-your-sleeve sweetness, and its lesson is that it is the trying that counts: life can be hard, weird, and cruel, but sitting down for a meal with our nearest and dearest means that for at least a little while we do not need to have it all figured out.
