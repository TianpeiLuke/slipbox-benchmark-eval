# Amazon's Buy Box Price Parity Enforcement: The Ripple Rug Case

Late in the day on Oct. 27, 2021, seller Fred Ruckel received an automated email telling him that his popular cat toy, the Ripple Rug, would no longer be shown in Amazon's all-important buy box — the area of the listing where shoppers click "Add to Cart" — because Amazon's software had detected the product being sold somewhere else for a cheaper price.

Ruckel is the sole seller of the Ripple Rug on Amazon, so the move all but ensured his product would disappear from the website, costing him thousands of dollars per day. The email, viewed by CNBC, read: "Below is a list of product(s) in your catalog that are not currently eligible to be the Featured Offer because they are not priced competitively compared to prices for those products from retailers outside Amazon." Unbeknownst to him, Chewy was running a discount promotion and had dropped the price of his product by a few dollars to $39.99 — less than the $43 offer on Amazon. The algorithm flagged it as a lower offer even though the item on Chewy cost $48.54 after shipping and taxes. Ruckel had to choose between lowering the price on Amazon or asking Chewy to raise the price of his product; he opted for the latter.
