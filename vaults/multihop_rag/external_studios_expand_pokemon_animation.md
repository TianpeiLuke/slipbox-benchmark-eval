# External Studios Expand Pokémon Animation

For the first time, external partners were brought in to expand the world of Pokémon beyond Ash Ketchum: OLM, the long-term Pokémon animation studio since 1998, passed the baton to other studios with the production of a spinoff series, Pokémon Origins. While OLM did assist in production and animated the finale for the four-episode series, the rest of production was a collaborative effort led by Production I.G and Xebec, and it proved a hit with audiences craving something new from the world of Pokémon.

That was just the beginning. Since then no fewer than six unique web series have been produced by various external partners from around the world, each set in different regions within the world of Pokémon. The most recent, this summer's Path to the Peak, was unique for taking place in the real world with a focus on the experience of competing in the TCG, while the upcoming Netflix series Pokémon Concierge marks the franchise's series debut in stop motion.

The films changed too. On top of a slew of one-off short films and music videos, the long-running movie franchise refocused its priorities: in 2017 the annual film franchise abandoned continuity with the TV anime in order to tell its own original stories without being confined by external events, allowing it to experiment with CG animation, remake the early episodes of the Pokémon TV series in movie form, and let Wit Studio — animators on the original series Attack on Titan — helm their own movie with 2018's The Power of Us.

The company did more than simply introduce dynamism into an anime that had long since gone stale in the eyes of many: greater efforts were made to integrate Pokémon into the lives of communities in Japan and further afield, both through relief efforts and merchandising campaigns that provided more than toys and light relief to an aging audience.
