# TikTok's Expansion Into Music Services

TikTok has built out music-related services to the point that one could be forgiven for thinking the company plans to reinvent itself as part of the music industry — a distribution service, a streaming service, a live event, artist upload deals, an AI music app and a save-to-streaming feature, layered on in about eighteen months.

The pieces, as MBW listed them on 2023-11-21: in 2022 TikTok launched SoundOn, its own music distribution service, which has partnered with Believe-owned distro company TuneCore and Downtown Holdings-owned B2B tech and services company FUGA. In the summer of 2023 it launched TikTok Music, a premium-only streaming service initially available in Brazil and Indonesia featuring the catalogs of all three major global recording companies (Sony Music, Warner Music Group and Universal Music Group), and quickly expanded it to Australia, Mexico and Singapore, though without Universal's catalog. In October 2023 TikTok announced its first-ever live music event, "TikTok In The Mix," and in the same month struck a deal with Distrokid to have indie artists upload their music to TikTok Music and to Capcut, TikTok's video editing software, as well as to TikTok's Commercial Music Library, which offers a catalog of music for brands to use in TikTok ads. Meanwhile parent ByteDance has launched an AI music-making app, and most recently TikTok unveiled "Add to Music App," which enables users to save music they have discovered directly to their Apple Music, Amazon Music (Prime or Unlimited) or Spotify accounts. MBW reads these partnerships as TikTok moving away from what at times appeared to be a trying relationship with the music industry — in one notable incident, TikTok disabled the use of copyrighted music for users in Australia, in what the platform described as an experiment and many observers saw as a hardball tactic in its licensing negotiations with music rights holders.
