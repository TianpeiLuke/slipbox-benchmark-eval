# 6PPD-Quinone And Coho Salmon Die-Offs

6PPD-quinone is a toxin that forms when 6PPD — an additive that helps tires resist environmental damage and so last longer between replacements — reacts with ozone, and a 2020 study from the University of Washington's Center for Urban Waters found that a dramatic decline in coho salmon populations was the result of it. The salmon have now been placed on some West Coast endangered lists.

The causal chain runs from road to water. "Shortly after birth, they were essentially going mad and dying," Molden says. "The researchers finally linked it to this compound in tires because it was happening after rainfall. The rainfall was washing all the accumulated tire material off the road into the harbors and into the shore."

The relation does not stop at fish. That is bad not just for the salmon and the Pacific Northwest's fishing industry — 6PPD-quinone is in us too, and it only comes from tires.

How far the harm extends is still open. "The product 6PPD-quinone, which was identified in late 2020, requires more research to understand what impact, if any, it may have on aquatic life or the environment," Goodyear SVP of global operations and CTO Chris Helsel says. That is hardly a mea culpa, but the tire industry genuinely has been an active partner in studying the problem.
