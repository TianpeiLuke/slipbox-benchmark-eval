# Leagues Should Partner With Twitch Streamers Rather Than Block Markets

Esfand's claim, reported by Polygon on 2023-10-20, is that if done properly a league can partner with a streamer who has a large audience and find a way to allow rights that reduce the barrier to entry for users — and that doing so attracts the younger audience who is not watching cable TV as much anymore.

The grounds start from where power sits. "The NBA is king," Esfand says. "When it comes to hockey, the NHL is king. But what's happening is these different leagues, they are the boss. They're the governing body. They have all the rights to everything and when it comes to content, they can be in control." The failure mode he names from experience is geoblocking: "You can't just block it out in certain markets. When I did a stream [of a football game], I think Canada couldn't watch. Most of Europe couldn't watch."

The warrant is a track record of partnerships that worked. LiveMode and FIFA partnered with Brazilian streamer Casimito to broadcast the 2022 World Cup on Twitch in Latin America, while the Men in Blazers podcast hosted its popular live watch-along shows for some World Cup matches in the U.S. Earlier in 2023 Casimito also struck a deal to stream the 2023 Women's World Cup on his channel, and the NBA signed a deal in 2021 with Alexandre "Gaules" Borba Chiqueta and Budweiser to stream games in Brazilian markets.
