# Gio Reyna's Return Under Berhalter, And His 45 Minutes Against Germany

Gio Reyna was called into the USMNT for the first time under Gregg Berhalter since their falling out at the 2022 World Cup, having recovered from a leg fracture suffered in summer Nations League duty under an interim head coach. The 20-year-old Borussia Dortmund playmaker's return was announced alongside that of defender Cameron Carter-Vickers, who had only just made his Celtic debut that season after a hamstring injury.

The working relationship had been a major talking point since the 2022 fallout: Reyna's lack of playing time at that World Cup was exacerbated by his parents' actions in the following weeks, which sparked a domestic violence investigation of Berhalter before his eventual re-hire as national team head coach. Berhalter had been forthcoming, admitting on his re-hire that the two had a "lot of work to do" while insisting time was needed to broach the situation, and later confirming they had broken the ice by phone. After the October roster release he confirmed they had spoken "weeks ago" on Zoom, said he appreciated Reyna's willingness to move forward, and — because Reyna had not yet played for his club that season — set the goal of getting him minutes so he could build fitness and return to Dortmund in the best position to begin his club campaign. Berhalter said they saw Reyna able to play three positions: central midfielder, attacking midfielder and winger, clarifying that his skill set fit a "between the lines" style of wide play rather than one glued to the touchline.

In the Oct. 14 friendly against Germany, Reyna looked sharp in the starting lineup in all the ways a young man can: unyielding energy, a surprising eagerness to cover the whole of the midfield, periodic flashes of skill, and bleach-blond hair that let viewers spot him instantly. He was exceptional throughout the first half — less influential than he might have been with greater access to the ball, but showing a welcome taste for covering ground to help on defense or retrieve the ball and launch an attack — while Weston McKennie was his typical self, particularly connecting with Christian Pulisic down the left. Playing 45 minutes was a victory of sorts after only 27 minutes in his one club appearance that season for Dortmund, having been injured in June with the U.S. and missed all of preseason and nearly two months of the Bundesliga. The one substantial element missing was being able to endure from starting to finishing. "The goal is to send him back to Dortmund in a really good spot," Berhalter said. "I think he played a good game, showed his quality. I think everyone in the first half played well. So it was good to see him, good to have him on the field." Many national team fans had sided with Reyna during the World Cup controversy; in this game they got what they argued for, but not what they wished.
