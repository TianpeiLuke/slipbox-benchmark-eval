# Radiative Forcing and Climate Feedbacks

Forcing is the global energy surplus created when greenhouse gases are added to Earth's atmosphere so that less terrestrial radiation escapes to space, and feedbacks are the changes that warming itself induces in the climate system, which amplify or dampen how much the planet warms in response to that forcing.

To restore the energy balance after a forcing, the planet must warm. But warming causes changes in the climate system: the concentration of water vapor — itself a greenhouse gas — in the atmosphere increases, the spatial coverage of highly reflective snow and sea ice decreases, and cloud properties change. These and other radiative feedbacks are what determine, for a given forcing, the climate sensitivity.
