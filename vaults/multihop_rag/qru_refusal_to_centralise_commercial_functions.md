# The QRU Refuses To Centralise Commercial Functions

The Queensland Rugby Union stopped short of wanting to hand over its commercial property, rejecting the step from high-performance centralisation to full centralisation: "While the QRU is very supportive of high-performance alignment, it will not agree to any proposal on centralising commercial or corporate functions."

The premise it attacks is that consolidating commercial operations under Rugby Australia would strengthen the game. The QRU's reason is local expertise: "The very strength of our State unions is the expertise they bring in their own markets. We are open to work on commercial initiatives together but will always retain our independence for the benefit of Queensland Rugby, its members and stakeholders."

It backed the position with its own financial and participation record. The letter cited a sustainable business model built through the elimination of legacy operating debt, four straight operating profits, and work in partnership with governments to deliver $31 million of improvements to Ballymore through the construction of the National Rugby Training Centre, home of the Wallaroos and Queensland Reds. It also pointed to 71,000 players across the state, including more than 16,000 female participants, 257 schools featuring rugby as part of their program, and the Hospital Cup and regional club competitions, as evidence the game was in good health in Queensland.
