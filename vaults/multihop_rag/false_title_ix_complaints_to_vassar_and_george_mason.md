# False Title IX Complaints To Vassar And George Mason

By November 2022, just as Sinha had predicted from monitoring S.'s tweet storm, S.'s letters arrived at the Title IX offices of Vassar and George Mason, though his own campus, Hofstra, did not receive anything as far as he knows. Ever since Catherine Tan warned Vassar about S. earlier that spring, school officials had blocked her email in the system, and Tan had reached out again to her department head and the campus investigator: "You might get something from her in the next couple of days. Be on the lookout for it." S. circumvented Vassar's digital barriers by using the online form on the school's Title IX page. Tan, who received a copy, explained: "She wrote this long letter accusing me of sexually harassing her, forcing her to be a lesbian." S. signed with her full name. A similar email reached the Title IX office of George Mason University: "My name is [S.] and I'm not a student nor have I ever been one at George Mason. I'm writing to you today regarding one of your employees, Janani Umamaheswar of criminology, at the university and their sexually harassing behavior towards me online on twitter," continuing, "She has called me a lesbian, and has recommended that I have sexual relationships with women."

Twitter was not the only place S. was writing. Her comments extended to Instagram and Facebook, where some of her friends supported her online by commenting or liking her posts. On February 23rd, 2020 she wrote on Facebook: "I filed a report that I was being harassed, stalked, defamed and studied by my professor and all I got was a joke of an investigation and this stupid legal warning," adding, "The university is gaslighting me. Most of the officials I dealt with did NOT follow proper Title IX policy procedure throughout the process and has consequently made my experience much worse." She also posted the cease and desist letter that had been sent to her, and received a supportive reply from a graduate student at Rutgers University School of Social Work who specializes in violence against women and children and founded a Students Against Sexual Violence club: "I work for an organization called Know Your IX. We do work around Title IX and I've gone through the Title IX process at my school as well. I'm so sorry you're dealing with this treatment from your school." One Twitter post showed a screenshot of a conversation with someone who appeared to be a friend of S.'s and seemed concerned: "I don't know if I'm being too blunt but I promise I'm saying this out of love and concern and not meanness. I think your mind is playing tricks on you…And it's not like I think you're crazy because when I spend time with you you're totally normal and you're you. But specifically this ordeal seems crazy. It worries me… I honestly don't see what you're talking about. All I see is mundane posts."

So far, the Title IX offices, departments, and administrators at Tan, Umamaheswar, and Sinha's schools have been responsive and understanding about their experiences with the harassment. But all three professors also know the potential threats reach beyond their campuses: the internet has amplified so much of this behavior, making it easier for someone to become a stalker and easier for anyone to be stalked.
