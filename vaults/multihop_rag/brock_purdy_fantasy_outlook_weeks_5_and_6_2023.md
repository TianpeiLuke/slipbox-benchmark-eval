# Brock Purdy's Fantasy Outlook In Weeks 5 And 6 Of 2023

CBS Sports benched Brock Purdy in Week 5 of the 2023 season and then reversed itself in Week 6, admitting the first call was a mistake: the Week 5 argument was that Dallas' pressure plus man coverage created too much downside, and the Week 6 argument was that the author would not make the mistake of benching Purdy again after Dallas played zone and paid for it.

The Week 5 "Sit Him" case, published 4 October 2023, holds that it is safe to expect Purdy to feel a lot of pressure against the Cowboys, and that man coverage behind that pressure could create havoc that speeds him up; the column calls him consistent and the 49ers loaded, but treats this as one of those rare matchups with too much downside. After ranking him high early in the week the author moved him behind Anthony Richardson, Jordan Love and Trevor Lawrence, while keeping him ahead of Joe Burrow, Dak Prescott and Daniel Jones. The evidence for the downside: Dallas lost top cornerback Trevon Diggs and stayed red hot, leading the league in pass-rush pressure rate at 55.6% and ranking top-10 in blitz rate at 31.9%, which helped them into the top five in completion rate allowed (57.4%), passing touchdowns allowed (two) and missed tackles on pass plays (five, best in football); only Joshua Dobbs, who ran for 55 yards, had scored double-digit fantasy points against them, at 18, and their other victims were Daniel Jones, Zach Wilson and the Patriots quarterbacks. Dallas had also become one of the most aggressive defenses in the league, third from the bottom in zone-coverage snaps, having played heavy zone only in the closing quarters of blowout wins over the Giants and Jets — a completely different look from their January playoff meeting with Purdy, when they played zone on nearly 86% of snaps. In that playoff game the Cowboys pressured Purdy on 48.5% of dropbacks while blitzing just 21.2%, and Purdy completed 65% of his throws but struggled for 214 yards with no touchdowns. The counterweights the column concedes: San Francisco's 36.4% pressure rate allowed is only a shade worse than league average and is weighted by the Giants blitzing on 84.6% of Purdy's Week 2 dropbacks, a game in which Purdy still threw for 310 yards and two touchdowns without Brandon Aiyuk; Purdy had hit at least 20 fantasy points in eight of nine regular-season starts, which should be nine of nine but for a Week 2 touchdown ruled a lateral, and in nine of 11 including two playoff games, with at least 23 points in five of those nine including each of his past two; and the previous week he executed the offense flawlessly against Arizona, missing on one throw and again losing points to a pass ruled a lateral for a touchdown. His splits were strong against both man and zone — higher completion rate and yards per attempt against zone, four of his five passing touchdowns against man — but he was at his worst when pressured, completing 48.6% for 5.6 yards per attempt with a gaudy 18.9% off-target rate.

The Week 6 "Start Him" case, published 12 October 2023, is a correction. The author expected Purdy to struggle against a defense that pressured and played man; instead the Cowboys played a lot of zone and paid the price through the air, a choice a friend suggested was meant to avoid getting burned by long Christian McCaffrey runs. Against Cleveland, the combination of a hot 49ers defense and a Browns offense figuring to get stuffed should produce at least a game script for decent numbers, and the author would start Purdy over Trevor Lawrence, Geno Smith and Jared Goff. The breakdown lists San Francisco at Cleveland, the 49ers favoured by 7, an over/under of 37, an opponent ranked 1st versus quarterbacks, 16.5 projected points, an 11th quarterback ranking, and year-to-date totals of 1,271 passing yards, 24 rushing yards, 11 touchdowns, no interceptions and 23.4 fantasy points per game. In Week 5 Purdy beat one of the toughest pass rushes in football for four touchdowns on 24 attempts, with his seven incompletions comprising a drop, two balls batted at the line, a throwaway and two defensed passes, and an off-target rate of 0.0%. His 20-point streak extended to nine of 10 regular-season starts — 10 of 10 but for the lateral ruling — and 10 of 12 including playoffs, with at least 23 points in six of those 10 including each of his past three games. Cleveland has played the least zone coverage of any team in football, never even reaching 65% zone in a game and exceeding 70% in only three quarters all season, and had just watched Dallas play 90% zone while Purdy cooked them like BBQ chicken. The Browns rank third in pressure rate at 41.5% and blitz at an above-average 29.3%, but the Cowboys ranked higher and lost anyway; Purdy's pressured splits had improved to a 50% completion rate, 6.7 yards per attempt and a 15.9% off-target rate, and five of his seven passing touchdowns had come against man. Lamar Jackson's precedent is offered as the closing warrant: facing the Browns in their previous game he saw a ton of man coverage and heavy pressure yet threw two touchdowns and completed 79% of his passes for 9.8 yards per attempt without his starting left tackle and two of his top receivers, helped by his defense turning over an ill-prepared Browns offense.
