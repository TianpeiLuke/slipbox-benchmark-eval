# Brisbane Outwork Perth 84-79 At RAC Arena

The Brisbane Bullets shrugged off the absence of two important players to outwork the Wildcats at RAC Arena in Perth and produce a performance full of heart for an 84-79 NBL win on the Friday of round 5.

Both teams came in 2-4 and on losing slides, but it was the Wildcats who had the pressure building, even with Brisbane missing the suspended Aron Baynes and injured Shannon Scott. Brisbane scored the first seven points, led 20-9 at the end of the first quarter and 42-36 at the long break, and opened up a 20-point lead in the third before holding out a furious late charge. Seventeen-year-old Rocco Zikarsky stood tall in the paint in the third quarter with Tyrell Harrison in foul trouble, and Nathan Sobey and Chris Smith then knocked down shots as the lead ballooned. The Wildcats made a Keanu Pinder-inspired 11-0 run in the fourth to cut the deficit to three, but the Bullets answered with strong inside finishes from Harrison and Josh Bannan to secure the win.

The team effort was outstanding: captain Sobey added another 17 points, Smith put up 16, Bannan 15, and Harrison 12 with five rebounds and three blocks in just 14 minutes. First-year Bullets coach Justin Schueller could not have been prouder: "It was a big game for us having dropped four, with two marquees out and we knew we had to come in here and play near a perfect game. My initial thought is just pleasure in how the guys stuck to a plan, and found our advantages consistently throughout the night."

Perth's shooting undid them: they missed 18 of their first 19 three-point attempts before Pinder made a couple midway through the last quarter, finishing with 22 points and five rebounds as a lone shining light for a team that had lost four straight, while superstar Bryce Cotton again struggled to 10 points and five assists on 3-of-19 shooting. "My words during the week were that we're just not playing good, that showed again tonight," Perth coach John Rillie said. "Our offence isn't functioning very good right now. We're doing a great job holding teams to scores where we could put enough points on the board to win, in saying that we're not doing that. That's certainly something we have to address and resolve here pretty quickly."
