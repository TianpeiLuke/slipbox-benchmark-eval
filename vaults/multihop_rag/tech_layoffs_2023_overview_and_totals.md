# Tech Layoffs 2023: Overview And Totals

The tech industry saw more than 240,000 jobs lost in 2023 — a total already 50% higher than the previous year and still growing — as last year's techwide reckoning continued.

Earlier in the year, mass workforce reductions were driven by the biggest names in tech: Google, Amazon, Microsoft, Yahoo, Meta and Zoom. Startups across many sectors also announced cutbacks through the first half of the year. Tech layoffs slowed in the summer and fall, but cuts appeared to be ramping up again. By a second measure, the running total of layoffs for 2023 based on full months to date is 224,503, according to Layoffs.fyi, and tech layoffs conducted to date that year already exceeded the total number of tech layoffs in 2022 according to the data in that tracker.

The explanation TechCrunch offers is macroeconomic rather than sectoral collapse: many economists have cautioned against fears of a recession, which would seem like a reason for optimism, but the momentum for a tech sector rebound has been slow to build, resulting in tech companies continuing to cut back on their workforces and pivoting from a growth mindset to one based on efficiency in the face of stubborn market conditions. TechCrunch frames its tracker — a comprehensive list of all known 2023 tech layoffs, updated monthly — as a way to understand the impact on innovation, which companies face tough pressures, and who is available to hire for businesses lucky enough to be growing, as well as a reminder of the deeply human impact of layoffs.
