# Newcastle's Squad-Depth Deficit In Europe

The Independent argued on 13 December 2023 that Newcastle's Champions League exit owed less to effort than to strength in depth and injuries: Eddie Howe had the luxury of one attacking substitute, Alexander Isak, while three replacements combined for AC Milan's decider — Luka Jovic finding Noah Okafor, who picked out Samuel Chukwueze to place a shot past Martin Dubravka.

The grounds are the pattern of the campaign and of this game. After Kylian Mbappe's controversial 98th-minute leveller in Paris, Newcastle could rue the loss of another lead, and once again they may feel entitled to believe they should have won, though for different reasons: for much of the night they were rampant and had Milan reeling, yet last season's Champions League semi-finalists hung in the game, scored twice and hit the woodwork twice more. A third consecutive defeat may come with the sense that their injuries came at a cost. Theirs was another colossal effort and a far better performance than at Everton or Tottenham.

The warrant is that at this level effort is not a substitute for resources. Newcastle do not possess a talent advantage but they can seek to outwork anyone; they rattled Milan by running at them, and it took two astonishing interventions to stop them scoring both before and after Joelinton's strike. Theirs was the right response to the disappointment in Paris, a stirring effort. But the loss of the injured Nick Pope may have proved pivotal, and perhaps the seven-time winners, a side who reached the last four the previous year, displayed greater knowhow — or maybe they simply had greater strength in depth on a night when Kieran Trippier and Anthony Gordon were taken off because they seemed exhausted.
