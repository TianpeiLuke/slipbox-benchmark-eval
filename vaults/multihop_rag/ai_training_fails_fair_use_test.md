# The NMPA Argument That AI Training Fails Every Fair Use Factor

The National Music Publishers' Association argues, in its submission to the US Copyright Office, that training AI on copyrighted works is not "fair use" under almost any circumstance — because the way AI developers use materials to train AI would fail every one of the four tests for determining fair use. It is an area in which the NMPA and Universal Music Group are aligned.

Factor by factor, the grounds run as follows. On purpose and character, AI algorithms are not used for non-profit or educational purposes, as "the training of AI models is fundamentally a commercial endeavor, especially in the case of generative AI." The NMPA adds a warrant against the research exemption: any argument that an algorithm was trained on copyrighted material for "noncommercial" or "research" purposes should be "viewed skeptically," because any business could make that claim and "then shift entirely to commercial exploitation, leaving the creators of the copied works with no compensation." On the second factor, training fails because generative AI can be used to create "expressive" works of music. On the third, it fails because AI training means the entirety of a copyrighted work is ingested, "although there is no inherent need for that." And on the final factor it fails because "AI can be used to generate works that compete in the marketplace with the copied works, thereby reducing revenue from existing licensing markets as well."
