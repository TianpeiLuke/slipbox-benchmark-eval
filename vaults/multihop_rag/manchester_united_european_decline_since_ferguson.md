# Manchester United's European Decline Since Ferguson

The Independent argued on 12 December 2023 that Manchester United's 1-0 defeat to Bayern Munich should have been a landmark low but instead felt grimly routine, because group-stage elimination has become the club's normal European outcome in the post-Ferguson era rather than an aberration.

The grounds are frequency and pattern. Manchester United, one of the grandest and richest names in this competition, have again gone out of the Champions League at the group stage; it is the third time it has happened since Sir Alex Ferguson, meaning it is their most common round of exit in that period. The night is presented as one more entry in a decade of disappointment in a competition in which this club rose to its most emotional heights — a low, but just part of that longer run.

The warrant is the gap between the occasion and the performance. These are not the sounds we are used to hearing after that Champions League theme; something does stir when the anthem is played at Old Trafford amid the lights — well, for most, that is, except the players. United should not be performing like this on what is supposed to be the classic European night at Old Trafford, and the display was a reminder that all they are going to have for some time are their memories. Having been involved in so many ludicrously chaotic matches, the biggest of all ended up being a non-event: this game never threatened to stir anything, and United never threatened at all. That, the argument runs, can now be talked about definitively, since it has been decisively ended.
