# Gary Wang's Ownership Stake And Cooperation Deal

Gary Wang, co-founder and chief technology officer of FTX and co-founder of Alameda Research, owned only 10 percent of Alameda and 17 percent of FTX and drew a $200,000-a-year salary with no performance bonuses — though at times he was a billionaire on paper — while Bankman-Fried owned 90 percent of Alameda and 65 percent of FTX. He returned to the US on November 16th, met with the US government on November 17th without having been arrested or charged, offered to cooperate immediately, and pled guilty to various charges a month later. Wang was clear that he was cooperating because he did not want to go to jail, and said he hoped his assistance would lead a sentencing judge to be lenient.

The Verge's reporter drew from the disparity a puzzle she could not resolve: Wang took bizarre risks for a relatively small share. Bankman-Fried did not code, so none of his fingerprints are on the technical changes that let FTX allegedly defraud its customers and investors, while Wang supervised the code behind a key prosecution claim — that Alameda Research was afforded secret, special privileges with FTX's funds. When FTX went down, Wang left himself with no plausible deniability. Wang, a college roommate of Bankman-Fried's, took the stand after Adam Yedidia and, in the reporter's phrase, sang like a canary about the secret privileges he had created for Alameda in the FTX code.

The delivery was heavy going. On October 6th, 2023 Wang had to explain to the jury the difference between a front end and a back end, explain futures trading, and read a great deal into the record, which took a while; it was a tough day for the jury, and probably for Wang, who spoke quickly, perhaps from nerves.
