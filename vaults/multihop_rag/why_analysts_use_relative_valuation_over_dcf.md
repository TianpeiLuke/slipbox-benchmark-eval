# Why Analysts Rely On Relative Valuation Rather Than DCF

Relative valuation is well accepted and widely prevalent as an approach to picking stocks even though it is not regarded as the better method, and the reason is practical rather than theoretical. Valuation experts like Aswath Damodaran and many successful hedge fund managers believe that absolute valuation, discussed in the bl.portfolio Big Story dated 8 October, is the better way to arrive at the value of a stock; the concession is explicit and is the starting point of the argument.

The grounds for using relative valuation anyway are a combination of the complexities involved in building appropriate models, insufficient information, time constraints, and the fact that there are hundreds of stocks an investor wants to consider. The warrant joining these to the conclusion is a feasibility constraint: it is just not possible to do a discounted cash flow model for all of one's stock investments, so an approach that can be applied at that breadth is legitimate even if a better method exists in principle.
