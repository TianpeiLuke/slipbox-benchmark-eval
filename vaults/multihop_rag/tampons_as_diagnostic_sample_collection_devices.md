# Tampons As Diagnostic Sample Collection Devices

Daye repurposes the ordinary tampon as a sample collecting device: its core tampon offering is the basis for a growing range of vaginal- and sexual-health-screening services, turning what could be an intrusive test into something its customers are intimately familiar with — literally as easy as inserting and removing a tampon.

The product relations behind this run from menstrual use outward. Daye sells tampons for regular menstrual use, and its signature product is a CBD-infused tampon for combating period cramps, though it also sells a "nude" version without CBD; the company has always been interested in doing more with the staple female hygiene product than just catching flow. Daye did not invent the idea of using tampons to test for STIs, as founder Valentina Milanova explained when TechCrunch dug into its wider mission the previous year — U.K. university researchers pioneered menstrual tampon screening back in the 1990s — but Milanova is on a mission to expand the technique's use and utility, seeing it as a convenient way to help women learn a lot more about their own bodies from the comfort of their own bathrooms.

Daye also claims a performance advantage over clinical alternatives: its test tampons collect more vaginal fluid and cover a larger surface area than the swabs or other protocols a patient might be administered at a sexual health clinic or doctor's office, so the startup contends this is both a more accurate way to STI test than a swab and more comfortable than a speculum.
