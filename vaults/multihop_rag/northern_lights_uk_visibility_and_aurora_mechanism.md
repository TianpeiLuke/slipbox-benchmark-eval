# How The Aurora Works And Where In The UK It Can Be Seen

The Northern Lights are produced when electronically charged particles enter the Earth's atmosphere from space, producing dazzling displays of green, yellow, purple and even red lights. At certain periods during the year the lights regularly illuminate the skies of northern countries that sit in the "aurora zone" of the Arctic Circle.

The geographic relation holds most strongly at high latitudes: the phenomenon is visible every year in some areas of European countries like Finland, Iceland, Sweden and Norway, with regular sightings in parts of Canada and Greenland too. But the range extends further south than that suggests — sightings have become more frequent in the UK, with the lights seen as far south as Wiltshire in February 2023, so Brits don't necessarily need to trek to those countries to see the aurora borealis.

The limits of the model are its own unpredictability. Although numerous UK locations have reported sightings in recent years, these do tend to be in northern areas of the British Isles, and it is tough to predict exactly when and where the spectacle will make an appearance — which is why The Independent's 21 December 2023 guide rounds up the best places where you might be lucky enough to see it.
