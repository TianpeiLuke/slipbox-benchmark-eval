# The Manchester United Crisis News Cycle Under Ten Hag

The Guardian's argument is that results at Manchester United no longer settle anything, because the club's crisis news cycle consumes wins as quickly as defeats: a stirring recovery against Chelsea bought Erik ten Hag only a few days' grace.

The grounds are the pattern of the week itself — rancour, rumour and recrimination, barbs in the press and barbs at the press, defeat at Newcastle followed by the Chelsea win and the startling realisation that United were three points behind Manchester City, all before Thursday. The warrant is a claim about the institution rather than the team: such is the way of things at football's most reliable content provider, and this beast must always eat, so the warm flume of goodwill would last only as long as it took for Dominic Solanke to run through on goal for Bournemouth on the Saturday afternoon, returning United to the grip of crisis and the now-familiar lexicon of surly unsourced stories about dressing room discontent. A second, related argument is that assessing any United manager requires filters: Ten Hag would of course take the blame for the early Champions League exit, and plenty of mistakes had been made, not least in the players the manager had been allowed to sign — but to be United manager is to undertake three concurrent roles.
