# The Settlements That Left Epic Standing Alone

Other plaintiffs brought app store antitrust claims against Google and then all settled: all 50 state attorneys general and a number of consumers have tentatively settled, though it is not clear what the deal might include, and Match Group — the company behind Tinder, Match.com, OkCupid and other dating apps — just reached a surprise last-minute settlement too, one that makes it sound like Match effectively gave up. Epic stands alone.

Epic seems to have no interest in settling as long as the "Google tax" exists, and the company has repeatedly said it is not interested in cash. So far it has been willing to push these cases all the way through multiple appeals, cost be damned. It is not clear what Google could proactively offer that would satisfy Epic, since CEO Tim Sweeney has also publicly suggested that Google would simply circumvent any settlement it offers. If you want to see a vision of a future Epic would accept, look to the letter it sent to state attorneys general, laying out a settlement that would basically prohibit Google from doing the anticompetitive things Epic claims it did. Google would not tell The Verge whether it has offered Epic any kind of settlement.
