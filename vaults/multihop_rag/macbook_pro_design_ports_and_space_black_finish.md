# MacBook Pro Design, Ports And The Space Black Finish

The clearest functional difference between the MacBook Pro and the Air is ports: the Pro has three Thunderbolt 4/USB-C ports to the Air's two, plus an HDMI port that is key for external display support, and an SD reader that Apple keeps to the Pro system.

On the SD reader, the reviewer concedes it is a niche need in these days of everyone shooting everything on their phones, so Apple's justification for restricting it to the Pro is understandable — the new iMac does not have one either — but he recounts having to locate an Apple Store in the Venetian at the previous year's CES because his external SD reader failed. On ports generally: more ports is better, it is simple math, and on the Air all of the ports save the headphone jack are on the same side, so things can get crowded fast. Both systems have the latest MagSafe connector, though despite being a bespoke charger you can top the laptops up faster via Thunderbolt.

From an industrial design perspective, not much has changed from the 2021 model. Apple has settled into a look it likes, balancing rounded edges with a flat, almost boxy design not dissimilar from the iPhone in that respect. On top is the familiar reflective Apple logo, while "MacBook Pro" is etched into the bottom; around that is a series of small screws that signal the company's newfound push into repairability spurred on by the broader call for right to repair legislation, and there is a heat vent on either side. The Pros are a good bit thicker than the Air — the 14- and 16-inch are 0.61 and 0.66 inches tall respectively versus the 15-inch Air's 0.45 inches — due in no small part to the Pros' inclusion of a fan and airflow system; you are unlikely to trigger the fan during most everyday operations, but more complex tasks like 3D rendering require a lot of compute power, so it is there when you need it. The 14-inch Pro at 3.4 pounds weighs just a hair more than the 15-inch Air at 3.3 pounds, while at 4.7 and 4.8 pounds for the M3 Pro and Max there is a pronounced increase with the 16-inch Pro.

Space black is the new color, and its distinguishing property is fingerprint resistance. Apple fans love a new color, which in the MacBook line may be attributable to there being little variation in the laptops' aluminum design; the last few generations traded almost exclusively in space gray and silver, though the new Airs opened this up with starlight, a subtle light-gold, and midnight, a much darker gray. Space black gives off a nice glow when the light hits it, but the biggest thing is the fingerprint factor: the midnight Air is a notorious fingerprint magnet, with pages and pages of conversations about it on Reddit including "tips" for a fingerprint-free midnight Mac. Apple describes the new color in the most Apple way possible: "The finish features a breakthrough chemistry that forms an anodization seal to greatly reduce fingerprints." The company has not perfected a wholly fingerprint-proof surface, but the new finish does an excellent job keeping things to a minimum.
