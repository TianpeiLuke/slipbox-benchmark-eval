# A DNA Test Uncovers the Hospital Baby Switch

A home DNA test that Diane Bazella took in 2017 — technology that was newer on the market — showed she did not match with any of her known relatives, and four years of follow-up genealogy work led to the conclusion that she had been switched at birth.

Still searching for a connection, Bazella had decided in 2017 to see whether her birth father had other children. The results brought more confusion instead. "I was shocked and just so confused," Bazella said. "But at the same time, I wasn't." Over the next four years she reached out to people listed on her genealogy report, trying to figure out how they could be related, with no success. Finally one woman she contacted, who showed up as a close DNA match, emailed her back: "She reached out and said, 'I want to figure this out with you,'" Bazella said. It took the two women more than a year to unravel the story. "We were thinking [that] maybe I had a different birth father … and that maybe it was one of her relatives," Bazella said. "She went and confronted her dad and came back with the name Sherri Nordlie." The woman's father admitted to having fathered a baby girl with Sherri Nordlie — now Geerts — in 1960.

That was when Bazella realised her birth certificate, and everything she had thought for the past 40 years including the identity of her birth mother, was wrong. "I knew right away I was switched at birth," Bazella said. "There had been a huge mistake, and it was like it all just came together." Two baby girls had been born within hours of each other on Sept. 29 at the hospital for unwed mothers: one was named Kelly Jean and the other was named Dawn Marie. Bazella, it turns out, was Dawn Marie — not Kelly Jean, as she had originally thought — and Sherri Nordlie Geerts was her mother.
