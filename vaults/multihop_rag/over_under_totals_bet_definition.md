# Over/Under Totals Bet Definition

A totals bet, commonly known as an over/under bet, is a wager on the combined score of both teams in a game: bookmakers set a predicted total score, and bettors choose whether the actual combined score will surpass it ("over") or fall short of it ("under"). Rather than picking the winner or the winning margin, totals betting asks only about the game's combined score, and it sits alongside point spreads and moneylines as a favorite option in sports betting.

The discriminating feature is that the bettor's only concern is whether the final combined score falls over or under the line set by oddsmakers — the identity of the winner is irrelevant. Sporting News illustrates with an NBA game whose total is set at 210.5 points: those who bet the "over" win if the combined score of the two teams is 211 or more, while those who bet the "under" win if the score totals 210 or less. The 0.5 ensures no chance of a tie, since teams cannot score in half-points.

The category is broader than points. Over/under bets involve wagering on whether a particular number will be over or under the figure the bookmaker set, and that number can be combined points scored in a game, total rounds in a fight, or total games in a tennis match.
