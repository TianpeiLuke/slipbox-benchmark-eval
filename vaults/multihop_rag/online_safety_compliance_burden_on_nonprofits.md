# The Compliance Burden On Nonprofits

The Wikimedia Foundation's objection is not to regulation in principle but to the assumption that a proportionate regime is affordable for organisations that are not tech juggernauts: it supports the idea of regulation in general, yet tells The Verge it is proving increasingly challenging to comply with different regulatory regimes across the world.

The grounds are capacity. "We are already struggling with our capacity to comply with the [EU's] Digital Services Act," the Wikimedia Foundation's VP for global advocacy, Rebecca MacKinnon, says, pointing out that the nonprofit has just a handful of lawyers dedicated to the EU regulations compared to the legions companies like Meta and Google can dedicate. "We agree as a platform that we have responsibilities," MacKinnon says, but "when you're a nonprofit and every hour of work is zero sum, that's problematic." Sheer volume compounds the problem: BBC News notes that Ofcom's initial set of guidance is over 1,500 pages long.

Ofcom concedes part of the point. Whitehead admits that the Online Safety Act and Digital Services Act are more "regulatory cousins" than "identical twins," which means complying with both takes extra work, and says Ofcom is trying to make operating across different countries easier, pointing toward the regulator's work setting up a global online safety regulator network. Passing the Online Safety Act during a turbulent era in British politics was already difficult; as Ofcom begins filling in its details, the real challenges may be only beginning.
