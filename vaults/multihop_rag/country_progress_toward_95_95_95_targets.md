# Country Progress Toward the 95-95-95 Targets

Botswana, Eswatini, Rwanda, the United Republic of Tanzania and Zimbabwe have already hit the 95-95-95 target set for 2025, and an additional 16 countries are close to reaching those milestones, while the United States, Eastern Europe and Central Asia lag behind.

In the U.S. in 2021, 75% of the people diagnosed in the country received "some HIV care" and 66% were virally suppressed. Men who have sex with men made up the highest proportion of new infections in the U.S., with Black, Hispanic and Latino populations predominantly affected. Dr. Raphael Landovitz noted that countries that have hit the 95-95-95 targets offer universal, free ART access, while the U.S. government only has programs to help cover uninsured people's HIV treatment; racism, homophobia and transphobia often keep people from getting care, he said. Especially in urban centers, people dealing with housing insecurity, substance use and mental health issues struggle to access ART consistently, Dr. Monica Gandhi said. Eastern Europe and Central Asia also lag far behind, with just 51% of people with HIV getting ART and less than half being virally suppressed.
