# MIT Hydrogel Drinking Water Device

The MIT team is designing a hydrogel-based device that produces drinking water rather than growing food: at night, an open box containing their gel captures water vapor from the air; the next day the sun heats the hydrogel in the box, releasing the moisture; the box also contains a material that can be cooled without using electricity, so that when the water vapor touches that cold surface it condenses into droplets — like morning dew on grass — and the clean water falls into a storage chamber.

The team is working to engineer a device that can extract 2 to 5 liters (0.5 to 1.3 gallons) a day, which should supply someone's average drinking-water needs. As places get drier, new sources of drinking water will be needed, and Díaz-Marín says it is great that hydrogels can make water anywhere; for ultra-dry places with few other options, he says, they could be a lifesaver.
