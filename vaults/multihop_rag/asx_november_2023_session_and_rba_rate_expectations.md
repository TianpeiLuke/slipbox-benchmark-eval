# ASX November 2023 Session and RBA Rate Expectations

In the Australian session reported by The Sydney Morning Herald on 5 November 2023, energy companies were the main drag on the index, down one per cent, with Woodside off 0.8 per cent, Santos down 0.4 per cent and Ampol down 1.2 per cent, alongside coal miners Yancoal (down 1.7 per cent) and Whitehaven (down 2 per cent). Lynas Rare Earths was the biggest large-cap decliner at down 3.1 per cent, while industrials fell 0.2 per cent, including Computershare (down 2.9 per cent), Seven West Holdings (down 1.9 per cent) and Worley (down 1.3 per cent).

TMS Capital portfolio manager Ben Clark said the big news for markets on the Monday was Westpac's trading update, which he said led a resurgence in the banking sector's performance. "Westpac's results were better than expected, especially because it's been a bit of a perennial underperformer in the past few years," he said, adding that it set the tone for results from NAB and ANZ due to report in the following week. Clark also described a reversal of the interest-rate trade of recent weeks: "Long-term bond yields came off quite significantly in the last few days following weaker than expected employment data on Friday, which has seen some investors swivel back into growth stocks." On his account the market had switched from pricing in an 80 per cent chance of a rate rise on the Tuesday to 50-50.

Other commentary framed the risk of a further increase. "Our view is that in the high likelihood there is another cash rate increase on Tuesday, that is going to be the nail in the coffin for consumer confidence," said CreditorWatch chief economist Anneke Thompson, amid warnings from some economists that another rate rise would dampen spending intentions just in time for Christmas. The same wrap noted that the men who built pubs and liquor juggernaut Endeavour Group into the world's largest poker machine operator had triggered a public spat over the future of a business they say has lost its way.
