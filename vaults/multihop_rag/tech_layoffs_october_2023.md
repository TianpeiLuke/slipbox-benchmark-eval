# Tech Layoffs In October 2023

October 2023's cuts were dominated by Nokia, which announced on October 19 that it would cut up to 14,000 jobs — 16% of its workforce — following a sizable dip in Q3, the largest single tech layoff of the month.

The month opened with Chainalysis announcing on October 2 that it was laying off around 15% of its workforce, approximately 150 employees; Hopper announcing on October 3 that it was reducing full-time staff by 30%, or 250 job cuts; and Qualtrics announcing on October 4 that 780 roles were being eliminated and that "several hundred" roles were changing or moving locations in 2024. Bird issued a new round of layoffs after acquiring Spin, according to an email interim CEO Michael Washinushi sent to the company, one week after Bird was delisted from the New York Stock Exchange, with the exact number unknown. Synapse confirmed on October 6 that it had laid off 86 people, about 40% of the a16z-backed fintech company, having earlier in the year laid off 18% of its employees after growth slowed. Stitch Fix said it would lay off 558 employees after the personal styling subscription service closes a distribution center in Dallas. Blue Origin laid off 40 people on October 10, including software engineers and program managers, according to The Information. Qualcomm was cutting 1,258 jobs in two of its California offices, with the semiconductor company planning the reductions in December according to a filing with the California Employment Development Department. Flexport began laying off 20% of its workers — about 600 people — on October 13 as it prepared for restructuring, continuing a cost-cutting campaign that began with rescinding offers and working to lease office space.

Mid-month brought a cluster of announcements. On October 16 LinkedIn confirmed it would cut 668 more jobs, bringing the total to nearly 1,400 that year, with the bulk of the most recent cuts hitting R&D roles; Bandcamp eliminated 50% of its staff after ownership changed from video game company Epic to music licensing platform Songtradr; and Stack Overflow, the Prosus-owned company, announced it had laid off 28% of its staff, more than likely impacting 100 people. Expedia Group cut around 100 jobs, Skift reported, in its second round of layoffs in recent months, with cuts reportedly impacting employees across data and AI. Google confirmed on October 18 that it had eliminated at least 40 roles in its news division, and TechCrunch learned on October 18 that Plume had laid off more than two dozen workers, about a year after the trans healthcare startup raised a $24 million Series B. LegalZoom disclosed in a Worker Adjustment and Retraining Notification Act letter that it planned to lay off more than 100 workers in its sales division. Convoy halted operations due to a "massive freight recession," CEO Dan Lewis announced in an October 19 memo, a week after laying off 20% of its workforce. Tropic eliminated 26 roles, CEO David Campbell confirmed in an October 21 LinkedIn post.

Late in the month, Roblox China cut 15 roles on October 24, two years after the joint effort with Tencent abruptly paused its service; Pebble, the X alternative previously known as T2, announced on October 24 that it was shutting down operations; and Shipt announced on October 24 in a company statement that it had laid off 3.5% of its workforce and would close many open roles. Slync shut down months after its former CEO Chris Kirchner was arrested on fraud charges and sued the company to pay for his legal bills. Salsify cut 110 jobs and moved some roles abroad, CEO and co-founder Jason Purcell announced on October 25. Liberty Mutual confirmed on October 27 that it planned to cut 850 members of its staff, and Karat reportedly cut an estimated 10% of its workforce on October 27 after the financial startup for creators raised a $70 million Series B earlier that year. Hippo said in an SEC filing on October 30 that it planned to eliminate 120 roles, and Bungie announced on October 30 that it was laying off about 100 employees, with the Sony-owned game studio also delaying two of its upcoming titles as a result.
