# Ray-Ban Meta As Open-Ear Headphones

You do not have to use the Ray-Ban Meta camera at all: the glasses are a viable replacement for headphones, and unlike the camera, the audio features work like any other Bluetooth headset in terms of what apps you can use, though there is one built-in Spotify integration you can enable. Audio is controlled by a gesture area on the right arm just under the capture button — the app holds a library of gestures, but the basics are tapping once to pause or play, sliding forward and back for volume, tapping twice for the next track, and three taps to go back a track; with Spotify Tap enabled, tap and hold plays automatic recommendations based on your favorites. On the left arm, right on the hinge, a small toggle cuts the Bluetooth connection and power to the camera. The arms are thick because they contain much of the smarts, including mics and directional speakers.

Sound quality lands where open-ear audio usually does. Music and podcast quality is on par with other smart glasses: like other open-ear headphones they are not the best at reproducing thumping bass, but they are better than any ambient mode at helping you maintain situational awareness, while noise-canceling headphones are much more effective at blocking out the world. Calls are another story — these glasses trump the majority of smart glasses the reviewer has tested. She took calls from her spouse on the go in loud environments with no trouble hearing them and no issues being heard, provided she had good cell service, likely because there are now five mics, one of them in the nose pad; it made her sound clear in all her videos, almost like the main character in a first-person video game. Spatial audio is a further perk: when you watch videos you can hear where people were when it was recorded, as when Verge managing editor Alex Cranz crept behind her while she was recording at the office to say "Victoria sucks," audible on replay as trolling from behind.

Lens choice determines how usable they are as headphones. Meta sent a pair of sunglasses, which made it tough to judge a normal workday: the reviewer's greatest regret was not getting clear lenses or transitions, since she is not trying to be one of those people who wear sunglasses indoors and trying it for the review hurt her eyes after an hour or two. Transition lenses cost $80 more and are the best option for using them in as many scenarios as possible. Prescriptions can be ordered directly from Ray-Ban or a participating LensCrafters within the -6 to +4 range; those with worse vision may want to use them with contact lenses, since taking them to a local optician would void the warranty.
