# Search Quality Complaints Explained By Data Volume Growth

The Age argued that the common online complaint that "Google Search was a lot better 10 years ago" could absolutely be true, but that it would be a stretch to blame the company's monetisation practices when there is a much simpler explanation: the sheer growth in the data Google has to index.

The grounds are the volumes. In 2013, people were creating around 9 ZB of data every year — nine zettabytes, or nine trillion gigabytes — which Google had to index constantly, whereas in 2023 it is more like 120 ZB. The warrant is a change in kind as well as quantity: not a whole lot of that is static websites as it was a decade ago; it is live information that may be nested within various services and apps, which is harder to index well. The article then turned to what actually happens when we search, noting that Google has published extensive information about its Search procedures, so it is not difficult to get the company's own take on what happens in the background when you send off a query.
