# Australian Advertiser Responses To The TikTok Pixel Findings

Of the Australian websites tested, the national mental health organisation Beyond Blue removed the TikTok pixel from its site after being alerted to the tracking issue, while Sportsbet defended its use of advanced matching and Kmart did not respond to requests for comment.

Beyond Blue's spokeswoman said the organisation "takes privacy and security extremely seriously, and we apologise for any concern this has caused," and added: "When The Age and Sydney Morning Herald alerted us to this issue, we immediately commenced a review of our privacy policy and removed the TikTok pixel from our website. Our investigations are continuing as a priority. Like many health organisations, Beyond Blue uses tools such as pixels to help us deliver safe and relevant content to people online." A Sportsbet spokesman said: "We use advanced matching, and that's consistent with targeting advertising methods that a lot of companies use. Our understanding is they don't decrypt or use hashed data that has been shared with them."

The comparison that gives those responses their force is with other platforms. Similar data is sent to Google and Meta, but only after "I consent," for example, has been ticked: the masthead's tests found that for Google and Meta's tracking pixels, email addresses and phone numbers were sent only after a user had consented to the websites' privacy policies. The testing method was a Chrome extension called Omnibug, which is used to test marketing and analytics tools. According to TikTok's website, its tracking pixel can "help you find new customers, optimise your campaigns and measure ad performance."
