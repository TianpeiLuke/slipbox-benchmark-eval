# Pochettino Defends James And His 'Chelsea DNA'

Mauricio Pochettino refused to criticise Reece James for his touchline indiscipline, arguing on 20 October 2023 that the outburst was an understandable one-off from a committed young player and that James remains the right captain because of his character.

The grounds are motive and remorse. "It's part of the business," Pochettino said. "He was upset about the situation [against Villa] and then you need to understand that he is so committed to this project from the beginning. He feels really bad because he cannot help the team [because of his injury]." He added: "He made a mistake, we all make them and he was really sorry about it. I think he's not going to repeat the situation. This is a young guy that wants to play football. He was frustrated and he made a mistake. That's it. Finish."

The captaincy claim rests on separate grounds: Pochettino made James his captain because of "his personality, his leadership, the respect he has from the club and his teammates … he has Chelsea DNA", describing it as an appointment "for the present and the future — we cannot change captain every season, every six months". The Guardian's warrant for why Pochettino is so forgiving is recognition: it is easy to imagine that Pochettino sees something of himself in James — the self-belief, the running on the emotions of a match day — and Pochettino made the point that he had got himself booked for dissent during the Burnley game. The same thread runs to his connection with Arsenal manager Mikel Arteta, where there are similarities, albeit in a deeper relationship going back to their time together as players at Paris Saint-Germain.
