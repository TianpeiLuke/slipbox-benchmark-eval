# The Israeli Blockade Of Gaza Since 2007

The Israeli blockade of Gaza is a set of permanent air, land and sea blockades that Israel set up around the enclave in 2007, one year after Hamas rose to political power. Its discriminating features are physical control of movement and of goods: Israel controls two physical checkpoints along Gaza which monitor both the entry and exit of people and trucks, limits the kind and quantity of materials that pass into Gaza, and generally prohibits Gazans who do not have work permits or special clearance — for medical purposes, for example — from entering Israel. Egypt, which borders Gaza on its southern end, also oversees one checkpoint that specifically limits people coming and going.

The blockade is not total, and its boundary cases matter. Israel has granted permits to about 17,000 Gaza residents to enter and work in Israel, and while trade continues, the food, fuel and medical supplies that people in Gaza use all first pass through Israel. Gaza is about the size of Philadelphia and requires trade with different businesses and countries in order to maintain and grow its economy, but it is heavily dependent on foreign aid — partially as a result of this blockade regime. Israel's restrictions intensified after Hamas' surprise attack on 20 Israeli towns and several military bases on Oct. 7, 2023, when Israel announced a broad blockade of imports into Gaza that stopped all food, fuel and medical supplies from entering the region.
