# Eylon Levy's Aliyah And IDF Service

In 2014, on completing his master's degree in international relations at the University of Cambridge, Eylon Levy reached a crossroads: one option was to study law and work as a lawyer in London, the other to go on a Zionist adventure. That summer he was on the March of the Living, and remembers standing wrapped in a flag and crying, realising a decision had been made: he would immigrate to Israel and enlist in the army at the age of 23. The decision was made just as Operation Protective Edge began in 2014, and he wanted to enlist to help in the war, calling it the scariest experience in his life and recalling a child who looked out the window and said, "Dad, I think I saw a missile." When he actually immigrated the taxi driver told him the war had finished six months previously, and even so he reported the next day to the IDF Induction Center at Tel Hashomer.

Levy served in the command centre of the IDF coordinator of activities in the administered territories, which is responsible for civil policies and security coordination with the Israeli government in Judea, Samaria and the Gaza Strip. After his army service he worked as a newsreader for the English news on the Israel Broadcasting Authority and then for i24News, served as foreign media advisor to President Isaac Herzog for two years until May 2023, and has translated some 30 books from Hebrew to English. He was also active in the protests against the government's judicial reform.
