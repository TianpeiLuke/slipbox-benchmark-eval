# Whether Founders Should Attend Events

TechCrunch's 2023-10-13 startups roundup argues that the question of whether founders should attend events cannot be settled by anecdote, and reports that a survey of more than 50 founders found no real consensus — only a set of pros and cons and circumstances in which the return on investment is decent.

The grounds are three competing pieces of evidence. A few months earlier, Alexis Ohanian, the co-founder of Reddit, tweeted that if he could go back in time and do one thing differently when he was building Reddit, he would have spent significantly less time attending events. The newsletter's author reports the opposite personal experience: he always regrets getting on a plane, or into an Uber, to go to an event, but on the way back he has never regretted it, because in his experience something magical always comes out of going. Against both, the TC+ team, being data-driven journalists, surveyed more than 50 founders to work out whether attending events still makes sense in 2023.

The warrant is the explicit rejection of anecdote as evidence: the TC+ team is "not interested in anecdotes, however: neither mine nor Alexis'," so a survey of many founders is treated as the thing that can answer the question where two conflicting personal accounts cannot. The reported result is deliberately non-committal — there is no real consensus, but there are good pros and cons — and the practical payoff is conditional rather than general: the article is worth reading to figure out in which circumstances a founder can expect a decent return on investment on event-going. The scope is founders and the year 2023.
