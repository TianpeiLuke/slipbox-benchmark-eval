# Roblox Advertising And Brand Experiences

David Baszucki described Roblox advertising as the next step in a progression of fandom formats: seeing a poster of something you like, historically seeing something in a newspaper, more recently a web banner, and more recently still a very native natural video segment — with something beyond that, the 3D experience. His analogy is retail memory: if you are in a store together shopping, you remember what is on the shelf and how it looks, whether it is shoes or makeup, and he believes that will be a very powerful experience for brands. The connection to demographics is engagement: north of 5 billion hours per month on the platform, more and more of it from over-17 users.

The model has three layers of brand value. There is brand recognition, which is maybe harder to measure; there is the acquisition of virtual goods, such as Gucci purses; and ultimately, at some point — not yet really announced or promised — physical shopping as well, which Baszucki framed as a very social, fun experience when done with a friend. Hundreds of brands are already on the platform, and he cited the most revised Gucci experience, a simulation of their runway show, as radically amazing.

On size, he called the opportunity "interestingly big" but arguably undetermined. Roblox is in a good position because it generates a really good business without advertising, which gives it the opportunity to layer ads in for older people in a very civil, careful way; how big it becomes will depend on the power of the memories people have of brands. His anecdotal evidence for that spillover: the company's CFO's friend was in Santa Monica walking by a Vans store, and younger people wanted to go in because they were familiar with it from Vans World on Roblox. Building digital memories, connecting with brands that are fun and exciting, and then merging that with the physical world is, he said, new, uncharted territory.
