# Bank Run

## Definition

In the corpus, a bank run names the specific event in which rival exchange Binance's withdrawal of its remaining BUSD and FTT holdings "essentially triggered a bank run on FTX," the acute liquidity event that preceded FTX and Alameda's Chapter 11 filings.

## Context

In the corpus the term names the acute phase of FTX's November 2022 collapse: after a leaked Alameda Research balance sheet showed the firm's largest asset was billions of dollars of FTX's own FTT token — more FTT than existed in the entire market — rival exchange Binance began pulling out its remaining $2.1 billion equivalent of cash in BUSD and FTT, and TechCrunch reports this action "essentially triggered a bank run on FTX." The run was the mechanism that converted a confidence crisis into an immediate liquidity failure, and it directly preceded FTX and Alameda filing for Chapter 11 bankruptcy in mid-November 2022.

## Key Characteristics

- **Confidence-driven, not solvency-driven at onset** — the run began once the leaked balance sheet destroyed trust in FTX/Alameda, independent of whether the underlying assets were ultimately worthless.
- **Triggered by a large counterparty's exit** — it was Binance's withdrawal of its remaining BUSD and FTT holdings, not a broad retail panic, that set off the run described in the corpus.
- **Fast-moving** — the run happened in the same early-November-2022 window as the balance-sheet leak, ahead of the Chapter 11 filings that followed in mid-November 2022.
- **Terminal for the institution** — in this case the run was followed by Chapter 11 filings for both FTX and Alameda, and Bankman-Fried's resignation.

## Background (external)

Outside this corpus, a bank run is a general banking-system phenomenon: it occurs when many depositors, fearing a bank may fail, rush to withdraw their money at once, and because banks hold only a fraction of deposits as cash, a large enough run can become a self-fulfilling prophecy that drives the institution into sudden insolvency. (Wikipedia, "Bank run," accessed 2026-09-02: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bank_run)
