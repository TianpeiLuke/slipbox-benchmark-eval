# Steamboy (Katsuhiro Otomo)

Steamboy is Katsuhiro Otomo's 2004 steampunk action anime, starring Anne Suzuki, Manami Konishi, and Katsuo Nakamura, which Polygon argues does not get nearly as much love as it deserves.

The film follows the adventures of James Ray Steam, the wizkid grandson of a genius scientist living in an alternate 1860s Britain, who finds himself in the crosshairs of a nefarious robber baron who wants to conquer the world using the "Steamball," an invention capable of generating an infinite and dangerous amount of energy.

Polygon's Toussaint Egan positions Steamboy against Otomo's 1988 Akira: it lacks the neon-lit metropolises populated by roving teenage biker gangs and children with terrifying psychic powers, but it does not lack for its own gorgeous Victorian-era visuals, thrilling action sequences, and beautifully realized mechanical animation. He calls it a marvel of early 21st-century animation, situated at the faultlines of traditional hand-drawn cel animation and the anime industry's gradual embrace of digitally rendered and painted set-pieces and 3D-animated assets, and a gorgeous love letter to pulp action serials, steampunk alternate history stories, and the writings of Jules Verne, cast in a similar mold to another 2004 cult classic, Sky Captain and the World of Tomorrow. The qualified verdict is that while the film was not quite the second coming of Akira anime fans so eagerly wanted, it is a more than worthy follow-up to Otomo's feature debut in its own right — and enough to tide viewers over while waiting for an update on Orbital Era, the director's first anime feature in 20 years, announced in 2019.
