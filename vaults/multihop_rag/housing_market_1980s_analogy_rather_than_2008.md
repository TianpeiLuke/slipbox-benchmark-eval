# The 1980s, Not 2008, Is The Better Analogy For Today's Housing Market

Bank of America economists argued in a Thursday note that "Looking back at previous housing recessions, we think the 1980s are a better analogy for today's market than the 2008 housing crash." The grounds they gave are structural: back in the early 1980s, just like today, home prices had boomed for years before Fed officials were ultimately forced to hike interest rates aggressively in an attempt to fight inflation.

First American chief economist Mark Fleming made the same case in a Tuesday report titled "1980s Déjà Vu for the Housing Market," arguing from the absence of the specific 2000s failure modes: "Today's housing market isn't anything like the housing market of the mid-2000s. The housing market today is not overbuilt, nor is it driven by loose lending standards, subprime mortgages, or homeowners who are highly leveraged." The claim is offered with a scope qualification — the housing market today isn't identical to that of the '80s, but it's pretty close — and with a demographic warrant: in a lot of ways, millennials are being forced to follow the housing journey of their boomer parents as they face a frozen, unaffordable market with rising interest rates, as noted by both BofA Research economist Jeseo Park and Fleming.
