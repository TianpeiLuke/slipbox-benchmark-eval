# Rate My Professors Harassment Of Janani Umamaheswar

In the winter of 2019, Janani Umamaheswar, then an assistant professor in sociology at Southern Connecticut State University, began noticing strange remarks on her Rate My Professors profile, where most comments had previously been positive or at least constructive. One review, rated "awful" with a score of one out of five, read: "Textbook only discusses crimes of the poor. I get discriminated against all semester. I felt like I was in Germany in the 1930s with my grandparents." More followed into early 2020, including "This is the worst professor I've ever had," a claim she had been dishonest about going to school in Canada (she completed her bachelor's and master's at the University of Toronto), an allegation that she had a "tyrannical authoritarian ideology" and practised "discrimination against students with prior substance abuse histories," and one reading: "Emailed me a bribe offering a grade boost if I did her a favor of attending a meeting she was hosting. I did not respond because accepting a bribe is illegal." She had never bribed any students; someone was inventing specific defamatory accusations about her behavior.

Because Rate My Professors users post anonymously, there is no surefire way to verify or screen the people who write reviews, and Umamaheswar and her husband Alex Sinha — then a law professor at Quinnipiac University — could not work out who was posting. He suggested she contact the site to remove the comments, and they assumed the company complied, but a short time later comments in the same tone resurfaced under a new class code; she reached out again, weeks passed, and more posts emerged. A separate signal then appeared: a report from her web hosting company logging IP addresses that visited her lightly trafficked professional website showed one local address appearing repeatedly at all hours, sometimes more than two dozen hits an hour. Sinha checked his own site and found the same IP address visiting frequently, lingering on his résumé and published writing.

In December, Southern Connecticut State University police contacted Umamaheswar's department: a student — referred to here as S. — had filed a police report against her and two other sociology professors, claiming "a possible hacking of her personal laptop computer" perpetrated by the three faculty members and accusing them of using students in the class to follow her around and look at her papers. S. had also emailed accusations to school officials, reaching the president and the Title IX office; in one message to administration on February 9th, 2020, S. wrote, "I genuinely believe Dr. Umamaheswar is a danger to other students as she was to me," and called her "a pathological liar and capable of twisting words to get what she wants." The other two accused professors filed police reports against S. for harassment; Umamaheswar, who studies law, social inequality, and incarceration, decided not to, believing S. was probably someone struggling with mental health issues and knowing how the legal system might treat her. Administrators from four separate university offices and the police department investigated, and after two trying months dismissed S.'s claims as "factually incorrect, disparaging" and "legally actionable." The school sent S. a cease and desist letter and banned her from the university, warning on February 9th, 2020 that violating either directive would subject her to arrest and prosecution; shortly after, law enforcement arrested S. for harassment and later released her to await a court date. Police issued alerts about S., offered to relocate professors to a more secure area of the school, and at one point offered to install a panic button in Umamaheswar's office; Sinha installed a security camera at home. A month later the pandemic shut down campuses, S. seemed to quiet, and a year passed: Umamaheswar took a job at George Mason University and the family moved to Virginia, Sinha teaching remotely and later commuting to Hofstra University. Then, one afternoon in March 2022, a former colleague now teaching sociology at Vassar College texted about a strange, hidden comment on her Twitter account bearing a name she had heard two years earlier. S. was back.
