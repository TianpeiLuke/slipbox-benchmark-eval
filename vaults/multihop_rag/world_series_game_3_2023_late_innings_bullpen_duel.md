# Late Innings Of World Series Game 3, 2023: The Bullpen Duel

The Rangers protected a 3-0 lead through a bullpen duel from the sixth inning on in Game 3 of the 2023 World Series, conceding one run in the eighth before José Leclerc closed out a 3-1 win at 10:56 p.m. by striking out both Christian Walker and Tommy Pham swinging — Texas' ninth straight road victory.

Arizona threatened repeatedly without cashing in. In the top of the sixth at 9:42 p.m. Evan Carter punched a single to left but Miguel Castro worked out of the jam, with Mitch Garver flying out to center and Jonah Heim grounding out to third, where Evan Longoria made a nice play and throw. In the bottom of the sixth at 9:49 p.m. the Diamondbacks picked up only their third hit of the game, a two-out Ketel Marte line-drive single off Marcus Semien's glove, but Geraldo Perdomo and Corbin Carroll struck out and Gabriel Moreno flew out to right. Kyle Nelson entered for Castro at 9:53 p.m. with lefties Nathaniel Lowe and Josh Jung due, and at 9:58 p.m. sent the Rangers down in order — Lowe reached the warning track before Lourdes Gurriel Jr. tracked it down, then Jung grounded out to short and Leody Tavares to third. In the bottom of the seventh at 10:03 p.m. Christian Walker grounded out but Tommy Pham injected life into the crowd with a double to deep center; at 10:07 p.m. Josh Sborz battled out of the jam, striking out Gurriel and Alek Thomas swinging and stranding Pham at second, leaving the seventh at Rangers 3, Diamondbacks 0.

The eighth produced Arizona's only run. Luis Frias came in for Nelson at 10:10 p.m. and at 10:15 p.m. retired the Rangers' top of the order — Semien grounding out to second, Seager striking out swinging and Adolis Garcia flying out to center, with Garcia coming up holding his left side after the swing that ended the inning, something the blog flagged to monitor. In the bottom half at 10:20 p.m. left-hander Aroldis Chapman replaced Sborz; Arizona had planned to pinch-hit left-handed Pavin Smith for Longoria but pivoted to right-handed Emmanuel Rivera once Chapman came in. The decision paid off at 10:25 p.m. when Rivera doubled down the line in right, and at 10:26 p.m. Perdomo ripped an RBI single into left to drive him in — the No. 8 and 9 spots delivering with no outs and the top of the order coming up, Rangers leading 3-1. Chapman then escaped: at 10:30 p.m. he froze Corbin Carroll on a filthy slider for a strikeout looking, and at 10:31 p.m. Seager made a great backhand play on a Marte grounder to set up a 6-4-3 double play ending the inning.

In the ninth the Diamondbacks stayed with Frias, who walked Evan Carter to start the top of the inning at 10:35 p.m. At 10:39 p.m. Garver flew out to center and Arizona nearly doubled up Carter at first, challenging whether he had touched second base on his way back — unsuccessfully. At 10:44 p.m. Heim struck out swinging and, with two outs, Andrew Saalfrank replaced Frias to face left-handed hitter Lowe; at 10:46 p.m. Lowe hit a comebacker that ricocheted off Saalfrank to Marte, who pitched it to Walker to end the inning. Leclerc came in for the save at 10:49 p.m.; at 10:51 p.m. Moreno thought he had a lead-off walk but the home plate umpire called a strike even though it was off the plate, and Moreno grounded out to third on the next pitch — a tough break for Arizona — before Leclerc finished the game.
