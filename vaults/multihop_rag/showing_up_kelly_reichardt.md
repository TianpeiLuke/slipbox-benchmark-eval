# Showing Up (Kelly Reichardt)

Showing Up is Kelly Reichardt's film starring Michelle Williams, Hong Chau, and John Magaro, ranked 10 on Polygon's best movies of 2023 list of 2023-10-07 and available for digital rental/purchase.

Lizzy, played by Michelle Williams, is a 30-something artist living on campus at her hometown art school who wants two things in life: time to prepare for her exhibition, and her landlord Jo, played by Hong Chau, to fix her hot water heater. Superficially there is little more to Lizzy's plight, for the better — Reichardt, who directed and co-wrote, never permits the stakes to rise too high or the plot to get too complicated, because the film at its core is about working artists working in less financially stable mediums such as ceramic figurines and trippy visual projections. Without plot filling the film, there is time to just be present in a beautiful Pacific Northwest scene.

Critic CP identifies Reichardt's genius as getting the audience giggling at the artists but never the art: it is funny to think an artist dedicated a year of her life to crocheting a jumpsuit, except then you see the outfit and it is beautiful, an intentional undermining of the punchline; a teacher smugly opines on ceramics, but each piece he holds up is so lovingly crafted that they confidently speak for themselves. That decision — rib artists, celebrate art — sets the tone: humans are artifice, a bunch of contradictory masks put on to match the situation and the crowd, but our creations, when we commit to a craft in whatever medium, are an expression of our most vulnerable selves. Reichardt knows that art created from a place of sincere intent deserves compassion and respect, even if its creator is still a desperate, self-conscious ball of anxiety.
