# Simulcasting

## Definition

Simulcasting, as the corpus uses the term, is airing or streaming the same live content on two or more channels or platforms at the same time.

## Context

In the corpus the term spans two different media businesses: linear sports broadcasting and livestreaming.
For sports, simulcasting means a single live event airs at the same time on multiple TV and streaming outlets — the Seahawks-Giants "Monday Night Football" game was simulcast on both ABC and ESPN, and under NASCAR's new $7.7 billion media-rights deal with Fox Sports, NBC, Amazon, and Warner Bros. Discovery, races will be simulcast on Warner Bros. Discovery's MAX streaming service and on TNT cable simultaneously.
For livestreaming, Twitch's 2023 policy reversal reframed simulcasting as a creator freedom: streamers were newly permitted to stream to Twitch and other livestreaming platforms at the same time, reversing a long-standing exclusivity requirement.

## Key Characteristics

- **Simultaneity across outlets** — the defining feature is that the same content airs at the same time on more than one channel or platform, not that it is merely repeated later.
- **Can mix distribution types** — a simulcast can pair a free-to-air or cable channel with a streaming service, as in NASCAR's races airing on both TNT (cable) and MAX (streaming).
- **Reach vs. audience fragmentation** — Sports Business Journal's Adam Stern noted that simulcasting NASCAR races onto cable-gated TNT (rather than keeping them on a free-to-air network) risks lower ratings because cable requires a paid subscription, even though the deal nominally broadens distribution.
- **A policy lever in livestreaming** — Twitch's decision to allow simulcasting to other platforms, announced at TwitchCon 2023, reversed a prior ban on simultaneous multi-platform streaming.
- **Framed as community-driven, not competitive** — Twitch's Chief Monetization Officer Mike Minton said the simulcasting change (along with Partner Plus and ad-embed features discussed in the keynote) was an acknowledgment of community feedback, explicitly saying "I wouldn't call it out of competitive pressures" from rivals like Kick.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, Wikipedia describes "simulcast" as a portmanteau of "simultaneous broadcast," meaning the broadcasting of a program or event across multiple mediums, resolutions, or bitrates, or across several services on one medium, at the same time (Wikipedia, "Simulcast," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Simulcast).
