# Jerry Neumann Recommends "How Data Happened"

Jerry Neumann recommends "How Data Happened: A History from the Age of Reason to the Age of Algorithms," by Chris Wiggins and Matthew L. Jones, because you cannot reason about the place data now occupies without knowing its history. Neumann is the veteran VC half of "Founder vs Investor," a recently published non-fiction book that crosses two perspectives on venture-backed startups — entrepreneur Elizabeth Zalman is the founder and Neumann the investor — in which they share insights on how both sides can best work with one another.

His grounds begin with a shift he observes: ten years ago data was "the new oil," and now it is something more, to the point that reading the tech news you have to wonder if anything else even matters. As a humanist he hopes something else does, but he does not think you can reason about that unless you know the history — and "How Data Happened" is that history, covering the idea of data from the advent of statistics through today.

He adds a quality judgment: the book is well-researched, as you would expect from a couple of Columbia's top professors, but it is also an interesting and engaging read, making it a perfect gift for anyone who wants to know how our data-driven society got where it is and where it might go tomorrow.
