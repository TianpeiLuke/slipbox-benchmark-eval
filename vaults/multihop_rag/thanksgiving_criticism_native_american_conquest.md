# Criticism of Thanksgiving as a Celebration of Conquest

The case against Thanksgiving attacks the premise that the holiday commemorates a peaceable shared meal: although many Americans view it as a beloved holiday, it is viewed by many who have abandoned it as an erroneous celebration of what was actually the conquest of Native Americans and Native American lands.

The grounds are historical rather than about the modern celebration itself. The celebration has become controversial because Thanksgiving has a contentious history that goes far beyond when the first feast was held — the event has been viewed as deeply controversial over the years, due to the contentious history of the first Thanksgiving celebration in the 17th century.

A second strand of the objection concerns how that history is transmitted. In addition to being a holiday steeped with cultural appropriation, the period of American history involved is frequently white-washed, which leads some Americans to ignore or condemn the holiday, or instead to observe a National Day of Mourning. So while Thanksgiving is often viewed as a day for Americans to express what they are grateful for, that does not mean that everyone will celebrate.
