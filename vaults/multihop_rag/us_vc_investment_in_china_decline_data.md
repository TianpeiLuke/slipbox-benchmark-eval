# The Decline In US VC Investment In China: The Data

China's USD investors continue to deploy capital in industries that are not in the crosshairs of U.S.-China competition, but overall their activity has dwindled significantly due to the changing regulatory and geopolitical currents, coupled with a slowing economy.

The year 2022 saw just $14.5 billion invested in Chinese companies by U.S.-headquartered VCs, compared to $45.4 billion the year before, according to a report from research firm PitchBook. The number of deals nearly halved to 595, and the share of deals with U.S. investor participation dropped to 18.2% in 2022 after hovering above 30% for half a decade.

The scaleback is most notable in prolific investors like Sequoia Capital China, which recently changed its name to HongShan after splitting off its China operation; despite its proactive move to decouple, Sequoia still faces scrutiny from the U.S. government over its decades of activity in China. For the first three quarters of 2023, HongShan completed just 47 deals, compared to 99 deals in the same period of 2022, according to Crunchbase data.
