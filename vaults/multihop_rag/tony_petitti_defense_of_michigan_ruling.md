# Tony Petitti's Defense Of The Michigan Ruling

Sporting News argued that Big Ten commissioner Tony Petitti "did the right thing" in ruling against Michigan, because the penalty showed he was not afraid to take on one of the conference's Big Two rather than issuing a "slap on the wrist." In a letter to Michigan reported by The Athletic's Nicole Auerbach, Petitti tried to dispel any notion of favouritism: "The Conference does not play favorites among its members, nor does it take actions towards its members based on prejudgment or bias. Failing to act under the extraordinary circumstances here could lead other Conference members to reasonably conclude that the conference has chosen to favor the University over all other members." Auerbach also reported, on Nov. 10, 2023, that Petitti said in the letter that the school initially refused to consent to the Big Ten obtaining the spreadsheet and other documents from the NCAA related to its investigation.

The severity was larger than expected. Most speculation had pinned a potential Harbaugh suspension at two games, on the reasoning that the regular-season finale against No. 1 Ohio State is a huge television draw and that Petitti has a background in TV with the MLB Network. Petitti went heavy-handed instead — maybe a little too heavy, Sporting News allowed, given the pending legal response from Michigan.

The outlet also set out the risks. The Big Ten made clear that it still had not linked Harbaugh to any wrongdoing and that this was an institutional penalty based on the findings, while the NCAA would continue its own investigation into the Wolverines. The timing of Friday's announcement was poor, coming less than 24 hours before the game against Penn State. On this account Petitti upheld sportsmanship within the conference, but the ruling could lead to a long-term rift with Michigan.
