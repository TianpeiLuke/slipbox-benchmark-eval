# Britney Spears' Estrangement From Her Family

In *The Woman in Me*, as reported by The Guardian on 24 October 2023, Spears concludes that she made peace with her family in the sense that she "realised I never wanted to see them again, and I was at peace with that" — a break she traces to a lifetime of being made to feel she was not enough.

She writes about her father's alleged alcoholism, neglect and brutal standards, and about a mother who always made her feel less-than. She is unsparing about her younger sister, whom she characterises as a brat spared the pain of Britney's impoverished childhood because of Britney's success: "My mom and I had to witness the ugliness and the violence without believing that there was anywhere else to go." Even once she was the biggest pop star in the world her parents still seemed to have an undue say in running her career, including making her sit for humiliating TV interviews — why exactly that was remains unanswered in the book. Her father seized control legally with the imposition of the conservatorship, telling her, chillingly, "I am Britney Spears now", and her family appear to live off her dime while she lived an extraordinarily restricted life.

Returning home from the forced stint in a brutal rehab facility, she found that her parents had thrown away her childhood things, including a binder full of her poetry: "I felt an overwhelming sadness. I thought of the pages I'd written through tears. I never wanted to publish them or anything like that, but they were important to me. And my family had thrown them in the trash, just like they'd thrown me away." She resolved to start over with a new notebook, and writes that she is working to feel "more compassion than anger" towards them: "It's not easy."
