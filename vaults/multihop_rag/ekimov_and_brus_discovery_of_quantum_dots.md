# Ekimov and Brus's Discovery of Quantum Dots

In the early 1980s, Alexei Ekimov and Louis Brus each showed independently that nanoparticles whose size determines their properties could actually be made: Ekimov created quantum dots in glass, where adding copper chloride produced tiny crystals, and showed that the size of those crystals was linked to the colour of the glass, while Brus showed there was a link between size and colour for nanosize particles floating in a solution or in a gas.

Scientists had suspected nearly a century earlier that nanoparticles' sizes could alter their properties, but at the time it seemed impossible to actually make such particles: researchers would need a material with a perfect crystal structure and would have to control the size of that tiny speck extremely precisely — so precisely that they would have to sculpt the particle one layer of atoms at a time. The two laureates did not work together. Their discoveries stirred up intense interest in how such dots could be used, but making quantum dots for specific uses would require precisely controlling the dots' size.
