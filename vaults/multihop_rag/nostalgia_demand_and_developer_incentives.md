# Nostalgia Demand And Developer Incentives

Elizabeth Harper argues that nostalgia re-releases are community-responsive rather than merely cynical: "Nostalgia sells, but it only sells because there's demand. Gamers want to go back and revisit their favorite games as they originally were, and I think developers who re-release games are developers who pay attention to their communities."

She concedes the commercial motive and then bounds it: "Sure, there's a cynical side to this: game companies are in it to make money. But if we didn't love these classic games, they wouldn't be nearly as popular as they are." The warrant is that commercial success is itself evidence of genuine player attachment, since a re-release cannot sell without an audience that wants it.
