# The 1992 World Cup: White Ball, Coloured Kits And South Africa's Return

The 1992 World Cup, held in Australia and New Zealand, saw a number of landmark moments: it was the first tournament in which teams wore coloured kits, and it also introduced the white ball, changes in fielding rules, day/night matches and the inclusion of South Africa in the event for the first time following the end of the Apartheid regime.

Those innovations arrived together in a single edition, which is why the Sporting News treats 1992 as a turning point in the competition's history rather than one isolated rule change.

The tournament's result matched its novelty: after a slow and disappointing start to their year, Pakistan made an historic comeback in the tournament to eventually win the trophy by defeating England in the final.
