# The Middle Innings Of World Series Game 4, 2023

Between the third and seventh innings of Game 4 of the 2023 World Series on Oct. 31, Arizona managed only a single run against Andrew Heaney while Ryne Nelson quieted the Rangers, leaving the score at Rangers 10, Diamondbacks 1.

The bottom of the third was quiet: Emmanuel Rivera opened with a lineout at 9:15 p.m., Geraldo Perdomo grounded out up the middle at 9:16 p.m., and Nathaniel Lowe made the play to retire Corbin Carroll at 9:20 p.m., bringing the Rangers back up with a 10-0 lead. Ryne Nelson then gave Arizona what it needed on the mound, striking out Mitch Garver to open the fourth at 9:24 p.m. and retiring Evan Carter and Josh Jung at 9:27 p.m. for a much-needed quick inning — leaving it, as the blog put it, up to Arizona's offense to put up 10 or so runs.

Arizona's one run came in the bottom of the fourth. Gabriel Moreno walked to open the inning at 9:29 p.m., Christian Walker lined a double down the left field line at 9:30 p.m. to put runners on second and third with nobody out, and Tommy Pham worked a 3-1 count before Heaney struck him out looking at 9:33 p.m. At 9:35 p.m. Lourdes Gurriel Jr. hit a sacrifice fly caught near the warning track in right, scoring Moreno to put Arizona on the board at Rangers 10, Diamondbacks 1, and Heaney retired Alek Thomas at 9:37 p.m. so the Diamondbacks came away from the opportunity with only one run.

Nelson kept rolling — he had retired all five batters he faced by 9:43 p.m. and went 6-for-6 through two scoreless innings by 9:44 p.m., with a fan on the field at 9:40 p.m. providing the only other action. Heaney allowed a one-out walk to Perdomo at 9:49 p.m. while up to 76 pitches, and finished five innings of one-run ball by 9:52 p.m. with the Rangers leading 10-1; the middle of the fifth was MLB's annual Stand Up to Cancer tribute, which the blog called a fantastic moment at Chase Field. Nelson had retired the first eight hitters he faced by 9:59 p.m. with Seager coming up, and Arizona threatened again at 10:07 p.m. when Walker and Pham put back-to-back singles on the board with one out, only for Gurriel to ground into an inning-ending double play at 10:09 p.m. Nelson allowed his first baserunner at 10:15 p.m. — Josh Jung, who had a three-hit night — but got through the inning unscathed at 10:17 p.m., preserving what was left of the Diamondbacks bullpen for Game 5. Cody Bradford entered to pitch for Texas at 10:20 p.m., and at 10:24 p.m. Travis Jankowski collided with Marcus Semien in foul territory but made a nice catch for the final out of a quick inning.
