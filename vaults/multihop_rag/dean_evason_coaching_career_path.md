# Dean Evason Coaching Career Path

Dean Evason's coaching career, as summarized by Yardbarker on Nov. 27, 2023 at the time of his firing, followed a junior-to-AHL-to-NHL ladder: he had been a member of the Wild organization since being brought on as an assistant in 2018, while Woods joined the Wild bench one season prior.

The 59-year-old Evason began his coaching career with the WHL's Calgary Hitmen as an assistant in 1998-99 after finishing out his playing career in Germany, working his way up the junior hockey ranks over the next handful of seasons. His first NHL job came courtesy of an assistant role with the Capitals immediately after the 2004-05 lockout, coaching Alex Ovechkin in his first seven NHL seasons. After departing Washington in 2012, Evason spent the next several seasons working in the Predators organization as head coach of their AHL affiliate, the Milwaukee Admirals, where he helped guide more than a few future NHL stalwarts — namely Mattias Ekholm, Ryan Ellis, Filip Forsberg, Calle Järnkrok, Viktor Arvidsson and Kevin Fiala, among many others. After joining the Wild, he earned a promotion to head coach within two seasons when the Wild fired Bruce Boudreau late in the 2019-20 campaign.

Just a couple of years later, Evason had coached the Wild to the best season in franchise history: the 2021-22 campaign saw Minnesota break the 50-win mark for the first time since its inception in 2000, nearly sitting atop the Central Division with 113 points, in an extremely tough group with the 119-point Stanley Cup champion Avalanche and the 109-point Blues, to whom the Wild bowed out in the first round of the playoffs. The Wild remained without a playoff series victory since the 2015 postseason and had not advanced to a Conference Final since the 2002-03 season.
