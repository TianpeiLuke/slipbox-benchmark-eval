# Sam Altman Reinstated As OpenAI CEO With A New Board

On November 21, 2023, OpenAI announced that it and Sam Altman had "reached an agreement in principle" for him to return as the company's CEO, alongside a restructured "initial" board of directors.

The new initial board named in the announcement comprised former Salesforce chief executive Bret Taylor as chair, former US Secretary of the Treasury Larry Summers, and Quora founder Adam D'Angelo. OpenAI posted: "We have reached an agreement in principle for Sam Altman to return to OpenAI as CEO with a new initial board of Bret Taylor (Chair), Larry Summers, and Adam D'Angelo. We are collaborating to figure out the details. Thank you so much for your patience through this." Altman also posted about the deal, saying his decision to join Microsoft on the Sunday "was the best path for me and the team"; since then, the new board's composition and Microsoft's support appeared to have been enough to bring him back to the company he co-founded. On November 29, OpenAI announced that Microsoft would gain representation on the new initial board in the form of a non-voting observer — it was not immediately clear who this observer might be, only that they would not have an official vote in board business.

The significance for ChatGPT, as TechCrunch framed it, is that the restructuring pushed aside the board members who were more focused on the nonprofit side of OpenAI and who held the most concerns over the commercialization of its tools.
