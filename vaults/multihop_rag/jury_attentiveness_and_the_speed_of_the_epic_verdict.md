# Jury Attentiveness And The Speed Of The Epic Verdict

Tim Sweeney told The Verge that the rapid verdict initially caused "some trepidation," because the conventional wisdom attorneys give is that a fast jury verdict is typically not good for plaintiffs making a complicated case — but the outcome was "a great relief," and in the moment he smiled, shook the Google attorney's hand, and clapped Epic lawyer Bornstein on the back.

Sweeney also said Epic had suspected all along that the jury was following the case carefully: jurors "weren't snoozing off as you might expect with the complexity of these documents," but were looking at the question asker and the witness, taking notes, and reading documents. He took this as evidence "that the justice system works, even with the most complex type of tech antitrust case that you can possibly throw at a jury" — the jury "got it, they got it quickly," and were able to pull the case apart and contrast it with what he called the fiction of the story Google was trying to tell. The Verge added an editor's note that every journalist in the courtroom agreed the jury was attentive, and that most were also surprised by the rapid verdict.
