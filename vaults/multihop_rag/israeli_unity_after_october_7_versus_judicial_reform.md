# Israeli Unity After October 7 Versus The Judicial Reform Fight

Asked how it fits together that one day he was at a demonstration against the government and the next its spokesperson, Eylon Levy argues that 7 October made the earlier fight subordinate to a single task. He concedes the premise openly, saying that like many he participated in the protests against the reform and it is no secret, but claims there was an Israel before 7 October and there is an Israel after, that nothing will return to what it was before, and that there is now only one task, to win the war, for which the wars of the Jews must be put aside so that Israelis unite.

The warrant he offers is a change in perspective on what the pre-war quarrels were worth. He says it is amazing to see how Israeli society has come to its senses after the traumas, and that one of its strengths is that everyone works for everyone; leaving synagogue on Yom Kippur after the concluding services and seeing what was happening in Dizengoff Square with all the shoving and shouting, he found himself on the verge of tears, and today he misses the days when Israelis were on the verge of a civil war over a gender partition in Dizengoff Square. What they argued about and pulled their hair out over before 7 October now looks ridiculous against a date that reminds everyone of the big picture, why they are there, why they owe each other and in the end also love each other; it is sad that it took such a tragedy to remind them of the importance of unity in the nation, but it must be maintained.
