# Intestinal Stem Cell Balance and Wnt Signalling

The intestine imposes a two-sided constraint on its stem cells. "In our intestines, cells are exposed to extreme conditions," says Gabriele Colozza: cells face harsh conditions due to factors like mechanical stress, digestive enzymes and fluctuating pH levels, so stem cells within the intestinal lining constantly evolve into new cells to replace damaged ones.

Both directions of imbalance are pathological. "Uncontrolled stem cell proliferation may lead to tumor formation; on the other hand, if too many stem cells differentiate, the tissue will be depleted of stem cells and ultimately unable to self-renew."

This intricate balance is maintained through a complex network of signalling pathways and regulatory feedback loops that enable intercellular communication. A crucial player is the Wnt signalling pathway, a well-established mechanism in embryonic growth. The condition on which the model depends is regulation: without proper regulation, an overstimulated Wnt pathway may result in abnormal cell proliferation and, consequently, tumour development.
