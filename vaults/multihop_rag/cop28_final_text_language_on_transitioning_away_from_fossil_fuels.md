# The COP28 Final Text's Language On Transitioning Away From Fossil Fuels

Draft language explicitly calling for the phaseout of fossil fuels was stricken from the final text of the agreements brokered at COP28; what the world got instead was a weaker call for "transitioning away from fossil fuels in energy systems, in a just, orderly and equitable manner, accelerating action in this critical decade."

More than 100 countries came to the table pushing for an official agreement to "phase out fossil fuels," which would have addressed a glaring omission in the 2015 Paris accord — an international agreement to stop global warming that never actually mentions coal, oil, or natural gas — but bringing that deal to the finish line required nearly 200 countries to agree on the same language. The final text also calls on countries to work toward "accelerating efforts towards the phase-down of unabated coal power." The Verge notes that every word in that phrase is pretty dirty: coal is the most polluting fossil fuel, one reason it's hard to ignore in a climate agreement, but a commitment to phase down its use is decidedly weaker than one to phase it out, and the wording mirrors a recent letter to participating governments from COP28 president Sultan Ahmed Al Jaber, who is also the CEO of the Abu Dhabi National Oil Company. A landmark United Nations-backed climate report in 2018 found that countries need to cut their emissions nearly in half by 2030 to meet the Paris goals. Before the draft was finalized at the closing plenary, Andreas Sieber, associate director of policy and campaigns at 350.org, said: "This text is a step forward on our path towards phasing out fossil fuels, but is not the historic decision we hoped for … given the overwhelming momentum among countries in support of a renewable energy package and a long overdue fossil fuel phase out, we needed a far more ambitious result."
