# Why The League Cup Is Called The Carabao Cup

The Carabao Cup is the knockout tournament historically called the League Cup or EFL Cup, distinguished from the FA Cup by its organiser: unlike the FA Cup, which is overseen by the English Football Association, it is organised by the English Football League, per Sporting News on Dec. 19, 2023.

The EFL in the past incorporated each of the top four divisions of English football, before the formation of the Premier League in 1992/93. Today the EFL is comprised of the second-tier Championship, third-tier League One and fourth-tier League Two — but Premier League clubs are still invited to participate in the League Cup every season, which is the boundary case that keeps the competition national in scope.

The naming convention began in the 1990s, when corporations started paying to attach their name to the tournament, and the sponsor names caught on for brief periods when it was known as the Coca-Cola Cup, the Worthington's Cup and the Carling Cup. The latest sponsor is Thai energy drink company Carabao, which bought the title rights beginning with the 2017/18 edition, leading to the Carabao Cup moniker popularly used today.
