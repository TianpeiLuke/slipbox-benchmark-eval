# Thanksgiving As Communal Need Fulfillment

Polygon argues that the meaningful core of Thanksgiving is not the spirit of giving but the admission that people have innate, basic needs — food and social comfort — and that those needs are best fulfilled when people work in tandem.

The argument is framed against the holiday's baggage: Thanksgiving has a complicated history tied to the roots of American colonialism, and more recently processed through a lens of joyful capitalism. Against that, the piece holds that feeding friends and family is perhaps one of the most humane acts we can pursue. It then extends the claim beyond the day itself: we should try to do it more on every other day of the year, since the number of people volunteering to help feed the homeless and families in need peaks around November and December, and that energy needs to be carried through the preceding 10 months too.
