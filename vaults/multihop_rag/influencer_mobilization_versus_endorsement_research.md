# Influencer Mobilization Beats Endorsement

Even if Jake Paul were less despised online, endorsements aren't as valuable as they used to be — that is the argument TechCrunch advances on 2023-10-02, grounded in published research. A paper published in the International Journal of Communication this year reported that mobilizing influencers to build support for political causes is more effective than using their endorsements to gain votes.

The mechanism is an authenticity gap: blatant endorsements lack personal connection, and create an "authenticity gap," whereas authentic creators are trustworthy, and in campaign strategy that is more valuable than the size of their following. Martin Riedl, a University of Tennessee, Knoxville professor who studies social media and co-authored the paper, said that society tends to reward public figures for speaking out on social justice issues because there's more "situational awareness." The warrant he states directly: "If you use influencers in your campaign, it's important that they can authentically believe in what you promote," Riedl said. "And if that's not the case, that makes it really difficult for people to take your campaign seriously."
