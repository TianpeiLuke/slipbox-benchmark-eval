# Big Ten Coaches' Anger Over Michigan Sign-Stealing

Big Ten coaches voiced their frustration loud and clear on the conference's Wednesday coaches video call with commissioner Tony Petitti, after Jim Harbaugh left the call, saying they don't feel the new commissioner is "motivated" to do anything about the Wolverines, as reported by The New York Times on 3 November 2023.

The tone is documented through three anonymous coaches. "There is just a ton of frustration," one Big Ten coach told The Athletic on the Thursday morning. "Look at Jim Harbaugh's record before this started. The guy was on the hot seat before 2021, and now he's like the king of college football. … No doubt this all has had a profound effect." Asked to describe the tone of the sentiment expressed to Petitti, another coach called it "angry" — particularly at the Big Ten's lack of action, or even apparent interest in taking any: "Everybody's upset. Why is nothing being done? We want to know, what else do you need to know to take action? We (the Big Ten head coaches) want something done now. I don't think people understand the advantage that what they're (allegedly) doing gives you. People think, 'OK, now that everybody knows, we all can just move on.' Like, 'now, it's fair.' Well, no, it isn't. Not at all. This changes the way you operate. A lot of teams have been doing things a certain way for years. Now, it's forcing you to teach your players a whole new way to communicate just for them. People think that this is just advanced scouting. This was damn near espionage." A third Big Ten head coach called it "one of the most egregious breaches in the spirit of the game" he has ever heard of.

The article frames this as more than curiosity inside the conference: for the past two weeks coaches across college football had been riveted by the alleged Michigan sign-stealing scheme, and it references a poll in which 94% of college football coaches said Michigan should be punished.
