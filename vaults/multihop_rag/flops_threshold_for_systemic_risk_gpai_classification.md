# The FLOPs Threshold For Systemic-Risk GPAI Classification

The EU AI Act classifies a general purpose AI as carrying systemic risk by reference to the compute used to train it — 10^25 floating point operations — but the Commission confirmed during a technical briefing with journalists that this is only an "initial threshold," and that it will have powers to update it over time via implementing and delegating acts, that is, secondary legislation.

The structure has three moving parts and one relation between them. First, the threshold is not intended to stand alone: the idea is for the FLOPs figure to be combined, over time, with "other benchmarks" that will be developed by a new expert oversight body to be set up within the Commission, called the AI Office. Second, classification is self-assessed: a Commission official said it will be up to makers of GPAIs to self assess whether their models meet the FLOPs threshold and therefore whether they fall under the rules for GPAIs "with systemic risk." Third, the number was picked to capture current generation frontier models, though the Commission claimed lawmakers did not discuss nor even consider whether it would apply to any models currently in play, such as OpenAI's GPT-4 or Google's Gemini, during the marathon trilogues.

The official put the uncertainty plainly during the press briefing: "There are no official sources that will say ChatGPT or Gemini or Chinese models are at this level of FLOPs. On the basis of the information we have and with this 10^25 that we have chosen we have chosen a number that could really capture, a little bit, the frontier models that we have. Whether this is capturing GPT-4 or Gemini or others we are not here now to assert — because also, in our framework, it is the companies that would have to come and self assess what the amount of FLOPs or the computing capacity they have used. But, of course, if you read the scientific literature, many will point to these numbers as being very much the most advanced models at the moment. We will see what the companies will assess because they're the best placed to make this assessment." They added: "The rules have not been written keeping in mind certain companies. They've really been written with the idea of defining the threshold — which, by the way, may change because we have the possibility to be empowered to change this threshold on the basis of technological evolution. It could go up, it could go down and we could also develop other benchmarks that in the future will be the more appropriate to benchmark the different moments."
