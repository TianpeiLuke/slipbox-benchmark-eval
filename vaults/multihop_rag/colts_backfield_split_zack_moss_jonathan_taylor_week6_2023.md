# The Colts Backfield Split Between Zack Moss And Jonathan Taylor In Week 6 2023

CBS Sports argued that Zack Moss, not the returning Jonathan Taylor, was the Colts back to start in Week 6 of the 2023 season at Jacksonville: the claim is that Moss should lead the way and could see as many as 15 touches while Taylor sees as many as 10, and the warrant is that Taylor is still ramping up and it takes as many as four weeks to "get right."

The evidence is usage plus film. In Week 5 the Colts eased Taylor back into action after he sat out the first four games and barely practised at all: he played just 16% of the snaps to Moss' 80%, Moss handled nearly every third down, fourth down and snap inside the 10-yard line, and Taylor played just three second-half snaps. On film Taylor looked as big and strong as remembered and wasn't shy about contact, fighting for yardage on pretty much every carry, but seemed rusty at processing his blocking and finding where to run — more running up the backs of his linemen than scanning for space to cut and elude defenders — which the column calls a good first step. Moss, meanwhile, had consistently great vision and used his combination of speed and power to crash through the Titans' front, especially when they stacked the box against him on his 56-yard touchdown run; there is nothing to suggest Moss is struggling, which means his playing time doesn't figure to dwindle. Colts coach Shane Steichen said he is ramping up Taylor's workload in practice and the official team website believes Taylor's snap share will increase both this week and as the season goes on, but Moss still figures to be the lead back and handle a majority of high-value touches.

The matchup evidence is mixed and is what makes the Moss call a hunch rather than a projection. The Colts have an edge with their offensive line getting a step on Jacksonville's front: Moss already averages 2.13 yards before contact while the Jaguars give up 1.35 yards before contact, and 1.94 on power-scheme runs. Against that, Jacksonville's showing against a Bills run defense disinterested in running the ball pushed their numbers to new heights, and on the season they have allowed one rushing touchdown, 3.6 yards per carry, a 6.0% explosive run rate and 2.27 yards after contact per rush to running backs, with only the explosive run rate falling outside the top six in the league. Moss' breakdown lists Indianapolis at Jacksonville, the Jaguars favoured by 4, an over/under of 44.5, an opponent ranked 7th versus running backs, 8.8 projected points, a 20th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 445 rushing yards, eight receptions, 72 receiving yards, four touchdowns and 21.4 fantasy points per game; Taylor's lists the same game, 10.6 projected points, a 27th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 18 rushing yards, one reception, 16 receiving yards, no touchdowns and 4.4 fantasy points per game. The verdicts follow: Moss is a "Start Him" as an RB2 over Rhamondre Stevenson, Emari Demercado and Taylor, while Taylor is a "Sit Him" viewed as at best a bye-week replacement, with an eye on his Week 9 matchup at Carolina as his breakout game.
