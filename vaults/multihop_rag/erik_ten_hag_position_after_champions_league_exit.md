# Erik Ten Hag's Position After The Champions League Exit

The Independent argued on 12 December 2023 that the chances are Erik ten Hag has managed Manchester United in the Champions League for the last time, with Sir Jim Ratcliffe set to finalise his investment and United listing in the Premier League; the group-stage elimination was a chastening setback, and there are growing questions for Ten Hag himself even if everyone is constantly keen to put it in a wider context.

The grounds are both circumstantial and historical. United fans sang the name of Ten Hag's predecessor — not in a gesture of dissent, but as a reminder of a time when United were Europe's best, and of an evening without which a certain Norwegian would never have become their manager two decades later; after all, who put the ball in the Germans' net? Ole Gunnar Solskjaer. But that was a different millennium and a different United. Nostalgia underpinned Solskjaer's management, whereas Ten Hag cannot call upon his personal history in such a way: the Champions League final he almost reached was with Ajax, and his past does not offer the protection that means his name will always be sung at Old Trafford.

The warrant is that the elimination exposed how remote United's qualification chances always were, and that a rookie centre-forward is not the culprit in chief. Go back four years and it required a 96th-minute goal by Lucas Moura to stop Ten Hag reaching the final; now the less heralded Lukas Lerager signalled United's demise, putting Copenhagen ahead against Galatasaray when United needed a draw to preserve their own chances of qualification — that, and a victory over Bayern, which always felt unlikely given the German champions have not lost in 40 group-stage games in this competition and United have not beaten anyone resembling a top team this season. With ownership change arriving at the same moment, the case is that responsibility lands on the manager.
