# Polygon's Thesis That 2023 Games Broke the Scope-Versus-Scale Tradeoff

Polygon's claim, in its 2023-10-31 best-video-games-of-the-year piece, is that for the first year in recent memory scale did not necessitate tradition and scope did not preclude getting weird: in 2023 nothing was sacred in video games, and so they felt more vibrant than ever.

The grounds are a series of cases spanning budget tiers. Among the more "focused" games, Dredge begins as a lonely fishing sim before transforming into something otherworldly, Humanity morphed from a pensive art project into an all-out war, and Dave the Diver is not so much about being a diver as it is about running a sushi restaurant, or hunting for alien artifacts, or conversing with said aliens. This amorphousness was not confined to newcomers: Larian Studios, fresh off two years of early-access development and riding the reputation garnered from Divinity: Original Sin 2, released a role-playing game in which you can kill off nearly every main character the moment you meet them; Remedy Entertainment made a sequel that is equal parts horrifying, hilarious, fun, and fabulous; and Nintendo, in another banner year, finally let go and let players toy with the digital molecules of its most revered series.

The warrant joining grounds to claim is that these examples span every size of developer, every genre, and every tier of budget — so the weirdness cannot be dismissed as the province of small or low-risk projects. Polygon's summary, credited to Mike Mahardy, is that it is intoxicating to see developers of all sizes mining the depths of interactive design, branching this way and that as they follow their respective veins of gold, and that they are nowhere near the bottom of that expanse — a heartening thought. The piece also notes the platform context in passing: whether you booted up your Steam Deck for a cross-country flight or hid your Switch off screen during a boring Zoom meeting, the game you returned to was rarely the one you left behind.
