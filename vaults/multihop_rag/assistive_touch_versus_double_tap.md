# AssistiveTouch Versus Double Tap

Assistive Touch and double tap are related but structurally different features: Assistive Touch, introduced by Apple in 2021 as an accessibility feature designed for people with limb differences or mobility issues, is "a comprehensive navigational system," says Eric Charles, senior manager of Apple Watch Product Marketing, while double tap is not designed to help you navigate anything. The Verge's analogy: Assistive Touch is like the mouse to your computer — it scrolls, it selects, and it is highly programmable — whereas double tap is more like the double-click portion of using a mouse, used solely to perform the main action of an app.

The differences show up in the interface and the gesture set. With Assistive Touch you get a blue outline that visually cues the parts of a screen you can interact with when you can interact with them, and you can enable a motion-based cursor as well; it also offers four gestures — single tap, double tap, clench, and double clench. Charles says it became evident early on that not everyone has the full strength to clench their hand, while others may not be able to do two quick subsequent motions such as a double tap, so clenching exists to widen the range of options for people who may not be able to make a pinching motion. As he frames the design intent: "We don't think of it necessarily as 'Hey, there are four available gestures here.' It's that audience needs the ability to customize those gestures in a way that you don't because your ability goes further than what they might be able to do."

The range over which the simpler feature holds is set by that narrower purpose: because double tap performs only the main action of an app, Apple had to spend a lot of time researching what people wanted or expected a single double tap to do. The original goal of Assistive Touch, by contrast, was to give people a way to navigate through menus and control the Apple Watch without needing a second hand.
