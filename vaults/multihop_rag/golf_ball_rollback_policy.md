# Golf Ball Rollback Policy

The golf ball rollback policy is the Royal & Ancient (R&A) and United States Golf Association (USGA) decision to limit how far golf balls travel, taken because with new technologies balls have been covering far more distance — which lengthens courses and imposes a greater financial burden on golf clubs — and because a growing number of voices feel golf has become far less competitive, as Essentially Sports set out on Dec. 3, 2023.

The officials' framing is that the distance golf balls travel has increased multifold, so a change is necessary; otherwise the courses need to be perpetually extended. The defining feature that shapes everything downstream is that the rules are non-binding in nature: tours and regional governing bodies can decide for themselves whether to implement them. That opens the boundary case the piece flags — the possibility of bifurcation, which no one likes. If the R&A and USGA, which organize 35 championships between themselves, put this into action and the PGA and other tours reject them, amateurs might be playing with "corrected" balls while the Tour pros play with the same balls: steeped in rich irony, indeed. The proposed rules are not set to be implemented before at least 2026.
