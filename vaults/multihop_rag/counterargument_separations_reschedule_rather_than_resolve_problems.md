# Counter-Argument: Separations Reschedule Problems Rather Than Resolve Them

UK-based dating coach Jacob Lucas attacks the premise that an indefinite separation is a constructive alternative to divorce, arguing instead that it merely postpones the breakup. Speaking to The Independent in October 2023, he acknowledged that not all separations end in resolutions: when couples stop living together it can create an emotional disconnect, and if a lack of connection already existed before they separated, the separation does not make those issues go away. In such cases, he believes, couples may be avoiding an inevitable breakup for the sake of comfortability. "They're not resolving their problems, they're rescheduling them," he said. "So, if you separate from someone, but you live in a separate house and you're still together, it seems to me you're just not letting go of the past because you want to stick to what's familiar. People stick to what they know, and it's like you have a tether to that person. Your brain's like: 'I cannot let go of them because I am scared of the unknown.' I don't think I've ever seen a couple separate and come back again."

Lucas also attacks the second premise — that a promise never to divorce is a strength. While he agrees both parties must be invested in a relationship, he warns that promising not to get a divorce could spark feelings of resentment down the line, and that an agreement of the kind the Smiths made could create a false narrative about divorce. He notes that fans do not know why the couple made that promise. His warrant is that the stigma the promise defends against is not real: "Getting divorced shouldn't feel like a taboo because people grow apart and that's fine. People get stuck in relationships because of their friends and family or societal pressure, and they worry about getting divorced. They think a divorce has a stigma to its name, which it doesn't, and they push aside the fact that they still essentially feel trapped with someone they don't want to be with."
