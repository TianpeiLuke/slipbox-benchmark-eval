# Draft Pick

A draft pick is the corpus's term for a team's claimed selection in a professional sports league's draft — either the numbered turn itself or the player obtained with it.

The corpus uses "draft pick" and its variants ("drafted," "first-round pick," "overall pick") to describe how teams acquire players and how each individual selection is later labeled, ranked, and judged by its number, round, and outcome.

## Context

The corpus uses "draft pick" and its variants ("drafted," "first-round pick," "lottery pick") across several leagues to describe how teams acquire young talent and how commentators later judge those acquisitions. In the NHL, the Chicago Blackhawks are described as having "drafted" Connor Bedard, and teammate Kevin Korchinski is identified as "the No. 7 overall pick in the 2022 draft, one of three first-round selections by Chicago that year" — showing picks are numbered by overall selection order and grouped into rounds. In MLB, pitcher Alek Manoah is called a "former first-round pick," used as a lens to argue he still carries trade value because he remains under team control. In the NFL, Brock Purdy's "Mr. Irrelevant" nickname marks him as the final pick of the 2022 NFL Draft, contrasted against the eight quarterbacks selected earlier in the same draft class, including Kenny Pickett (pick 20) and Chris Oladokun (pick 241) — illustrating that picks are graded individually against draft order. In the AFL, the term applies pre-selection: Kevin Sheehan's "top 40 prospects" list previews players before the 2023 AFL Draft is even held, drawn from the pool who attended the AFL Draft Combine.

## Key Characteristics

- **Ordered by pick number** — picks are numbered across the draft (e.g., Purdy was "Pick 262," Korchinski was "the No. 7 overall pick"), and that number is itself used to grade or rank the selection after the fact.
- **Organized into rounds** — the corpus distinguishes "first-round" picks (Manoah, Korchinski) from later-round or final picks (Purdy, "Mr. Irrelevant"), with round number signaling perceived talent tier at selection time.
- **Retrospective judgment attaches to the pick, not just the player** — Golden State's "draft misses" piece frames entire draft classes (2020, 2021) as successes or failures by comparing whom the team picked against whom they passed over (e.g., Kuminga picked one spot ahead of Franz Wagner), and tallies how many of a team's draft picks are still on the roster years later.
- **Selection status carries lasting team control** — a player's history as a draft pick is tied to a team's rights over him for a fixed period; Manoah's status as a "former first-round pick" is invoked because he remains "controllable for four more seasons" before reaching arbitration.
- **Pre-draft coverage treats prospects as pending picks** — outlets rank and profile players before the draft occurs (the AFL's top-40 list, built from Combine attendees), so "draft pick" also describes a prospect's anticipated slot, not only a completed selection.

## Background (external)

The corpus never defines "draft pick" itself — it only reports specific picks, their numbers, and the players attached to them.
Per Wikipedia's article on sports drafts, a draft is a system in which "teams take turns selecting from a pool of eligible players," and a pick "refers to a team's turn or selection right within the draft" that can itself "be traded between teams" (Wikipedia, *Draft (sport)*, accessed 2026-09-02).
Draft order is commonly set to promote competitive balance, either by reverse order of the previous season's standings — "the previous season's worst team picking first" — or via a weighted lottery among non-playoff teams intended to "discourage teams from tanking" (Wikipedia, *Draft (sport)*, accessed 2026-09-02).
