# Limits of the Cannabis Pregnancy Causal Claim

The study reveals only a link between cannabis and certain poor pregnancy outcomes — it does not directly demonstrate that cannabis causes these effects, which is the inference a reader is most likely to draw and the one the study's design cannot support.

The authors nonetheless wrote in the report that "cannabis use should be avoided during pregnancy to optimize maternal and neonatal [newborn] outcomes." Other limitations qualify the result further: the molecules in the frozen urine samples may have degraded somewhat over the years, although they were stored appropriately, and the way people used cannabis is unknown, so it is unclear whether inhaling or taking edibles would come with different levels of risk. And again, the study's design allows a link to be drawn between cannabis and pregnancy outcomes, but it cannot say whether or how the drug actually caused the outcomes.
