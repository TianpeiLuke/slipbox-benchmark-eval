# FanDuel Week 14 DFS Receiver, Tight End, Flex And Defense Picks

Sporting News filled out its Week 14 FanDuel DFS lineup with receivers Mike Evans ($8,500), Michael Pittman Jr. ($8,000) and Deebo Samuel ($7,500), tight end Gerald Everett ($5,100), flex running back Dameon Pierce ($6,100) and the New Orleans Saints defense ($4,300), publishing on Dec. 7, 2023.

Evans (Buccaneers @ Falcons) hauled in six of eight targets for 82 yards and a score the last time he saw the Falcons and had only gotten better, putting together another great season as Baker Mayfield's top option in the passing game, with an incredible target share giving no reason to think that would not continue. Pittman (Colts @ Bengals) formed a Minshew-Pittman stack Sporting News called one of the best in fantasy that season and one that flew under the radar: Pittman was coming off back-to-back 100-yard games and had just put up a season-high 16 targets against the Titans, volume that does not grow on trees, with no signs of slowing as the Colts pushed for a playoff spot. Samuel (49ers vs. Seahawks) had rewarded them with a big performance against the Eagles and had historically feasted against Seattle, whom he carved up on Thanksgiving, and with the Niners eyeing the top spot in the NFC that should continue.

Everett (Chargers @ Broncos) came with a caveat — the Chargers offense was tough to watch between injuries and drops, and Everett had not posted 50 yards or more in a game that season — but he had a great matchup Sporting News had targeted often that year, this being his best chance to clear 50 plus a good shot at a touchdown, so they took the discount for someone who should outperform his salary. Pierce (Texans @ Jets) was taken at flex because MetLife Stadium promised yet another bad weather game, and all that rain meant plenty of running backs: Pierce appeared to have regained the RB1 spot from Devin Singletary with 15 carries to Singletary's eight the previous week, and although efficiency had been a problem for the sophomore back, the Jets presented another decent matchup and an ugly game meant opportunities. The Saints defense (vs. Panthers) was chosen because the Panthers had been a complete disaster all year, with a quarterback who could not compete with the extreme lack of talent up and down the roster and a lame-duck coaching staff, sitting 1-11 against a divisional rival that at 5-7 was somehow not out of the playoff race; New Orleans' offense was a mess, but the defense should be primed to bounce back after a rough Week 13.
