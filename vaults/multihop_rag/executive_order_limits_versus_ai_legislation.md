# Schumer's Objection: An Executive Order Cannot Replace Legislation

At an event hosted by The Washington Post on Thursday, Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer (D-NY) argued that the executive order did not go far enough and could not be considered an effective replacement for congressional action — attacking the implicit premise that a sufficiently sweeping EO settles the AI regulation question.

His grounds were the inherent limits of the instrument rather than any defect in the order's content. "There's probably a limit to what you can do by executive order," Schumer told WaPo. "They [the Biden Administration] are concerned, and they're doing a lot regulatorily, but everyone admits the only real answer is legislative." The concession embedded in the objection matters: Schumer credits the administration with doing a lot regulatorily, and his complaint is about the ceiling on executive authority, not about intent. He also acknowledged that congressional action has to date been slow in coming.

The context around the order underlines how much of the activity was executive rather than legislative. The White House reportedly did not brief the industry on this particular swath of radical policy changes, though administration officials noted they had already been collaborating extensively with AI companies on many of these issues. The Senate held its second AI Insight Forum event last week on Capitol Hill, while Vice President Kamala Harris was scheduled to speak at the UK Summit on AI Safety, hosted by Prime Minister Rishi Sunak on Tuesday.
