# Streaming's Walls Are Crumbling: Exclusivity Gives Way To Rights Payments

The Sydney Morning Herald's claim, in its 30 November 2023 December guide, is that the corporate and creative philosophy behind streaming television is in a state of flux and cracks in the prevailing wisdom are starting to appear — specifically around the value of exclusivity. For the past five or so years entertainment companies have been focused on creating their own streaming platforms built around exclusive control of their original content, whether original new series or a sister studio's Hollywood movies. But now that income is desired most, some streamers are once again willing to sell non-exclusive rights to others.

The grounding case is Yellowjackets: in December, one of the best original shows on Paramount+, the compelling horror-laced drama, will become available on Netflix. The warrant is that both sides gain — Netflix gets a buzzy show the vast majority of its subscribers have never had access to, while Paramount+ earns a rights payment and broadens the audience for a show that has a third season commissioned. "Streaming's walls are crumbling." The scope condition matters, though: there are still plenty of promising new shows and movies, most of them exclusive to their services, debuting in December, whether you're after an awards season blockbuster (Netflix's Maestro) or an Australian comic-drama (Binge's Strife).

The month's top Netflix recommendation is Maestro (December 20). Not since Warren Beatty in the late 1970s has a Hollywood leading man so completely — perhaps obsessively — given themselves over to filmmaking as Bradley Cooper: the credit he banked from the Hangover comedies, Silver Linings Playbook and American Sniper let him co-write, direct and star in the 2018 hit A Star is Born, and he now fills the same three positions in another movie about a tormented musician, the superstar American conductor and composer Leonard Bernstein. Cooper bristles at the term biopic; the film instead focuses on Bernstein's conducting, his nurturing marriage to Felicia Montealegre (Carey Mulligan), and his parallel relationships with male lovers. There's no Shallow to be found here, but Cooper appears to inhabit Bernstein across decades and the film is primed for the Academy Awards.
