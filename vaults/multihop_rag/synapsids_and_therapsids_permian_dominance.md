# Synapsids and Therapsids: Permian Dominance

Synapsids are creatures with a high metabolism, distinguished by a single hole in each side of their skull that led to improved jaw function; they were part of a diverse group of four-legged terrestrial animals — carnivores, insectivores and herbivores — that first arose in the late Carboniferous and would evolve into the group known today as mammals.

High oxygen levels favoured them, and by the early Permian they were the dominant terrestrial species. By the mid-Permian another group of proto-mammals — the possibly warm-blooded, somewhat rodent-like therapsids — emerged and became the new dominant species, and by the late Permian they may even have developed fur. One group, known as Theriodontia (Latin for "beast tooth"), displayed a number of evolutionary innovations: a shift in the bones supporting the jaw allowed the jaw to open wider and may have aided hearing as well, while the skull and teeth became larger, the teeth more specialized and the jaw more powerful. "They seemed primed to take over. But it was not to be." Inostrancevia is a genus from the extinct group Theriodontia that appeared during the Middle Permian, and Rubidgeinae is an extinct subfamily of gorgonopsid therapsids.
