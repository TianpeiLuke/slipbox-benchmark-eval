# Patent Litigation

## Definition

In the corpus, patent litigation is the set of formal legal proceedings companies use to fight over patent rights — a patent holder's lawsuit in federal district court and, in parallel, a case before the US International Trade Commission (ITC) — with remedies that can include a monetary verdict or an ITC import ban, and with the accused party able to counter-sue or get the asserted patents ruled invalid or unenforceable.

## Context

The corpus documents two concrete disputes that illustrate the mechanics of patent litigation. Sonos sued Google and won a $32.5 million verdict over patent infringement, but a federal judge later threw out that win after finding Sonos' patents "unenforceable and invalid" — showing that a litigation win is not final until it survives post-trial and appellate review. Separately, Masimo sued Apple in the US District Court for the Central District of California in 2020 for trade-secret theft and infringement of 10 patents related to pulse-oximetry (SpO2) sensing technology, and when that case dragged on, Masimo additionally filed a parallel case with the US International Trade Commission (ITC) in 2021; Apple counter-sued Masimo the following year. The ITC route matters because, unlike a district court, it can issue an import ban that blocks the accused product (the Apple Watch Series 9 and Ultra 2) from entering the US market, and any such ban is then subject to a 60-day presidential review period during which the President or US Trade Representative may veto it.

## Key Characteristics

- **Dual venues** — patent disputes can run in federal district court (damages, injunctions) and, in parallel, before the ITC (import bans), as in the Apple-Masimo dispute where Masimo pursued both.
- **Invalidity/unenforceability as a defense** — a defendant can defeat an infringement verdict after the fact by getting the asserted patents ruled unenforceable and invalid, as happened to Sonos' win against Google.
- **Countersuits** — litigation can escalate into cross-litigation, as when Apple sued Masimo back, alleging Masimo's own watch cloned Apple's design.
- **ITC import-ban remedy plus presidential review** — an ITC finding of infringement can trigger a ban on importing the infringing product, but the ban is not final until a 60-day presidential/USTR review period lapses without a veto.
- **Long timelines** — the Apple-Masimo dispute originated roughly a decade before the resulting Apple Watch import ban took effect, illustrating how a single patent dispute can span years across multiple proceedings.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, patent litigation is generally described as the process of enforcing patent rights, and it is decided exclusively by federal (not state) courts in the US.

Remedies available in such litigation typically include a permanent or preliminary injunction ordering the infringing act to stop, and a damages award such as a reasonable royalty.

A defendant can raise defenses including that the patent has expired, that it holds a license, or that the patent is invalid or unenforceable, and disputes may also end in a settlement that includes a licensing agreement.
