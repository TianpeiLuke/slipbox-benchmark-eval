# Women in Leadership and Company Performance Argument

Rupal Shah Hollenbeck argues that companies with more women in leadership perform better, and asks why the finding has not changed corporate behaviour faster. She is proud that four of the top executives at Check Point are women and that three women sit on the board — "That's pretty amazing," she said.

Her evidence is external research: according to McKinsey, companies that improved women's representation outperformed their industry from 2019 to 2022 when compared with other companies with data from the same time period. She states the relation directly: "When there is gender parity of the board, that company tends to perform higher than the average."

The warrant she draws from this evidence is that the performance link should be acted on rather than merely noted, which is why she puts the challenge as a question: "why is it the best kept secret and why aren't organizations responding to this faster and better?" She frames progress as real but from a low base: at most tech events "there's never a line [outside] of the lady's room. I think it's gotten materially better, but it's only gotten materially better because we started from such a low point."
