# Playoff Seeding

Playoff seeding is the numbered ranking assigned to each team that has qualified for a postseason field, used to set which team hosts each game, which teams earn a bye, and how the bracket is arranged.

## Context

In the corpus, seeding shows up in two different forms depending on the sport's postseason structure.

For the NFL, Sporting News' Week 13 playoff-picture roundup builds seeding directly from division standings and tiebreakers: the No. 1 seed in each conference gets home-field advantage and the lone first-round bye, and teams below the four division leaders are ranked as wild-card "in the hunt" entrants — for example the Dolphins held the AFC's No. 1 seed on the conference-record tiebreaker over the Ravens, while the Cowboys, as a division runner-up, could finish "No. 1, No. 2 or No. 3" if they won their division or were "locked into No. 5" otherwise.

For college football, the corpus shows a different seeding mechanism entirely: the four-team College Football Playoff field is not set by a standings formula but by a selection committee that ranks and numbers teams by subjective judgment of "best" versus "most deserving" — the 2023 field was numbered 1. Michigan, 2. Washington, 3. Texas, 4. Alabama, with an unbeaten Florida State left out of the numbered field altogether.

MLB's postseason coverage in the corpus treats seeding's practical payoff — home-field advantage — as uneven in impact: The Athletic's ranking of playoff ballpark atmospheres notes that a Rangers team with nine fewer regular-season wins than the Rays still swept the Rays at the Rays' home ballpark, illustrating that seeding-conferred home-field advantage does not guarantee an edge.

## Key Characteristics

- **Numbered rank within a bracket** — each qualified team gets a single ordinal number (No. 1, No. 2, etc.) that determines its bracket position, as in the NFL's AFC/NFC seed lists and the CFP's 1-4 field.
- **Drives home-field advantage and byes** — in the NFL, the No. 1 seed in each conference gets the lone first-round bye and home-field advantage, a stake the corpus describes the Eagles as being "in real danger of losing" to the 49ers, while the Ravens held only a half-game lead for the same spot over three teams right behind them.
- **Built from different inputs by sport** — the NFL derives seeds from win-loss record, division titles, and tiebreakers (head-to-head, conference record); the CFP derives its numbered field from a committee's ranked judgment rather than a fixed statistical formula.
- **Division winners are seeded above wild cards** — the NFL's seeding format ranks all division leaders (seeds 1-4) ahead of the best non-division-winning teams (wild cards, seeds 5-7), regardless of overall record, as when the 6-6 Falcons held a division-winner seed while stronger wild-card contenders sat lower.
- **Home-field advantage from seeding is not decisive** — MLB coverage of the 2023 postseason shows the Rangers, with nine fewer regular-season wins than the Rays, sweeping the Rays at their home ballpark despite two of the smallest postseason crowds in the past 100 years there, so seeding sets the schedule and venue but not the outcome.
