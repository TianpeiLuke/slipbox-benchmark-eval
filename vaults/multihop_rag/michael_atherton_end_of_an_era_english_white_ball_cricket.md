# Atherton: The Sri Lanka Defeat Marked The End Of An Era

Michael Atherton argued in The Times that the Sri Lankan defeat officially represented the "end of an era" for English white-ball supremacy, describing the England players as looking "dazed and shell-shocked, unable to comprehend what has befallen them".

His grounds are cumulative rather than single-match: "Each defeat, now four in five matches, has chipped away at the confidence of a group of players at the end of a cycle. No batsman looks in any kind of form. Every player has been used; some discarded and used again." The warrant is that repeated defeat plus visible selectorial churn produces a loss of collective belief — "They lost faith in their all-rounder strategy before the South Africa match, making three changes, and then went back to it here, making three more. Players sense uncertainty in decision-making – and there has been a lot of uncertainty."

Atherton's closing observation set the Bengaluru performance below even the previous low: "It was hard to think that they could put in a worse performance than in Mumbai but they managed it with the bat in Bengaluru."
