# Ray-Ban Meta: Design And Discreetness

The Verge's reviewer found the Ray-Ban Meta glasses both good-looking and genuinely inconspicuous — in many ways, she wrote, they feel like they belong in a spy movie, since James Bond always looks good and the gadgets Q gives him are discreet. The reason design matters is blunt: you will never wear a pair of smart glasses if they make you look like a low-rent Warby Parker model, and aside from the original Bose Frames she had never liked the way she looked in smart glasses before. That these are Ray-Bans and come in more styles than typical smart glasses is what changed it — while Wayfarers are considered universally flattering, rounder frames look better on her, and instead of the usual tortoiseshell or black she could pick a round, transparent blue frame with pink lenses, whose colors she wished were more noticeable in darker lighting but which popped in sunlight. She wore them dolled up for a wedding, in functional but hideous running gear, in sweatpants, and with leather jackets: they go with everything.

The discreetness has a specific shape. No one will know you are wearing tech on your face unless you walk around saying "Hey Meta" at the top of your lungs; nobody knew she was listening to EXO's EXIST album on her commute. There is some faint audio leakage at full volume, but full volume is only necessary to block out the squeaky rails of the New York City subway, and fellow commuters likely will not notice because they have AirPods in or the ambient noise is louder. More troubling, no one batted an eye whenever she took photos and videos in public or private areas. The capture button sits on the top of the right arm.

She compared the experience to Eggsy in Kingsman: The Golden Circle, who takes a call through his glasses during an important dinner and appears to everyone else as a dapper gent in snazzy frames while hearing and seeing things they cannot. Her version had no bombs to defuse, but the point stands: she spent a lot of time wearing these in public doing and listening to things that people looking straight at her were not aware of. That comes with pros and cons — but at least, she noted, you need not fear looking like a total jabroni, as in the picture of her wearing the Bose Frames Tempo.
