# Discogs' Compounding Cost Pressures

The Discogs fee increase did not land in isolation: it stacked on top of several other cost and demand shifts hitting the same sellers at the same time, which is why its effect on their margins was larger than the one-percentage-point change suggests. The fee increase arrived shortly before USPS raised the price of its Media Mail service — the lower rates at which anyone can send media products like books, music, and movies — by an average of 7 percent, and a year after the site had switched all its transactions to PayPal, which charges its own fees on each transaction, higher on international purchases, and which requires that every shipment has a tracking number, a significant extra expense for international sales.

The demand side moved against sellers at the same moment. The changes arrived at the tail end of a record sales bubble created by the pandemic: people who were already vinyl aficionados were stuck at home with their record players, stimulus checks, and nothing to spend them on besides survival and things you could do at home, like listen to music. Gene Melkisethian says his sales actually grew during the pandemic despite his brick-and-mortar sales disappearing, and that the boom inflated record prices. The relation the sellers describe is compounding rather than additive: with the higher fees Discogs is imposing, a sales decline that was almost inevitable post-lockdown has become steeper.
