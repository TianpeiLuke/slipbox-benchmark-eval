# Benavidez vs Andrade: The Stylistic Case For A Benavidez Decision

CBS Sports' pick for Benavidez-Andrade is Benavidez via UD12, and the argument turns on whether Andrade's accumulative, defensive style can bank enough rounds against a come-forward attacker before Benavidez's pressure takes over. The setup: Andrade might be better known for his ability to hit and not get hit, but that hasn't stopped him from declaring his willingness to defeat Benavidez under any means possible, even if that means standing in front of him and trading — a prospect Benavidez would prefer, though he is well prepared to do the chasing if he has to, similar to how he broke down Caleb Plant eight months earlier. Benavidez said that if Andrade gives him a war he is looking forward to it because he is a warrior, that he goes into every fight thinking every opponent will be the best they can be, that Andrade is a slick boxer he expects at his best, and that he loves figuring guys out and has hurt everyone he has been in the ring with: "I am the smarter fighter and I am the faster and hungrier fighter." Andrade, installed as a 3-1 underdog, said he does not expect Benavidez to be anything but in the best shape of his career but wanted one thing clear to critics and oddsmakers: he is not Caleb Plant. "Listen, people definitely get up for Michael Jordan, you know? So, I would assume [Benavidez] would get up to fight myself. But I'm not Caleb Plant, in no way, shape or form. There are sayings out there that we fight alike because we both box but we do not fight alike... This will be two cats fighting each other rather than one cat fighting a mouse."

The analytical grounds: those who have followed Andrade's career closely will realize he does have the skill set to pull off the upset, with the biggest questions surrounding whether he can take the kind of pounding that Benavidez's downhill style unleashes, especially in the second half when "The Mexican Monster" seems to only get stronger. One of the most underrated aspects of Benavidez's game is how deft he can be blocking or avoiding counter fire while operating at close range and trading punches. While most would expect Andrade to rely on his footwork in the first half, he will need to be certain he is banking enough rounds before Benavidez's inevitable mid-fight adjustment kicks in and his pressure increases. The warrant against Andrade is that his power is more of the accumulative kind over the course of rounds and not of the one-punch variety, which makes it difficult to dissuade Benavidez from coming forward — and that is the key to bringing an edge into the ring against him, because Benavidez's style is so fan- and judge-friendly as a come-forward attacker that it is difficult to bank close rounds against him: he is not only typically the busier fighter but also the one landing the majority of the power shots. The conclusion: if Andrade cannot hurt Benavidez or confuse him so thoroughly that it forces him away from his game plan, the best he can hope for is a moral victory of going the distance, as Benavidez takes one massive step closer to the Alvarez bout that will likely define his pro career.
