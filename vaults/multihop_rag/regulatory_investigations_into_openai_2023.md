# Regulatory Investigations Into OpenAI In 2023

OpenAI faced regulatory action on several fronts in 2023: Italy ordered ChatGPT blocked over GDPR concerns, Poland opened its own GDPR investigation, China's licensing regime pushed ChatGPT clients out of the App Store, and the FTC was reported to be investigating the chatbot's statements about people.

Italy moved first. Italy's data protection authority ordered OpenAI to stop processing people's data locally with immediate effect, saying it was concerned the ChatGPT maker was breaching the EU's General Data Protection Regulation and opening an investigation; OpenAI began geoblocking access to ChatGPT in Italy. The DPA then laid out what OpenAI needed to do for the order to be lifted, giving OpenAI a deadline of April 30 to complete the regulator's compliance demands, with a slightly more generous timeline of May 15 for a local radio, TV and internet awareness campaign. Poland's authority publicly announced it had opened an investigation regarding ChatGPT, accusing the company of a string of breaches of the GDPR.

In China, multiple generative AI apps were removed from Apple's China App Store ahead of the country's latest generative AI regulations, set to take effect August 15. Apple wrote in a letter to OpenCat, a native ChatGPT client: "As you may know, the government has been tightening regulations associated with deep synthesis technologies (DST) and generative AI services, including ChatGPT. DST must fulfill permitting requirements to operate in China, including securing a license from the Ministry of Industry and Information Technology (MIIT). Based on our review, your app is associated with ChatGPT, which does not have requisite permits to operate in China."

In the United States, the FTC was reportedly in at least the exploratory phase of an investigation into whether ChatGPT made "false, misleading, disparaging or harmful" statements about people. TechCrunch reporter Devin Coldewey noted that such an investigation does not appear out of thin air — the FTC does not look around and say "That looks suspicious"; generally a lawsuit or formal complaint is brought to its attention and the practices described imply that regulations are being ignored, much as a person suing a supplement company over pills that made them sick can prompt an FTC investigation because there is evidence the company lied about side effects. Separately, FTC chair Lina Khan and fellow commissioners warned House representatives in a congressional hearing that modern AI technologies like ChatGPT could be used to "turbocharge" fraud: "AI presents a whole set of opportunities, but also presents a whole set of risks," Khan said. "And I think we've already seen ways in which it could be used to turbocharge fraud and scams. We've been putting market participants on notice that instances in which AI tools are effectively being designed to deceive people can place them on the hook for FTC action."
