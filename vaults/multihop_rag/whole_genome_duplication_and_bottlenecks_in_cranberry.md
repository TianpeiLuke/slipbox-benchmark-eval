# Whole Genome Duplication and Bottlenecks in Cranberry

Although cranberries have only been grown by humans for a short period, they entered agriculture with a long genetic history that includes whole genome duplication events and genetic bottlenecks, which collectively change which genes are gained or lost over time in a population.

Whole genome duplication events occur when two species' genomes collide to form a new, larger genome encompassing all the traits of the two parental species, while genetic bottlenecks occur when a population is greatly reduced in size, limiting the amount of genetic diversity in that species; both events are extremely common in the plant world and can lead to gains and losses of different genes. Analysing the cranberry's genome can indicate when it diverged evolutionarily from relatives such as the blueberry, lingonberry and huckleberry, and understanding how modern species evolved can teach plant scientists about how different traits are inherited and how to effectively breed for them in the future.
