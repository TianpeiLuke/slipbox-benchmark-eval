# Digital Creator "Jail" And Why Anthology Formats Are The Escape

Sam Reich describes a structural trade-off between digital and traditional entertainment, framed through a line from his friend Adam Conover: "The thing about digital is you create for yourself a jail, and then you have to live in that jail forever, because your audience wants that from you, again and again and again. Whereas in traditional media, you have a certain amount of seasons, and maybe you get canceled, then you move on to something else." Conover remains pretty mixed about digital versus traditional entertainment and isn't sure where he wants to fall between those relative opportunities.

Reich places himself and Brennan Lee Mulligan inside that structure and identifies the mitigating variable: "I think Brennan and I both are in jail right now. The way in which we're lucky is we both jailed ourselves inside anthology series." The relation holds because anthologies let them "strut our stuff in lots of different ways despite the fact that we're beholden to these shows, which, so long as Dropout continues to be successful, will never be canceled" — he calls it foresight that turned out to be a happy accident. Mulligan's addition: "'no gods no masters' is a pretty exciting place to have found that [success]."

The range over which the trade-off bites is relentlessness rather than hardship. Reich: "The big one I was going to point to is that this is relentless. Not in a bad way — like, we spent our lives as entertainers starving, and then at some point, we arrive at the all-you-can-eat buffet — that's like when we've really made it — and we go, My god, I'm gonna have to eat so much, forever." Mulligan checks his own complaints against his past self: "every time that I go, Woof, we make a lot of this show, I turn around and see the ghost of my absolutely starving 25-year-old self being like, Oh, you got big problems, huh?" This came in answer to a question about what hurdles are overshadowed by the two famous ones — the corporate sale that kneecapped them and the onset of the COVID-19 pandemic. Mulligan disclaimed strategic overview entirely: asking him what the strategy was is "sort of like — you've pulled up alongside a man sprinting from a bear, and are like, What was the thought process behind going left at that tree back there?", saying Reich is much better at top-down, bird's-eye strategic oversight.
