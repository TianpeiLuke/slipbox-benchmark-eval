# Dinosaurs as Beneficiaries of the P-T Extinction

Dinosaurs were direct beneficiaries of the P-T extinction event: the event wiped out many of the groups that had dominated life on land, freeing up ecological niches to be filled by new organisms, including reptiles such as crocodiles and the earliest dinosaurs.

The advantage came from anatomy. Diapsids are a wide-ranging group of tetrapod vertebrates that first emerged during the Carboniferous around 300 million years ago and include the reptiles, birds and now-extinct dinosaurs; what distinguished them from their relatives, the synapsids and therapsids, was the presence of two holes instead of one on each side of their skull. One subgroup, known as archosaurs — which includes crocodilians and the earliest dinosaurs — exploited that innovation to develop a more efficient respiratory system that could make more effective use of the available oxygen, which gave them a leg up on the competition when oxygen levels plummeted at the P-T boundary. As the excerpt puts it, there were once again both winners and losers, and who won and who lost in this case came down to geology and geochemical weathering cycles.
