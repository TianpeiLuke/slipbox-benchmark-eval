# Google's Denial That Search Quality Has Declined

Danny Sullivan rejects the premise that Google results have gone downhill — because they have not, he insisted; if anything, search results have gotten a lot better over time, and anyone who thought search quality was worse needed to take a hard look in the mirror.

His alternative explanation puts the change in the users, not the engine: "We have an entire generation that grew up expecting the search box to do the work for them," he said. "We might do a better job of matching for a bulk of people, but for people who are super sensitive, when they have that fail moment, now it becomes, 'All my searches aren't good.'" The problem was not Google; the problem was not SEOs; the problem was kids these days.

He also rejects the secondary charge of reduced transparency. Speaking on the phone with a communications chaperone on the line, Sullivan came across as angry and defensive, annoyed that anyone would think his era at Google has been less transparent than Cutts': "We have reams of help documents! We have more people assigned to work with SEOs than we did when Matt worked here!" He is frustrated that people want to know every last detail about Google's algorithm because even "if we listed all one thousand of the ranking signals" and how much each was worth, that would not actually help SEOs do their jobs better anyway.

The interest that qualifies the denial is stated plainly: of course Sullivan would say this — he works for Google. The contrast with Cutts is what many SEOs pointed to as a turning point emblematic of Google's transition from idealistic startup to one of the most valuable and powerful companies to ever exist: over the phone Cutts came off as humble and thoughtful, acknowledging the nuances and challenges of the search engine business, while Sullivan sounded like an impatient corporate stooge, trying to gaslight the writer into believing the sky was red.
