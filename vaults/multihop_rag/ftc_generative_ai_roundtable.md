# FTC Generative AI Roundtable

At the FTC's conversation on generative AI and the creative industries, reported by TechCrunch on 2023-10-06, chair Lina Khan set out the agency's framing: "I know that generative AI in particular poses a unique set of opportunities and challenges to creative industries. We've already heard significant concerns about how these technologies could virtually overnight significantly disempower creators and artists who may watch their life's creation be appropriated into models over which they have no control."

The concerns raised by participants clustered on two points. In the comments, representatives from myriad creative communities expressed concerns around opt-out requirements that by default train AI models on artists' original work, and around how existing copyright law could be a useful if not comprehensive tool for setting out regulatory guardrails.

A representative with the WGA used the conversation to say the fight was not finished by collective bargaining: while striking writers obtained their own protections in a newly won agreement, the fight for artists' livelihoods "doesn't stop at the bargaining table."

Whether Congress mobilizes in time to address mounting concerns around AI and creative industries or not, for its part the FTC does appear to be very tuned into the technology's risks — and the power of bringing voices together across industries. FTC commissioner Rebecca Slaughter put the agency's position in categorical terms: "Art is fundamentally human. Humans may use technology to assist in creating art, but something cannot be art without human input. Technology is, by definition, not human . . . humans may endeavor to make generative AI that is ever more intelligent, [but] it cannot and will not replace human creativity."
