# The Mist: One Of Only Two Stephen King Adaptations That Match The Source

The Mist is Frank Darabont's third adaptation of a Stephen King story, starring Thomas Jane, Marcia Gay Harden and Laurie Holden, and available by digital rental or purchase. Polygon's framing is comparative: the so-called king of horror has a lot of movie adaptations, and few films have managed to eclipse, let alone successfully adapt, King's capacity for horror storytelling, with the exception of Stanley Kubrick's The Shining and Darabont's The Mist.

Jane plays a Hollywood poster artist living in Maine who, along with his wife and son and the rest of his neighbours, takes shelter in a supermarket in the wake of a mysterious storm that covers the town in a deadly mist. Supernatural, otherworldly horrors abound, but the greatest horror of all is humanity itself, as seen in the way the townspeople succumb to the temptation to scapegoat those among themselves under the influence of a local religious fanatic. The ending is a gut-punch and sincerely one of the most chilling in any mainstream horror film of its time — the reviewer declines to spoil it for anyone who has gone unspoiled so far.
