# NFC Playoff Seeding After Week 13 Of The 2023 Season

The Philadelphia Eagles held the NFC's No. 1 seed after Week 13 of the 2023 season, ahead of the San Francisco 49ers, Detroit Lions, Atlanta Falcons, Dallas Cowboys, Minnesota Vikings and Green Bay Packers, per Sporting News' Dec. 3, 2023 playoff picture.

At No. 1 were the Eagles (10-2), first place in the NFC East, who fell for the second time that season as their run of beating high-quality opponents in comeback fashion ended in getting routed by the 49ers at home; they now led the division by only one game over the Cowboys and were in real danger of losing home-field advantage and the lone bye to the 49ers too. At No. 2 were the 49ers (9-3), first place in the NFC West, whose red-hot ways since getting fully healthy around Brock Purdy after their bye continued with a dominant offensive performance against the Eagles — a statement that they were the new team to beat in the conference wherever they played — and who, up three games on the Rams and Seahawks, had another division title coming. At No. 3 were the Lions (9-3), first place in the NFC North, still in great shape to win the division with a three-game lead over the Vikings and the Packers after beating the Saints in New Orleans in Week 13, though looking more like the No. 2 seed would be their ceiling. At No. 4 were the Falcons (6-6), first place in the NFC South, in control of the NFL's weakest division thanks to beating the Saints and earning the head-to-head tiebreaker in Week 12; they got to .500 and increased their lead to a full game by beating the Jets in Week 13.

At No. 5 were the Cowboys (9-3), second place in the NFC East, who opened Week 13 by rallying to beat the Seahawks on Thursday night, keeping the heat on the losing Eagles ahead of them before hosting that team in Dallas in Week 14; they could finish No. 1, No. 2 or No. 3 if they won the division and were locked into No. 5 again otherwise. At No. 6 were the Vikings (6-6), second place in the NFC North, who jumped up a spot during their bye with the Seahawks losing on Thursday night to match their record, and who held the tiebreaker over the three other .500 NFC teams based on conference record — with the team in third place in their own division right behind them. At No. 7 were the Packers (6-6), third place in the NFC North, who moved into the final playoff spot thanks to having the second-best conference record after the Vikings, to whom they had already lost but would face again in Minnesota in Week 17; the Packers also held key wins over the Rams and Saints in the wild-card race.
