# COP28 Renewable Energy Commitments And The IEA Outlook

The final texts coming out of COP28 included notable new commitments on clean energy, chief among them a call for the tripling of renewable energy capacity globally by 2030 — something more than 100 countries had already pledged to do while negotiations were taking place the week before.

Leading up to the conference, the world's biggest greenhouse gas polluters, the US and China, committed to working toward that goal together when each country's climate envoys met in California in November. The Verge argued the lack of a clear phase-out plan is a blow to climate action but not a zero sum game: solar and wind power are already cheaper alternatives to coal, oil, and gas in most of the world for meeting new electricity demand, and nearly all of the world's new electricity supply over the next few years is expected to come from renewable and nuclear energy, according to the International Energy Agency. The agency — initially founded to safeguard the world's fuel supply after the 1970s oil crisis — called a global transition to clean energy "unstoppable" earlier in 2023 and forecast that demand for coal, oil, and gas would peak this decade. On that basis, getting cleaner sources of energy online looks inevitable at this point.
