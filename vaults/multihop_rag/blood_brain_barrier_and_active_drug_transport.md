# The Blood-Brain Barrier and Active Drug Transport

A major challenge for dementia treatment is the blood-brain barrier, which controls the movement of ions and molecules from the body to the brain and makes it difficult to get drugs into the central nervous system to tackle problems such as amyloid plaques.

Researchers are developing active transport methods for getting drugs across that barrier, said Cath Mummery: "For example, we are now exploring ways of helping drugs get into the brain much more efficiently, and that could have a major impact." Scientists caution that such developments will take years to realise, and that a great deal needs to be done in the short term to deal with dementia. "It is clear we have taken a step in the right direction but there is much more to do before we can have successfully dealt with these processes that are happening to our brains," said David Thomas. "It is a challenge, and a very important one."
