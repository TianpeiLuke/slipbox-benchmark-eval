# The House Paralysis Following Kevin McCarthy's Ouster

For more than two weeks after hard-liners ousted Kevin McCarthy at the start of October 2023, the stalemate shut down the U.S. House, leaving a seat of American democracy severely hobbled at a time of challenges at home and abroad. By Wednesday, 2023-10-18, the House had been stuck 15 days without a speaker — a position of power second in line to the presidency — and the vote for House speaker, once a formality in Congress, had devolved into another bitter GOP showdown for the gavel. As Republicans upset and exhausted by the infighting retreated for private conversations, hundreds of protesters, if not more, amassed outside the Capitol over the Israel-Hamas war, a stark reminder of the dangers of having the House adrift.

By Friday, 2023-10-20, the impasse had deepened into a full-blown crisis, with Republicans holding no realistic or workable plan to unite the fractured GOP majority, elect a new speaker and return to the work of Congress. Majority Leader Steve Scalise said they would "come back and start over" on Monday. Angry, frustrated Republicans left the private session blaming one another for the divisions they had created; a closed-door meeting Thursday to regroup had grown heated at times, with factions blaming one another for sending their majority into chaos. Next steps were highly uncertain as lawmakers started offering new ideas for a possible speaker, but it appeared no one at present could win a GOP majority. "We're in a very bad place right now," McCarthy said earlier.

Republicans predicted the House could essentially stay closed for the foreseeable future — perhaps until the mid-November deadline for Congress to approve funding or risk a federal government shutdown. "We're trying to figure out if there's a way we can get back with a Republican-only solution," said veteran legislator Rep. Tom Cole, R-Okla. "That's what normal majorities do. What this majority has done is prove it's not a normal majority."
