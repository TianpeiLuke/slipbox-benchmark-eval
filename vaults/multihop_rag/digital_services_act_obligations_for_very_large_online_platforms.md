# Digital Services Act Obligations For Very Large Online Platforms

The EU's Digital Services Act places an obligation on so-called very large online platforms (VLOPs) to assess societal risks attached to their use of algorithms and to put in place "reasonable, proportionate and effective mitigation measures" for identified risks, with their reporting and mitigation plans subject to independent audit and oversight by the Commission.

Back in April 2023, X/Twitter was named as one of 19 larger platforms that face the strictest level of regulation under the DSA. The bloc's executive, which also devised the anti-disinformation Code, is responsible for oversight of VLOPs' compliance with the DSA, and has powers under the law to fine violators up to 6% of global annual turnover; the Commission has previously wanted that not following the Code of Practice on Disinformation would be factored into its assessments. The key distinction is that the Code is voluntary while compliance with the DSA is not: the DSA is now legally binding on X/Twitter, including in particular the obligation on VLOPs to mitigate the risks their platforms pose for the spread of disinformation. On timing, the Commission noted that the TrustLab study to measure two Structural Indicators covers signatories of the Code at the time of the launch of the exercise, which then included X/Twitter, and that during this period the DSA had not entered into force yet for VLOPs and VLOSEs including X/Twitter. The Commission said it could not comment further on the substance of the risk assessments, that it was analysing available information on a range of issues and in close contact with X but could not comment on specifics and timelines, and that — as stated by Commissioner Thierry Breton — minor protection and integrity of elections are priorities in the enforcement of the DSA.

One practical limit on the deterrent: while the price for X ignoring the EU's warnings on disinformation could be steep, its ad revenue has cratered since Musk's takeover as a result of his failure to tackle toxicity and harmful disinformation encouraging an advertiser exodus, and shrinking earnings would effectively downsize any future penalties X could face under the DSA.
