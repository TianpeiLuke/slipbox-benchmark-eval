# Melbourne United Beat New Zealand 97-88 In Round 4

Chris Goulding fired a series of clutch three-pointers to pilot NBL ladder leaders Melbourne United to a 97-88 win over New Zealand at John Cain Arena, in his 300th game for the club.

Goulding had a quiet first half but found range after the main break, twice launching crucial bombs to help United edge ahead in the dying stages of a tight contest; his fade-away from the corner gave them a game-high nine-point lead with three minutes left as he finished 4-of-5 from distance. "We can't not talk about CG's couple of threes down the stretch," coach Dean Vickerman told reporters post-game. "They were pretty incredible." Jo Lual-Acuil (16 points) and import Ian Clark (18) also came up with big plays in the final period as Melbourne outscored their opponents 33-25 with the game on the line — Lual-Acuil shining in his second game back from a long-term wrist injury, and Clark's wide-open three restoring a nine-point buffer with 38 seconds left, a bucket Goulding was so confident in that he turned to celebrate with the home crowd before Clark had released the shot. Melbourne had five scorers in double figures in an even team performance without injured guard Matthew Dellavedova, who had missed two games with his latest concussion setback. Shea Ili (16 points, four assists) was important and Luke Travers finished with 16 points, including 15 in the first half.

The result was Melbourne's ninth consecutive home win over New Zealand and gave them a 6-1 record for the season. The Breakers (1-2) led by six points midway through the third period but ran out of steam in a tough loss that ended a gruelling road trip which included two exhibition matches against NBA opposition in the United States. Zylan Cheatham (30 points) and Parker Jackson-Cartwright (18) threatened to drive them to a rousing victory until the guard fouled out inside the last four minutes, and former NBA forward Anthony Lamb had 15 points and five rebounds on his NBL debut after signing as a replacement for injured import Justinian Jessup.

Breakers coach Mody Maor refused to look for positives: "We have very high standards for how we want to play and this isn't it. Lack of focus, missing key players, making mistakes on covers. I don't care where we flew from, what our schedule is, what happened the day before – I care zero. This isn't the level of execution that we expect from ourselves."
