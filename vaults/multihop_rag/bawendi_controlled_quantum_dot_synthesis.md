# Bawendi's Controlled Quantum Dot Synthesis

A decade after Ekimov and Brus's discoveries, Moungi Bawendi devised a method to stop the growth of tiny crystals in a solution when they reached a desired size: he first injected chemicals into a solution that instantly formed tiny crystals, then tweaked the temperature of the solution to halt the crystals' growth.

The precondition for the method was a research need rather than a product goal. Bawendi started working on quantum dots after meeting Brus, when the two worked at Nokia Bell Labs, headquartered in Murray Hill, N.J., where researchers needed high-quality quantum dots to study the physics of nanoparticles. "It wasn't because I wanted to make the best quantum dots possible for application," Bawendi said. "It was because we needed to make the best possible quantum dots to study them." It took years of trial and error to work out the method. "I'm deeply honored and surprised and shocked by the announcement this morning," Bawendi said on October 4 at an MIT news conference. "I'm especially honored to share this with Lou Brus," he said of his mentor. "I tried to emulate his scholarship and his mentoring style as a professor myself when I came to MIT."
