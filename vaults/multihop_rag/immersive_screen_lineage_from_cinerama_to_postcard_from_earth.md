# The Immersive Screen Lineage From Cinerama To Postcard From Earth

The immersive wraparound screen has a lineage running from 1950s Hollywood formats to Sphere's 270-degree view, The Sydney Morning Herald reported on 19 October 2023 — and by that measure the earlier formats fall short of the new venue.

The defining members of the lineage are three. As far back as the 1950s, Hollywood used three synchronised 35mm projectors to send a single image into a huge, deeply curved screen and called it Cinerama; it clocked in at just 146 degrees, well short of the Sphere's 270-degree view. Another 1950s technology, Circle-Vision 360 — essentially nine cameras positioned in an outward-facing circle — was used to create 360-degree film images, the most famous of which was Disneyland's America the Beautiful film. The newest entry, drawing a little less fanfare than U2's debut on the Sphere stage, was the premiere of Darren Aronofsky's Postcard From Earth, a 50-minute 18K resolution film shot to fit the 270-degree wraparound screen using an 18K Big Sky camera, with sequences shot in Greece, India, Mexico and the USA; the Big Sky, in layman's terms, delivers an image about 40 times greater in resolution than a 4K television screen.

The boundary condition is that scale is not the point. Like Lipson, Williams is cautious about becoming lost in the dramatic differences of scale, particularly when it is the storytelling of the bands and their discographies that is the key draw for the audience; the same principles apply, Williams says, whether the show is U2 at Sphere or, in another of his shows, Australian playwright Suzie Miller's Prima Facie starring Jodie Comer on the substantially smaller stages of London's Harold Pinter Theatre and Broadway's John Golden Theatre.
