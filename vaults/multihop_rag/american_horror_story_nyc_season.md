# American Horror Story: NYC

American Horror Story: NYC is the season Polygon's critic says solidified the reason she watched the show in the first place — namely that when AHS is good, it is really good. It was the Oct. 13 pick in the 2023-10-10 Halloween Countdown, listed on Hulu.

The season opens with a serial killer on the prowl, which, coupled with the leather-clad spectral figure that seems to be haunting the main characters, seems like typical AHS schlock. There is a serial killer who uncannily resembles Jeffrey Dahmer, and the scenes where he stalks and tortures his victims are pretty scary; there is a leather daddy ghost with no name or face; and there are a lot of dubiously consensual BDSM scenes. But critic Petrana Radulovic argues the real horror comes from the powerlessness of the queer community in the face of the AIDS crisis.

Creator Ryan Murphy builds an overwhelming, inescapable sense of dread. All AHS seasons hinge on their titular premise, which more often than not is a location, and it is not always done effectively — but in NYC, Murphy and the writers heighten the foreboding terror of being alone in a city of millions, of feeling helpless in a crowded room, of knowing there is something out to get you but not being able to do anything about it. The whole season is surprisingly subdued for AHS, and builds to a painfully poignant finale scene in which, for 10 minutes set to Kraftwerk's "Radioactivity," one of the main characters wordlessly trudges through the next 10 years of his life, watching everyone around him slowly succumb to AIDS as horror and reality blend together.
