# The Fashion Industry's Plus-Size Inclusivity Gap

2023 in fashion was especially devoid of inclusivity or plus-size models: according to Vogue's Business Size Inclusivity Report, only 0.6 per cent of looks shown throughout the autumn/winter 2023 fashion season were modelled by plus-size people — this despite the body positivity movement having made waves over the previous 10 years.

Plus-size models have long advocated for more inclusive sizing, both in stores and on the runway. Luxury brands and labels, however, often avoid creating plus-size fashion over claims that it is too expensive to make. "Designers won't make plus sizes unless it's for a photo opp where a thin person can be quirky," one commenter pointed out.

The gap persists despite the market's size. The plus-size clothing market was expected to reach $288bn in 2023, according to Future Market Insights, yet advocates maintain that they are unable to purchase clothing that is both sustainable and inclusive. "I have to wear fast fashion and high street to major red carpet moments, and that's great, but I want the option to wear high end items of my choosing and it's not available," Tess Holliday told Elle UK in 2019. "But it's either that or I run around naked. Plus-size fashion is not there yet."
