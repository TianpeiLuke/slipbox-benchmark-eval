# Kyle Pitts' Fantasy Outlook In Weeks 5 And 6 Of 2023

CBS Sports listed Kyle Pitts under "Sit Him" in Week 5 of the 2023 season and then under "Start Him" in Week 6, but the second call is deliberately hedged: after one good game against a zone-heavy defense, the author refuses to rush back to Pitts as a must-start because four really measly weeks preceded it.

The Week 5 case, published 4 October 2023 ahead of a home game with Houston, says it is hard to see the good in Pitts' game so far this season beyond his finding holes in zone coverage. Even Jonnu Smith has more red-zone targets than Pitts, three to two. On film there was no change in his explosiveness whatsoever: he was running at a slower-than-expected pace while the Jaguars still paid him plenty of attention defensively, a double whammy that left him without many targets; his longest opportunity was a well-placed deep ball from Desmond Ridder that he leapt for perfectly but couldn't secure to the ground, and he never separated from cornerback Tyson Campbell, with his best work coming on shorter routes against zone. Coach Arthur Smith said that week that Pitts isn't at 100%, adding "there are certain things, you see him, and he's doing really well right now and there are certain things that he can't [do well]. It's been a journey back. He's going to get there." The column concedes that if Smith is right then holding on to Pitts in fantasy makes sense, and that the matchup could create some receiving opportunities — maybe five catches for 50 yards, which is nothing to be excited about. The breakdown lists Atlanta at home versus Houston, the Falcons favoured by 2, an over/under of 41.5, an opponent ranked 18th versus tight ends, 7.6 projected points, a 13th tight end ranking, and year-to-date totals of 11 receptions on 21 targets for 121 yards, no touchdowns and 5.8 fantasy points per game. The verdict prefers Jake Ferguson, Zach Ertz and Dallas Goedert.

The Week 6 case, published 12 October 2023 ahead of a home game with Washington, upgrades him with an explicit cap on expectations: around 70 yards on six catches. If Week 5 had been Week 1 the author would have been encouraged and kept expectations high, but the matchup is a little tougher for Pitts and for Ridder, and teams have beaten the Commanders on deep shots to wide receivers rather than lumbering tight ends, so he is not convinced Pitts will maintain his target share or develop more explosive plays. In Week 5 Pitts came alive against Houston's zone-heavy defense with season highs in targets (11), catches (seven) and yards (87), his second game in three with a target share over 23% — though he played a season-low 53% of the snaps. Two things helped: he looked a little quicker off the snap and faster running straight, still lumbering when changing direction but perhaps slowly getting healthier, and Ridder seemed very comfortable throwing into tighter windows in zone coverage, the two showing good timing mainly on dig and out-breaking routes. Pitts continues to harvest a high average depth of target — 11.36 yards that week and 11.72 on the season, the highest among qualifying tight ends — while also carrying the lowest catch rate (56.3%) and worst yards after catch per reception (1.33) among them, with just 24 of his 208 yards coming after the catch. Washington plays zone at a slightly below-league-average rate, typically turning to man as soon as games get sideways, as in the second quarter the previous week or the fourth quarter against Buffalo; opponents average 32.0 pass attempts per game against them, ninth-fewest in football, and against tight ends who line up in the slot or out wide like Pitts the Commanders rank top-five in yards per catch and yards after catch per reception and have yet to allow a reception of 20-plus yards. They have allowed the seventh-most receptions on throws of 15-plus air yards, 19, but only two were caught by tight ends — Cole Kmet and Dallas Goedert — and neither went for more than 23 yards. The breakdown lists the Falcons favoured by 2.5, an over/under of 42.5, an opponent ranked 10th versus tight ends, 10.3 projected points, a ninth tight end ranking, and year-to-date totals of 18 receptions on 32 targets for 208 yards, no touchdowns and 7.8 fantasy points per game. The verdict still trots Pitts out over Logan Thomas, Dallas Goedert and Cole Kmet.
