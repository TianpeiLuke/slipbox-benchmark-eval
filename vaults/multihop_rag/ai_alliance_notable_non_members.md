# Notable Absences From The AI Alliance

Several prominent AI institutions were missing from the AI Alliance's early membership, as TechCrunch noted on 5 December 2023: Stanford, which has a prominent AI research lab in Stanford HAI; MIT, which is at the forefront of robotics research; and high-profile AI startups Anthropic, Cohere and Adept. TechCrunch asked IBM's PR, which first informed it of the Alliance's founding, about these omissions, and a press rep did not respond as of publication time. Its own surmise was that the same philosophical differences that kept Google and Microsoft away were likely at play, and that it is no accident that Anthropic, Cohere and Adept have relatively few open source AI projects to their names.

Nvidia is not a member either — a suspect absence given that the company is by far the dominant provider of AI chips and a maintainer of many open source models in its own right. TechCrunch speculates that the chipmaker perceived a conflict of interest in collaborating with Intel and AMD, or that it decided to cast its lot with Microsoft, Google and the rest of the tech giants opting out of the Alliance for strategic reasons.

Sriram Raghavan, VP of IBM's research AI division, told TechCrunch via email that the Alliance is, for now, focused on "members that are strongly committed to open innovation and open source AI" — implying, the reporter notes, that those who are not participating are not as strongly committed, a characterisation he doubts they would accept. "This of course is just the starting point," Raghavan added. "We welcome and expect more organizations to join in the future."
