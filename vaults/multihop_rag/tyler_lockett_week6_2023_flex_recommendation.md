# Tyler Lockett As A Week 6 2023 Flex Starter At Cincinnati

CBS Sports listed Tyler Lockett as a "Flex Starter" for Week 6 of the 2023 season at Cincinnati: the claim is that starting Lockett is a bet on upside, and the warrant is that Lockett is markedly better against man coverage while Geno Smith is markedly worse against it — so the call depends on Smith managing a minor resurgence against man, or on Seattle trailing and having to throw.

The coverage logic is the core of the argument. The Bengals consistently tailor their defensive coverage to the weaknesses of the offenses they face, so when a quarterback struggles against zone they play more zone, an approach that has worked in their favour in every game this year except Week 2 against the Ravens. Geno Smith has a 47.8% completion rate and a lowly 4.1 yards per attempt against man coverage this season and has barely thrown deep, with 4.3% of attempts travelling 20-plus air yards; a huge reason for the low numbers is dreadful pass protection, and the anticipated return of left tackle Charles Cross should give him more time, which in turn helps his receivers. Lockett, already the team leader in target share for the season, sees more targets on a route-run basis against man coverage (28%) than zone (21%), and does more with them — a full 2.0 more yards per catch and a much higher explosive play rate, 14.3% against man versus 5.0% against zone. Cincinnati has also turned on the juice for its pass rush, generating a pressure rate over 40% in each of its past three games, all against teams with suspect offensive lines, though they are expected to be without top cornerback Chidobe Awuzie, with D.J. Turner replacing him, while rotating a safety spot between Nick Scott and Jordan Battle.

The matchup profile explains why the recommendation stops at flex. Outside receivers have caught just 50% of their targets against the Bengals this season, third-lowest in football, but when they have caught the ball they have averaged 17.9 yards per catch, third-most in the league — a defense that has really only let up big games to receivers who catch touchdowns. The breakdown lists Seattle at Cincinnati, the Bengals favoured by 2.5, an over/under of 45, an opponent ranked 11th versus wide receivers, 11.9 projected points, a 36th receiver ranking, and year-to-date totals of 17 receptions on 27 targets for 157 yards, two touchdowns and 11.7 fantasy points per game. The verdict treats Lockett as more of a top-30 receiver who should be sat for Zay Flowers and Gabe Davis but started over Chris Godwin, Nico Collins, Garrett Wilson and Drake London.
