# Why The 1991 Debt Relief Bargain Cannot Simply Be Repeated

Replicating the 1991 debt-relief scenario would be difficult, which undercuts the inference that Egypt's current cooperation could earn comparable rewards. Back then there was general consensus that since Kuwait had been invaded it needed to be supported, and while Egypt's participation "was not met with any great joy in military circles, it was not a particularly hard sell and the debt relief was an immense added incentive," according to the Middle East Institute's Mirette Mabrouk. That consensus does not exist today.

The second attacked premise is the comparability of Egypt's contribution. Egypt's commitment would not be comparable with its burden in 1991, when it was the second-largest source of manpower in the US-led coalition, and so it couldn't expect anything like a similar level of economic assistance, Robert Satloff said.
