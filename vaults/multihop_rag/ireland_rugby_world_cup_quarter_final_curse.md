# Ireland's Rugby World Cup Quarter-Final Curse

Ireland's "quarter-final curse" is the pattern by which the team has never won a Rugby World Cup knockout match: on quarter-final weekend, for the eighth time in 10 Rugby World Cups, Ireland headed home defeated, and the wait for a first-ever knockout win goes on. This time was supposed to be different — this Ireland team were different, are different — but come the end, the result was the same.

The boundary of the pattern is how rarely Ireland have even threatened to break it. Since 1991, when Michael Lynagh's late try handed Australia victory, Ireland had not come close to ending their last-eight hoodoo; never mind winning one, they had not led a World Cup quarter-final since the first half of the 1995 defeat to France, a mammoth 10,353 days earlier.

The 2023 defeat will hurt more than any other, because here they had a legitimate chance to reverse the curse. Multiple times they got within a point of the All Blacks, only for their opponents to agonisingly pull away once more — and then there were those 37 phases, the most painful finish in Irish rugby history.
