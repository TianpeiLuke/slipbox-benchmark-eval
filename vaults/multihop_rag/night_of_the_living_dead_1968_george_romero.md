# Night Of The Living Dead: The Film That Launched The Modern Zombie Movie

Night of the Living Dead is the movie that launched the modern zombie film in the United States. George A. Romero's debut feature — starring Duane Jones, Judith O'Dea and Marilyn Eastman, and watchable on Max, Peacock, MGM Plus, Paramount Plus and Roku Channel — was written, directed, photographed and edited by the nascent zombie film master on a shoestring budget, which only adds to the eerie atmosphere and grounded terror.

A group of survivors hide out in an abandoned house in western Pennsylvania at the start of a zombie apocalypse; led by the level-headed Ben (Duane Jones), the group has to deal not only with zombies trying to break in but with internal conflicts stemming from disagreements over how to handle their precarious predicament. The film is the first example of Romero's typical blend of jaw-dropping and stomach-churning practical effects with astute social commentary.

Two boundary facts distinguish it. It came out a month before the MPAA film rating system existed, which meant a heaping amount of controversy when children were able to see the quite graphic movie in theatres. And it was never copyrighted, so it is in the public domain, because of an error by the original theatrical distributor.
