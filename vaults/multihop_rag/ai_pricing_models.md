# Pricing Models For AI Products

AI products divide across three pricing structures whose fit is governed by whether an LLM sits in the backbone: usage-based or consumption pricing, conventional periodic SaaS pricing, and hybrid structures that combine the two. The trajectory of usage-based pricing has organically aligned with the needs of large language models, given that there is significant variation in prompt/output sizes and resource utilization per user; OpenAI itself racks upward of $700,000 per day on compute, so to achieve profitability, these operation costs need to be allocated effectively.

The countervailing relation runs from buyer preference. Tying all costs to volume is generally unpopular with end users, who prefer predictable systems that allow them to budget more effectively. That pressure defines where the second structure applies: many applications of AI do not rely on LLMs as a backbone and can provide conventional periodic SaaS pricing, and without direct token calls to the model provider, companies engaged in establishing infrastructural or value-added layers for AI are likely to gravitate toward such pricing strategies.

The hybrid structure is the third arrangement, and it segments by customer size — tiered periodic payments and usage limits for SMBs, and uncapped usage-based tiers tailored to larger enterprises. This possibility is tied to the condition of widespread LLM adoption.

The range over which the model holds is bounded by the maturity of the technology and by the data dependency of LLMs. The technology is still nascent, and many companies will likely find success with both kinds of pricing models. As long as large language technology remains heavily dependent on the inflow of data, usage-based pricing will unlikely go away completely: the interdependence between data flow and cost structure will maintain the relevance of usage-based pricing in the foreseeable future. The question that prompted this analysis was whether the recent slowing growth of consumption- or usage-based priced tech products would lead startups building modern AI tools to pursue more traditional SaaS pricing, with OpenAI's token- and usage-based pricing schema as the prompting case.
