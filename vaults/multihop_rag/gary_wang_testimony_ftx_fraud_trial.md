# Gary Wang's Testimony At The FTX Fraud Trial

Gary Wang, the lesser-known co-founder of FTX, told a Manhattan courtroom on Thursday that he had committed financial crimes while working at FTX — including wire and commodities fraud — carried out with the help of Sam Bankman-Fried, FTX ex-engineering head Nishad Singh, and Caroline Ellison, who ran sister hedge fund Alameda Research and had been Bankman-Fried's girlfriend.

Asked by Assistant U.S. Attorney Nicolas Roos, "Did you commit financial crimes while working at FTX?" Wang responded, "Yes." Asked whether he saw any of the people he committed those crimes with in the courtroom, Wang — dressed in an oversized and wrinkled suit with a red tie and glasses — awkwardly stood up, looked around, and answered, "Yes"; asked who, he said, "Sam Bankman-Fried." He was one of two former friends of Bankman-Fried's from MIT who also worked at FTX while living with the founder in the Bahamas and who took the stand that week to testify against their former classmate, confidant and boss, a man who allegedly ran a crypto empire that defrauded thousands of customers out of billions of dollars. The trial, set to last six weeks, was to resume on Tuesday with key testimony expected from Ellison, considered the prosecution's star witness, who had already pleaded guilty to multiple charges. Bankman-Fried faces seven federal charges, including wire fraud, securities fraud and money laundering, that could put him in prison for the rest of his life. Thus far Bankman-Fried, 31, has remained mostly quiet in court, intently listening to witnesses and at times writing notes to his attorneys — but as Wang testified against him, he looked visibly upset, shifting his gaze from his former friend to the ground and at one point putting his head in his hands.
