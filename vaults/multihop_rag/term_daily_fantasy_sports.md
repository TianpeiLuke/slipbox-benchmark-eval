# Daily Fantasy Sports (DFS)

## Definition

Daily fantasy sports (DFS) is the corpus's term for the weekly, single-slate NFL lineups that outlets like Sporting News build each week for operators such as DraftKings, FanDuel and SuperDraft, distinct from a season-long fantasy league roster (doc_0056, doc_0276, doc_0331).

## Context

The corpus treats DFS as the distinct alternative to season-long fantasy football leagues, most explicitly in DraftKings' Week 14 picks column: "Most season-long fantasy leagues are entering their final week of the regular season in Week 14, and if your team isn't going to qualify for the postseason, then welcome to the DFS side of things, where every week is a new season" (doc_0331) — DFS is framed as the format you turn to once season-long fantasy is decided, because it does not carry any of a season-long league's injury reports, trades, or bad-matchup risk from week to week. The dominant DFS operators named across the corpus are DraftKings, FanDuel and SuperDraft, each running weekly "GPP tournament" lineups on a main slate (doc_0056, doc_0276, doc_0331). The corpus also shows DFS as the historical root of today's sportsbook industry: DraftKings is described as "known as a daily fantasy sports operator before becoming a renowned sportsbook" (doc_0062), and CBS Sports likewise reports that DraftKings' Kentucky sportsbook rewards program "caters especially to those who are daily fantasy sports players" (doc_0071), showing that the same companies and customer base carried over from DFS into legal sports betting.

## Key Characteristics

- **Short, fixed contest windows** — a DFS lineup is drafted for a single week's slate rather than an entire season, so a bad week does not carry forward (doc_0331).
- **Salary-cap roster construction** — DraftKings and FanDuel DFS contests assign each player a price against a fixed budget (e.g. a $50,000 budget on DraftKings, $60,000 on FanDuel for the Sunday main slate), forcing owners to balance expensive stars against cheaper "value" plays (doc_0331, doc_0056).
- **Operator-specific scoring rules** — the same players score differently depending on the DFS site: DraftKings uses full-point PPR with three-point yardage bonuses, while FanDuel uses half-point PPR with no such bonuses, and both use four-point passing touchdowns (doc_0331, doc_0056, doc_0276).
- **GPP tournament structure** — DFS lineups compete in "GPP tournaments" against the whole entry field for that slate, distinct from head-to-head season-long league play (doc_0056, doc_0331).
- **Budget-allocation strategy** — winning a large-field DFS tournament ("the milli-maker") depends on rostering cheap sleepers and value plays to free up salary for elite players elsewhere, rather than simply drafting the best players regardless of price (doc_0544).
- **Historical link to sports betting** — major DFS operators such as DraftKings later expanded into licensed sportsbooks, carrying their DFS-era branding and rewards programs into sports wagering (doc_0062, doc_0071).

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, daily fantasy sports is generally defined as a subset of fantasy sports in which participants assemble a roster of professional athletes under a salary cap and score points based on those athletes' real-game performance, with contests run over a short period — a single week or even a single day — rather than a full season; contests are typically split into cash games (head-to-head or 50-50 formats) and guaranteed prize pools (GPP) with tiered payouts, and the operator retains a cut of entry fees as rake (Wikipedia, "Daily fantasy sports," accessed 2026-09-02, https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Daily_fantasy_sports).
