# A Streamer's Case For Government Regulation Of Platforms

Kelly, the streamer known as MrsViolence, argues that the streaming community cannot rely on platforms to operate in the best interest of their creators, and instead has to hope that local governments will step in to regulate safety.

Her grounds are that no platform is better: she experienced "just as much abuse" as a woman on Twitch, and she is not optimistic about other livestreaming platforms like Rumble or YouTube; she adds that in its early days Twitch was just as unmoderated and violently misogynistic as parts of Kick are today. Her own position is constrained — as a creator who has worked in the games industry for more than a decade, she says she will continue making content wherever she can make money, because her chronic health conditions prevent her from returning to in-person hosting and coaching.

That is not to say the Ice Poseidon stream did not affect her. "I broke down in tears," she said. "I was like, now I'm part of this website that has pieces of shit running fucking wild and there's nothing I can do about it, and I refuse to go back and make pennies on Twitch." And on the futility of creator-side pressure: "I feel very small. There's nothing we can do and it's a fight that I think too many people are investing their lives in, and changing their careers over, their money, their income, because of something you can't change. I'm very numb. Desensitized is a great word. I feel for people, I get it. But at the same time I got to survive in this dumb world. Like, gas is up again!"
