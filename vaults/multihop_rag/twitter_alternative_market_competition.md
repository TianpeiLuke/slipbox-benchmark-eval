# The Twitter-Alternative Market

The market for Twitter alternatives is a crowded one, and its structure has two parts: a large field of rivals, and an incumbent whose network effect none of them has broken. On the rival side there are numerous services for consumers to try, including the open source-based platform Mastodon, a soon-to-be decentralized system from Bluesky, plus smaller startups like Spill, Spoutible and Post, as well as a new app from Meta called Instagram Threads.

Co-founder and CEO Gabor Cselle, who had exits to Google and Twitter prior to starting Pebble, blames that density in part: "I think the competitive landscape evolved faster than we had thought. I didn't think that quite as many people — established organizations and newcomers — would try to do the same thing that we were doing and in very similar ways." The second part of the model is the X factor: the default platform for short posts retains some pull, despite — or perhaps because of — new owner Elon Musk's antics, and though the company may not have yet succeeded from a business and financial perspective, it's been hard for others to duplicate its function as a breaking news platform and place for spirited debates. Pebble co-founder and CTO Michael Greer, previously Discord engineering head, states the general relation: "I think Threads and others have encountered this as well. The durability of the network effect of Twitter is stronger than anticipated."
