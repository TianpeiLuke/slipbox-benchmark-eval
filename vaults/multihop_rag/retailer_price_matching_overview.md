# Retailer Price-Matching Overview

To avoid overpaying, check the price-matching policy of the retailer you bought from: many retailers promise to match a lower price you have found elsewhere in order to keep customers happy and prevent them from going elsewhere, which The Verge noted on October 25th, 2023 was information that could come in handy with Black Friday and Cyber Monday just around the corner. The frustration it addresses is buying a new pair of headphones, an OLED TV, or a backpack only to find out you could have got it for a lot cheaper somewhere else.

Three cautions apply before using any of these policies. First, all price-matching policies have a number of rules attached to them: The Verge summarised some of them but advised readers to follow the links it provided and read each policy carefully. Second, and logically enough, retailers that serve as marketplaces for other sellers, such as Costco, do not have price-matching policies. Third, there are always exceptions to the rules — if you are looking for a deal on a product and you know it is available for a lower price elsewhere, or was previously offered at the same retailer, it never hurts to call or chat with a customer service rep and ask if they will match the price.

The illustration The Verge used: if Best Buy offers a better discount than Target on the latest Kindle Paperwhite, Target will refund you the difference.
