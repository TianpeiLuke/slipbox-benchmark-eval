# The Bring Them Home Now Hostage Billboard Campaign

Billboards with images of Israelis held hostage in Gaza went on display in Times Square and Madison Square Garden in Manhattan at 8am on the Friday of Emily Hand's ninth birthday, and in 1,500 other locations across 48 US cities, FOX News Lifestyle reported on 17 November 2023.

The effort is meant to remind Americans that some 240 Israeli civilians, including children like Emily, were snatched from concerts, from their homes and even from their beds in a surprise attack by Hamas terrorists against unarmed civilian targets. Bring Them Home Now, an organic volunteer group that came together in the hours after the attack, heads the international outreach effort. Emily, even at 9, is older than many of the hostages: the youngest was just 9 months old when snatched away "as a trophy of terror".

Thomas Hand said he hopes the billboards will remind Americans of the ongoing terror and antisemitism suffered by Israeli families, in a medium that cannot be defaced or removed from public view amid ongoing anti-Israeli hatred. "The great thing about these billboards is that these — I don't know what to call these people — won't be able to rip down the images," Hand said. "That's the one thing I can't understand. That they, children and the babies — they've been taken away, they're kidnapped, they're lost to us. And you know, just trying to make awareness, and they're ripping them off with glee, with happiness."
