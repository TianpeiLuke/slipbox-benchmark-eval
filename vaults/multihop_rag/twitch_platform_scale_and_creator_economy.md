# Twitch's Scale and the Unsustainable Creator Economy

Twitch is the livestreaming platform where the games industry's disasters converge, home to over 30 million daily viewers, as Polygon described it on 2023-10-19. Many would say the writing is on the wall for Twitch after endless headlines about losing top talent, poor management, bad platform policies, and creator burnout — but Polygon argues the idea that Twitch has lost its way after an ostensible golden era is an easy narrative that misses something much starker: if Twitch is in trouble in 2023, it is not because things have gotten worse, but because everything visible is the natural progression of an unsustainable system whose prognosis has always been terminal, unrecognized because it has been profitable.

The origin conditions were specific to their moment. In 2011, when Twitch first launched, content creation was not fully a career and people still uploaded their photos to Facebook; normal, everyday teens were not yet decrying the hungry maw of the internet, and the generational shift toward work-life balance had not taken hold. Twitch happened at exactly the right time, benefiting from a cadre of optimistic young users who grew up on the internet and were happy to give away as much of themselves as they could — in many cases without realizing it was happening, and unable to imagine the unsustainable system it would create. People were just having fun.

Things have changed. Twitch has crowned millionaires, like Félix "xQc" Lengyel, who wear thick diamond chains around their necks while streaming. At the same time viewership is dropping after years of platform growth partially bolstered by the COVID-19 pandemic, while top creators are streaming less. The gaming industry, which likes to boast about how much money it makes compared with other forms of entertainment, is laying off workers nearly every week at top studios, and the overall gaming market is shrinking — the cracks in the system that produces and promotes big-budget games are getting hard to ignore.
