# The CFP Committee's "Different Team" Justification For Florida State

The New York Times attacked the CFP committee's justification for omitting Florida State on 3 December 2023, arguing that CFP committee chair Boo Corrigan's statement — "Florida State is a different team. You look at who they are as a team without Jordan Travis — they are a different team" — is a well-informed opinion that is probably true, but is not a fact.

The premise under attack is that a team's quality without its injured starter can be reliably judged in advance. Choosing the best is entirely subjective and ultimately misleading given this is a sport that routinely features unpredictable results and unforeseen runs. The precedent cited is that the last time a team was relying on a third-string quarterback heading into the College Football Playoff — Ohio State in the inaugural season of the four-team field — it won the national title.

The rebuttal also concedes the disanalogy and then answers it. The difference between those Buckeyes and this Florida State team was that Ohio State won the Big Ten title game that year 59-0, while Florida State was in a close game with Louisville that was, quite frankly, not an enjoyable watch for people who love the excitement of big-time offense — perception, wrongly, became reality. Against that, one could make the case that Florida State is so good that it won a Power 5 conference championship game with a true freshman quarterback, and that in the CFP the Seminoles would have gotten second-string quarterback Tate Rodemaker back with a month to prepare for a semifinal game.
