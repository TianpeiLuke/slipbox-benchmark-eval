# Trade and Free Agency

## Definition

Trade and free agency are the two channels by which professional sports rosters change hands outside the amateur draft: a trade moves a player between clubs in exchange for other players, picks, or salary-cap considerations while he is still under contract, and free agency is the period in which a player whose contract (or club control) has ended is free to negotiate and sign with any team, including his current one.

## Context

The corpus documents both mechanisms operating in parallel across MLB, NFL, NHL, and AFL. In baseball, the trade market and free-agent market are explicitly linked: Juan Soto is framed as a top trade candidate precisely because he is "entering his final year of club control" and the price to acquire him will only rise "now that he's a year and a half closer to free agency," and he was ultimately traded from the Padres to the Yankees at the 2023 Winter Meetings rather than reaching the open market at all. The Winter Meetings tracker shows signings (Eduardo Rodriguez to the Diamondbacks, Craig Kimbrel to the Orioles) and trades (Soto to the Yankees) being reported side by side as the offseason's two parallel transaction tracks. In the NFL, the corpus ties trades to a fixed calendar event — the "2023 trade deadline," before which the 49ers acquired Chase Young — while pending free agency (Kirk Cousins "approaching free agency," Michael Pittman Jr. "scheduled to be a free agent at season's end") is treated as a separate, later-arriving roster risk that mock-draft analysts weigh when projecting a team's draft needs. The NHL and AFL passages show the same free-agent path playing out for individual players: Patrick Kane was "traded last year at the deadline" and then, once his contract with the Rangers ended, "hit free agency for the first time in his career"; Adams and McStay are each described as having "arrived... as an unrestricted free agent."

## Key Characteristics

- **Contract-status distinction** — a trade moves a player who is still under contract to his current club; free agency begins only once that contract or club-control period has lapsed, as with Soto's approaching free agency versus his active trade candidacy.
- **Fixed deadlines govern trades** — leagues set a cutoff (the NFL's 2023 trade deadline, before which the 49ers acquired Chase Young; MLB's "summer deadline," which the Soto piece contrasts with the harder-to-project offseason) after which in-season trades are no longer permitted.
- **Compensation differs by channel** — trades exchange players, draft picks, or cap-related pieces between clubs (the 49ers sent a 2024 third-round pick for Chase Young), while free-agent moves are direct contract signings between a player and a new or returning club (Eduardo Rodriguez's four-year, ~$20M/year deal with Arizona).
- **A single player can pass through both channels sequentially** — Patrick Kane was traded away from Chicago at last year's deadline, then, once his Rangers tenure ended with a playoff exit, reached unrestricted free agency, illustrating that trade and free agency are sequential stages of the same player-movement pipeline rather than mutually exclusive categories.
- **Roster-planning implications extend beyond the transaction itself** — pending free agency (Cousins, Pittman) is treated by draft analysts as a signal of future roster need, just as trade acquisitions (Young) raise downstream questions about whether the move is a short-term "rental" or requires salary-cap restructuring to retain the player long-term.
