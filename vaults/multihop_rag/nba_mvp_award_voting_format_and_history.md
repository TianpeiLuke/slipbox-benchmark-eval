# The NBA MVP Award: Voting Format And History

The NBA MVP is selected annually by a panel of sportswriters and broadcasters who vote for the players they believe are the best in the league, under a format that started in 1980 in which each panel member votes for their top three players.

The scoring is weighted: every first-place vote is worth five points, every second-place vote three, and third-place a single point. One player rarely receives all of the first-place votes — that last happened in the 2015-16 season, when Steph Curry of the Golden State Warriors earned all possible first-place points.

The award's defining feature for bettors is the absence of a rulebook: because the award has no set criteria for selection, it is notoriously difficult to predict who will get the nod, there are often late-season shifts among the favorites, and there are even seasons in which the winner is considered an upset and more prominent players are snubbed. Historically, Kareem Abdul-Jabbar has the most-ever NBA MVP awards with six — three with the Milwaukee Bucks and three with the Los Angeles Lakers — ahead of Bill Russell (Boston Celtics) and Michael Jordan (Chicago Bulls) with five each.
