# Prediction: AI Copyright Lawsuits Stall And Multiply

TechCrunch predicts that in 2024 a lot of the AI lawsuits filed in 2023 will fall by the wayside, as companies stonewall critical information like training data and methods, making allegations such as the use of thousands of copyrighted books difficult to prove in court. A fair number of suits were filed in 2023 but few saw any real movement, let alone success, and most suits over copyright and other missteps in the AI industry are still pending.

The forecast has a hedge built in. Many of these lawsuits were filed essentially on principle, and though they may not succeed, they may crack the process open far enough during testimony and discovery that companies would rather settle than have certain information come to light.

The second half of the prediction is expansion into new grounds: 2024 will bring new lawsuits pertaining to misuse and abuse of AI, such as wrongful termination, bias in hiring and lending, and other areas where AI is being put to work without a lot of thought. But while a few egregious examples of misuse will be punished, a lack of relevant laws specific to it means enforcement will necessarily only haphazardly be brought to court.
