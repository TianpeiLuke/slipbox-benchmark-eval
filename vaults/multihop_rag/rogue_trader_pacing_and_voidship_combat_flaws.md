# Rogue Trader: Pacing And Voidship Combat Flaws

Against her own praise for the characters, environments, writing, lore, and flow of battles, Polygon's reviewer raises two complaints that target the game's progression and its naval system rather than its content.

The first attacks the value of levelling up. By the end of the first chapter she had leveled up 16 times, with each level offering marginal rewards such as moving slightly farther during a turn in combat or having a higher parry chance when attacked; the slow drip-feed makes each level feel less important, so that even while growing stronger she does not get a sense of long-term satisfaction. That becomes acute at a roadblock like the end of Act 1, where an incredibly tough boss rolls out of nowhere and spanks her crew and she cannot leave to grind experience elsewhere — she eventually found a way to cheese the fight by focusing on her melee fighters' positioning, but only after far too long banging her head against the wall, and the victory tasted like ash after the frustration. The second complaint is voidship combat, which she finds clunky and frustrating: like the squad-based skirmishes, naval encounters are turn-based and position-dependent, but where space naval battles should feel tense she is mostly annoyed at having to continually rotate her ship and set up zones of attack. Her wish is to delegate both character levelling and ship combat to her Senechal, the better to appreciate everything Rogue Trader is doing so well.
