# Price To EBITDA Hides Debt: A Worked Example

Price by revenue or price by EBITDA is an incorrect multiple, because those denominators sit above the interest line and so make two companies with different debt look identically valued. The worked example: companies A and B both have a market cap of ₹100 and are similar in all respects except debt levels, with the same revenue of ₹100, the same gross margin, the same EBITDA margin of 20 per cent and thus the same EBITDA of ₹20; A has zero debt while B has debt of ₹10. Because revenue and EBITDA are the same, market cap divided by revenue, price by sales, or market cap by EBITDA all give both companies the same valuation.

Now assume depreciation of ₹10, an interest cost of 10 per cent and zero taxes. Net profit of A is ₹10 while that of B is ₹9, so although the two look identical on Price/Revenue and Price/EBITDA, A is cheaper on the PE ratio: A's PE is 10 and B's is 11.1. The warrant is that on a relative valuation basis you must choose A instead of B, and the point of the example is that you would not have spotted the difference had you used the incorrect metrics.
