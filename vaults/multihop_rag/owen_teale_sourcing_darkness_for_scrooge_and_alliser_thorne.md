# How Owen Teale Sources Darkness For Scrooge And Alliser Thorne

Owen Teale's account of playing villains is a simple three-part mechanism: an inner "sense of nothingness" that appears when he is away from other people, which he channels into hatred on screen, and which he will summon again to make Dickens' Ebenezer Scrooge convincing in an upcoming Melbourne production of Jack Thorne's award-winning adaptation of A Christmas Carol, The Sydney Morning Herald reported on 20 October 2023.

The structure of the relation runs from his own sociability to its inverse. "It's true, I have started to play a lot of dark characters," he says, smiling, "and at first it was a great challenge because I'm quite a sociable fellow." Asked how he finds that darkness, he answers: "It's a sense of nothingness. In all honesty, I'm not very good at being completely on my own. I come alive when I'm with other people. So staying in hotel rooms away from my family isn't great and it doesn't get any easier. There's something empty and a bit scary within me when I'm not with people. I think that's where that stuff comes from." The interviewer's framing is that the avuncular man in amber-coloured spectacles and leather jacket on the line from Leeds is a master of darkness.

The range over which the mechanism holds is demonstrated by his most famous scene: when Ser Alliser Thorne drove a dagger into the heart of Game of Thrones' Byronic hero Jon Snow in the final episode of Season 5, he left the show's 10 million viewers in a profound state of shock. "There was Kit … who was so beautiful. And he's such a lovely man. By connecting to this feeling of emptiness, of having nothing inside of me, I was able to look at him and everything he had - his looks, his grace - and feel pure hatred," Teale says of Kit Harington. "For all of Ser Alliser's explanations [for the murder], deep down he just has a hatred of Jon Snow's popularity and gifts." Offered the comparison with Iago, he agrees: "Very like Iago."
