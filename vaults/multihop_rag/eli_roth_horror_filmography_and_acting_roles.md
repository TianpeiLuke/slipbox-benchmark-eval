# Eli Roth's Horror Filmography And Acting Roles

Eli Roth has helmed over 20 films as a director, more than double that as a producer, and has also appeared onscreen — notably as Sgt. Donny "The Bear Jew" Donowitz in Quentin Tarantino's Inglourious Basterds (2009) and, most recently, in 2023's comically maligned The Idol, where he played a wracked Live Nation rep, The Age reported on 15 November 2023.

He has been horror's most provocative maestro for decades, a filmmaker who finds sinister glee in pushing the envelope; it was his film Hostel (2005) that spawned the term "torture porn". Of returning to the slasher form he says: "Every time I make a horror movie, it's like a badge of honour for me. It's like, this is my chance to make a slasher film, you know? I grew up with Freddie, Jason, Michael Myers, Chucky – I loved those movies so much – and in the '90s, we had Ghostface and other slashers that were overlooked, like Mute Witness (1995) which is one of my favourites. I just thought, well, it's time for a new slasher. I want a new killer in town, so let's create another one." Recalling a Q&A screening for his feature debut Cabin Fever (2002) at Glebe's now defunct Valhalla cinema about 20 years earlier, Roth remembers "a kid coming up to me after, with his friend Angus, and we talked for half an hour about how much he loved the movie. And I was doing Angus' radio show the next day and that kid came up to me again and he's like, 'Yeah, I made a short film called Saw.' It was Leigh Whannell."

Although he was not involved in The Idol's troubled first incarnation, Roth says he enjoyed his experience on creator Sam Levinson's set — "You saw this negative stuff come up [in the press] and I'd be thinking, 'What are people talking about?'" He completed The Idol right before directing Thanksgiving and credits the experience with "switching up my brain to use another side of my creativity". "I think that's why I really am an actor's director and why actors like working with me, is that they know I understand what they go through," he says. "In the rehearsal process with the cast, or getting ready to do a scare scene, I want to give them the room to go off-script a bit until we find something interesting, and that's all from my personal experience as an actor."
