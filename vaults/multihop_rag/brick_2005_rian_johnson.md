# Brick (2005)

Rian Johnson's Brick splits audiences: for fans it is possibly the best ever neo-noir not made by the Coen brothers, while for detractors it is a weird "Is this serious or satire?" experience built around a mannered, oddball script that reduces noir to a literal high school teen drama — though Polygon notes the fans might agree with every word of that description and simply mean it positively. It is rentable on Amazon, Vudu, and Redbox.

Joseph Gordon-Levitt stars as the hard-bitten PI equivalent, a high school kid devoted to a girl who jilted him and then turned up dead. Johnson's script — presaging his later, equally complex murder mysteries Knives Out and Glass Onion — treats high school drama like big-time drama and puts Dashiell Hammett-style dialogue in the mouths of modern teenagers, for an effect Tasha Robinson calls a wee bit comedic and a whole lot of fun. Her key point about its genre standing is that the film is full of fun surprises but also respects the genre and plays fair by noir fans rather than subverting the classics.
