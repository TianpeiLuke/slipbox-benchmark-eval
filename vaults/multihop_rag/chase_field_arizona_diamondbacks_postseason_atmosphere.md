# Chase Field: Arizona Diamondbacks Postseason Atmosphere

Chase Field ranked No. 8 — last — among the eight remaining 2023 playoff teams' home-field environments in The New York Times' 8 October 2023 ranking, chiefly because of the risk that Dodgers fans would fill it. The park opened in 1998, has a capacity of 48,405 and drew an average attendance of 24,212 in 2023.

Its memorable postseason moment is Game 7 of the 2001 World Series, which started with a pair of 20-game winners in Roger Clemens and Curt Schilling and ended with Hall of Famers on the mound in Mariano Rivera and Randy Johnson; in what is remembered as one of the greatest postseason games of all time, Luis Gonzalez blooped a bases-loaded single to score Jay Bell and deny the Yankees their fourth consecutive title.

The ranking's reasoning was explicitly not about the fans: "We're sorry, D-Backs fans. It's not you. It's them." A tremendously impressive two-game sweep in Milwaukee carried the Diamondbacks into a division series showdown with the Dodgers. It is not that the writers doubt Arizona can beat L.A. at home — the Diamondbacks split six games against the Dodgers at Chase Field that season — but it is less than a seven-hour drive from Dodger Stadium to downtown Phoenix, and they were concerned that all the quirks of the ballpark could be rendered meaningless if there is a bunch of Dodgers blue in the crowd. There is a lot to like: the pool, the retractable roof. The writers invited fans to prove them wrong and added that if they had to go on the road for a playoff game, at least in that round, they would choose Chase Field — "Consider it a compliment! Or maybe we just like the dry heat."
