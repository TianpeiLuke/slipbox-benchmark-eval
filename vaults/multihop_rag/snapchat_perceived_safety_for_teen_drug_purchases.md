# Snapchat's Perceived Safety For Teen Drug Purchases

Amy Neville, a parent in the suit against Snap, argues that Snapchat creates an aura of safety around an otherwise dangerous activity, drawing teenagers into drug purchases they would never have made in person.

Her grounds are her son's own disposition and behaviour. She described Alexander, who died at 14 after taking a counterfeit Oxycontin tablet he procured through the app, as sensitive, impulsive and curious about many things — including drugs — but also hesitant about putting himself in dangerous situations.

The warrant joining the two is what the app removed from the transaction: by using Snapchat, Alexander was able to avoid an in-person meetup and have the pills delivered straight to his door. "The old way of scary back-alley type transactions – Alexander would have never done that," she said.
