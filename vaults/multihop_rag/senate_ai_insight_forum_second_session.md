# Second Senate AI Insight Forum, October 2023

Senator Charles Schumer (D-NY) hosted Silicon Valley's AI leaders on Tuesday as the US Senate reconvened its AI Insight Forum for a second time, and on leaving the meeting Schumer told the assembled press that "it is clear that American leadership on AI can't be done on the cheap," reporting that almost all of the experts in the forum called for robust, sustained federal investment in the private and public sectors to achieve American-led transformative and sustainable innovation in AI.

The guest list for this second session included manifesto enthusiast Marc Andreessen and venture capitalist John Doerr, along with Max Tegmark of the Future of Life Institute and NAACP CEO Derrick Johnson. According to a release from Schumer's office, the agenda covered "the transformational innovation that pushes the boundaries of medicine, energy, and science, and the sustainable innovation necessary to drive advancements in security, accountability, and transparency in AI."

On cost, National Security AI Commission estimates put the required federal investment at around $32 billion a year. Schumer believes those funding challenges can be addressed by "leveraging the private sector by employing new and innovative funding mechanisms — like the Grand Challenges prize idea." He framed the goal as a balance: "We must prioritize transformational innovation, to help create new vistas, unlock new cures, improve education, reinforce national security, protect the global food supply, and more," but in doing so "we must act sustainably in order to minimize harms to workers, civil society and the environment." As he put it, "We need to strike a balance between transformational and sustainable innovation. Finding this balance will be key to our success."

Schumer closed by saying the participants would leave "with a deeper understanding of how to approach American-led AI innovation," and that the conversation would continue "in weeks and months to come — in more forums like this and committee hearings in Congress — as we work to develop comprehensive, bipartisan AI legislation."
