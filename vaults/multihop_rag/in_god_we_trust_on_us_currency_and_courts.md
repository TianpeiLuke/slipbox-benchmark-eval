# "In God We Trust" On Currency, In The Anthem And In The Courts

"In God We Trust" first appeared on coins from the U.S. Mint in 1865 at the end of the Civil War, and was added to paper currency in 1957 after it was made the official national motto. Some reports indicate the phrase was used by Pennsylvania troops in the American Revolution and again by the state's soldiers in the Civil War, and Francis Scott Key wrote in the now largely unknown final stanza of the poem that became the National Anthem: "Then conquer we must/when our cause is just/and this be our motto/in God is our trust."

Its legal standing has been repeatedly tested and upheld: "In God We Trust" on currency has been deemed constitutional in numerous rulings by the Supreme Court and lower courts. The U.S. Congress voted to reaffirm it as the national motto by 396-6 in 2011.
