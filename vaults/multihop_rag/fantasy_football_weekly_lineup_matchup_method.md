# Weekly Fantasy Lineup Method: Set Lineups By Matchup, Not By Draft Order

CBS Sports' Week 13 lineup-decisions column prescribes a matchup-first procedure for setting a weekly Fantasy Football lineup: Fantasy Football is all about the matchups, and weekly lineup decisions should not be determined by the order in which players were picked in the draft, whatever hopes and intentions accompanied that draft. The steps are: first, check who your players play; second, make sure the right guys are in the lineup and the wrong guys are out; third, base those judgments on the evidence available — healthy personnel, defensive schemes, track records and key details of offenses — accepting that these are educated guesses rather than certainties; fourth, work through every game and focus only on the players who are not obvious starts or sits, because the things we know can help minimize the impact of the things we don't know.

The method carries explicit scope conditions. It is too early in the process to be absolutely sure which matchups will be easy and which will be tough, so the outputs are educated guesses, not settled answers. The procedure also does not apply to obvious plays: the column deliberately skips them, on the reasoning that you don't need to be told to start Christian McCaffrey. A fifth, forward-looking step extends the method beyond the current week — having a keen eye for matchups dictated by the remaining schedule, supported by projected Strength of Schedule rankings broken down by position, which the author updates on SportsLine to show how the schedule affects every Fantasy-relevant player over the upcoming four weeks, the playoff stretch and the entire season, along with buy-low, sell-high and other trade advice. The stated payoff of following the procedure is comfort: feeling comfortable with your Fantasy lineup before the games start is described as the best feeling in the world.
