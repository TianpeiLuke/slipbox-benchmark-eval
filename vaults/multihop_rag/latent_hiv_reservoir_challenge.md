# The Latent HIV Reservoir Challenge

The core obstacle to curing HIV is that the virus builds resilient genetic reservoirs inside human cells: "The existing challenge in HIV treatment lies in the virus creating resilient genetic reservoirs within human cells," Elena Herrera-Carrillo, an associate professor of infectious disease at Amsterdam University Medical Centers, told Live Science.

The mechanism runs as follows. HIV infects immune cells that are normally used to fight infection in the virus's host, and uses an immune cell's machinery to insert its own DNA into the host's genome, which allows the virus to replicate. If an HIV infection is not treated, it can lead to acquired immunodeficiency syndrome (AIDS), which results in a severely weakened immune system and leaves the infected person highly vulnerable to other infections, cancers and early death. The reservoir phenomenon, known as "latent HIV," occurs when the virus infects a type of immune cell called CD4+ memory T cells, which can persist for decades. Herrera-Carrillo's lab focuses on using CRISPR to edit cells that harbor HIV reservoirs despite ongoing cART therapy, because cART can suppress viral replication but, if the treatment is interrupted, "the dormant proviruses can reactivate, making a cure elusive," she said. That relation — suppression holds only while therapy continues, and integrated proviral DNA survives it — is what defines the boundary of what current therapy can achieve.
