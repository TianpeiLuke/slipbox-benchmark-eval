# Twitch Demographics Versus Las Vegas Culture

The argument is that Las Vegas is a poor fit for TwitchCon specifically, because the Strip's costs and culture clash with Twitch's user base — even though these complaints about the Las Vegas Strip aren't new, and are usually accepted in the trade-off for a weekend of partying or networking at a business conference. Twitch's usership skews younger: Twitch reports that in 2022, over 70% of its viewers were between 18 and 34, and they may not have the disposable income to drop hundreds every day on meals and rides; attendees who do drink complained that Las Vegas is significantly more expensive than San Diego.

The second ground is exclusion by venue policy and habit: the city's emphasis on drinking and gambling is incompatible with much of Twitch's usership, since Las Vegas venues are overwhelmingly 21+, which excludes a significant portion of TwitchCon's streamers and viewers — and even if they are of legal drinking age, today's young adults tend to drink less than previous generations did. The third is sensory: Las Vegas' frenetic pacing is enthralling for many, but some Twitch users complained that the city's energy didn't fit the Twitch demographic, and Moni's UserVoice post noted that the sensory overload that makes Las Vegas feel like Las Vegas isn't as alluring for introverted gamers. Twitch streamer herokerrey supplied the warrant: "I want to be mindful of the target audience Twitch serves — I would probably assume many of us felt the same about the overstimulation. While I understand in-person events are a hype/stimulating experience, pairing it with one of the most active cities in the world may not have been the best pick."
