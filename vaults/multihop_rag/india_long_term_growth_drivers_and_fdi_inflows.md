# India Long-Term Growth Drivers and FDI Inflows

The long-term outlook for the Indian economy is supported by a set of connected growth drivers. An important positive factor is India's large and fast-growing middle class, which is helping to drive consumer spending, and the rapidly growing domestic consumer market alongside a large industrial sector has made India an increasingly important investment destination for multinationals in many sectors, including manufacturing, infrastructure and services.

A second driver is digital transformation, which is expected to accelerate the growth of e-commerce and change the retail consumer market landscape over the next decade, attracting leading global multinationals in technology and e-commerce. The quantified basis is connectivity: by 2030, 1.1 billion people in India will have internet access, more than doubling from the estimated 500 million internet users in 2020, and the rapid growth of e-commerce together with the shift to 4G and 5G smartphone technology will boost home-grown unicorns.

These drivers feed back into investment. The large increase in FDI inflows to India evident over the past five years is continuing, with strong momentum even during the pandemic years of 2020-2022, boosted by large investments from global technology multinationals such as Google and Facebook attracted to India's large, fast-growing domestic consumer market, as well as a strong upturn in inflows from manufacturing firms. Overall, India is expected to remain one of the world's fastest growing economies over the next decade, making it one of the most important long-term growth markets for multinationals across industries from autos, electronics and chemicals to banking, insurance, asset management, health care and information technology.
