# Norway's Ban On Meta's Consentless Tracking Ads

Norway's data protection authority asked a European Union regulator to take a binding decision on whether its emergency sanction on Facebook and Instagram tracking and profiling users for ad targeting without their consent should be made permanent and applied across the EU single market, not just locally.

The move could lead to a blanket ban on Meta running tracking ads without consent across the EU single market if the European Data Protection Board agrees the action is merited — though Meta may switch to asking users for permission to run "personalized ads" before any Board action, as it has claimed it intends to. The Datatilsynet issued its local ban on Meta tracking and profiling users without consent in July, using powers in the GDPR that enable concerned regulators to apply temporary measures, lasting three months, in their markets if they see an urgent need to act to protect citizens' data. Meta's lead regulator for the GDPR remains the Irish Data Protection Commission, which would normally lead on any enforcement, so the Norwegian DPA's emergency action circumvents the regulation's so-called "one-stop-shop" mechanism — and gives the Norwegian authority the option to refer ongoing concerns to the EDPB, as it has now done.

Meta has continued to flout the Datatilsynet order — which includes a daily fine of up to one million NOK (around $100,000) per day for non-compliance — ignoring the authority's requirement not to run tracking ads without permission, per a spokesperson for the DPA. The company instead sought a court injunction against the order, but earlier that month an Oslo court rejected Meta's arguments, affirming the DPA's right to act. Reached by email, the EDPB confirmed receipt of the authority's request: "The EDPB Secretariat will now assess completeness of the file. Once this assessment is complete, the deadline under art. 66(4) GDPR starts running and the Board will have two weeks to adopt its urgent binding decision," a spokeswoman said, declining to offer any steer on how long the Board's assessment would take.
