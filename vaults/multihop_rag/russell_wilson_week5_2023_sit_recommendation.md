# Russell Wilson As A Week 5 2023 Sit Against The Jets

CBS Sports listed Russell Wilson under "Sit Him" for Week 5 of the 2023 season: the claim is that this should be one of his toughest matchups of the season, and the warrant is that Wilson's fantasy value depends on outside receivers and the deep ball, exactly the two things the Jets take away, behind a pass rush Denver's line cannot hold.

The grounds are matchup-specific. The Jets have given up over 16 fantasy points to a quarterback only once all season — Dak Prescott in Week 2 — with Josh Allen in Week 1 and Patrick Mahomes in Week 4 both held under 16. They get to the quarterback without blitzing: a 19.5% blitz rate is bottom-five while their 41.6% pressure rate is third-best in the league and consistent, their only low game coming at Dallas where they still bothered Prescott 32.5% of the time. Denver's front five allows the sixth-highest pressure rate in the league at 39.5%, and the Bears pressured Wilson on 32.3% of dropbacks the previous week, their second-highest rate of the season, albeit with heavy blitzing. Wilson's own pressure numbers do not look bad over the season, but they are weighted by Week 1 against the Raiders; across his past three high-volume games, against Washington, Miami and Chicago, he completed 56.4% under pressure for 8.0 yards per attempt with a better-than-most 5.1% touchdown rate and a strong 2.6% off-target rate — and of those, only the off-target rate is better under pressure than without it. He shouldn't have much time this week.

The deep-ball and alignment evidence closes the argument. The Jets are good against throws of 15-plus air yards, allowing a 40.7% completion rate with four interceptions and an in-context low 23.1 yards per grab, seventh-best. Wilson ranks top-10 among quarterbacks in completion rate (50%) and yards per attempt (17.2) with the second-lowest off-target rate (12.5%) on those long throws but is below league average in attempts, and his numbers are inflated by the Commanders game and the Hail Mary he hit. Against outside receivers the Jets allow the second-highest catch rate to perimeter players at 79.4% but are top-five in yards per catch (10.7), average depth of target (10.38) and completions allowed of 20-plus yards (three, best in the NFL), and outside players have seen the third-fewest targets because teams know not to attack there — a problem for Wilson, who has sent 50 of his 132 passes, 38%, to outside receivers, along with seven of his nine touchdowns. His recent scoring is also flattered by soft conditions: his 28-plus fantasy-point game the previous week came against a bad Bears defense with a beat-up secondary, and his other high score, Week 2 against the Commanders, included 92 yards and a Hail Mary touchdown in the final 0:48 worth an extra 9.7 points, though he still had 22.2 without it. The forecast is a much more subdued and boring version of Wilson's game, possibly resembling Mahomes' line against the Jets; the author would rather start Jared Goff, Trevor Lawrence, Joshua Dobbs and C.J. Stroud.
