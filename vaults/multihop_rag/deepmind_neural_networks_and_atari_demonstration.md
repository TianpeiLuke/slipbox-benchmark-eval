# Neural Networks And DeepMind's Atari Demonstration

A neural network is a complex algorithm created in the image of the human brain, essentially a giant mathematical system that spends days, weeks or even months identifying patterns in large amounts of digital data. First developed in the 1950s, these systems could learn to handle tasks on their own: after analysing names and addresses scribbled on hundreds of envelopes, for instance, they could read handwritten text. Flush with cash, DeepMind hired researchers who specialised in them.

DeepMind took the concept further, building a system that could learn to play classic Atari games such as Space Invaders, Pong and Breakout to illustrate what was possible. This got the attention of another Silicon Valley powerhouse, Google, and specifically Larry Page, who saw a demonstration of DeepMind's machine playing Atari games and wanted in. The boundary of what the field could then show was pushed again in the spring of 2012, when Geoffrey Hinton, a 64-year-old professor at the University of Toronto, and two graduate students published a research paper that showed the world what AI could do by training a neural network to recognise common objects such as flowers, dogs and cars.
