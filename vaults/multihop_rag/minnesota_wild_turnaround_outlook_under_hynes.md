# Minnesota Wild Turnaround Outlook Under Hynes

Yardbarker argued on Nov. 27, 2023 that John Hynes's main job in Minnesota was simply to restore confidence in his group, because the roster had the ability to play better even though the division would make climbing back hard.

The grounds: the Wild would certainly get a boost in the standings once Marc-André Fleury and Filip Gustavsson improved their play between the pipes, a likely scenario given their stellar performance the previous season. Against that, they again had the misfortune of being in quite a competitive division — the Avalanche and Stars occupied first and second place as expected powerhouses, while the Blues, Predators and Coyotes all sat at or above the .500 mark and, especially in Nashville's case, had posted decent underlying numbers. The warrant joining the two is that the roster's underperformance is a confidence problem rather than a talent problem: Kirill Kaprizov in particular, despite okay point production, had looked like a complete non-factor on some nights. The team also hoped a significant change behind the bench could boost its league-worst penalty kill, operating at just 66.7%, given that penalty killing had been an area of strength for Nashville the previous season, when it finished tied for fourth in the NHL at 82.6%.
