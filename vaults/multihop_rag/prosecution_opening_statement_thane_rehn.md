# Prosecution Opening Statement By Thane Rehn

Assistant US attorney Thane Rehn opened the government's case by pointing at Bankman-Fried and telling the jury, "This man stole billions of dollars from thousands of people." Delivering the statement in the cadence of a high schooler reciting lines in a student play, Rehn dodged explaining cryptocurrency to the jury and instead punched hard on Bankman-Fried lying and stealing, listing the evidence to come: internal company files, what customers were told, the testimony of his co-conspirators, and his own words.

Rehn's narrative contrasted the lifestyle with its foundation. At this time last year, he said, Bankman-Fried had a luxury lifestyle as CEO of the crypto exchange FTX — he hung out with Tom Brady, was on magazine covers, lived in a $30 million penthouse, and spent time with world politicians. "All of that was built on lies," Rehn said. He also told the jury Bankman-Fried sold stock in FTX and borrowed millions from lenders by lying, and laid out the sequence familiar from the news: in May and June of 2022, Alameda Research — the crypto trading company ostensibly helmed by Caroline Ellison — did not have enough to pay its bills, so it pulled customer money to repay loans; by September the hole in the FTX balance sheet was so big that customers could never be repaid.

The Verge's reporter, writing on October 4th, 2023, recorded the defendant's demeanour as the counterpoint: she had never seen Bankman-Fried so still, with the characteristic leg-jiggling absent and only occasional glances at Rehn. His hair was shorn, the result of a haircut from a fellow prisoner per the Wall Street Journal, and he wore a suit bought at a discount at Macy's, also per the Journal; it hung on him, and he appeared to have lost some weight.
