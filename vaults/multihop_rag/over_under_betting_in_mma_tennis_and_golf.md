# Over/Under Betting In MMA, Tennis And Golf

In individual sports the over/under line is denominated not in combined points but in the sport's own unit of duration or performance: rounds in MMA, games or sets in tennis, and strokes relative to par in golf.

In an MMA context, over/under bets often refer to the duration of the fight rather than points or scores. In a UFC bout between Fighter A and Fighter B the bookmaker sets an over/under line at 2.5 rounds; a bettor who believes both fighters have aggressive styles and the fight will end early bets the "under." If the fight ends midway through the second round by knockout or submission, the total rounds would be 1.5, below the set total of 2.5, so the "under" wins. Conversely, if the fight extends beyond the midway point of the third round, "over" bets win.

At tennis betting sites, over/under betting typically revolves around the total number of games or sets played in a match. In a Grand Slam match between Player A and Player B the bookmaker sets the total at 40.5 games for the entire match; a bettor anticipating a closely contested match with many extended sets bets the "over." With set scores of 7-5, 6-7, 7-6 and 6-4, the combined total is 42 games, which exceeds 40.5, so the "over" wins.

At golf betting sites, over/under betting often pertains to a golfer's score for a specific round or the entire tournament relative to par. In a major tournament where Rory McIlroy is playing, the bookmaker sets an over/under line at 69.5 strokes for his first-round score; a bettor who analyses the challenging course conditions and expects difficulties bets the "under," predicting 69 strokes or fewer. If McIlroy concludes the round with a 68 — 3 under par on a par-71 course — the "under" wager wins; if he shoots 70 or higher, those who bet the "over" win.
