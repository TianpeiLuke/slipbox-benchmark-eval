# Concert Film Versus Live Concert

Polygon's writer argues that seeing a concert film in a movie theater is not the same as attending a live concert but is not an inherently lesser experience either — a claim she grounds in having attended the BTS Yet to Come concert both in person and in cinemas.

Her case is that the concert film has value past its re-creation of a specific intersection of place, time, and performance: she went to theaters to relive a once-in-a-lifetime show, but the film version was also its own unique collective fan experience. The practical advantages she names are arriving at the theater just 20 minutes before showtime, being able to go with all her BTS fan friends this time, and watching in a theater full of fellow fans with BTS lightsticks in hand and a BT21 Tata headband on head, which gave the same sense of fandom camaraderie as the in-person concert. She concedes the losses: she missed some of the freedom of the in-person experience, such as choosing where to direct her attention across an expansive stage bedecked with intricate sets calling back to different eras in BTS history. But she argues the clarity and focus of detail in the film evened the score, giving a much closer view of the choreography and the members' interaction with a close-up camera intimacy she had not gotten in person, and letting her share those fandom moments not only with her cinema audience but with millions of other fans viewing the same film around the world. She adds that Yet to Come would stream on Amazon Prime Video starting November 9, further expanding the number of fans able to see it.
