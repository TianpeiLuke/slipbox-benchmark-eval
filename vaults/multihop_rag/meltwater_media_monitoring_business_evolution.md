# The Evolution Of Meltwater's Media Monitoring Business

Meltwater's business has evolved through three stages, each driven by a change in where the media lives: manual press clipping, digitized monitoring of print decline, and then social-media-era sentiment analysis over unstructured data.

The company's roots are in businesses where humans physically sifted through piles of newspapers daily to clip mentions of a company's name, collate those and send them to clients to help them track how they are being covered in the media. The decline of print media digitized that effort, and then the rise of social media turned it into a wider game — sentiment analysis — as words became structured and, more usually, unstructured data. The influx of a whole new set of tools to glean insight out of that data turned a media challenge into a technical one.

Meltwater's response was to build AI in-house and to acquire a stream of businesses in an analytics consolidation play. The most high-profile of these acquisitions was undoubtedly DataSift, the groundbreaking firm that was an early friend of Twitter's in monetizing its firehose, only for that relationship to turn sour. Companies like Meltwater are at a crossroads these days in technology terms.
