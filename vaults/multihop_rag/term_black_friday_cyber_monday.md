# Black Friday / Cyber Monday

## Definition

Black Friday and Cyber Monday are the paired retail sale events — the Friday after the U.S. Thanksgiving holiday and the Monday that follows it — that together anchor the start of the U.S. holiday shopping season, with retailers running discount campaigns across both dates and the days between them.

## Context

Retailers treat the two days less as isolated events than as bookends of a single extended sale window: Engadget reported that Amazon, Target, Best Buy, and Walmart all kicked off Black Friday-branded deals as early as late October 2023, so that "what was once a one-day event filled with limited-time deals has grown into a month-long event with a plethora of sales to sift through each day." Amazon itself began its Black Friday sale on Friday, November 17 — a week ahead of the calendar date — then switched to "Cyber Monday" branding in the early hours of Saturday, November 25, carrying many of the same discounts forward under the new label through the official close on Monday, November 27. Polygon likewise noted that the two named days (November 24 and 27 in 2023) no longer bound when the best prices actually appear, since retailers now front-load doorbuster-style deals across the preceding weeks. The pairing also functions as the header under which media outlets organize their own coverage: The Verge's "under $25" roundup groups both days together as one shopping period with "always a ton of hype about the discounts on big-ticket items."

## Key Characteristics

- **Two named days, one continuous season** — Black Friday (the day after Thanksgiving) and Cyber Monday (the following Monday) are distinct calendar dates, but retailer promotions now span from late October through the Monday close, blurring the boundary between them.
- **Retailer-driven timing shift** — multiple retailers (Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, Target) started Black Friday-branded deals days to weeks before the actual date in 2023, a strategy aimed at spreading out demand rather than concentrating it on one doorbuster day.
- **Deal carryover across the pairing** — items discounted for Black Friday commonly remain discounted, or are re-labeled, once Cyber Monday branding begins, rather than the two events offering wholly separate deal sets.
- **Broad retailer participation** — coverage spans Amazon, Walmart, Best Buy, Target, and several other retailers, indicating the pairing is not a single-retailer promotion but an industry-wide sale convention.
- **High online-spend period** — the surrounding Thanksgiving-to-Cyber-Monday window ("Cyber Week") is tracked by analytics firms as a major driver of U.S. holiday online sales, with Thanksgiving Day alone producing billions in online spending as the de facto season opener.
