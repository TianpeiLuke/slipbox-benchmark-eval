# Why 2023 Is Not A Repeat Of The 2008 Housing Crash

Against the premise that today's housing market is heading for a 2008-style crash, Bank of America's Jeseo Park and her team concluded flatly: "We reiterate that we do not expect another housing crash like 2008." The attacked premise is the assumed similarity of conditions — the 2008 crash was fueled by the collapse of subprime mortgages, and the real estate market of that era was very different from what Bank of America's experts see today.

The specific rebuttals are four. There are no "noticeable signs" of excess housing development today like there were back then. Households are not nearly as burdened by mortgage debt: household mortgage debt represented roughly 65% of U.S. consumers' disposable income in the second quarter, compared to 100% before the Global Financial Crisis. The ratio of Americans' mortgage debt to their real estate assets — loan-to-value — was just 27% in the second quarter, compared to over 40% in 2008 and roughly 50% in 2010, on Bank of America's data. And new legislation enacted since the GFC has helped prevent worst-case scenarios, most obviously by leaving far fewer risky adjustable-rate mortgages, which can lead to higher default rates when interest rates rise: they now represent less than 5% of total purchase and refinance loans, compared with over 35% at the peak of the pre-GFC housing cycle.
