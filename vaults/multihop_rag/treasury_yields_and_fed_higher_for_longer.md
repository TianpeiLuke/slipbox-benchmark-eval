# How Treasury Yields And "Higher For Longer" Transmit To Equities

Treasury bonds are seen as some of the safest investments possible, and when they pay higher yields, investors are less likely to pay high prices for stocks and other riskier investments — which is a big reason why the S&P 500 dropped 4.9 per cent in September 2023, dragging what had been a big gain for the year down to 11.7 per cent.

The chain running into those yields is the Federal Reserve's stance. Treasury yields have been climbing sharply as Wall Street accepts a new normal in which the Fed is likely to keep interest rates high for longer; the Fed is trying to push still-high inflation down to its target, and its main tool of high interest rates does that by trying to slow the economy and hurting prices for investments. The Fed's main interest rate is at its highest level since 2001, and the central bank indicated the previous week that it may cut interest rates next year by less than it earlier expected. The data cuts both ways within this model. Friday's economic data showed that not only was inflation a touch cooler than expected in August, so was growth in spending by US consumers, which can be a positive for inflation because it means not as many dollars are pouring into purchases and that in turn could give companies less encouragement to raise prices further — but it may also dent what has been a big driver keeping the US economy out of a recession.
