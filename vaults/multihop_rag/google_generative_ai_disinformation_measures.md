# Google's Generative AI Disinformation Measures

In its report to the EU disinformation Code, Google — an early adopter of generative AI in search via its Bard chatbot — discusses "recent progress in large-scale AI models" which it suggests has "sparked additional discussion about the social impacts of AI and raised concerns on topics such as misinformation."

Google summarised its approach: "Google is committed to developing technology responsibly and has published AI Principles to guide our work, including application areas we will not pursue," adding, "We have also established a governance team to put them into action by conducting ethical reviews of new systems, avoiding bias and incorporating privacy, security and safety. Google Search has published guidance on AI-generated content, outlining its approach to maintaining a high standard of information quality and the overall helpfulness of content on Search. To help address misinformation, Google has also announced that it will soon be integrating new innovations in watermarking, metadata, and other techniques into its latest generative models. Google also recently joined other leading AI companies to jointly commit to advancing responsible practices in the development of artificial intelligence which will support efforts by the G7, the OECD, and national governments. Going forward we will continue to report and expand upon Google developed AI tools and are committed to advance bold and responsible AI, to maximise AI's benefits and minimise its risks."

On forward commitments over the next six months, Google's report states it has no additional measures planned for YouTube. With generative image capabilities rolling out internally over the next year, it commits Google Search to leveraging the IPTC Photo Metadata Standard to add metadata tags to images generated by Google AI, and notes: "Creators and publishers will be able to add a similar markup to their own images, so a label can be displayed in Search to indicate the images as AI generated."
