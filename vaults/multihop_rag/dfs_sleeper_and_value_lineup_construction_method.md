# The DFS Sleeper And Value Lineup Construction Method

Sporting News' weekly DFS method is to pinpoint and play the cheapest sleepers and highest-ceiling value plays each week, freeing budget to afford elite performers at the remaining roster spots.

The routine runs on a fixed cadence and a fixed set of sources. Every Tuesday the writers scour DraftKings, FanDuel and SuperDraft for affordable sleepers and under-the-radar value plays that will set a lineup apart from the DFS pack. They pore over the player pools, then handpick the most promising sleepers and explain why each is worth drafting; the reader's remaining step is simply to choose favorites. Each Tuesday they also share a big list of the previous week's hits, with each hit typically netting at least 16-22 fantasy points. The governing logic is budget allocation: if daily fantasy were as easy as picking all top-end skill-position players from one week to the next, winning the milli-maker would be much easier — to win big you need to hit on some diamonds in the rough, and to afford a CeeDee Lamb at $9,200 on DraftKings you have to invest in the occasional Demarcus Robinson at $3,600. By finding stud production at modest prices you keep plenty of budget for elite weekly performers elsewhere, and when the studs and the sleepers both hit, you usually win. The column's scope conditions: all player values come from DraftKings, FanDuel and SuperDraft, and all stats come from NFL.com and Pro Football Reference. Over the season the approach had been consistently ahead of the game on breakouts like Puka Nacua, Kyren Williams and Tank Dell.
