# Why Line Shopping Improves Returns

Line shopping is worth a bettor's time because it offers the chance to lay less, win more and improve your chances — and because it need not take long. The grounds start with a feature of how bets settle: when you bet, you lock in the odds or line as currently posted, and if they later change, for better or worse, you are stuck with the odds from when you wagered. That is precisely why the shopping has to happen before the bet, though if significant changes occur afterward you can take more action at the improved odds or more favorable line.

The warrant is that a better price changes outcomes and cash flows, not skill. Getting more points or laying fewer on a typical NFL or NBA point spread might turn a bet you would otherwise lose into a winner, and finding better odds on any bet means dropping fewer dollars when you lose and earning more when you win. Sporting News is explicit about the limit: shopping lines will help you consistently find the best prices, but it will not help you pick more winners — it improves your chances and leads to bigger wins and more affordable losses.

The worked example is a half-point. Suppose you like the Kansas City Chiefs to beat the Cincinnati Bengals in a mid-season NFL game, and three of four licensed online sportsbooks in your state have the Chiefs at -3 while the fourth has them at -2.5. If the Chiefs win by exactly three — a distinct possibility, since a single field goal decides most NFL games — bets at the first three books push, while the fourth is a winner; without shopping you would never have found that half-point and would be stuck with a push instead of a profit.

The effort required is small: a live odds feed makes a basic survey a matter of seconds, and installing multiple licensed online sportsbook apps on your phone allows a more thorough shop in just a few minutes.
