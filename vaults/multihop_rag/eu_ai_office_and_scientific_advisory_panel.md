# The EU AI Office And Its Scientific Advisory Panel

The AI Act will be implemented through a new AI Office set up within the EU's executive, which will coordinate with the Member State oversight bodies that will need to enforce the rules on AI firms and will oversee the most advanced AI models, including by contributing to fostering standards and testing practices — with a scientific panel of independent experts appointed to advise it about GPAI models.

Per internal market commissioner Thierry Breton, work to implement the agreement starts immediately with the set up of the AI Office. "We will welcome new colleagues… a lot of them," said Breton. "We will work — starting tomorrow — to get ready." The AI Office will play a key role in setting risk classification thresholds for GPAIs, but the Commission confirmed there is no budget nor headcount defined for the expert body as yet. Asked about resourcing, a Commission official said it will be decided in the future by the EU's executive taking "an appropriate and official decision," adding: "The idea is that we can create a dedicated budget line for the Office and that we will be able also to recruit the national experts from Member States if we wish to on top of contractual agents and on top of permanent staff. And some of these staff will also be deployed within the European Commission."

The scientific advisory panel, which the law will also establish, is meant to help the Office better understand the capabilities of advanced AI models for the purpose of regulating systemic risk. "We have identified an important role for a scientific panel to be set up where the scientific panel can effectively help the Artificial Intelligence Office in understanding whether there are new risks that have not been yet identified," the official noted. "And, for example, also flag some alerts about the models that are not captured by the FLOP threshold that for certain reasons could actually give rise to important risks that governments should should look at."
