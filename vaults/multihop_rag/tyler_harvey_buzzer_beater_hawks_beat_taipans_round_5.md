# Tyler Harvey's Buzzer-Beater Sinks Cairns 84-83

Illawarra Hawks import Tyler Harvey sealed an 84-83 upset defeat of the Cairns Taipans with a clutch three-pointer in the final seconds at WIN Entertainment Centre on the Thursday night of NBL round 5.

Staring down the possibility of a 1-5 start, last year's wooden spooners looked intent on staging an ambush as captain Sam Froling fired early, but the Taipans chased down a 13-point lead and edged in front in the final quarter with Patrick Miller (24 points, seven assists) leading the charge. Miller's jump shot in the final 10 seconds gave Cairns a two-point lead that looked as though it could have been match-winning, but the Hawks belted down the court and the ball found Harvey for their final roll of the dice: his deep three swished through with one second remaining, sending the home crowd into raptures and improving the Hawks to 2-4. "I turned around to my assistants and I said, 'That's why we have him (Harvey)'," said Hawks coach Jacob Jackomas. "It's good that people can be reminded how special he is at the end of the clock. In that space, he always gives us a chance."

The game swung repeatedly. Nine first-quarter points from Froling helped Illawarra to a nine-point lead at the first change, and he had his double-double (16 points, 13 rebounds) with seven minutes left in the third, though Cairns did a better job targeting him later and kept the rangy centre scoreless in the second half — "He had a great game," Jackomas said. Import Justin Robinson, who had struggled from the field to begin the season, hit a three that forced a timeout and restored a game-high 13-point lead in the third quarter. But lapses in concentration invited Cairns back: the Hawks shot only one of their last nine attempts from the field in the third, having gone almost four minutes without scoring from the field in the second. After the break a Sam Waardenburg free throw tied things up, one of nine occasions in the final quarter alone that scores were level, until Harvey's moment of magic split the sides.

Cairns fell to 2-2 to start the season, having also dropped their two games against NBA sides as part of that league's pre-season. "It's a tall order to expect us to win if we keep giving up second-chance points," said Taipans coach Adam Forde. "I don't want to discredit Illawarra (but) during the game, I felt like we were better-equipped to close it out, but we didn't and Illawarra did. It's the one that got away." Jackomas kept perspective: "I'm really happy for the guys. It is a bit of a relief, but we need to move on into being a basketball team now, not the team that's relieved to win. I'm really confident in these guys."
