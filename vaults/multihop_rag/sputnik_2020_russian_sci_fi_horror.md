# Sputnik: A Russian Sci-Fi Horror Built On A Twisted Trolley Problem

Sputnik is Egor Abramenko's 2020 directorial debut, starring Oksana Akinshina, Fedor Bondarchuk and Pyotr Fyodorov, and available on Hulu — Polygon's pick if you are hungry for a great piece of contemporary Russian sci-fi horror not directed by Andrei Tarkovsky or Yakov Protazanov. Set in 1983 at the height of Cold War tensions, the film (whose title is Russian for "fellow traveler") centres on Tatyana, an uncompromising young psychiatrist with a staunch attitude regarding the ends justifying the means, who is recruited by the Soviet military to treat Konstantin, a wounded cosmonaut and the lone survivor of a mysterious satellite crash. Only on arriving at the remote hospital facility and interacting with him does Tatyana realise the horrifying truth: Konstantin did not return from space alone — his body has become the unwitting host to an organism unlike anything seen on Earth. Caught between her duty to study the creature and her desire to save Konstantin from further harm, she must make a hard decision on which the survival of all humanity may rest.

What makes the debut compelling is how it takes the basic premise of the "trolley problem" thought experiment and twists it repeatedly, and successfully, to dramatic emotional effect. Akinshina (The Bourne Supremacy) delivers a convincing performance as a woman forced to confront and overcome the uncompromising attitude that had once assured her success but now threatens another man's life, potentially everyone on the planet, and her own soul; Fyodorov delivers a sympathetically complex and on occasion implicitly sinister performance as a Russian "hero" torn between his perceived duty to his country and his emotional obligation to a loved one he all but abandoned before his most recent mission. The creature design is terrific, as are the cinematography and the score.

Its distribution history is part of why Polygon spotlights it: having previously been slated for a world premiere at the 2020 Tribeca Film Festival and subsequently dumped on video-on-demand in the wake of the COVID-19 pandemic, Sputnik is exactly the kind of rare gem of intellectually and viscerally stimulating horror that otherwise goes unappreciated if not given the opportunity to shine.
