# Nacon Revolution X Controller

RIG's Nacon Revolution X may be worthy of an award for terrible naming, but its real strengths are in its deep customization: if you are the type who likes to endlessly tinker with lots of detailed settings, this is the controller for you. Specifications: wired connectivity, USB-C connector, four mappable rear buttons, software customization, wired power. Normally priced at $99.99, its added features do not come cheap, but the price is a little fairer considering the competition it squares up against.

First impressions are unassuming and borderline cheap. It is not a looker, but it is a form-follows-function type of controller: comfy to use, with four rear buttons built into the grips that are large and nicely contoured.

The real stars are the analog sticks and their software customizations, plus the expansive custom options for the controller's other inner workings. While other user-customizable controllers have three user profiles you can swap between on the fly, the Revolution X has four, plus a "classic" mode with basic settings and a ring of RGB light around the right stick to remind you which mode you are in. The software app is not the most user-friendly but the controls go very in-depth, and there are helpful starting points with presets for arcade fighting games, racing, FPS games, and even sniping. Add the ability to customize and remap every button, plus a Dolby Atmos for Headphones app license, and you have a lot of options.

The customization is physical too: the Revolution X comes with ring spacers to adjust the range of the analog sticks, and a series of small weights ranging from 10g to 16g that you can insert into its grips — accessible through rear grip covers that open up — to give them additional heft. The controller, cable, stick tops, grip weights, and stick rings are all packaged in an included zipper case. It took the reviewer time to warm up to it, but once set up the way he preferred it made for a very compelling controller in a variety of game types.
