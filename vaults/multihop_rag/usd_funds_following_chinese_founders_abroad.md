# Why USD Funds Follow Chinese Founders Abroad

The claim is that the diaspora financing gap is the natural hunting ground for USD fund managers looking beyond China. "It's just a lot easier to raise their first round from China's USD funds," said a former investor at one of China's top VC firms. "In some sense, the entrepreneurs are taking these investors on an international expedition."

The grounds for why they cannot simply compete for American deals instead are twofold. Besides the low-hanging fruit of the diaspora community, Chinese VCs flying in from Beijing and Shanghai have limited avenues to source deals in the U.S.: American startups are courted by a plethora of local investors, let alone the geopolitical risks of accepting Chinese-managed money. These parachuting investors also run into competition from local investors already tailored to U.S.-China cross-border opportunities, most famously UpHonest Capital.

The warrant is a claim about what venture investing actually depends on: "VCs thrive on information arbitrage. In the U.S., we don't really have that same extensive network as at home," said an investor from the China arm of a global VC firm who now spends a third of their time abroad. Lacking that network locally, the diaspora is where their information advantage still holds.
