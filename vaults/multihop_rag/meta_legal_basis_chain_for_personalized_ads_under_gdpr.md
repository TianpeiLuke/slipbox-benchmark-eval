# Meta's Legal Basis Chain For Personalized Ads Under GDPR

Meta's justification for running personalized ads in the EU has moved through a chain of legal bases, each in turn rejected by regulators or courts, leaving consent as the only remaining option.

The chain runs as follows. Late in 2022 the European Data Protection Board settled a dispute between DPAs on a complaint against the legal basis the adtech giant claimed for running the ads; that led on, in January, to a final decision issued by the Irish DPC rejecting Meta's claim of contractual necessity to justify the processing. Since then Meta tried another switch of legal basis for the processing — to a claim of legitimate interests — but the EU's top court quashed that gambit with a ruling in July, related to a separate challenge brought by Germany's competition authority, which confirmed Meta cannot claim a legitimate interest to run its "personalized ads" in the absence of user consent.

That landmark strike was followed at the start of August by Meta announcing an "intention" to legalize its tracking ads business in the region by asking users for their permission. But it had still not done so, continuing to run unlawful ads — which is why the Norwegian DPA decided to take emergency action, pointing out that millions of EU people's rights are being infringed.
