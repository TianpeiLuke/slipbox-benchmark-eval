# Meta AI Assistant Limits On Smart Glasses

Asked whether the Ray-Ban Meta glasses deliver the sci-fi smart-glasses experience, The Verge's reviewer answered: not really — at least not in the way most people have envisioned from movies. The Meta AI and voice assistant is nothing like Peter Parker issuing commands to EDITH via the smart aviators Tony Stark gave him in Spider-Man: Far From Home. It sounds very natural, but it cannot do a whole lot of stuff yet and takes a hot second to process commands. She mostly used it for taking photos and videos hands-free, listening to texts as they came in, and sending messages — and even there it got tripped up, because her spouse apparently has multiple entries in her contact list. You can send normal texts, but most of her contacts are not on Messenger or WhatsApp, and the glasses do not natively share to non-Meta apps, so the assistant will be more useful to people who use those apps frequently.

The AI features, which are in beta, did not tempt her either: she was never itching to use a ChatGPT-esque bot in the ways Meta suggested, such as writing raps and poems or generating excuses to get out of parties. She allowed that down the road audio AI bots could be a useful form of augmented reality — the first version of the Bose Frames tried to make audio augmented reality apps a thing but crashed and burned when third-party developers did not glom onto the idea, while Meta's approach is a bit different: in a future feature drop, you will be able to ask the AI to identify objects in your surroundings via the camera, which she called a cool idea she is eager to see if and when it arrives.

The counter cuts in Meta's favour on one point: she is glad Meta did not attempt a smart display, because the tech is not there yet. Trying Google Glass Enterprise Edition, Epson Moverio glasses, and Focals by North, she found focusing on the information overlays hard on her eyes, since these tend to rely on projection tech that gets easily washed out by bright ambient light, and navigating screens usually requires some kind of physical control — clunky, battery-draining, and saddled with the problem of developing third-party app ecosystems.
