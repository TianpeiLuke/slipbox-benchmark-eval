# The FTC's Antitrust Lawsuit Against Amazon

The Federal Trade Commission's sweeping 172-page antitrust lawsuit, filed in late September 2023, accuses Amazon of wielding its monopoly power to squeeze merchants and thwart rivals, which the agency alleges has led to artificially inflated prices and a degraded shopping experience for consumers.

The mechanism the FTC describes is an "anti-discounting strategy" backed by a "massive web-crawling apparatus that constantly tracks online prices" to stifle competition: Amazon punishes third-party sellers who offer cheaper products elsewhere by threatening to disqualify them from appearing in the buy box if it detects a lower price, and losing the buy box is an "existential threat" to sellers' businesses, the complaint alleges. The end result, the FTC argues, is elevated prices across the web — the company steadily hikes the fees it charges sellers and prevents them from discounting on other sites, so sellers often inflate their prices off Amazon, creating an "artificial price floor everywhere." The FTC is seeking to hold Amazon liable for allegedly violating anti-monopoly law but has not yet outlined the specific remedies it believes would best resolve its concerns; in antitrust cases, remedies are often determined only after a court finds the defendant liable. Amazon general counsel David Zapolsky responded in a blog post that third-party sellers set their own prices on the marketplace and that the company invests in tools to help sellers offer "competitive prices": "Even with those tools, some of the businesses selling on Amazon might still choose to set prices that aren't competitive. Just like any store owner who wouldn't want to promote a bad deal to their customers, we don't highlight or promote offers that are not competitively priced." Zapolsky argued the lawsuit could force Amazon to stop highlighting low prices, "a perverse result that would be directly opposed to the goals of antitrust law."
