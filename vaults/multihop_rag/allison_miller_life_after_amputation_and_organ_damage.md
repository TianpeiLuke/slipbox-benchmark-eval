# Allison Miller's Life After Amputation And Her Permanent Organ Damage

In what Allison Miller calls her "new normal," she wears a prosthesis and, with the help of "very qualified people," has learned to walk again and to "navigate the world and all of its terrain" -- but she also still lives with permanent lung damage and below-average heart function left behind by the illness.

Miller has been able to get back to the things she loves to do, such as traveling the world, without feeling limited by her disability. "It's just different, and I have learned to adapt and adjust," she said.

In addition to the amputation, the organ damage is lasting. "There was some permanent organ damage because of the strain of the virus, as it was such a severe case of pneumonia," she said. "I have a terminal lung condition that needs regular treatment." She added, "It's manageable, but it's something I will forever deal with."
