# The Swift Effect: How 104 Million Swifties Could Complete The NFL's Revenue Target

Essentially Sports argues on 2023-10-25 that Taylor Swift's fanbase is, in arithmetic terms, exactly the audience the NFL needs to hit its revenue goal. The grounds: most of the 12-time Grammy Awardee's songs are about that warm fuzzy feeling and chemical reaction we call love, and those songs have earned the support of 103.7992 million Swifties in the world — a figure given to four decimal places, showing how much data exists on her. Football has 184 million fans, and the league generates $18 billion in revenue from those 184 million; if it adds 104 million (after rounding 103.7992) to that number, the article computes revenue of $28 billion — and just like that, the target will be met.

The supporting evidence is behavioural rather than purely arithmetical, and the article confronts the critics directly: while analysts like Stephen A. Smith may go around telling people they should be embarrassed for getting so invested in someone else's relationship, it is good business for the NFL. TMZ reported that #87's jersey sales skyrocketed 400% and his Instagram followers by 276%. Yahoo Finance reported a 53% rise in viewership among teen girls — a part of the population nobody would have thought in their wildest dreams the NFL would rope in. The October 1 matchup between the Chiefs and the Jets drew 27 million viewers and became the most-watched Sunday TV show since the last Super Bowl, which itself had an actual concert by Rihanna. An accompanying image credit shows Taylor Swift reacting while sitting next to Donna Kelce, watching the Kansas City Chiefs versus Chicago Bears game during the first half at GEHA Field at Arrowhead Stadium, Kansas City, Missouri.
