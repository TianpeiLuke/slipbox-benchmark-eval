# The Rogers Sisters' Oppression Lawsuit Of 2023

Two members of the billionaire family that controls Rogers Communications Inc. — Melinda Rogers-Hixon and her sister Martha Rogers — sued the company, saying they had been frozen out of board meetings and blocked from getting information, and their court petition argues that the company has been "oppressive" to them. Melinda Rogers-Hixon says she is the target of a "personal vendetta" by her older brother, Rogers Chair Edward Rogers, according to a letter contained in the court filing: the siblings were close to an agreement that would have resolved their longstanding differences, but Edward Rogers reneged on it, according to her Sept. 20, 2023 missive, which was sent to Chief Executive Officer Tony Staffieri.

Both sisters said in documents that they are still being excluded from some parts of board and committee meetings and are being given redacted board documents. "I cannot properly perform my duties as a director if information that is provided to the rest of the directors is being withheld from me," Rogers-Hixon said in her affidavit. The two are seeking a judge's order to force the company to share information with them and to allow them to attend, in full, all meetings of the board as well as board committees they belong to. The court filing also suggests that Rogers-Hixon was forced out of her role as a director of Maple Leaf Sports & Entertainment, the company that owns Toronto's professional hockey and basketball teams and in which Rogers Communications is a large minority investor.

Shares of Rogers extended losses on news of the lawsuit, closing down 2.2% to C$53.07 in Toronto; they had dropped 16% in 2023 to that point.
