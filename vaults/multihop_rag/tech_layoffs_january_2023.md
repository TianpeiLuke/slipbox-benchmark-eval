# Tech Layoffs In January 2023

January 2023 was the month the tech layoff wave peaked in scale, with Amazon eliminating more than 18,000 roles, Alphabet cutting 6% of its global workforce (12,000 employees), Microsoft impacting 10,000 employees, and Salesforce cutting 10% of its workforce, more than 7,000 people.

The month began on January 4 with Salesforce announcing it was cutting 10% of its workforce, impacting more than 7,000 employees — a month later some Salesforce employees had only just found out they were part of the same 10% announcement — and Vimeo announcing it was cutting 11% of its workforce. On January 5 Amazon announced it was eliminating more than 18,000 roles, extending a previously announced round of layoffs from November 2022; on January 19 the company announced it would end AmazonSmile. The NFT marketplace SuperRare announced on January 6 that it was cutting 30% of its staff. Coinbase announced on January 10 that it would cut 950 jobs, about 20% of its workforce, and shut down "several" projects — the second round of major layoffs at the crypto exchange, which had eliminated 18% of its workforce, nearly 1,100 jobs, the previous June. On January 11 Citizen cut 33 staff members; Carta, the equity management platform, cut 10% of its staff, which judging by LinkedIn data could have impacted around 200 employees; and Informatica reported it would lay off 7% of its workforce, 450 staffers globally.

On January 12, DirectTV announced plans to lay off about 10% of its management staff on January 20; Career Karma, the learning navigation platform, laid off another 22 people across its global and domestic workforce; Greenlight, the fintech startup offering debit cards to kids, laid off 104 employees, over 21% of its total headcount of 485; TechCrunch confirmed that Alphabet's robot software firm Intrinsic was laying off 40 employees, around 20% of its headcount; and SmartNews announced a 40% reduction of its U.S. and China workforce, around 120 people. ShareChat announced on January 15 that it had laid off 20% of its workforce, over 400 employees, just a month after eliminating more than 100 roles. Clearco announced on January 17 that its cuts impacted 30% of staff across all teams. On January 18 GoMechanic laid off 70% of its workforce; Microsoft announced 10,000 employees would be impacted; and Sophos announced it had let go 10% of its global workforce, about 450 people. On January 20 Swiggy announced plans to lay off 380 jobs and shut down its meat marketplace, and Fandom announced an unspecified number of employees impacted across multiple properties — according to Variety the company employs around 500 people and the layoffs affected roughly 10% of its staff across different sites.

Alphabet announced on January 21 that it was laying off 6% of its global workforce, 12,000 employees, with cuts impacting divisions such as Area 120, the Google in-house incubator, and Alphabet's robotics division Intrinsic. Spotify announced on January 23 cuts impacting around 6% of its global workforce, around 600 employees. Waymo, the self-driving technology unit under Alphabet, quietly laid off workers on January 24 according to The Information and several posts on LinkedIn and Blind, with the number affected not yet clear. On January 30 Arrival, with a newly appointed CEO, slashed 50% of its workforce, 800 employees globally, and PayPal announced about 2,000 full-time employees, 7% of its workforce, were affected. Impossible Foods was reportedly cutting 20% of its staff, over 100 employees, as Bloomberg reported first. On January 31 Groupon announced another 500 employees impacted, with the new set of layoffs to be spread across the first two quarters of 2023; NetApp announced cuts impacting 8% of its staff, about 960 people; and SoFi Technologies announced it was cutting 65 jobs, about 5% of its 1,300-person workforce, as first reported by The Wall Street Journal.
