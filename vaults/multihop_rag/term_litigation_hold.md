# Litigation Hold

## Definition

In the corpus, a litigation hold is the obligation the DOJ says Google was subject to during its antitrust trial — the obligation the DOJ accused Google of breaching by auto-deleting internal chat messages. What such a hold requires in general is given in Background (external) below, not in the cited corpus document.

## Context

In the corpus the term names the obligation Google was under during the DOJ's antitrust trial, and it is what makes Google's chat-deletion practice controversial rather than merely an ordinary retention policy: during Pichai's testimony the DOJ "touched on Google's policy of deleting internal chat messages, despite being subject to a litigation hold"; earlier, in February, the DOJ had accused Google of "systematically destroying chats through its history-off option, which deletes messages every 24 hours unless a user manually changed the setting."
Pichai's own account ties the hold directly to a change in Google's product defaults: he said he took action against the history-off default for chat in February specifically "to comply with the DOJ's litigation hold," and, when confronted with a 2021 message asking for history to be turned off in a group chat, said that exchange concerned a personnel matter and "had nothing to do with the litigation hold, which he said he takes great care to comply with."

## Key Characteristics

- **The deletion dispute is the whole point** — the litigation hold is what turns Google's default chat auto-deletion (its "history-off" setting, deleting messages every 24 hours) from an ordinary retention policy into the DOJ's accusation of destroying material Google was obliged to keep.
- **Tied to the specific litigation** — the corpus frames the hold as arising from the DOJ's antitrust suit against Google, not as a general corporate record-keeping rule.
- **Distinguishes intentional destruction from routine practice** — the DOJ's accusation frames Google's chat auto-deletion as destruction of material Google was already obliged to preserve, not an ordinary retention decision.
- **Compliance is asserted, not self-evident** — Pichai maintained he "takes great care to comply with" the hold even while defending a 2021 request to turn off chat history, showing the same conduct can be read either as compliant or as evasive depending on intent.

## Background (external)

The corpus establishes that Google was subject to a litigation hold and disputes whether its conduct complied with it, but does not define the obligation itself. A litigation hold (also called a legal hold) is a process an organization initiates to preserve all forms of relevant information when litigation is reasonably anticipated, so that potentially relevant evidence is not lost, destroyed, or altered before or during a legal proceeding (Wikipedia, *Litigation Hold*, accessed 2026-09-02).
