# Lured (1947)

Douglas Sirk's Lured is a strange crime thriller that Polygon's Tasha Robinson recommends as a particular blast for people who know Lucille Ball only as a comedian. It streams free with a library card on Hoopla or Kanopy, free with ads on Roku, and is rentable on Amazon, Vudu, and YouTube.

Ball plays Sandra Carpenter, an American in London working as a taxi dancer when a friend disappears, possibly the victim of a serial killer who sends odd little poems to the police with every murder. The police ask Sandra to act as bait, which sends her on a bizarre, sometimes darkly hilarious tour of seedy London as she answers lonely hearts ads and meets a series of strangers, any of whom might be a murderer. Robinson finds Ball touchingly open-hearted as a civilian who decides early on that she is a hard-bitten cop like so many other noir protagonists, and is careful about the register: this is not a satire or comedy but more in the mode of M than The Naked Gun, focused on the noose tightening around the killer and on the sense that anyone Sandra meets could be a harmless kook or a deadly predator.
