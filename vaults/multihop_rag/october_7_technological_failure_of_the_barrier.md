# The Technological Failure Of The Barrier On October 7

On the morning of Saturday, October 7, the above-ground section of the barrier turned out to be completely insubstantial, as did its fundamental concept of defense: using drones easily purchased on the Internet, Hamas disabled both the cameras transmitting images to control room monitors in real time and the long distance stationary remote controlled weapon stations (See and Fire RCWS).

The backup layers failed too. There had been a second line of defense, observation balloons, but for unknown reasons all three had stopped working some weeks before the attack, and repairs were postponed until "after the holidays." Israel's drone warfare fleet, capable of jamming the signals of a hostile drone, and the attack drone fleet known for warfare against incendiary balloons, also did not work for unknown reasons, and dozens of Hamas drones operated without interruption. The IDF's Iron Beam high energy laser interception system, developed for such cases among other things, had not yet entered operational activity after many years of development. All of this allowed Hamas terrorists to quickly reach the IDF bases along the Gaza border and disable the information and communications technology systems, neutralizing the ability to call reinforcements without arousing much suspicion at IDF Kirya headquarters in Tel Aviv. Above all there was a sense of security and belief in technology and the sophisticated barrier's ability to prevent any form of intrusion: with the fence as protection, manned deployment for Gaza border settlements was cancelled — these had in any case been reduced immediately after Operation Protective Edge — and units deployed in the area were reduced and replaced with cameras and other electronic means.
