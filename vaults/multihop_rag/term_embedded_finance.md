# Embedded Finance

## Definition

In the corpus, embedded finance is the practice a software platform adopts when it integrates financial services and payments directly into its own product surface — exemplified by Rainforest, which "works with software companies to help them embed financial services and payments into their platforms."

## Context

In the corpus the term surfaces through Rainforest, an Atlanta-based startup that "works with software companies to help them embed financial services and payments into their platforms," positioning itself against incumbent processors Fiserv and FIS as well as fintech rival Stripe and its Connect product.
The concept is framed against a counter-claim from a16z general partner Angela Strange, who argued in 2019 that every company would eventually become a fintech; Rainforest's CEO Joshua Silver disagreed, arguing that most software companies do not want to become fintechs and absorb the associated regulatory and compliance burden — they simply want to accept payments and capture the associated revenue.
That disagreement marks the boundary of the concept in the corpus: embedded finance is presented as a narrow slice of financial functionality a software platform adopts, not a full transformation of that platform into a financial institution.

## Key Characteristics

- **Embedding, not becoming** — the software platform integrates financial services and payments into its own product surface; it does not itself become a bank or a fintech.
- **Processor-agnostic competitive layer** — providers enabling embedded finance (Rainforest, Stripe Connect) compete against both legacy processors (Fiserv, FIS) and each other for the same underlying volume.
- **Revenue motive over financial-identity motive** — per the corpus's framing, platforms adopt embedded finance to accept payments and generate revenue, not to take on the regulatory and compliance profile of a fintech.
- **Distinct from the platform's core compliance burden** — because the platform is embedding rather than becoming a financial company, it can avoid the "regulatory and compliance issues" Silver describes as the cost of the alternative "everyone is a fintech" path.

## Background (external)

Outside the corpus, embedded finance is a recognized industry term: Plaid defines it as "the integration of financial services, like payments, lending, or banking services, into non-financial offerings," letting users access financial tools within products they already use (Plaid, "What Is Embedded Finance?", https://plaid.com/resources/fintech/what-is-embedded-finance/, accessed 2026-09-02).
