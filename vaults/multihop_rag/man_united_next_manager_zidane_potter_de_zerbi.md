# Zidane, Potter And De Zerbi As Manchester United Manager Candidates

Sporting News argued on Dec. 13, 2023 that if Manchester United decided to pull the plug on Erik ten Hag's managerial tenure at Old Trafford, there were not any perfect options for his replacement — which is what allowed it to speculate about who could slot in as the next man hoping to take United back to former glories, glories that had still evaded the club since the departure of Sir Alex Ferguson over a decade earlier.

Zinedine Zidane, the former Real Madrid manager, had been out of work since departing the Spanish club in 2021, clearly waiting for the right position to open up; he was heavily rumoured to be in line for the France position once the 2022 World Cup finished, but Didier Deschamps surprised many by signing a new contract. Zidane would be a top choice for United, as he would be for many top clubs with a managerial vacancy, but there were questions around whether he would be open to signing on: the 51-year-old told Marca back in June of 2022 that he was not exactly thrilled about a Premier League job due to the language barrier. Asked specifically about a possible job opening in Manchester, Zidane said: "I understand English, but I don't master it completely. I know that there are coaches who go to clubs without speaking the language, but I work in a different way. Many elements come into play to win. It is a global context." Sporting News' open question was whether he still felt the same way.

Graham Potter's time at Chelsea crashed and burned, but many still rated the former Brighton & Hove Albion boss highly and he could be in line for a project job such as this; there had previously been rumours that Sir Jim Ratcliffe, the INEOS founder on the brink of purchasing a 25 per cent stake in United, would like Potter at the club. Potter had been out of work since his Blues tenure came to a close in April, but he would be a hard sell to fans after his time with the London club went so poorly. Roberto De Zerbi, one of the fastest rising managers in the game, was the Brighton manager known for his tactical innovation in transitional play, and his name had cropped up a number of times when big clubs have an opening: the 44-year-old Italian had led the tiny club to new heights, qualifying for the Europa League for the first time in club history and establishing themselves as top-four contenders, building upon the success under Potter. The counterweight Sporting News raises is whether Potter's failure at Chelsea could cause some trepidation around another Brighton success story moving on up.
