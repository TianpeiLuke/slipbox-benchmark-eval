# The Possibility Alliance Homestead, Belfast, Maine

The Possibility Alliance is an 11-acre homestead in Belfast, Maine where a small group of people is trying to figure out how to live in a world headed for a breakdown, as they see it — operating without power, running water or a sewer system, with the Hughes family that runs it paying only one monthly bill, for landline phone service.

Its leaders eschew capitalism, grow much of their own food, and live without electricity; they have dedicated their lives to protest, living off the land as simply as possible, and are preparing their community for ecological disaster and to host climate refugees. The sprawling homestead is filled with fruit trees, goats, chickens and a garden, and the Hughes family and its guests live largely without electricity or modern technology. The family of four does not fly because of climate concerns and does not own a car; their main use of fossil fuels is to take passenger trains to climate protests. In place of capitalism they have built a "gift economy" where resources are shared and skills are traded among neighbours in this tiny corner of Maine, growing much of their own food, holding trainings for climate protesters and planning to host refugees as climate disasters worsen. Nights are illuminated by candlelight and neighbours drop by without notice. Ethan Hughes, who operates the alliance, rejects globalization and thinks capitalism is a ruinous symptom of "greed and separation"; he is trying to build a gift economy in Belfast in which neighbours share resources and services with no expectation of repayment. "I'm trying to role-model post-petrol," Hughes said, and of the homestead: "We've created what existed 100 years ago." Kalmus's search for ways to live with climate change brought him there, and while on his working vacation in Maine he invited NBC News for a short visit to see the crisis through the eyes of Americans taking it as seriously as anyone.
