# DeAndre Hopkins As A Week 5 2023 Start At Indianapolis

CBS Sports listed DeAndre Hopkins under "Start Him" for Week 5 of the 2023 season at Indianapolis: the claim is that after a stretch in which benching him had been the cool move, the Colts present a terrific matchup Hopkins can take advantage of, and the warrant is that Indianapolis' depleted outside cornerback group and league-worst tackling on pass plays reward exactly the role Tennessee has started giving him near the end zone.

The matchup grounds are the strongest part of the case. The Colts are described as a frozen yogurt sundae of a dream matchup: outside receivers like Hopkins have caught 72.3% of their targets for 12.7 yards per catch against them this season — a bottom-six catch rate allowed and a top-12 yardage rate — and the Indianapolis defense leads the NFL in missed tackles on passing plays with 10. They also lost top outside corner Dallis Flowers to a torn Achilles and will roll with rookies JuJu Brents, who has allowed an 80% catch rate, and Jaylon Jones, who has played two snaps this season, plus already-benched second-year corner Darrell Baker, who allowed a touchdown in each of his first two games. Their pass rush ranks fifth-worst in pressure rate and seventh-worst in blitz rate and might be without edge rusher Kwity Paye, while Tennessee is helped by the return of rookie guard Peter Skoronski and tackle Nicholas Petit-Frere, so Ryan Tannehill could have more time to throw accurately. Hopkins' own trajectory supports the call: in Week 4 he was so close to a monster game, targeted on a designed trick play with the ball falling just past his outstretched hands in the end zone, and he drew two targets inside the 10-yard line and two end-zone targets — his first two of each on the season — which the column reads as positive signs that the Titans are trying to get him going as a playmaker. On film, at 31 he moved really well, creating separation with outstanding footwork and just enough speed to stay a step ahead, getting open on a handful of third downs where no target came.

The two named obstacles bound the recommendation. The first is Tannehill: Hopkins' 38-yard deep-ball catch could have gone as far as 50 with a better throw, the gimmick-play touchdown would have been a massive point-getter had Tannehill been slightly more on-target, and there was an end-zone target lost to a miscommunication between them; Tannehill's season off-target rate is 13% but has ballooned to 18% over the past two games. The second is Tennessee's run-first preference, at least when they win — they throw 54.6% of the time on the year, tenth-lowest, but only 46.4% in wins against 64.4% in losses, and with the Colts one-point home favourites the game should at minimum be competitive, which does not help the pass-run ratio outlook. The breakdown lists Tennessee at Indianapolis, the Titans favoured by 2, an over/under of 43, an opponent ranked 25th versus wide receivers, 11.6 projected points, a 30th receiver ranking, and year-to-date totals of 18 receptions on 31 targets for 216 yards, no touchdowns and 9.9 fantasy points per game. The verdict starts Hopkins as a No. 2 receiver over Terry McLaurin, any Broncos wideouts and any Packers wideouts, expecting continued high-value targets near the end zone and hopefully a small uptick in targets, just as other receivers — Puka Nacua as recently as the previous week — have exploited this matchup.
