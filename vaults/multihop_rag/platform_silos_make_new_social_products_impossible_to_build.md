# Platform Silos Make New Social Products Impossible To Build

The Verge argued that today's arrangement — where every social platform is a universe unto itself — is not just a bad user experience but a ridiculous way to run a company, because it makes new social products effectively impossible to build.

The grounds: the only way to see your Facebook posts is to sign up for Facebook's rules, aesthetic, features, moderation, algorithm and business model, and Facebook has to invent and maintain all of those things, just as every one of its competitors does. That is an impossible task, and just as impossible to compete with — you can't build social products without building an entire social graph from scratch, "and good luck with that." The warrant for preferring the fediverse is the removal of that precondition: in a fediverse world, rather than trying to build another all-encompassing Facebook from scratch, an enterprising developer can pick one or a few features and try to do better, while users pick their favourite app — or two, or two hundred — through which to get their posts. Because everything is based on one set of posts and an open network of friends and followers, new social products can be useful even if you're the very first user.
