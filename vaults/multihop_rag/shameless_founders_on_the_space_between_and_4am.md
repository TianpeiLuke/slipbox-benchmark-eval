# Andrews And McDonald On Regretting The Space Between And Writing 4am

Michelle Andrews and Zara McDonald look back on their first book, The Space Between, as something written under a mistaken belief about what makes writing worthy. "We wrote it when we were so young," Andrews reflects. "We truly had internalised this belief that for our words to be worthy of attention, they had to mine the deepest, darkest events that have ever happened to us." She has spoken before about an essay she wrote for the book which included details of her sexual assault: "I wish I'd just saved that for myself," she says, adding that while she wouldn't take back the essay, she would take back some of the details. McDonald's reading is similar: "If I was to psychoanalyse us, I'd say we felt guilty for having that platform that young."

That imposter syndrome is nowhere to be seen in 4am, in which Andrews and McDonald are happy to take their rightful place as agony aunts to young Australian women, and in which readers trust them and other Shameless columnists with quandaries on everything from infidelity to sibling rivalry. The book arrives at a time when many young women have trust issues: TikTok is serving eating-disorder content to those already struggling, influencers and celebrities post undisclosed advertisements, and algorithms work to push everything from egg-freezing to injectables — everywhere young women look, someone is making money off them.
