# The Benefits Of Hayes' Delayed USWNT Start

The New York Times argued on 15 November 2023 that the six-month delay before Emma Hayes takes charge of the USWNT benefits both sides: the claim is that the arrangement works for U.S. Soccer and for Hayes personally, and the warrant is that it costs the federation no compensation while buying Hayes time she has never had with her family.

The federation's side is financial and logistical. U.S. Soccer won't owe any compensation to Chelsea, and Hayes will be fully committed to the programme, with a move to Chicago in the works for the following year once her time in London is complete. Eventually she is expected to relocate to Atlanta, thanks to U.S. Soccer's planned combined headquarters and national training facility in Georgia, though as yet there's no targeted date set for that move.

Hayes' side is personal rather than professional. She will benefit in ways other than compensation and prestige: she spoke to reporters about looking forward to spending more time with her five-year-old son, Harry — she has never been to one of his sports days, picked him up from school or taken him to an after-school club, and she wants to do that.
