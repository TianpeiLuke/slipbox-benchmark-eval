# Why Nagelsmann, Scaloni, Southgate And Pochettino Do Not Add Up For United

Sporting News pushed back on four names circulating as possible Manchester United managers on Dec. 13, 2023, attacking in each case the premise that the candidate would actually be available or sellable.

Julian Nagelsmann's name had come up in betting circles, but Sporting News saw no sense in it actually taking place: Nagelsmann had only recently been hired as head coach of the German national team, meaning there was no chance he was available until after Euro 2024 at the earliest — and in that case it was likely that Germany had flopped at their own tournament, meaning his hire would probably be quite sour. "This one just doesn't add up."

Lionel Scaloni could conceivably become available at a moment's notice amid speculation of his potential exit with the defending World Cup champions, but this felt an unlikely pairing: should he leave Argentina, Scaloni had been open about his mental fatigue and was likely to take some time off from management, and in the event he returned quickly he had been heavily linked to Real Madrid as a possible replacement for Carlo Ancelotti, who was expected to become the next permanent Brazil head coach. It was hard to imagine that Scaloni — who seemingly craves stability and routine, has no experience at club level, and lacks ties to the Premier League or Old Trafford beyond a brief loan spell with West Ham as a player in 2006 — would be a match for Manchester United in any capacity.

Gareth Southgate, another currently employed national-team manager, had been linked to the United job before, but it would take a perfect storm for him to be installed, whether permanently or as an interim stopgap. Some believed he could be considered the following summer once the Euros were over, since it had been speculated Southgate could leave his England post after the tournament with his contract expiring in December the next year; however, it was also possible he would stay on for the 2026 World Cup cycle, particularly if England had a good tournament in Germany, in which case he would only be available for double duty on a temporary basis — simply too messy to outweigh the benefits, as Brazil's experience showed, where it had all gone wrong for Fernando Diniz despite his Copa Libertadores victory with Fluminense. Finally, if Todd Boehly proved to have just as itchy a trigger finger as Roman Abramovich at Chelsea, Mauricio Pochettino could become available — but would he really be a sellable hire, having just failed in London after bombing at PSG and departing Tottenham in acrimonious manner? It seemed unlikely United would be interested in someone who demands significant club control and would have just been fired from a struggling league rival.
