# Uber Assault Litigation

Hundreds of women have filed lawsuits against Uber claiming the company hasn't done enough to prevent instances of sexual assault by drivers, and a panel of judges has ruled that more than 80 cases can be consolidated into federal court, TechCrunch reported on 2023-10-13. The stakes are high for both parties, with implications extending to future Uber riders and drivers: the outcome of the case could result in sweeping changes to Uber's platform, which plaintiffs argue will reduce sexual assault and also raise new concerns over privacy. As TechCrunch put it, "Technology lies at the center of this very human story."

Uber has attempted to address sexual assaults by drivers — which the lawsuits claim Uber has known about since 2014 — through new safety features in its app, like a 911 button and the ability to share location with a friend. The additions arrived on a five-year timeline: the 911 button and location-sharing feature were both introduced in 2018; in 2021, Uber introduced a feature that allows riders and drivers to record audio during a trip; and the following year, Uber launched a pilot to provide passengers with live help from an ADT safety agent, as well as PIN verifications to ensure passengers are connected to the correct driver.

Survivors and their attorneys say that response has been inadequate, and they're calling for better technological solutions like in-vehicle surveillance cameras. Rachel Abrams — a sexual assault attorney and partner at law firm Peiffer Wolf Carr Kane Conway & Wise, one of the law firms working on the multidistrict litigation, who filed the petition to consolidate the various actions in federal court — argues the app-based solutions are half-measures that haven't quelled instances of sexual violence against passengers on the platform. Mandatory in-vehicle cameras in Uber cars is "essential for safety," she says, citing data from studies of taxis equipped with in-vehicle cameras, which she says has drastically reduced instances of sexual assaults against passengers, as well as assault of passengers against drivers.

Uber told TechCrunch it cannot comment on pending litigation but said it remains committed to the safety of all users on its platform.
