# The Ecosystem And Unit-Economics Challenge Of Battery Swapping

Battery swapping's promise for consumers is large but its economics and ecosystem requirements are the binding constraint. A recent report jointly created by Bain & Company and Blume Ventures forecast that electric two-wheelers sold without batteries can reduce the upfront cost of vehicles by 40% to 50%, attracting price-sensitive Indian customers — but it also warned that building a battery-swapping ecosystem in India will be challenging in the near term, and suggested maintaining a battery-swapping inventory for top SKUs across manufacturers, identifying customer segments to target, and establishing "walled-garden" partnerships to support swappable-battery systems.

An investor told TechCrunch that to really succeed, the market needs interoperability and standardization in battery swapping, similar to how we have USB-C in phones nowadays. For Gogoro, it is just a start to explore how big a dent it can make in the overall EV market with its available battery-swapping solution.

Gogoro's CEO Horace Luke conceded the scale of the build-out required: "I got an entire ecosystem I need to bring in. It took us a little while to get ready, and it will take us a little while to ramp up. And I'm not fooling anybody that this thing is going to [happen] overnight. India's a huge place. There's a lot of different use cases. The price-performance people ask for is very high . . . So, through making more of the components and making more parts in India, with our Indian suppliers, we'll definitely start, and then more and more localization would certainly help."

The same tension was flagged more generally in TechCrunch's 9 December 2023 roundup: consumer EV battery swapping could usher in freedom for a wide range of people, allowing them to participate in the EV transition in ways that traditional built-in batteries do not, but the challenge is making the unit economics work.
