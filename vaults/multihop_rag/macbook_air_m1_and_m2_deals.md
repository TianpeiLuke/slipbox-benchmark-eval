# MacBook Air M1 And M2 Deals

As of The Verge's October 26th, 2023 roundup, the base MacBook Air with the M1 chip — 8GB of RAM and 256GB of storage, typically $999 — was discounted to $749.99 at Amazon and Best Buy, $250 off, matching its lowest price. The MacBook Air is Apple's entry-level laptop, best suited for typical productivity work with a comfortable keyboard, an excellent trackpad, and all-day battery life; the 2020 M1 version with its fanless design remains in the lineup as the budget option, and for many people still ticks the right boxes on performance and price, even if it is long enough in the tooth to have been fully dethroned in The Verge's guide to the best laptops.

The 13-inch M2 MacBook Air had also come down. Along with introducing a 15-inch M2 Air, Apple announced at WWDC 2023 that the 13-inch model now starts at $1,099 instead of $1,199; the base model with 256GB of storage and an eight-core GPU was selling for $899 at B&H Photo, $200 off, while Best Buy and B&H Photo offered an equal discount on the upgraded configuration with a 512GB SSD and 10-core GPU at $1,199, $200 off. The M2 Air is a super slim and light laptop with a 1080p webcam that is actually usable and a handy magnetic charger that frees up one of its precious two USB-C ports; its M2 processor did not kick-start a revolution like the M1 generation but is a great performer for any user outside more demanding creatives. Slight downsides include slower storage in the base 256GB configuration and a notch cutout in its otherwise excellent screen — even so, The Verge said there has not been a more travel-friendly Apple laptop since the polarizing 12-inch MacBook, and this one is good enough to be the No. 1 laptop it recommends.

The 15-inch M2 Air is a lot like a blown-up 13-inch Air, and judging from The Verge's glowing review there is nothing wrong with that: it has the same M2 chip, two Thunderbolt 4 / USB-C ports, MagSafe charging, and a base model with a 256GB SSD and 8GB of RAM, but differs with a larger 15.3-inch notched screen at 2880 x 1864 resolution, six speakers instead of four, a 10-core GPU that costs extra on the 13-inch, and a higher starting price of $1,299, with 512GB of larger, faster storage running $1,499. It started receiving discounts even before it shipped, letting early adopters save between $50 and $100; at the time of writing it was $300 off at Amazon or Best Buy, selling for $999, with the expanded 512GB model also $300 off at Best Buy at $1,199.
