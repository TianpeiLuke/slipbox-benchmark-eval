# Gaza As A Tech Outsourcing Hub

Gaza, despite being one of the most economically challenged regions in the world, has ironically always been a tech hub — not only for Palestine and Palestinians, but also for the world, with international companies having for many years sought out a presence there to collaborate with talented tech freelancers and the startups that gradually emerged from the region. According to sources who helped build those bridges, Nvidia, famed for its role in the new AI boom, has been working with at least 100 engineers from the region for years; tragically, the initiative to employ Palestinians in this outsourcing was that of Mellanox founder Eyal Waldman, whose daughter was brutally murdered in the attacks by Hamas.

The hub's scale marks its boundary. Since at least 2008, TechCrunch has been covering technology companies out of Palestine, some serving their direct audience and some serving the tech world internationally, and Silicon Valley had taken an increasing interest in Palestine as a tech hub — but like the ecosystem itself, that interest is nascent: to date, those working in the region estimate that as much as $10 million has been invested in the Palestinian tech ecosystem. Notably, in 2017, Salesforce founder and CEO Marc Benioff joined Silicon Valley luminaries in backing the first-ever coding academy to be created in Gaza.
