# How Sportsbooks Set And Move Betting Lines

The best sportsbooks employ oddsmakers and oddsmaking services to set their lines and odds, while other sports betting apps simply copy those lines. The oddsmakers and services use computer models and algorithms that consider analytics, statistics, injury and roster news, performance, matchup data and more to develop the lines and odds. Those lines are then subject to change based on the betting public's reaction and any additional information that emerges before the game, and a book will move the line or change the odds if the public bets heavily on one side.

The governing objective is balance rather than prediction. Every sportsbook's goal is to even out the betting on both sides of every bet, because that lets the book earn the small profit built into the odds and lines without worrying about which side wins. Sportsbooks want even betting so much that they will move a line or change the odds specifically to draw more bets to the other side.

The relation that matters to a bettor is that books do not all move together. The betting is rarely the same at every sportsbook: one book may be forced to move a line or change the odds because of the money it has taken while another is not, and the lines and odds may even differ right from the jump. That divergence is what creates the chance to book a bet at improved odds or a more favorable line.

The range over which this holds is narrow and specific. When a line moves for a legitimate reason such as an injury or roster move, every sportsbook jumps on board, and it is difficult to find value; when a line moves because of betting patterns, only the sportsbook that saw that betting makes the change, and that is the opportunity. Line shopping is the tool for spotting the second kind: choose a side, shop around at online sportsbooks, compare the lines and odds, and pounce when you find one offering something different and more favorable to your viewpoint.
