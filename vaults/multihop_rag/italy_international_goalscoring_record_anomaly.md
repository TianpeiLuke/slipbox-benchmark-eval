# The Italy International Goalscoring Record Anomaly

The New York Times argued on 17 November 2023 that Italy's is perhaps the most surprising international goalscoring record: the claim is that Gigi Riva's relatively insubstantial haul of 35 goals, the mark to beat since the 1974 World Cup, has never been matched or even closely approached, and the warrant is that this reflects Italy's tactical shape and the distribution of its strikers rather than an absence of prolific forwards.

The comparative grounds establish that the record is low. No one has ever got particularly near Riva's total — Roberto Baggio and Alessandro Del Piero both reached 27, and that's as close as anyone has come. For context, four Englishmen have reached 35 goals in that period: Gary Lineker, Michael Owen, Wayne Rooney and Harry Kane; four Spaniards too — Raul Gonzalez, Fernando Torres, David Villa and David Silva — while Alvaro Morata, on 34, should get there shortly. Nor do current Italians look set to challenge it: Ciro Immobile, with 17, has less than half the tally, turns 34 in February and has been omitted from recent squads, and nobody in Luciano Spalletti's current squad has scored more than eight international goals.

The explanatory grounds are twofold. Italy's shortcoming clearly isn't about a complete lack of prolific strikers — Christian Vieri, Pippo Inzaghi and Luca Toni all scored heavily at club level — and sometimes it has been the opposite, with various strikers competing for a starting place so none got to dominate the national side for a decade; though around a decade ago there was simply a dearth of prolific Italian strikers to choose from, with Antonio Conte using Eder and Graziano Pelle up front at the 2016 European Championship. There are also tactical considerations: not only have Italy traditionally been the most defensive of the major European nations, but their attacking play has generally been based around using a second striker, with Baggio, Del Piero and Francesco Totti each the golden boy at various overlapping stages and Italy's No 9 often selected primarily to bring the best out of Italy's No 10.
