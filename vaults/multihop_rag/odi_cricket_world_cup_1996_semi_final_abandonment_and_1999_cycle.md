# The 1996 Semi-Final Abandonment And The Short 1999 Cycle

The 1996 World Cup, jointly hosted by India and Sri Lanka, produced what the Sporting News calls one of the most controversial episodes in the game: the two host teams met in the semi-finals at Eden Gardens, Kolkata, India's poor performance sparked an unruly crowd to protest against the home team, and Sri Lanka was declared the winner by default due to the halt in play.

Sri Lanka went on to win the trophy that year, and they were back to try and defend it three years later in England, rather than after the usual four-year gap.

The reason was a scheduling experiment. Following the 1996 event the ICC opted to try and revamp the schedule, with three years between events rather than four, but the move proved unpopular and 1999 was the only tournament to follow that pattern.
