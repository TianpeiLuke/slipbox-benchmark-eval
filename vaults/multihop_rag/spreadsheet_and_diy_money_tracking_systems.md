# Spreadsheets And DIY Money Tracking Systems

A number of readers said they had tried the apps and built the systems but ultimately landed on a good old spreadsheet in Excel or Google Sheets for tracking their spending — especially for folks who just want a rough "here's what I have, here's where I want it to go" outline.

Templates make that approach cleaner. A few people recommended Tiller as a way to make the Excel and Google Sheets setup a little cleaner and more automatic, and a Notion template got some love too.

The manual route has a known failure mode. Some readers love having custom-built systems they can tend to for a few minutes a week, and there is certainly no beating "it's just a file on your computer that you own" — but almost everyone who recommended this strategy also said it is a lot of work and can be too easy to give up on.

The fourth option is to skip third-party tools entirely: a lot of banks now offer budget tools right within your banking app, so you can manage your money right next to where it lives — the writer hears good things about SoFi's features on this front — and if you do most or all of your banking in one place, a few folks said this is the way.
