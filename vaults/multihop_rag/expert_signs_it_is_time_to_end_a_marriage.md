# Expert Signs That It Is Time To End A Marriage

Asked when a couple should stop trying, relationship expert Susan Winter argues that the threshold is exhaustion of options rather than any particular grievance: "When there is no stone left unturned, or when they have tried every single combination possible to make the relationship function and yet it doesn't, that's the time to pull the plug. They've done their individual work and they have done the work together and ended up in the same place." Her warrant is that effort must have been made both separately and jointly; only when both routes converge on the same failure is the case closed. The Independent notes that every relationship is different, whether lived in the public eye or not, and that living separately may not be an issue for some couples at all.

Dating coach Jacob Lucas offers a different, behaviour-based test: "If you feel disrespected by your partner, feel humiliated, or were cheated on, you probably feel ready to split. I also think a real big one is, if you lose sexual attraction for your partner and you've actively tried to get it back and can't, it's time to go." Applied to the Smiths, the article sets out three open possibilities — the couple could stay separated, move back in together as a married pair, or get a divorce.
