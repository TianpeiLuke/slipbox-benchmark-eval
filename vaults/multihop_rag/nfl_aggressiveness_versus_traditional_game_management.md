# NFL Aggressiveness Versus Traditional Game Management

A longtime NFL executive challenged the analytics-driven push toward aggressive play-calling on 16 October 2023, asking whether coaches have "come too far with coaches wanting to 'win' the game instead of sometimes being more traditional" after the Eagles threw a killer interception on third-and-9 from their own 46-yard line while protecting a 14-12 lead against the Jets with two minutes left in the fourth quarter.

The premise attacked is not that aggression is generally wrong. Most would agree teams have gotten smarter in their handling of fourth-down situations, going for it when it makes sense instead of punting almost out of habit; making the aggressive play to win can absolutely be the smart play regardless of results, and it is progress when teams seriously wrestle with these questions, not just on fourth down but in all aspects of strategy. What the exec disputes is the automatic application of that reasoning to a particular game state: Philadelphia could have run the ball on third-and-9, then punted from around midfield, leaving the Jets with barely more than a minute remaining, no timeouts and Zach Wilson behind center facing a ferocious Eagles pass rush. "Are coaches not willing to go against the slight statistical benefit that WPA (win probability added) provides?" he asked. "Is it no longer OK to let your defense win the game when it clearly has a huge talent/performance advantage? This Eagles game was 14-12, not 37-35."

The inference the counter-argument targets is the neglect of situation-specific factors. In that game the Jets' offense had netted 19 yards on its previous three possessions, producing one field goal on a drive that gained 16 of those yards; the Eagles had already suffered three turnovers, and they knew Wilson would be taking over deep in his territory with very little time remaining. "To me, these are exactly the type of factors that a coach needs to consider when deciding," the exec said. "I think they were ignored here." A veteran coach added an incentive-based explanation — critics attuned to the trend toward aggressiveness pounce when teams pass up chances to go for the kill, coaches know team owners tend to be fans rather than football strategists and listen to those criticisms, which creates an incentive to go down swinging: "Everybody thinks they are playing Brady, Manning or Rodgers in two-minute. If before the game someone offered Philly to put its defense against Zach Wilson on a need-field-goal drive starting inside his own 15 with no timeouts and a minute left, you think they would have taken it?" The base rate is thin: the situation Philly faced was the seventh time since 2000 that a team leading by 1-2 points in the final two minutes faced third down with 7-10 yards to go from the minus-40 yard line to midfield with the opponent holding no timeouts; three of the seven passed, resulting in one first down, one sack and Hurts' interception, and of those three only the Eagles lost the game.
