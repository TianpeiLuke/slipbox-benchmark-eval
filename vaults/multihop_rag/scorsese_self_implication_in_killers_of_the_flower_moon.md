# Scorsese's Self-Implication in Killers of the Flower Moon

Polygon's claim, published 2023-10-26, is that Killers of the Flower Moon is a classic Scorsese Catholic reckoning in which the director expresses guilt and culpability as a beneficiary of inequality — a level of awareness that goes beyond the introverted morality plays he grappled with earlier in his career, and a mature framing of the individual and the responsibilities we have toward one another on a societal scale.

The grounds are both textual and literal. This time Scorsese is concerned with white supremacy and systemic racism, full of unsparing and punishing conclusions and self-recriminations in the wake of George Floyd's death and the 2020 protests; in a press conference on Oct. 16 he described the film as "a story of complicity, a story of sin by omission." And in the movie's closing minutes he literally puts himself squarely in the frame.

The warrant is contested, and Polygon says so. In a charitable light, adding himself to the movie as the voice of its final reckoning is a bold statement of accountability; in an uncharitable light it plays as performative, like an older relative getting worked up over a police brutality Facebook post. Polygon concludes it is too early to decide how the film tags into our present historical moment or how it will be remembered, with many viewings and conversations ahead.
