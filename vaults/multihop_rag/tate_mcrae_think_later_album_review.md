# Tate McRae's Think Later Reviewed

The Age's 30 November 2023 review argues that Think Later, Tate McRae's sophomore album, is best when it follows the Greedy template and is dragged down by an overemphasis on understated ballads imposed by producer Ryan Tedder's formula.

The grounds are track by track. The album is almost completely produced and co-written with Tedder (OneRepublic) and sports his hit-making trademarks "for better or worse" — pristine songcraft hooked around off-kilter concepts that cannot help but catch the ear, but punishingly midtempo, songs searching for gravitas over, say, fun; for an album supposed to be McRae's bold, confident, cheeky makeover, parts of it lack spark. Tellingly, the best songs — Exes, Hurt My Feelings and Guilty Conscience — follow the Greedy template of skittering Timbaland-style beats recalling Y2K-era pop, all syncopated and staccato and sultry kiss-offs to dumb boys; they feel strong and personally nostalgic, like McRae nodding to her countrywoman Nelly Furtado's classic mid-'00s run, but driven by McRae's playfulness. We're Not Alike shows another promising side, a post-punky thing built on an Interpol-ish riff that slyly builds into something anthemic, recalling her pal Olivia Rodrigo's sleeper Guts track Pretty Isn't Pretty.

The warrant is comparative: that these songs are so interesting is exactly what makes the ballads frustrating. Songs like opener Cut My Hair, Grave and Stay Done are slick but dour affairs, the classic Tedder formula. The reviewer allows one exception — Messier, "which weirdly is not another Canadian nod to ice hockey great Mark", shows McRae has the range, a bitter ballad where she lets her vocals get raw and unhinged.
