# The QRU's Position On High-Performance Centralisation

In the wake of the Wallabies' 2023 World Cup flop, the Queensland Rugby Union reaffirmed on Oct. 10, 2023 that it is committed to moving to a centralised high-performance model — but will not follow the NSW Waratahs in handing over the keys to Rugby Australia.

The QRU's stated commitment reads: "The QRU is committed to working with Rugby Australia to lead reform of our game at a high-performance level, and help Rugby Australia develop an improved game through high-performance centralisation." The letter defined the scope of that centralisation: it "would include a national player-contracting model, and alignment of key high-performance staff, such as coaching, strength and conditioning, and medical to operate as one national system." The letter, an open letter to members published on Wednesday morning by QRU chief executive David Hanham and chairman Brett Clark, also pointed to RA's involvement in Les Kiss' appointment as Reds coach — the former State of Origin winger turned coach, who has spent the best part of two decades coaching in Ireland and England, replacing Brad Thorn — as an example of the model in practice.

The QRU also expressed strong support for Rugby Australia to conduct a "fully independent and transparent top-to-bottom review of the Wallabies program and how it can be improved" in light of the historic first World Cup pool exit, adding: "It should also provide a clear view of what is needed in a leadership and governance framework to ensure we put the right people, in the right roles, in the right system." The Roar noted the boundary of the consensus: while all five Australian Super Rugby sides are fundamentally on board with moving to a centralised high-performance program, some states, including the ACT Brumbies as well as the QRU, don't want to give up their commercial assets.
