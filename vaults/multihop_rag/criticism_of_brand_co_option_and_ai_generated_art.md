# Criticism Of Brand Co-Option And AI-Generated Art

Against the celebration of Gag City, TechCrunch sets out the case on the other hand: memes tend to die the minute brand accounts start co-opting them, and nothing is more tiresome than seeing a fun joke turn into a corporate-friendly marketing ploy.

The second objection is to the medium rather than the marketing. AI-generated images are already ethically fraught, and critics have raised concerns over AI generators trained on artwork without the consent of the work's artists. Artists have also criticized brands for using AI-generated art instead of commissioning work from a real, human artist; and though using AI-generated art for commercial use is legal, as copyright laws pertaining to AI are virtually non-existent, it is generally seen as a shitty move by many in the art world.

The objection is then conceded in this instance. Brand Twitter tends to turn fun trends into advertising opportunities, taking organic community interactions and spitting out contrived versions clearly made to go viral; it may be grating, but in this case it is working in Minaj's favor, because this week everyone wants to go to Gag City.
