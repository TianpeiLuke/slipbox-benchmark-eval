# The GDPR Track Running Alongside The Consumer Complaint

As of November 30, 2023 the data protection question about Meta's pay-or-consent offer was unresolved and running on a separate track from the consumer complaint. "The company's approach also raises concerns regarding the GDPR," BEUC's Ursula Pachl noted, and a spokesman for BEUC told TechCrunch it might, at a later stage, file a complaint about Meta's data protection compliance with the relevant privacy authority once it has completed its own assessment of the issues — though he emphasized it was too early to say whether or not it would take that step.

Meta's lead data protection authority in the EU, Ireland's Data Protection Commission (DPC), had for several months been assessing its pay-or-consent offer but had yet to communicate a conclusion. In the meanwhile, Meta maintains that the model it has devised for obtaining users' consent to its ads processing complies with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) — although, TechCrunch notes, the adtech giant also said that when it was claiming performance of a contract and then legitimate interests for the processing, both of which were subsequently found to be incompatible with the GDPR.

TechCrunch's reading of the incentive: Meta is likely banking on another multi-year round of GDPR complaints, legal challenges and finally a referral to the CJEU, followed by another long wait before a ruling gets handed down, buying it several more years to run with its new legal basis fix and keep feeding its profits by doing what it likes with Europeans' data.
