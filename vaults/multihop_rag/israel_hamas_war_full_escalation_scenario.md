# The Full-Escalation Scenario Against The Non-Escalation Base Case

The full-escalation scenario attacks the premise that the current hostilities will stay contained, which is the premise the limited-market-impact base case rests on. What supports the counter-proposition is that while there have been many conflicts between Israel and Hamas in past decades, there have been none of this intensity: with at least 1,200 Israelis killed, this was the biggest single-day killing of Jews since the Holocaust, and the Israeli opposition is united in retaliating. Israel has termed it a "War", formed a war management cabinet with a key opposition member, and vowed to change the look of the "Middle East". The historical analogy that grounds the base case therefore does not straightforwardly apply, because the reference class of past low-intensity episodes does not contain an event like this one.

The escalation pathway runs through regional actors. A massive strike by Israel in Gaza, potentially killing civilians in collateral damage, may attract more Middle East countries, including the de-facto leader Saudi Arabia, to raise serious issues against Israel on humanitarian grounds, and it will surely slow down the Saudi-Israel deal the US was facilitating. Besides Gaza, clashes have already been ongoing along Israel's borders with Syria and Lebanon. If Iran gets dragged into the conflict, on account of claims of funding Hamas, this could embroil the entire Middle East in crisis. In that scenario, the article projects major upward pressure on world inflation, on account of disruption in crude oil and world trade logistics.
