# Nobel Prize

## Definition

In the corpus, the Nobel Prize is the award administered by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences for the physics, chemistry, and economics categories, exemplified by the 2023 chemistry prize given to Moungi Bawendi, Louis Brus, and Alexei Ekimov for the discovery and synthesis of quantum dots.
Winners split a cash award of 11 million Swedish kronor (about $1 million), drawn from a bequest left by the prize's creator, Swedish inventor Alfred Nobel.

## Context

In the corpus the term appears centered on the 2023 chemistry prize, awarded to Moungi Bawendi, Louis Brus, and Alexei Ekimov for the discovery and synthesis of quantum dots, announced by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences in Stockholm on 2023-10-04.
That announcement sat within a week of prizes: the physics prize went to Anne L'Huillier, Pierre Agostini, and Ferenc Krausz on 2023-10-03, and the medicine prize went to Katalin Karikó and Drew Weissman on 2023-10-02 for mRNA vaccine research, with literature, peace, and economics prizes following on subsequent weekdays.
The term also surfaces outside the sciences-announcement context: a financial column refers in passing to "Nobel Laureate" Paul Krugman to lend authority to an economics analogy, showing the label is used in the corpus as a general marker of eminence as well as a specific science-prize designation.

## Key Characteristics

- **Administered by the Royal Swedish Academy of Sciences** for the physics, chemistry, and economics categories, with nominations solicited a year in advance from thousands of university professors and scholars worldwide, and nominee deliberations kept confidential for 50 years.
- **Committee-driven selection** — a committee for each prize discusses candidates over a series of meetings before presenting proposals to the full academy for a vote.
- **Cash award** — laureates split a prize of 11 million Swedish kronor (about $1 million), funded by Alfred Nobel's bequest.
- **Multi-category, staggered announcements** — physics, chemistry, medicine, literature, peace, and economics prizes are announced on separate weekdays within the same week.
- **Confidentiality can be broken accidentally** — the 2023 chemistry winners were identified by Swedish media hours before the official announcement, after the academy sent an early press release "for still unknown reasons."
- **Used loosely as an honorific outside its own announcements** — commentary elsewhere in the corpus invokes "Nobel Laureate" status (e.g., of economist Paul Krugman) simply to signal authority in an unrelated argument.

## Background (external)

Outside this corpus, the Nobel Prizes are a set of six international awards — physics, chemistry, physiology or medicine, literature, peace, and economic sciences — managed by the Nobel Foundation and funded from Alfred Nobel's bequest, first awarded in 1901 and given annually (with rare exceptions, such as wartime gaps in 1940-1942); the economics prize was added later, in 1968, by Sweden's central bank in Nobel's memory. (Wikipedia, "Nobel Prize," accessed 2026-09-02: https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nobel_Prize)
