# Israeli Post-War Economic Rehabilitation Planning Proposal

Leo Leiderman proposed that, despite the uncertainty over how the war would develop, Israel should begin preparing a general economic plan for rehabilitating the economy after the war, alongside an emergency budget. The precondition he sets is that planning starts now rather than after the fighting ends: "this is the time to start preparing a general economic plan with a strategy for rehabilitating the economy after the war."

The scope of the planning he specifies is detailed and professional coverage of all aspects of rehabilitating the settlements that have been damaged or destroyed in the south, rehabilitation of infrastructure, the health system, and the civilian systems that need attention.

On execution, he proposes tasking the Bank of Israel and the Ministry of Finance Budgets Division with coordinating the work, with help from the various research institutes and from economists, engineers and professionals in academic institutions and in the private sector. A required step is consultation: "It's important to talk to the private sector, and to understand in every place and every industry what the main damage is and what they need in order to get back to normal." He attaches a constraint on how the work is conducted — it should be done professionally, preferably with no politicisation of the recommendations.
