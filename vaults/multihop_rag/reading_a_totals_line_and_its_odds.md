# Reading A Totals Line And Its Odds

To read a totals bet, take the combined score the sportsbook has set and then read the odds printed beside it. When you see a total such as 210.5 in a basketball game, that number represents the combined score anticipated from both teams; beside it you will typically find "over" and "under" options. If you believe the actual combined score will surpass the number, you bet the "over"; if you think it will be less, you choose the "under."

The second step is evaluating the odds listed alongside the over/under number, which indicate the potential payout for the bet. Typically the odds for totals bets are close to even, ranging from -105 to -120; that narrow range reflects the bookmakers' belief that the event has a roughly even chance of going either way, with neither the over nor the under heavily favored. For instance, odds of -110 — extremely common — mean you would need to wager $110 to win an additional $100.

Sporting News stresses that evaluating both the total and the associated odds is crucial for informed betting decisions; reading the number without the odds leaves out the payout half of the bet.
