# Epstein's Sex Offender Waiver Request And Its Denial

Around mid-2019, shortly after Denise George was named attorney general, Governor Bryan — who was sworn in in 2019 — told her that Epstein had made a request: he wanted a new waiver to loosen certain restrictions placed on him as a convicted sex offender, according to court filings. Among the restrictions, Epstein had to give the Virgin Islands 21 days' notice when he planned to travel. A previous attorney general had granted such a waiver but it had later been revoked, and Epstein wanted a new one, George testified during a deposition earlier in 2023. Bryan texted George in May 2019, she testified, telling her to make a decision. She denied Epstein's request.

George says she wasn't aware of the scale of Epstein's crimes when she was named attorney general in 2019, less than two months before he was arrested at New Jersey's Teterboro Airport after flying from Paris — despite the lurid record and all the stories surrounding him. It didn't take her long to find out: she was only a few weeks into the job when news reporters began calling to ask whether authorities in the US Virgin Islands were investigating what Epstein had been up to for all those years on Little St. Jeff. They weren't. Of the waiver request she recalls thinking: "In my head I am thinking, Relax? If anything, wouldn't you say, Tighten them?"
