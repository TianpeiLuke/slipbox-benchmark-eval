# Deep-Sea Mining and Hydrothermal Vent Protection

Jon Copley argues that active hydrothermal vents should be off limits to mining and that he is confident they will be protected, because scientists already know enough to say that mining them would risk species extinction — no further research is needed on habitats we know to be that vulnerable.

The grounds are specific: active hydrothermal vents are a tiny habitat globally, just 50 square kilometers (19 square miles), yet home to more than 400 animal species not found in any other habitat type. Copley also tempers the wider alarm: he thinks it is great that deep sea mining has made people care more about the deep ocean, but it has not actually started yet and research does not support some of the more hyperbolic headlines, while a lot of research is focused on how mining would be managed if it does go ahead. His confidence in protection rests on the fact that scientists have been making this case for years.
