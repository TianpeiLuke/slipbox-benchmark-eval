# Epoch Times Ad Spending And Falun Gong Affiliate Grants

The Epoch Times is pouring its revenue back into its own organization and into others connected to the Falun Gong religious movement: in 2021 it provided about $30 million in grants to its own affiliates and connected organizations, including $10.4 million to the dance troupe Shen Yun and $8.3 million to New Tang Dynasty, which produces videos.

On advertising, in the first half of 2023 The Epoch Times spent 65 percent more on ads than in the first six months of 2022, with about half of its budget going to the X platform, formerly Twitter, according to the market intelligence firm Sensor Tower; other recent ad buys were made on the right-wing sites Drudge Report, Breitbart and Rumble. Tax documents also show it paid a company called Data Managing Corporation $2.7 million for advertising services in 2021; that company's listed address is a single-family home in New Jersey owned by a reporter for The Epoch Times, who did not return requests for comment.

The spending sits inside a broader expansion. As of October 2023 the outlet had yet to throw its weight behind a candidate for 2024, but heading into the election cycle it was moving into new and bigger office spaces and production studios in California, hiring mainstream news veterans not affiliated with Falun Gong, and revving up an ad-buying blitz. Together, the newspaper, Shen Yun and New Tang Dynasty — the three groups united under the Falun Gong religious movement — made up a nearly quarter-billion-dollar industry in 2021 according to tax documents, and Li holds that the Falun Gong messages shared in articles, videos and dance performances will result in the salvation of humankind as the end of the world nears.
