# Criticism Of UNRWA's Textbooks And Internal Governance

Against the case for funding UNRWA, defenders of the 2018 policy change that cut U.S. money cited UNRWA-published textbooks that allegedly glorified jihad — attacking the premise that the agency's educational work is politically neutral. UNRWA, for its part, maintained that as an outside organization it can only use the educational materials the country it is working in wants.

Two further lines of criticism target the agency's conduct rather than its curriculum. Some Republican politicians have said that UNRWA has "cozied up" to Hamas. And an internal UNRWA ethics committee has accused top staff at the agency of "sexual misconduct, nepotism, retaliation … and other abuses of authority" that created a toxic work environment.
