# US Youth Fentanyl Overdose Death Statistics

Across the US, young people are dying from fentanyl in record numbers even as overall drug use is on the decline: nationally, the number of opioid overdose deaths for people 24 and under nearly doubled from 2019 to 2021, and according to the National Institute on Drug Abuse the number of overdoses attributed to synthetic opioids like fentanyl dwarfs that of any other substance.

The state-level figure is sharper still. In California, fentanyl-related overdose deaths among 15- to 19-year-olds surged by nearly 800 percent between 2018 and 2021, according to data from the California Overdose Surveillance Dashboard.

The mechanism behind those deaths is counterfeiting rather than intentional fentanyl use: many of the young victims are poisoned by counterfeit pills pressed to look like legitimate prescription drugs but laced with fentanyl, an opioid that is deadly even in granular quantities. Typically, those teenagers acquired what they believed to be Percocet, Xanax or other pharmaceuticals online through social media.
