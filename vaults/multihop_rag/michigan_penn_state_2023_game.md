# Michigan 24, Penn State 15 At Beaver Stadium

No. 3 Michigan beat No. 10 Penn State 24-15 at Beaver Stadium on Saturday, Nov. 11, 2023, in a risk-averse game plan under acting coach Sherrone Moore in which the Wolverines threw one pass in the second half — and that did not even technically count. The defense smothered the Nittany Lions, and the win set up more buildup ahead of the showdown with No. 1 Ohio State on Nov. 25.

The game was played in a 20-yard box: neither team completed a pass of 20 yards or more, and Michigan proved it could beat Penn State (8-2, 5-2) that way. Michigan trailed 3-0 after the first quarter with eight carries for 10 yards, and Penn State's Abdul Carter set the tone with a jarring hit on Blake Corum that nearly resulted in a fumble. The Wolverines then took control on the ground: Donovan Edwards ripped off a 22-yard run, Corum added a 44-yard run to set up a 3-yard touchdown run, and Edwards added a 22-yard touchdown run on the next drive, the same "Lightning and Lightning" tandem that burned the Nittany Lions the previous year staking Michigan to a 14-9 halftime lead.

Michigan then took the ball out of quarterback J.J. McCarthy's hands entirely, with 30 rushing attempts and zero passing attempts save a pass to tight end Colston Loveland that drew a pass interference call. McCarthy finished 7 of 8 for 60 yards in what Sporting News called the most conservative play-calling victory in a top-10 showdown in recent memory, asking whether this was old-school Big Ten football or Big Ten West football in disguise. Michigan's defense clamped down on Penn State quarterback Drew Allar (10 of 22, 70 yards, TD), the Nittany Lions had one play of more than 20 yards, and Corum closed the drama with a 30-yard touchdown; Corum finished 26 carries for 145 yards and 2 touchdowns and Edwards 10 for 52 and a touchdown, with the defense now allowing just 7.5 points per game.

The result reset the conference picture. The same old questions about Nittany Lions coach James Franklin in big games resurfaced but were back-page headlines in the Big Ten East race; the quirky tiebreaker involving Big Ten West opponents was out the door; and the winner-take-all showdown between Ohio State and Michigan now had a full two weeks of anticipation.
