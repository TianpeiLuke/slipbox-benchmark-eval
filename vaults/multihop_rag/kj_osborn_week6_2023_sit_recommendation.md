# K.J. Osborn As A Week 6 2023 Sit At Chicago

CBS Sports listed K.J. Osborn under "Sit Him" for Week 6 of the 2023 season at Chicago: the claim is that there is some potential here but several hunches keep the author from falling for Osborn this week, and the warrant is that with Justin Jefferson out the Vikings should lean on the run and on more explosive weapons, while Chicago's heavy zone coverage is the scheme in which Osborn does least.

Three grounds are given in order. First, the Vikings will try to get their run game going since they don't have Justin Jefferson. Second, Jordan Addison is far more explosive and T.J. Hockenson has been a much more relied-upon weapon, so both should see more targets than Osborn. Third, the expected zone coverage Chicago will play further limits him unless he sees more targets than expected — the Bears play zone at the fourth-highest rate of any defense this season, 79.2%, and because Kirk Cousins is much more explosive against man coverage, Chicago is expected to keep it up. Osborn's own splits make that a problem: he has a 50% catch rate on the year against both zone and man, but sees more targets per route run against man and averages more yards per catch and more yards after catch against man, and over his career targets have come more frequently against man coverage, where he has been more explosive with them.

The recent usage and film support the caution, with one countervailing precedent. Over 19 snaps without Jefferson the previous week, Osborn ran 18 routes and caught both of his two targets for 30 total yards — a delightful receiving average but a saddening small-sample target share of 12.5%; the odd part is that his target share was much higher with Jefferson playing, 23.3%, while his receiving average tanked to 6.3. On film he didn't beat coverage with speed and his burst and agility were just OK: his 22-yard catch came on a Kirk Cousins scramble where he waited in a spot and then ran inside instead of outside where he might have scored, and he ducked under an end-zone target instead of leaping for it — though a 36-yard crosser against the Chargers flies in the face of that, as he separated from man coverage with speed and technique, turned upfield, ran outside and raced past a defender to dive for the pylon. The historical upside is real: in 20 career games with at least five targets, Osborn has scored at least 12 PPR points in 13 of them, 11 of those involving a touchdown catch, and one of his two career 100-yard games came last January at Chicago. The breakdown lists Minnesota at Chicago, the Vikings favoured by 2.5, an over/under of 44.5, an opponent ranked 20th versus wide receivers, 10.7 projected points, a 33rd receiver ranking, and year-to-date totals of 13 receptions on 26 targets for 166 yards, two touchdowns and 8.3 fantasy points per game. The verdict gives him more upside than Jerry Jeudy, Jahan Dotson and Tutu Atwell, while preferring a shot on Drake London, Terry McLaurin or Chris Godwin.
