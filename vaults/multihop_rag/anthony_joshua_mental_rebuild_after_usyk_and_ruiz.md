# Anthony Joshua Mental Rebuild After Usyk And Ruiz

Anthony Joshua, the global star from the United Kingdom, had traversed a difficult road mentally from his pair of title losses to Oleksandr Usyk, which triggered yet another trainer change and more than a few bizarre interviews in which Joshua appeared defensive and agitated — and he had recently attended a darkness retreat where he spent four days alone in a pitch black room in isolation, CBSSports.com reported on Dec. 23, 2023.

The troubles appeared to link all the way back to his 2019 upset loss to late replacement Andy Ruiz Jr. at New York's Madison Square Garden, which came as part of a much-publicized U.S. debut. Even though Joshua regained his titles in their immediate rematch, as Ruiz came to Saudi Arabia in obese physical shape, he had been undergoing a bit of an identity crisis as a boxer in the aftermath, even with a pair of 2023 rebound victories over Jermaine Franklin and Helenius, who also fought Joshua as a late replacement.

"This is just purely about doing the right thing and being 100 percent perfect every second that I am in that ring," Joshua told CBS Sports. "That way I will be victorious and victory will come by knockout if I carry out my plan A, B and C; it will lead me to the KO. Otto Wallin is a southpaw so he's very tricky. We have to be very clever. He has a character that is very resilient, as we saw against Tyson Fury. He can box off the back foot and come forward so it's going to be a good challenge for us because he's a complete fighter."
