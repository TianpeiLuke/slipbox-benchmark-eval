# Sphere's Plug-And-Play Touring Economics

Ric Lipson argues that venues like Sphere make touring more "plug-and-play": because the immersive environment lives in the building, a production no longer has to bring its own structure, screen and sound system, The Sydney Morning Herald reported on 19 October 2023.

The grounds are the venue's own capacity — "The Sphere does a similar thing, [in that] you have a fully immersive space, the video screen in the Sphere is vast, it has a huge number of pixels, and you can immerse the audience within this environment" — set against an industry that has historically built its lucrative concert spin-off business on converting sports venues to rock concert stages. "It opens you up to thinking about performances in different ways, both from a creative point of view, from a business point of view, from a sustainability point of view, and the ability to not have to bring your own structure and screen and [sound system]," Lipson says.

The warrant is the contrast with current practice and the plan that follows from it: "We have Adele in Caesar's Palace right now, which again is another large show that immerses the audience, but we bring everything in and every time we load that show out, it takes a week or so. What's interesting about the Sphere idea is that they are planning multiple spheres, and then you can think about shows being able to move from sphere to sphere."
