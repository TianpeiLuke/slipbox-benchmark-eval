# Equity Multiples Must Use Shareholder Line Items

An equity multiple is a ratio whose numerator is price per share or market cap, or a variation of the same, and whose denominator must be a line item that belongs to equity shareholders. The reasoning comes from the accounts: on any company's balance sheet the liabilities side is primarily made up of two line items, shareholder funds and borrowings, whereas a Profit and Loss statement has many line items, starting with revenue, followed by variable costs, fixed costs and taxes, and the only claim equity shareholders have for distribution or appropriation amongst themselves is what is left after paying taxes, namely net profit.

From this it follows that there are only three items attributable to equity shareholders: net profit from the P&L statement, book value or shareholder funds from the balance sheet, and free cash flows to equity from the cash flow statement. Any relative valuation multiple will be linked to these three items one way or another, and any other metric could be wrong and misleading. Hence it is very important to use the right multiples: when it comes to equity metrics, it always has to be price or market cap, or variations of the same, in the numerator, divided by line items attributable to equity holders, or variations of the same.
