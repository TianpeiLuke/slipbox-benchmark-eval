# Crisis Response Mechanism

## Definition

The crisis response mechanism is a provision of the EU's Digital Services Act (Article 36) that lets the European Commission adopt rapid-fire measures on very large online platforms (VLOPs) in situations of "serious threat," such as war.

## Context

The mechanism surfaces in the corpus as one of the DSA tools regulators point to while pressing X and Meta over content moderation during the Israel-Hamas war.
In the EU's formal request for information to X, the Commission separately asked X to report on "the activation and functioning of X's crisis response protocol" by October 18, 2023 — a company-level protocol distinct from, but referenced alongside, the DSA's own Article 36 mechanism.
Meta's coverage of the same period notes that the Article 36 mechanism cannot yet be adopted by the Commission because it is not operational: it needs a recommendation from an advisory body, the European Board for Digital Services, which in turn is waiting on Member States to stand up their national Digital Services Coordinators.

## Key Characteristics

- **Commission-adopted, not automatic** — the mechanism is a power the Commission "may adopt," triggered by a "serious threat" such as war, not a standing rule.
- **Scoped to larger platforms** — the corpus describes it applying to VLOPs, the same tier subject to the DSA's broader systemic-risk obligations.
- **Not yet operational as of October 2023** — activation depends on the European Board for Digital Services being operational, which in turn depends on Member States establishing national Digital Services Coordinators; the corpus gives a target start date of February 17 (the following year).
- **Distinct from a platform's own crisis protocol** — the corpus separately shows the Commission asking X to report on "the activation and functioning of X's crisis response protocol," a company-run process that is not the same thing as the Commission's Article 36 mechanism, though the two are easy to conflate.
