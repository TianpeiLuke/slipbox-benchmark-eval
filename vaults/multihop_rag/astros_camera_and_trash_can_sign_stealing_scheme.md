# The Astros' Camera-To-Trash-Can Sign-Stealing Machine

The Astros' scheme was a three-stage relay running from a center-field camera to a dugout screen to an audible bang, as Essentially Sports described on 2023-10-22. Stage one: the Stros installed cameras in center field at Minute Maid Park to record the opposing team's catcher and pitcher's signs — cameras in a ballpark are ideally for challenging calls, but what was one more purpose? The article situates this in a long tradition: teams have tried to catch on to their rivals' signs since competitive baseball became a thing, because the communication between catcher and pitcher can be key to hitting; the 21st century simply added technology.

Stage two: behind the scenes, players could see a live feed from the camera, and then-bench coach Alex Cora decided to get a screen installed in the dugout, so players or managers would watch the live feed on the television placed in the tunnel leading from the dugout to the clubhouse. Stage three, the relay to the batter, is where the irony lay. Having tried out different methods of shouting and calling, the Astros landed on, of all things, a trash can: players would bang on them, often using bats, with different rhythms signifying different pitches. The system had limits — they couldn't do it at each home game, nor did they try. But whatever sorcery Houston had been practicing that year did the trick: it worked like a charm, and the Astros had their first World Series trophy in a long history.
