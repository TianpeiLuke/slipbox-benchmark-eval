# How War Escalation Transmits To Indian Equities

Under the escalation scenario, the chain runs from supply disruption to higher inflation to tighter-for-longer policy to a material correction in Indian equities. Supply disruption will lead to higher inflation, and central bankers will have to adjust their policies and hold rates higher for longer than anticipated; the article envisages a scenario where rates are held higher till the year 2025. A higher crude oil bill and higher global rates will prompt the RBI to also remain hawkish in its approach with regard to rates and liquidity measures. EBITDA margins of India Inc. may again get impacted on account of higher input costs and higher freight and fuel costs.

Two domestic conditions amplify the transmission: rural India is a key engine that is not firing at the moment, and valuations are rich and do not offer a margin of safety in the near to medium term. The near-term attractiveness of equities will reduce as investors demand a higher risk premium, and the Indian stock market will likely correct materially as FIIs look to withdraw funds from risk assets and seek safe havens in the US Dollar, US treasuries offering high yields, and Gold. The sectors named as negatively impacted are Petrochemicals, Oil Marketing, Paints, Packaging, Textiles and Chemicals. The whole model is conditional on the escalation scenario rather than the base case.
