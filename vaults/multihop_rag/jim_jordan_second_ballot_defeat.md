# Jim Jordan's Second-Ballot Defeat

Republican Rep. Jim Jordan failed again on Wednesday, 2023-10-18, on a crucial second ballot to become House speaker, with the hard-fighting ally of Donald Trump losing even more GOP colleagues who refused to give him the gavel: he was opposed by 22 Republicans, two more than he lost in first-round voting the day before. What was clear was that Jordan's path to become House speaker was almost certainly lost. As the roll call got under way he lost more than he gained, picking up three backers but adding more detractors, and no further votes were scheduled.

Ahead of the morning vote Jordan, the combative Judiciary Committee chairman and a founding member of the hard-right Freedom Caucus, made an unusual plea for party unity, almost daring his colleagues to put forward the alternative proposal for a temporary speaker. "We've been at this two weeks," he said at the Capitol. "American people deserve to have their government functioning." After the vote he vowed to stay in the race: "We'll keep talking to members, keep working on it." One new Jordan opponent, Rep. Vern Buchanan of Florida, explained his vote: "I think it's time to move on."
