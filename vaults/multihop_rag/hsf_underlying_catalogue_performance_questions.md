# What Really Matters Is The Underlying Catalogue Performance

MBW argues that amongst all the noise around Hipgnosis Songs Fund, what will most likely interest its investors, including the likes of Investec Wealth & Investment, Aviva Investors and BlackRock, is the ongoing underlying commercial performance of the firm's copyrights. The noise is substantial: the article notes plenty of drama surrounding HSF, from the discontinuation vote seen at the company in October through to a recent lawsuit launched against Hipgnosis Songs Fund by a former business partner of Merck Mercuriadis.

The questions that matter are posed as three: how are HSF's assets performing versus the market; how is the company's "premium-only" acquisition strategy holding up against its promise of growth "uncorrelated" to other industries; and what does all of this suggest about the ongoing growth potential of the HSF portfolio versus the multiples that Merck Mercuriadis paid to acquire it. The warrant for prioritising these over the governance drama is that such questions will only be answered when HSF's board elects to release the six-monthly financial results to 30 September, which makes the delayed results the decisive document. MBW names the most important KPI within those results, in its view: the company's Pro-Forma Revenue, and how it compares like-for-like against the previous year's equivalent period.
