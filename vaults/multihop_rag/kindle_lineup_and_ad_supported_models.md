# The Kindle Lineup And Ad-Supported Models

Amazon, one of the pioneers of the e-reader, has dominated the space for years with its ever-expanding Kindle lineup, which consists of several unique models with their own pros and cons. The bulk of the devices function as simple ebook readers; with the Kindle Scribe, however, Amazon looks to be moving beyond books and into the realm of writing — something that should make future Kindles function more akin to physical paper. The category itself is defined by what it leaves out: you can always read ebooks on a tablet or a phone, but those devices are multipurpose and can be used for a ton of things like surfing the web or doom-scrolling on Twitter, whereas e-readers, while niche, are designed to store all of your books in a virtual library with limited functionality.

The discriminating option across the lineup is advertising. Kindle e-readers come in different storage configurations, but there is also an additional option to buy the device with or without ads: ad-supported Kindles display personalized advertisements on the lock screen when the device is in sleep mode, or at the bottom of the homescreen when it is connected to Wi-Fi. You typically save about $20 by buying the ad-supported version, and if you decide to remove ads later you can make a one-time payment covering the difference to remove them.

The Verge's October 16th, 2023 guide lists each model currently available, noting that sometimes there is no deal on one or any of the products, in which case it gives the most recent sale price instead. It also flags an alternative route to savings: Amazon offers 20 percent off all of its Kindles when you trade in select devices, so there are still ways to save when no model is discounted.
