# Wonder's Disposable-Idea Design As An Alternative To "Forever Games"

Polygon argues that Super Mario Bros. Wonder's willingness to squeeze each idea for its fun and then throw the rind away is a potent alternative to the current era of AAA "forever games" filled with endless lists of chores and artificial rewards — because in Wonder the reward isn't XP or limited-edition outfits, it's the play itself. The reviewer calls the creators' abundance of new ideas "unapologetically wasteful," and means it as a compliment: many gimmicks here could be stretched to fill entire games, but instead the player is lucky to see them last a few minutes.

The grounds are a worked example of how a single idea is escalated and then discarded: one stage features platforms that drop when you step on them; the next has platforms that drop only after a certain number of jumps, with a countdown clock at their centre; then a stage of platforms made of batteries, each dropping as it runs out of energy; and finally a stage in which you build the platform out of huge batteries of different shapes, growing and shrinking rapidly as it skims above a yawning abyss. The reviewer heads off the obvious objection — that such refusal to outstay a welcome means a short game — by noting that levels are everywhere: the map features six worlds, but levels are also found in the hub area and, to avoid spoilers, elsewhere. The familiar rhythms of a Mario game are broken too, with bosses appearing without warning, some worlds ending with no boss fight at all, and the occasional surprise stage materialising like a mirage.
