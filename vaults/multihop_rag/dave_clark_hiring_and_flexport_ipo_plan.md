# Dave Clark's Hiring And Flexport's IPO Plan

Clark arrived at Flexport in 2022 as the apparently perfect hire for a tech startup trying to disrupt the age-old logistics industry — he had built Amazon's logistics unit into a juggernaut that rivalled carriers like UPS and FedEx — and among his goals was to help Petersen prepare Flexport for an IPO, something the company had discussed doing within a two- to three-year window.

Since 2021 Petersen had been seeking a successor for then-operating chief Sanne Manders, in part to address what several ex-employees described as lingering issues with the company's troubled billing processes; fixing that was Clark's job. Petersen and Clark worked together as co-CEOs for the first six months, and in March Petersen transitioned to executive chairman — an arrangement meant to free Petersen up to do what he loved, "getting beers with customers," in the words of two former Flexport employees, while Clark, a self-described "builder at heart," was at the wheel. "There's a perfect complement of skill sets," Petersen told Forbes in June 2022. "Mine are much more creative, zero-to-one founder time, and Dave is the supreme executor and a legend in the supply chain world." Buying Deliverr was meant to be the first step in turning Flexport into a more full-scale logistics service. Shopify had acquired Deliverr in May 2022 for $2.1 billion, but the e-commerce software company was being hammered by Wall Street as its Covid pandemic pop faded, and by January 2023 CEO Tobias Lutke knew he needed to get rid of it; around that time Lutke first approached Petersen to float a deal, and Petersen told Clark he should engage with Shopify's team. Initial talks fell apart but resumed when Flexport executives learned Shopify was about to execute deep cost cuts and was eager to sell. Clark and Petersen flew to Miami to meet Shopify's leadership, and as a transaction neared, Clark — who had a reputation as a deft negotiator — got Shopify, already an investor in Flexport, to sweeten it with $40 million in cash and the framework for a $260 million convertible note that could help Flexport on its path to an IPO, according to an internal document analyzing the deal. The sale would be announced alongside Shopify's first-quarter earnings report on May 4. "We did not change the terms of a deal or rush it just to have it line up with an earnings call," Shopify said in a statement, adding that with Flexport "we are tightly mission-aligned to ensure the success of our merchants, which is why we chose to deepen our partnership with them earlier this year." The night before the announcement, Petersen appeared at a "Tech Talk" at Flexport's Bellevue, Washington office to pitch the "Flexport vision" to hundreds of people; asked whether Flexport would ever get into last-mile logistics, he paused, glanced at his watch, and said to keep an eye on the morning news, according to an employee who witnessed the exchange and a person told independently. The comment alarmed Clark and Flexport executives, who were concerned Petersen had disclosed material nonpublic information about a publicly traded company. Petersen did not respond to calls or messages from CNBC, the company declined to make him available for an interview, and a Flexport spokesperson did not respond to CNBC's question about whether Petersen was aware of concerns about his statement.
