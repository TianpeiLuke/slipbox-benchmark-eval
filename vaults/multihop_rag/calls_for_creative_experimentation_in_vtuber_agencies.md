# Calls for Creative Experimentation in VTuber Agencies

"Agencies should allow for talents to experiment, so long as it isn't hurting anyone," the anonymous Nijisanji source told Polygon for its 2023-10-19 piece — the central claim of an argument that VTubing must reinvent itself and give talents more freedom to avoid further stagnation.

The grounds begin with content range: NagamiMugi says that if VTubers took cues from other content creators and explored options like lore analysis videos or short-form reviews, the scene would not be as stagnant. Mugi, who has dreams of going corporate and is already relatively successful with her own storefront hosting collaborations and merchandise featuring other VTubers and artists, has auditioned for several major agencies — and says one asked whether she would close down her business if she passed the interview phase and became one of its talents.

That is not entirely uncommon: the Nijisanji source mentions that some talents have been given strict restrictions on what content they can and cannot create once their contracts with the agency expire, though this varies between talents and what is agreed between talent and agency. So while agencies can be viable for a creator's long-term growth, that growth is often strictly tied to the avatar associated with the agency. Both Hololive and Nijisanji give creators very little freedom in terms of presentation, whereas newer agencies such as Idol allow talents to create their own concepts — which may feel overwhelming to some but lets the talents behind these models flex their creativity and create a persona that would not fit neatly into the clearly defined boxes some agencies slot them into.

The full statement adds a tolerance for failure: "Let people fail and still have that opportunity for growth in the future. More risk-taking and trying to branch out in other ways instead of simply just streaming or following preestablished methods." According to both people Polygon interviewed, this is an agreed-upon sentiment — that to prevent further stagnation, this not-so-niche form of content creation needs to reinvent itself and allow more freedom to its talents. Time will tell whether larger agencies like Hololive, which reported 50% growth in its 2023 financial report, and Nijisanji will let their talents break the mold.
