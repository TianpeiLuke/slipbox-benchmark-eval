# Licensing Or US Manufacturing As Apple's Exit Routes

Two remaining routes out of the ban would cost Apple money: it could sit down with Masimo and hammer out a licensing deal for the infringed patents, or it could avoid the import ban altogether by manufacturing the watches within the US instead of overseas.

Neither is straightforward. On licensing, Masimo CEO Joe Kiani told The New York Times that Apple "had not engaged in licensing negotiations," and any settlement requires both parties to want one. "Apple is a tough nut to crack," said Smith Brittingham, noting that Apple is one of the most sued companies in Silicon Valley and that its large cash reserves make taking Apple to court a costly endeavor — Masimo has reportedly spent $60 million litigating the issue thus far. "I think they tend to hold out as long as possible in order to make it less appetizing to sue Apple." On domestic manufacturing, Andrei Iancu said Apple could simply decide to build the watches in the US, but that is a significant supply chain change unlikely to happen any time soon.
