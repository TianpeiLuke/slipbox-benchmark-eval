# Pokémon As A Lifestyle: The Reinvention Thesis

The claim is that Pokémon is no longer a series for children but a franchise that promises something for everyone, and that this reinvention is what has kept it growing.

The grounds are commercial and cultural at once. In recent years The Pokémon Company has recorded record profits and revenue, while the games, anime, and TCG are more popular than ever, with both new mainline Switch entries selling over 20 million copies. The phenomenon of growing out of Pokémon is no longer a concern: even if young kids stop playing the video games as they grow, that does not mean they will not still engage with this world via other means, nor that they will not introduce their own children to the series in the future.

The warrant is that PokéTsume is only possible because of that reinvention, and so serves as its proof. In reality it is a mostly light, inoffensive promotional tool to sell games — it is no coincidence the second episode has a scene of a young kid playing the latest entries on his Nintendo Switch — but it represents everything the franchise has aimed to be in recent years. New adults seeking direction in life might find a fellow directionless 20-something employee more relatable than a kid wanting to be a Pokémon Champion; frankly, it is a way to see they are not alone, no different from how your party of pokémon kept you company through the deepest caverns of Mt. Moon.
