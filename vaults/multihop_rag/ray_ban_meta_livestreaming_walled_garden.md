# Ray-Ban Meta Livestreaming And Meta's Walled Garden

Against the idea that livestreaming makes the Ray-Ban Meta glasses a creator tool, The Verge's reviewer argued that Meta sort of shot itself in the foot: some streamers would love this if they could use it on TikTok or Twitch, but they cannot, and are instead corralled into Meta's services. It makes sense — Big Tech loves protecting its walled gardens — but from a broader adoption perspective it is silly, and it is one of the reasons Snap's Spectacles never took off. Meta is mostly lucky that Instagram is still popular with influencers.

Mechanically the feature works well. A glasses icon pops up automatically on the livestream screen in Facebook or Instagram, though, as the reviewer notes, most content creators are not livestreaming to Facebook; you can either tap the icon or double-click the capture button to seamlessly switch views between your phone camera and the glasses, and there is a teeny lag between what you see and what gets livestreamed, but nothing egregious. Her caveat on the evidence is that livestreaming was hard to test organically, mainly because none of her Instagram followers expect her to livestream.
