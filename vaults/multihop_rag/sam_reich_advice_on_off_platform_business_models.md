# Sam Reich's Advice To Creators: Find A Better Business Model

Asked what five years of Dropout has taught him for creators, producers or fans looking to get away from algorithmically powered viral demand, Sam Reich's answer is blunt: "My advice is find a better business model." His claim is that sustainability requires something to offer off the algorithmic platforms — "for you to make this sustainable you need to have something to offer that's off of those platforms."

His grounds are an account of how platforms shape content. "I think we live in an era of online content right now that is being defined and shaped by algorithms. And that's true no matter the platform." Different platforms shape content in different ways, which he finds exciting as a creator — "Which of these sports am I most excited to play?" — and he gives a concrete example: TikTok has lately been prioritising videos longer than a minute because it is trying to encourage watch time, while YouTube Shorts still doesn't allow anything longer than a minute. Platforms compete with each other and themselves and yield these kind of Frankenstein products. He is careful not to write the platforms off — he is regularly inspired by what he sees, loves hanging out on them, and thinks some creators are getting really creative.

The warrant is that off-platform offerings restore independence, and he names several routes: creating a subscription offering like Patreon and driving people to it; leveraging an online presence into opportunities in traditional Hollywood; or, for people who aren't content creators first and foremost, using it to market their music or physical products, which is working out well for them. For Dropout itself, "the heart and the soul of what we do is on Dropout. We are very lucky, very lucky, that social media has turned out to be such a good way for us to market this platform. And we are also gleefully no longer beholden to it." The context for the question was something Reich told NPR earlier in the year: that Dropout found success not by going broad for the viral video but by going for "something that feels special to a small group of people."
