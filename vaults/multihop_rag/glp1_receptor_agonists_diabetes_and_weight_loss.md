# GLP-1 Receptor Agonists In Diabetes And Weight Loss

GLP-1 is a normal peptide released by the intestine in response to a meal, which binds to GLP-1 receptors in the pancreas and activates insulin production, a process impaired in Type-2 diabetes patients; GLP-1 receptor agonists are external agents that bind to those receptors and elevate insulin secretion by the pancreas. Because GLP-1 receptors are also found in the kidney, lung, heart, skin, immune cells and several other tissues, GLP-1RAs attain a multi-modal result that improves cardiac and renal outcomes and aids weight loss through slower gastric emptying by binding to intestinal receptors, and it is this feature that separates GLP-1RA from other modes. Anti-diabetic treatment began with the discovery of the first animal-made insulin around a century ago, followed by insulin synthesised from humans, synthetic insulins and longer and shorter acting insulins; apart from pills and the ever-reliable metformin, three leading categories operate in anti-diabetes, DPP-IV inhibitors such as Januvia and Janumet, SGLT2 inhibitors such as Jardiance and Farxiga, and GLP-1 receptor agonists such as Trulicity, Victoza and Ozempic. GLP-1RA is inching over the other categories with improved glycemic control in clinical trials, and with positive impact on non-glycemic aspects including weight loss, cardiac events and kidney functions, GLPs will see more product innovation and R&D commitment in the short term.

Treatment regimens are now not restricted to glycemic control. Novo Nordisk's Semaglutide, a leading GLP-1RA regimen, has shown in studies that it can reduce an adverse cardiovascular event by 20 per cent in overweight patients, and in Type-2 diabetes patients its study to show improved outcomes in chronic kidney disease was successful even on interim analysis. Semaglutide branded as Wegovy showed 17-18 per cent weight loss sustained over a 68-week trial for obese patients, which has created a flutter as Pfizer, Eli Lilly and others have joined the race in the category. Convenience is another dimension carrying significant weight: once-a-week insulin, Ozempic, has been approved and a once-a-week long-acting basal analogue is in the works, and because frequent insulin injections are a primary complaint in diabetes regimens Novo Nordisk has also launched a pill version of its GLP-1RA, Rybelsus.
