# Theory: Copyright Liability Over OpenAI's Training Data

The proposition is that the board acted over mounting accusations that OpenAI's product was built on a dataset including thousands or millions of copyrighted works, with the CEO systematically downplaying the potential liability. TechCrunch's reporter notes that Altman told him at OpenAI's Dev Day earlier that month that the company does not want to incur any copyright problems by using datasets of pirated books, but that a lot of research he had been reading contradicts that, as does pretty much every AI data scientist he talks to. It is exceedingly hard to imagine that OpenAI built GPT-3 with the copyrighted books database, as seems to be the case, but not GPT-4 or succeeding models.

The framing of the hypothesis is a question about the board's likely reaction: if you were the board and facing those accusations, and your CEO had systematically downplayed the potential liability, how would you feel? "I'd feel very hurt."

What works against it is again the succession. If copyright liability was the reason, it seems unlikely that the board would promote the CTO; presumably OpenAI's Chief Science Officer Ilya Sutskever would also have been in the know, and he is still on the board.
