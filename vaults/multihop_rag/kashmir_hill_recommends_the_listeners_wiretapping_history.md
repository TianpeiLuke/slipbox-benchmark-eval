# Kashmir Hill Recommends "The Listeners"

Kashmir Hill recommends "The Listeners: A History of Wiretapping in the United States," by Brian Hochman, because it gives her a historical frame for what mass data collection may do next. Hill's own book "Your Face Belong to Us" is the story of a "small AI company that gave facial recognition to law enforcement, billionaires and businesses, threatening to end privacy as we know it" — and she does not need to resort to science fiction to make it chilling, since the company in question, Clearview AI, very much exists, with its Dutch publisher describing the book as a "real-life thriller."

Her grounds are the parallel between two technological moments. She is fascinated by the intersection of privacy and technology and how we navigate the clashes between the two, and Hochman — a professor of American Studies at Georgetown — looks back to a historical moment when society was confronted with an intrusive new technology, tiny recording devices and wiretaps, and how new norms and laws developed to address it.

The warrant is explicitly conditional: to the extent that past is prologue, she found it very compelling for framing her expectations for what is to come with the newest and most shocking forms of mass data collection and use.
