# The Gaza Information War And The Hamas Narrative

Eylon Levy argues that the conflict is not only a kinetic war in Gaza but also an information war, in which the Hamas narrative is adopted far more easily than Israel's account is accepted. His first ground is a case of a claim that collapsed: the day after what he calls the bluff about the Al-Ahli hospital, which the Palestinians claimed had been hit by an Israeli missile killing 800 civilians but which in the end turned out to have been caused by a splinter group of Islamic Jihad, with no hospital destroyed and certainly not 800 people killed, he explained on Sky News that Hamas lies and makes up numbers, and the reporter asked him, almost speechless with surprise, whether he doubts Hamas' numbers. He describes such moments as intensely frustrating, recounting a BBC interview in which the interviewer told him "Hamas denies what you are saying", to which his answer is that of course Hamas denies this, since Hamas denies that it is even holding hostages, and Hamas massacres, beheads, rapes, burns families and then day and night denies that it did so. Some journalists around the world see that Hamas continues to lie about the biggest things when the whole truth is in front of everyone's eyes, and they continue to echo the lies.

His second ground is a structural claim about where information from Gaza comes from: it comes either directly from Hamas or from independent reporters and photographers who are part of the system in Gaza, and everyone knows that even if they are not affiliated with Hamas they are not free to present the full truth because they are not allowed to. The evidence he offers for the constraint is what is missing: there is not a single photo of a terrorist, not a single photo of a missile being launched from a densely populated area, from a mosque or a school, not even a single photo from the Hamas tunnels under Shifa; the reason is that these reporters, even if not explicitly on Hamas' side, operate under a dictatorial regime and cannot provide the information even if they wanted to, which is why he thinks it very important to keep reminding the world how biased the information reaching them is. Levy qualifies the argument explicitly: he does not want to give the impression that all interviewers are hostile and every question delusional, and he has many interviews with reporters who understand what is happening and simply want information and to hear the Israeli side, which is completely legitimate.
