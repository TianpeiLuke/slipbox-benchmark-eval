# Turtle Beach Recon Controller

Turtle Beach is known for its headsets, so it makes sense that its Recon wired controller features robust audio controls, and its strengths come into focus when playing first-person shooters, where its Superhuman Hearing mode tweaks the audio of any wired headset to draw out footsteps and distant gunfire in the mix. Specifications: wired connectivity, USB-C connector, two mappable rear buttons, no software customization, wired power. At its regular price of around $60 — and once selling as low as $39.95 — it is a fairly affordable way to gain a slight competitive edge in multiplayer games without spending well over $100.

The audio controls are extensive. This pad is not going to win any beauty contests thanks to all those awkward buttons located smack-dab in the middle of its forehead, but each has a valid purpose: four levels of microphone monitoring, overall game volume controls, game chat volume mix, four EQ presets, four programmable modes for mapping the two rear buttons, and four levels of optional right-stick sensitivity adjustment. That last feature, dubbed Pro Aim, lowers the sensitivity of the right analog stick while you hold the right-hand rear button, allowing very small movements like sniping, though it is a bit clumsy to use. Learning all these controls may sound daunting, but the controller's quick-start guide makes it easier. Superhuman Hearing is very helpful for shooters and worth using extensively for games like Halo Infinite — just be aware it is going to muddy up the soundtrack a little, so you may want to leave it off during story-driven, single-player games.

Audio aside, the Recon lacks fancy features like hair triggers and swappable thumbsticks, and the D-pad looks like the standard Xbox Wireless Controller's but pales in comparison. The plastic build feels cheap, but the rubbery grips redeem it with a nice and comfy feel, and the bumpers, shoulders, and customizable rear buttons have an excellent textured pattern that feels great. The sticks, triggers, and additional rear buttons all feel tight and snappy, which combined with Superhuman Hearing makes a great recipe for shooters. The Recon has a bit of a learning curve, but if you play ranked matches and value a fairly priced wired controller with extra buttons and helpful audio functions, you cannot go wrong here.
