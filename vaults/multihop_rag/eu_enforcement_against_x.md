# EU Enforcement Steps Against X Over DSA Compliance

Between 10 and 13 October 2023 the European Union escalated against Elon Musk-owned X in two steps: a public warning letter on Wednesday from internal market commissioner Thierry Breton, and then, late on Thursday, a formal request for information sent by the Commission under the Digital Services Act. Crucially, no investigation had been opened at that point — EU officials confirmed to TechCrunch that an investigation had not been taken, and TechCrunch substantially revised its original report to make clear that a formal request for information, while it could prefigure a formal announcement of an investigation, is not yet that formal technical step.

The warning came first. In his "urgent" letter, shared on X by Breton himself, the bloc sent the strongest signal yet that it believed Musk's platform was in breach of the DSA — though this was by no means Musk's first warning. Breton wrote that the EU had "indications" that X was being used to disseminate illegal content and disinformation in the EU following Saturday's attacks, then reminded the company of the DSA's "very precise obligations" on content moderation: "When you receive notices of illegal content in the EU, you must be timely, diligent and objective in taking action and removing the relevant content when warranted," he warned, adding, "We have, from qualified sources, reports about potentially illegal content circulating on your service despite flags from relevant authorities." He also took issue with a change X had made the previous night to the public interest policy under which it judges newsworthiness — the test for whether posts that infringe its rules may nonetheless remain on the site — which Breton said had left "many European users uncertain" about how X applies its own rules. That matters under the DSA because the regulation requires platforms to be clear and transparent about their rules and how they apply them, "particularly relevant when it comes to violent and terrorist content that appears to circulate on your platform." Breton further said X should have "proportionate and effective mitigation measures" in place to tackle "the risks to public security and civic discourse stemming from disinformation." The EU asked Musk to make contact with relevant law enforcement authorities and Europol and to "ensure that you respond promptly to their requests", flagged unspecified additional DSA compliance issues his team would raise "shortly" — "with a specific request" — and gave Musk a deadline of 24 hours to respond, stipulating that his answer would be added to the bloc's assessment file on X's DSA compliance. The letter closed: "I remind you that following the opening of a potential investigation and a finding of non-compliance, penalties can be imposed."

The formal request for information followed. It came after the Wednesday warning about reports of illegal content and disinformation targeting the Israel-Hamas war circulating on the platform, and after Twitter's CEO sent a high-level, non-specific response to that warning. The Commission said it was looking at X's compliance with the DSA across a number of areas — its policies and practices regarding notices on illegal content; complaint handling; risk assessment; and measures to mitigate the risks identified. In its press release the EU wrote that "This request follows indications received by the Commission services of the alleged spreading of illegal content and disinformation, in particular the spreading of terrorist and violent content and hate speech," adding that the ask also addressed other aspects of DSA compliance.

The request carried explicit deadlines and consequences. X had until October 18 to provide information about "the activation and functioning of X's crisis response protocol", and until October 31 to respond to the other requests. "Based on the assessment of X replies, the Commission will assess next steps," the Commission wrote. "This could entail the formal opening of proceedings pursuant to Article 66 of the DSA." The regulation also includes powers to impose fines for "incorrect, incomplete or misleading information in response to a request for information", and the EU added that "In case of failure to reply by X, the Commission may decide to request the information by decision. In this case, failure to reply by the deadline could lead to the imposition of period penalties." TechCrunch noted that the pace at which regulators were moving was a strong indication of the bloc's direction of travel, and that its revised report also corrected an error in the original which had referred to Interpol rather than Europol.
