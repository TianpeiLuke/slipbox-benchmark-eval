# Adult Stem Cells and Tissue Renewal

Adult stem cells are the tissue-native cells that make organ regeneration possible: much like automobiles that require constant maintenance to remain operational, our bodies rely on the continual renewal of cells to sustain organ function, and adult stem cells supply that renewal.

The feature that discriminates them from embryonic stem cells is restricted potential. Unlike their embryonic counterparts, which can become any cell type, adult stem cells are programmed to only replace the specific types of cells in their respective tissues.

Determining how these cells are guided to develop into specific tissue cells has been a key question. Gabriele Colozza, a postdoctoral researcher with Bon-Kyoung Koo at IMBA — Koo currently directing at the Center for Genome Engineering at the Institute for Basic Science in South Korea — took on this challenge by delving into the mechanisms that direct intestinal stem cells.
