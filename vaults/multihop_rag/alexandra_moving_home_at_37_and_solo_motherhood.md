# Alexandra, Moving Home At 37 And Solo Motherhood

Alexandra moved back in with her parents at 37, stayed four months, and later returned for the first three months of her son's life after becoming a solo mother through fertility treatment — an experience she describes as both a privilege and a slide into infantilisation, The Age reported on 25 November 2023.

"While it was lovely to be taken care of, we all tend to regress when we're living with our parents. It's impossible not to feel infantilised, and there's a real tipping point where you start to lean into learnt helplessness," she says. She stayed for four months before moving out to house-sit a friend's place, then launched herself into dating to try to find a man to have a family with; after the roller-coaster of dating she decided to start fertility treatment on her own, and by the time it began she had found a stable job and was renting an apartment. When she got pregnant she was elated but also terrified about how she would raise a child on her own, financially and emotionally. Luckily, after she gave birth her parents invited her to live with them, so she moved back for the first three months of her son's life.

"While it was wonderful to have the emotional and practical support, after three months I felt that if I didn't get out there to live as an independent person, I would quickly lose any sense of what I was capable of. But I'm incredibly grateful for the time I had living with my parents; I know it's a huge privilege to have them as a safety net." She ended up writing a memoir, Inconceivable, about her journey to become a solo mum and the complexities of being a woman in her late 30s who has to start over and move back in with her parents: "I think while we're all embracing this contemporary feminist movement where women can create families of their own making, we're also realising that we need help, that parenthood can't happen alone."
