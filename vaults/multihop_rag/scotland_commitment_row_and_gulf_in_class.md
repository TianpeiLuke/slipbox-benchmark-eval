# The Scotland Commitment Row And The Gulf In Class

Against the suggestion that Scotland's efforts at Hampden should be questioned — that a defensive collapse almost helped England towards a Team GB place at the Paris Olympics — The Guardian argues that would be unfair.

The premise it attacks is that Scotland's players were less than fully committed. The counter-grounds are, first, that the gulf in class between the European champions, ranked fourth in the world, and their neighbours to the north, ranked 23rd, is clear, and that the manager, Pedro Martínez Losa, has been unconvincing in charge — so the scoreline needs no explanation in terms of effort. Second, the accusations had already been rejected by the players: Lucy Bronze called the claim that Scotland players might feel conflicted, with Olympic qualification on the line for them as much as for England, "the rudest thing I've ever heard," and Scotland's captain Rachel Corsie called it "disrespectful" and "outrageous." Third, any doubt about Scotland's intentions was put to bed, temporarily at least, in the opening three minutes, with Bronze clattered into late twice as Scotland looked to make an early impression, that rare foray forward urged on by a vocal Scotland crowd. Wiegman, for her part, had urged her players to "go wild from the first minute" while cautioning "but you don't want to get erratic," and England came out of the blocks searching for an early goal to assert their authority. Among the crowd, after Lauren Hemp raced free on the right, a hopeful "Come on Lauren" was met with a thick, cheery Scottish "Shut up!" behind the press box.
