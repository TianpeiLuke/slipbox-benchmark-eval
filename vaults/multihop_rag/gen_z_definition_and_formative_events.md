# Gen Z: Definition And Formative Events

Gen Z, as characterized in this account, is the cohort aged 11 to 26 as of October 2023 and the first fully digital native generation — the feature that discriminates it from every earlier cohort is not the scale of the history it has lived through but that it has been plugged into the world through the internet from such a young age. Social media posts are its lingua franca, and the "disturbing images" warning is one it is used to encountering there.

The formative events listed are dense. This generation has already lived through a once-per-century pandemic; the Jan. 6 insurrection, an event unseen for centuries in American politics, when armed right-wing extremists stormed the Capitol on Jan. 6, 2021 following the election defeat of former President Donald Trump; the first major European ground war since World War II, in Ukraine; and market crashes in 2008 and 2020 that recall the Great Depression. Three years before the article, as Gen Z emerged into young adulthood, online videos of the murder of George Floyd shook the country in May 2020, resulting in a summer of violent Black Lives Matter protests and riots. School shootings have only increased in frequency since Columbine in 1999, with more students documenting the terror on their phones and sharing it online — one of them, the Parkland shooting of 2018, tragically created the first spokespeople of the post-millennial generation. Most recently, on Saturday, 2023-10-07, the Islamic militant group Hamas launched an unprecedented attack on Israel, resulting in the worst war the region has seen in 50 years; in three days more than 1,500 people died on both sides, and graphic, difficult-to-watch videos blanketed the internet.

The boundary case the account itself raises is that every generation has lived through era-defining historical events: millennials remember the 9/11 terrorist attacks and the U.S. invasion of Iraq, and baby boomers lived through the civil rights movement and the Vietnam War. What sets Gen Z apart is the medium — experiencing it all through videos, images, and articles online, which is shaping their mental health, workplace attitudes, and financial habits in visible ways.
