# Wildest Dreams: Imagining The End Before The Relationship Begins

Wildest Dreams is defined by anticipatory disillusion — Taylor Swift imagines the end of a relationship before it has truly begun, asking her partner, when it all goes wrong, "Say you'll remember me / Standing in a nice dress / Staring at the sunset." It belongs to a wider strand on 1989 in which growing up means letting go of youthful optimism: on one hand she has developed an ability to laugh off her critics, on the other she seems increasingly jaded about love.

Swift described the shift in her own terms to Rolling Stone: "The way I used to approach relationships was very idealistic. I used to go into them thinking, 'Maybe this is the one - we'll get married and have a family, this could be forever'. Whereas now I go in thinking, 'How long do we have on the clock before something comes along and puts a wrench in it, or your publicist calls and says this isn't a good idea?'" An early draft of the lyrics had the couple fleeing the city and blotting out the pressures of the world, an idea which eventually resurfaced in I Know Places.
