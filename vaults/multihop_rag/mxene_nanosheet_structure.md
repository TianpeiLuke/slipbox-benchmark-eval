# MXene Nanosheet Structure

MXenes (pronounced MAX-eenz) are nano-scale materials named for their structure: alternating layers of M and X atoms, where "M" layers consist of some transition metal — bulky atoms that can shed a variable number of electrons — and "X" layers contain atoms of another element, such as carbon or nitrogen.

A single MXene sheet contains a few of these alternating M and X layers chemically bound to each other, in arrangements that may look like MXM or MXMXM, and each sheet is typically only about 1 nanometer thick; for comparison, a sheet of paper is 100,000 times thicker. MXenes normally come as stacks of these individual sheets, and the many separate sheets inside a stack add up to a huge overall surface area, much as the combined surface area of all the pages in a book is far larger than that of just the covers. The total surface area contained in one gram (1/400 ounce) of MXene "can cover a football field," notes Per Persson, a materials scientist at Linköping University in Sweden. Atoms across those large surface areas can interact with their environment, including by grabbing carbon dioxide out of the air around them — a process called carbon capture.
