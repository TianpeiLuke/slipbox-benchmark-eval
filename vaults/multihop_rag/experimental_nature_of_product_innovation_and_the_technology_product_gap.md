# The Experimental Nature Of Product Innovation And The Technology-Product Gap

Product innovation is experimental and should not always be expected to yield productive results: it requires a learning curve and patience, as the outcomes are often ambiguous and unknown.

That indefiniteness — in terms of both results and timelines — is what business leadership struggles with, since it is challenging to translate pure technology innovation into value creation. The consequence is a gap that opens between technology and product teams, in which technology teams strain to articulate the capabilities and value of a technology innovation, leading to unfilled promises and the perception of "technology for the sake of technology," or quips like "a solution looking for a problem."

The current hype cycle in AI serves as a concrete example. For technology or product executives, the challenge is how to do more than check the AI box — it is about how to meaningfully incorporate AI into a product. The remedy proposed is a reversal of direction: instead of forcing a technology onto a product, the design process flows to the technologies, and in this way the technology becomes a natural solution.
