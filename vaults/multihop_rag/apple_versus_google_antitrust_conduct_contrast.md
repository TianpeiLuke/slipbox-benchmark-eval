# Apple Versus Google: A Contrast In Antitrust Conduct

Asked to compare the Apple and Google cases, Tim Sweeney summed up the difference as "Apple was ice and Google was fire" — arguing that Apple keeps all of its antitrust trickery internal to the company while Google had to go outside itself, and that the paper trail this produced is why the Google case played out differently.

His grounds are the mechanics of each scheme. Apple uses its own store and its own payments, and forces developers, OEMs and carriers to all have the same terms. Google, to achieve things with Android, went around paying off dozens of game developers not to compete and dozens of carriers and OEMs not to compete — and when many different companies do deals together, lots of people put things in writing, so it is right there for everybody to read. The warrant is evidentiary rather than moral: Sweeney said the Apple case would be no less interesting if Apple's internal thoughts and deliberations could be seen, but Apple was not putting it in writing whereas Google was, and he observed that because Google distributes Android as open source it had to put these deals more out in the open, even though it still wanted to keep them secret. He was careful not to let Apple off: he called Apple's restrictions on competition absolute — "thou shalt not have a competing store on iOS and thou shalt not use a competing payment method" — and said Apple should receive at least as harsh antitrust scrutiny as Google, adding that documents from both companies show them similarly self-serving in their deals.
