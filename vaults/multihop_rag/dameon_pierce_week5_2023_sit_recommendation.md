# Dameon Pierce As A Week 5 2023 Sit At Atlanta

CBS Sports listed Dameon Pierce under "Sit Him" for Week 5 of the 2023 season at Atlanta: the claim is that Pierce belongs to a touchdown-needy running back tier that might yield only 13 or 14 PPR points even if he scores, and the warrant is that a hard matchup plus a leaky offensive line strips out the efficiency he would need to beat that ceiling.

The grounds are drawn from both sides of the matchup. The Falcons have run hot and cold against the run but have ultimately been good, by virtue of a 3.8 rushing average and zero rushing touchdowns allowed to running backs this season; they gave up just three runs of 10-plus yards to backs in their past three games, all against Jahmyr Gibbs and none to Travis Etienne or A.J. Dillon, and have held backs to 2.7 yards per carry on edge runs while yielding 4.3 yards between the tackles. No running back has taken more than 8 non-PPR or 11 PPR points off them this season, and no one has scored against them. Pierce's own inside/outside split runs against the part of Atlanta's defense that is strongest: in Week 4 he averaged 4.7 yards per run outside with two explosive plays and 2.8 yards per run inside with none, and on the season he has been a full yard worse on outside runs and about the same on inside runs, which the column reads as possible improvement on edge plays. The weekly breakdown lists Houston at Atlanta, the Falcons favoured by 2, an over/under of 41.5, an opponent ranked 2nd versus running backs, 12 projected points, a 26th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 181 rushing yards, eight receptions, 68 receiving yards, one touchdown and 9.7 fantasy points per game.

The column is careful to bound the negative verdict. It genuinely believes Pierce will become a quality fantasy starter in the near term; in Week 4 he had a touchdown called back on a short designed screen and two more runs from 10 yards or closer blown up, yet still began to separate from Houston's other rushers, finishing with 24 carries and a catch for over 100 total yards even though the runs weren't efficient. On film his vision and burst helped outside the tackles and in the screen game and he fought admirably for extra yards, with Houston doing a great job using him in spite of its offensive line issues — issues that show up frequently whenever he runs between the tackles. The recommendation therefore allows a conditional start: he is more than fine as a bye-week replacement, or as a back you have to start because you are thin at the position, but the author would prefer Breece Hall, Isiah Pacheco and Rhamondre Stevenson.
