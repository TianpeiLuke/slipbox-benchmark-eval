# Counterarguments On Uber's Assault Rate And Duty Of Care

Uber's reported decrease in the rate of sexual assault does not establish that the platform got safer, because the denominator moved and several categories moved the wrong way. While the rate decrease is positive, not all the data delivers the same message: over the same period, the total number of sexual assault reports across five categories fell from 5,981 to 3,824, a drop that could be explained by the decrease of trips from 1.4 billion in 2019 to 650 million in 2020.

The category-level breakdown attacks the inference more directly. While the total number of incidents as a percentage of total number of rides does decrease through the years, the number of incidents in certain categories actually increases — nonconsensual kissing, nonconsensual touching and nonconsensual penetration, or rape, all rose between 2017 and 2019. The total number of rape incidents also rose from 2017 to 2019, and while the number of reported rapes dropped in 2020, the rate has remained the same since 2017. In other words, there has not been an improvement in the rate of rapes on the platform. TechCrunch reported this reading on 2023-10-13.

A second contested premise is procedural. Uber has been sued numerous times over the past few years by passengers who claim they were sexually assaulted during a ride, and Lyft has faced similar lawsuits and accusations. Under Uber's terms of use, class action lawsuits can't be filed against the company in cases of sexual assault, so each case has to be heard individually — which has prevented survivors from advocating for themselves collectively. Judge Charles Breyer in the Northern District of California will preside over pretrial hearings, the first time a federal judge is able to make decisions for a large amount of these cases, which will streamline the proceedings; Breyer may preside over the trials depending on whether the parties agree, and if they do not, the cases go back to their home jurisdiction for trial. Another consolidated lawsuit has also been filed against Uber in California, but it only covers survivors in that state.

Uber's own counter goes to duty rather than to data. It has attempted, through several filings for motions to dismiss, to stop the consolidation of these cases, arguing that the company did not owe a duty to the plaintiffs to protect against criminal conduct. Lawyers representing the survivors will therefore have to prove that Uber owed a duty of care to passengers, including the duty of taking reasonable precautions to ensure their safety. The pretrial matters, including witness and expert depositions and document discovery, will be heard by Judge Charles Breyer, and Abrams expects the timeline of the proceedings to last over the next one to two years.
