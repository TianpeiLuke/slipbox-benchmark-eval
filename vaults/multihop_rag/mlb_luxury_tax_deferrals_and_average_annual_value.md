# MLB Luxury Tax: Deferrals And Average Annual Value

A team's luxury-tax payroll in Major League Baseball is calculated according to players' average annual values and discounted by deferrals, so deferring money effectively lowers a contract's average annual value and gives a club greater flexibility within a system that includes penalties for teams exceeding certain thresholds, The New York Times explained on 9 December 2023.

The relationship runs from contract structure to tax exposure. Deferrals reduce the figure that counts against the luxury-tax payroll, which in turn reduces the risk of crossing a threshold and incurring the associated penalties. The Dodgers' use of this device is illustrated by Mookie Betts: he has $115 million deferred within a 12-year, $365 million contract with the club, and under the league's calculations those deferrals lower his average annual value from $30.4 million to $25.5 million.

The range over which the relation holds is the luxury-tax accounting itself — it is the league's calculation of payroll for threshold purposes, not the money actually owed to the player, that the deferrals change.
