# Keep Labs' COVID Pivot And Leadership Change

The COVID-19 pandemic changed Keep Labs: the company was heading toward production when the pandemic hit, and nearly overnight all of its manufacturing contracts were canceled, leaving the preorders and the company up in the air. The year of customer conversations that followed produced the finding that reshaped the company — "What we found was that a lot of people were buying [the Keep] for medication tracking, and not for the primary use case of locking medication in a box" — and this critical feedback led Wilkins to step down as CEO and bring on Jeff Wandzura to lead the company. TechCrunch reported the sequence on 4 October 2023.

The company reached that point through a preorder run built on the CES controversy. Keep was born from a familiar story: Wilkins was using medication to treat a new condition, and his wife was concerned about their three-year-old getting into the drugs — a growing struggle for many households, especially with the rise of cannabis edibles packaged to look like candy. Looking at the market, he saw several pill dispensing devices, but "There were a lot of medication dispensing devices, but it was very clinical in nature. And so, like every crazy entrepreneur, [I said] 'let me see if I can go solve this problem for myself.'" The company saw early success despite not having a shipping product: it capitalized on the attention of getting kicked out of CES and, in December 2019, launched a preorder campaign in which over 15,000 people preordered the device, with 5,000 paying in full. The preorder said all sales were final, but the company reversed course and offered everyone a refund when COVID hit and Keep Labs lost its manufacturer; Philip said only 5% of the preorders were canceled.

The word "medicine" also solved a distribution constraint, not just a reputational one. By simply saying "medicine," the company feels it found a good middle ground with its messaging, because, as Wilkins told TechCrunch, Google and Meta heavily restrict the term "cannabis" in advertising, which limits the marketing reach of devices like the Keep. The restriction extended to crowdfunding: in advance of the launch, Keep Labs had to conduct preorders using a Shopify store because Kickstarter and Indiegogo also didn't allow products to be marketed with the term cannabis.

The response to losing manufacturing was to talk to customers rather than to rebuild the supply chain first. "What the hell do we do now," Wilkins said, adding that they took a step back and started talking to their preorder customers — all of them. For the next year, the company spoke to two to three customers weekly about what made them preorder the device, and it was that sustained listening exercise that surfaced the tracking-over-locking finding and, with it, the leadership change.
