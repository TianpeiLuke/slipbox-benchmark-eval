# USWNT Olympic Preparation Risks Under Hayes

The New York Times argued on 15 November 2023 that the delayed handover creates real risk for the USWNT's Olympic preparation, and that the federation may be accepting it deliberately: the claim is that U.S. Soccer could be willing to sacrifice coherent preparation for the upcoming Olympic tournament to focus more on the longer-term project of the 2027 World Cup, and the warrant is that the summer's World Cup revealed problems far more foundational than poor coaching decisions.

The immediate grounds are practical. There are logistical questions about roster selection over the next few months, some of which may be answered relatively soon with the roster for the camp beginning at the end of the month imminent. The greater challenge will likely be ongoing player evaluation over the next six months, at a time of great transition within the squad, and the specifics of how that will work without Hayes' involvement remain a mystery: it would be understandable for players to feel like they are still auditioning for an absent director until May, while still knowing they must perform at the USWNT standard.

The evaluative judgement is deliberately two-sided. Such a shift itself represents a marked change from the expectations and pressure of constant performance and winning that the team is known for. Is that a good or a bad thing? Perhaps a little bit of both: the USWNT shouldn't be ruled out of contention for the Olympics by any stretch, but the past summer's World Cup did reveal that the problems facing the team are far more foundational than just poor coaching decisions or the strange midfield chemistry, and balancing realism with the pressure to win feels like a much more sustainable path forward.
