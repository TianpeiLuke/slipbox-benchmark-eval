# Jerome Ford As A Week 6 2023 Sit Against The 49ers

CBS Sports listed Jerome Ford under "Sit Him" for Week 6 of the 2023 season against San Francisco: the claim is that in non-PPR there is almost no way to feel good about starting Ford given the matchup and his own inefficiency, and the warrant is that the 49ers' run defense is top-five in nearly every metric while Ford's own production has depended on one long run.

The grounds start with Ford's numbers since Nick Chubb's injury: he has averaged 4.3 yards per rush and 7.7 yards per catch with three total touchdowns, including the catch he had on the play after the Chubb injury. His 69-yard cross-field run at Pittsburgh has certainly helped those numbers; without it he has zero touches of 20-plus yards and his only run of more than 10 yards came with a holding penalty. He has also yet to dominate snaps, playing right around 60% in Weeks 3 and 4, and has not had more than 14 touches in a single game since taking over for Chubb, though that is only two games. The breakdown lists Cleveland at home versus San Francisco, the 49ers favoured by 7, an over/under of 37, an opponent ranked 11th versus running backs, 12.2 projected points, a 24th running back ranking, and year-to-date totals of 186 rushing yards, 10 receptions, 77 receiving yards, three touchdowns and 13.6 fantasy points per game.

The opponent evidence is the heavier half. On the year San Francisco has held enemy running backs to 3.7 yards per carry with an explosive run rate of 1.7%, which somehow is only second-best in football; the only rush-defense metrics they are not top-five in are defensive rush EPA, just below league average, yards before contact per rush at 0.88, where they are sixth, and yards after contact per rush at 2.80, just above league average — and they lead the NFL in fewest missed tackles. Tony Pollard managed an 8-29-0 rushing line against them the previous week. Through the air the 49ers see the fifth-most running back targets per game yet are better than league average in catch rate allowed (75%) and top-10 in both yards per catch (5.7) and yards after catch per reception (6.10) allowed to backs; Pollard caught 4 of 5 passes for 35 yards. The one PPR-scoring hope is quantified: another game with five catches, which isn't a stretch since Ford just had one against a good defense two games ago, and Cleveland's quarterback P.J. Walker has thrown 17.2% of his career pass attempts to running backs against a 2023 league average of 18.2%, so he is just below average in that respect. The verdict allows Ford as an end-of-the-barrel RB2 at most, with Rachaad White, Alexander Mattison or D'Onta Foreman preferred.
