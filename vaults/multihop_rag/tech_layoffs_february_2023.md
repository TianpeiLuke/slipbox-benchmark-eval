# Tech Layoffs In February 2023

February 2023's largest single tech layoff was Dell's, announced on February 6, impacting 6,650 people, or 5% of its worldwide workforce.

The month opened with Rivian announcing on February 1 that it was cutting 6% of its workforce for the second time in less than a year. On February 2 Pinterest announced 150 employees were impacted, its second job-cutting move within weeks of a first round in December 2022, and Getaround announced cuts of 10% of staff, about 42 employees. On February 6, alongside Dell, VinFast announced cuts; the company did not share how many employees were let go, but a LinkedIn post from a former employee said "nearly 35 roles" were affected. Zoom announced on February 7 the cut of 15% of its staff, 1,300 people. Affirm announced on February 8 that it was reducing staff by 19%, about 500 employees, and shutting down its crypto unit. On February 9 GitHub announced 10% of its staff would be impacted through the end of the company's fiscal year — GitHub had about 3,000 employees before the announcement, which was first reported by Fortune; Yahoo, TechCrunch's parent company, announced cuts of 20% of its staff, impacting 1,600 employees in its adtech business; and GitLab announced it was reducing headcount by 7%, impacting around 114 people, though the specific figure depended on actual headcount as of February 9.

Mid-month, Twilio announced on February 13 that its cuts would impact around 17% of its global workforce, about 1,400 people, and iRobot announced it would lay off 7% of its workforce, roughly 85 employees. Sprinklr announced on February 15 that its cuts would affect 4% of its global workforce, more than 100 employees. On February 16 Convoy announced it was shuttering its Atlanta office and laying off workers as part of restructuring — the third time in less than a year the company had laid off workers — and Jumia announced it had cut 20% of its staff, more than 900 positions across its 11 markets, in Q4 of 2022. Evernote confirmed on February 17 that it had laid off 129 people. Chipper Cash announced on February 20 a second round of layoffs just 10 weeks after cutting approximately 12.5% of its workforce; the African cross-border payments platform relieved almost one-third of its workforce, about 100 employees. Green Labs confirmed to TechCrunch that it was conducting a round of layoffs that could impact at least 50% of its workforce, though no exact figure was available. Poshmark confirmed with TechCrunch on February 24 that less than 2% of its workforce was affected, primarily in the U.S.; the company employs roughly 800-plus employees. On February 26 Twitter announced it had laid off more than 200 employees, including Esther Crawford, Haraldur Thorleifsson and Leah Culver; since Elon Musk took over Twitter in October of the previous year, the company's headcount had fallen by more than 70%.
