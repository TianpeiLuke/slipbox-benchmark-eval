# Michigan Did Not Need Harbaugh At Penn State, But Ohio State Is Different

Sporting News argued that whatever the Penn State win proved, Michigan still needs Jim Harbaugh for the Ohio State game, because the Wolverines will not be able to beat the Buckeyes the way they beat Penn State. The 20-yard box in which Michigan won at Beaver Stadium "will stretch across the entire country," and that kind of game requires a coach ready to make legacy-defining decisions.

The grounds are the quality of the opponent. Ohio State has Heisman Trophy candidate Marvin Harrison Jr., who was the separator when the Buckeyes beat Penn State 20-12 on Oct. 21, and a top-five defense as well; the rage between the rivals would reach a 1970s pitch — 1973 for those who know — as they barreled toward another 11-0 vs. 11-0 showdown at the Big House. Harbaugh reportedly watched the Penn State game from the team hotel, and after Sherrone Moore's postgame emotion, a reality set in through a brutal watch of a second half.

The stakes are national. Michigan (10-0, 7-0 Big Ten) was, on this reading, public enemy No. 1 heading into the final two weeks — "Michigan vs. Everybody" — and Ohio State and Michigan locked in their answers about hero and villain long ago. More than 17 million people watched the previous year's 45-23 Michigan victory, a game in which Connor Stalions' involvement continued to be scrutinized through a double-barrel Big Ten and NCAA investigation, and early forecasts said 20 million would tune in this year. Tony Petitti's Friday ruling sent a shockwave through the conference mainly because the third game means Harbaugh will not be on the sideline for The Game. In the short term, the College Football Playoff committee could slide Michigan up to No. 2, and the debate with the Buckeyes and No. 1 Georgia would perk up more than ever — the three best teams in the country, only one with uncertainty at head coach.

Whether Harbaugh would actually be absent was for the courts to decide, but Moore's comments suggested Harbaugh, president Santa Ono and athletic director Warde Manuel were on a united front, consequences be damned in 2024 and beyond. The conclusion: whether they admit it or not, Michigan still needs Harbaugh for those moments, especially in two weeks — and he might not be present for that one either.
