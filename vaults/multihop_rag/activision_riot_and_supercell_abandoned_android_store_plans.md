# Activision, Riot And Supercell's Abandoned Android Store Plans

Asked whether he believes Activision Blizzard really intended to build its own app store, Tim Sweeney said yes — Activision was building a store, with a large team of dozens of engineers building a mobile app store to launch on Android — and said that of the three companies, Riot is the strongest example, while he does not know about Supercell.

For Riot, Sweeney cited the documents in the case: Riot planned to distribute League of Legends directly through its website on mobile, exactly as it does on PC, and stuck to that plan until Google paid it off not to; at a minimum, he said, Google's payoff dissuaded Riot from distributing off Google Play. For Activision, his evidence is partly first-hand and partly documentary. Activision came to Epic and told Epic it was building the store; and after everything went down, Sweeney talked to an Activision employee close to that effort, working with the team building the store, who reported that the entire store team Activision had employed had been disbanded as soon as Activision signed its deal with Google. The Verge appended an editor's note that no such person was heard from at trial and that the interviewer would be eager to speak to them. Sweeney added that Activision was telling Google it was building the store, that Google's internal discussions said Google didn't want Activision building a competing store, and that after signing the deal Google staff were gleeful about having dissuaded perhaps their number one competitor at the time from launching its own store.
