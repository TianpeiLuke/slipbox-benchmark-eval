# PokéTsume, The Live-Action Pokémon Workplace Drama

Pack Your Pocket With Adventure (Pocket ni Bouken o Tsumekonde, or PokéTsume) is the first Japanese live-action foray into Pokémon, premiering in Japan last month, and it does not even take place within the universe of the games. Prior to it there had been only one attempt at live-action storytelling in the world of Pokémon: 2019's Detective Pikachu, with Ryan Reynolds' wisecracking gumshoe Pikachu.

The series follows Madoka Akagi, portrayed by popular Japanese idol Nanase Nishino of Nogizaka46, a recent graduate moving out of her childhood home to live in the city and follow her dream of becoming a creator with an upstart company called ADventure. She played Pokémon Red and Blue (Red and Green in Japan) as a kid but has long since grown distant from the franchise. The opening episode plays out as a nostalgic parallel between the trials of working life and the journey to become a Pokémon Master: the childhood friend who works for another company is your rival; the product presentation to an investment CEO with the company's status on the line is a gym battle. She rediscovers Pokémon in a box of items sent by her mother and begins to replay that formative adventure, with clips of the game and shots of the joy on the faces of both child and adult Madoka in the dim glow of the Game Boy screen. The music carries the same doubling: a relaxing bar plays a slow jazz-infused rendition of the Pokémon Center theme, and a synth rendition of Pallet Town marks the start of Madoka's journey.

As the show develops, the lessons from the game form the basis of a pseudo-episodic tale about Madoka's life at the company: the struggle to connect with a new client is paralleled by the way pokémon will not listen to you until you have proven your worth with gym badges, and learning and improving on the job is the experience that turns a flailing, floundering Magikarp into a job-leading Gyarados.

The execution is uneven but affectionate. The show can come across a bit silly in places and struggles with communicating itself by making its Pokémon overlay at times difficult to take seriously, but there is heart — plenty of touches that represent a series made with love, such as how characters have their own sprites integrated into the gameplay of Pokémon Red. With PokéTsume, Pokémon is not just a virtual journey to become a champion; it is a lesson we take with us into our daily lives long after our adventure is at an end, an integration of the morals of the franchise into daily life that epitomizes a 15-year transformation of the Pokémon brand.
