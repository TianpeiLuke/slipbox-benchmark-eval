# Scrapping Winter DST To Cut Energy Bills

Professor Aoife Foley of Queen's University Belfast argues that abandoning the winter clock change would cut household electricity bills substantially: she calculates that household consumers could save more than £400 a year, depending on their tariff, if the government scrapped DST at the end of October, amounting to about £1.20 a day, while dramatically reducing demand on the National Grid. Foley specialises in clean energy research.

Her mechanism is behavioural rather than technical: "By simply foregoing the winter DST in October, we save energy because it is brighter in the evening during winter, so we reduce commercial and residential electrical demand as people leave work earlier, and go home earlier, meaning less lighting and heating is needed." The urgency comes from the supply picture at the time of writing in October 2023: evening energy demand in winter peaks between 5pm and 7pm, and the National Grid had warned that UK households could face three-hour power cuts that winter due to a shortage of gas. "We are no longer in an energy crisis in Europe but an energy war and, dependent on weather conditions this winter, it is very likely we may need to start rationing energy very seriously to avoid bigger energy issues in December and January when gas reserves start to run low," Foley adds. "There is no doubt that by foregoing the DST in winter we would save a lot of energy, reduce our bills and carbon emissions during this energy war, especially during a cost of living crisis."
