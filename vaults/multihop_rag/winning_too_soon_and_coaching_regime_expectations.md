# Winning Too Soon And Coaching Regime Expectations

The New York Times argued on 16 October 2023 that a bad team winning right away can be counterproductive, because early success resets expectations for fans and ownership and makes it less practical to take one step backward in the short term for a better shot at taking two steps forward down the line.

The test cases are the New York Giants and Minnesota Vikings, both of which exceeded expectations under first-year head coaches in 2022 and reached the playoffs, and both of which were struggling in their second seasons and facing difficult questions about the future. The grounds are that every head coach and general manager wants to win as much as possible, but the goal is to build for the long term, which can require time to do things with sustainability in mind — so whether early success helps or hurts could depend on how well the football leadership has communicated with ownership.

A veteran coach with experience on a half-dozen teams supplied the warrant from inside the building: "The best first year can be that 7-10 season where you show some promise. The Giants won a bunch of games early, people were heaping praise on the head coach, people get the expectation of what the quarterback can be. But oftentimes, you know different internally because you know the house you are living in. You are inside the walls every single day and you know where the roof leaks, where the foundation is cracked." Buffalo is offered as the managed version of the same situation: the Bills surprisingly reached the playoffs in their first season under Sean McDermott, a euphoric moment after a 17-year playoff drought, went 6-10 the next year, then began a steady rise behind Josh Allen and a top defense — but there isn't a Josh Allen on the Giants or Vikings. Minnesota in particular is in an interesting spot, with Kirk Cousins approaching free agency and playing well enough, most likely, to keep the Vikings from climbing too high in the draft order. "They already gave a contract to (tight end) T.J. Hockenson, and they are going to give one to (receiver) Justin Jefferson," an exec said. "Now, what? Are you going into the rookie pool and taking the fourth-best quarterback in the draft? You can look like Atlanta with (Desmond) Ridder pretty quick."
