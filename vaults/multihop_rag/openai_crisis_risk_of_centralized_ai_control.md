# The OpenAI Crisis As A Case Against Centralized AI Control

The claim is that the OpenAI debacle exposes a structural risk in depending on a centralized proprietary AI player. The situation remained fluid as of November 21, 2023, with any number of potential outcomes still on the table, but the whole debacle has shone a spotlight on the forces that control the burgeoning AI revolution, leading many to question what happens if you go all-in on a centralized proprietary player and things then go belly-up.

Mark Surman, president and executive director at the Mozilla Foundation, put the ground this way to TechCrunch: "The OpenAI / Microsoft drama underlines one of the big near-term risks with AI — that this next wave of technology is controlled by the same tiny set of players who have shaped that last era of the internet. We might have a chance of avoiding this if GPT-X were responsibly open sourced, giving researchers and startups a shot at making this technology safer, more useful and more trustworthy for people everywhere." The warrant is that concentration of control is itself the hazard, and open sourcing distributes it.

TechCrunch qualifies the claim: it is still too early to make any surefire claims on what impact the OpenAI fallout will have on LLM development and uptake in the future, and Altman and Brockman are undoubtedly steady hands for a commercial AI startup who may even return to steward OpenAI. But some might argue it is unhealthy that so much focus lies on just a handful of people — and it is telling that their departure has created such widespread havoc.
