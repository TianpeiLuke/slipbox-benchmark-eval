# Anthropic's Founding By Former OpenAI Researchers

Dario Amodei, a researcher with ties to the effective altruist community, had been on hand at the Rosewood hotel when OpenAI was born, and was leading the lab's efforts to build a neural network called a large language model that could learn from enormous amounts of digital text: by analysing countless Wikipedia articles, digital books and message boards it could generate text on its own, and it also had the unfortunate habit of making things up. It was called GPT-3 and was released in the summer of 2020, and researchers inside OpenAI, Google and other companies thought this rapidly improving technology could be a path to artificial general intelligence. Amodei was unhappy about the Microsoft deal because he thought it was taking OpenAI in a really commercial direction, and he and other researchers went to the board to try to push Altman out, according to five people familiar with the matter; after they failed, they left, worrying, like DeepMind's founders before them, that their new corporate overlords would favour commercial interests over safety.

In 2021 the group of about 15 engineers and scientists created a new lab called Anthropic, with the plan of building AI the way the effective altruists thought it should be done, with very tight controls. Anthropic disputes the account of the board approach: "There was no attempt to remove Sam Altman from OpenAI by the co-founders of Anthropic," said an Anthropic spokesperson, Sally Aldous. "The co-founders themselves came to the conclusion that they wished to depart OpenAI to start their own company, made this known to OpenAI's leadership, and over several weeks negotiated an exit on mutually agreeable terms." Anthropic accepted a $US4 billion investment from Amazon and another $US2 billion from Google two years later. After OpenAI received another $US2 billion from Microsoft, Altman and another senior executive, Greg Brockman, visited Bill Gates at his sprawling mansion on the shores of Lake Washington, outside Seattle; the Microsoft founder was no longer involved in the company day to day but kept in regular touch with its executives.
