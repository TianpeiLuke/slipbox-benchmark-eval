# The USD Fund Copycat Playbook In China

USD-denominated funds in China have long operated on a benchmark model: draw inspiration from Silicon Valley startups and use them as templates for investment targets back home. They would seek out the equivalents of Facebook, Amazon and Uber on the other side of the Pacific Ocean and hope they become winners in the country's largely untapped internet market.

The range over which that model worked was two decades. Since their entry into China in the late 1990s, American venture capital firms — led by powerhouses like Sequoia Capital, IDG Capital and GGV — have played a major role in funding high-risk, high-reward startups in the country's consumer internet sector.

The relationship now hangs in the balance as changes at home and abroad diminish the pool of investment opportunities for outside financiers. This dealmaking strategy of American funds in China has become less effective in the face of shifting global and domestic landscapes; driven by a confluence of factors, from China's crackdown on the tech industry to escalating U.S.-China tensions, some of these investors are now turning their gaze to opportunities abroad, tracing the footsteps of a new generation of Chinese-founded startups that are expanding overseas.
