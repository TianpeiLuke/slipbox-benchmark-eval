# Microsoft's Acquisition Of Activision Blizzard King

Microsoft's purchase of Activision-Blizzard-King was finally approved on October 12, 2023 after nearly two years of regulatory hurdles, at a price of $69bn (£56bn), and it made Microsoft the world's third-largest video game studio by revenue. As the owner of the Xbox ecosystem, Microsoft was already a massive player in video games, but buying a tentpole AAA studio solidified its position in the top three.

Activision and Blizzard own Call of Duty, Diablo, Overwatch, World of Warcraft and Starcraft, but Engadget argued the real meat of the deal comes from King, the mobile division. King operates Candy Crush Saga, a game with 238 million monthly active users — more than twice as many as Activision Blizzard's combined player bases — which has generated more than $20 billion in lifetime revenue; King routinely outperforms Activision and Blizzard in quarterly returns. Mobile gaming remains a huge business, especially in the Chinese market, the largest and most lucrative audience in video games. The deal also surfaced in the app-store fight: Tim Sweeney noted that four years after Epic's 2019 proposal to Google, Microsoft acquired Activision Blizzard, and that one of Microsoft's big talking points to the European Union was that the merger strengthened the company overall in order to provide a viable computing store on iOS and Android. BBC News framed the closing question for players as what the deal actually means the next time they pick up a video game.
