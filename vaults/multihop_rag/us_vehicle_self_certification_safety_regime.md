# The US Vehicle Self-Certification Safety Regime

In the US, car companies "self certify" that their vehicles comply with federal safety standards requiring everything from sideview mirrors to airbags to automatic emergency braking — there is no "pre-approval" before an automaker is allowed to sell its cars to the public.

The consequences The Verge draws from that design: it allows Tesla to sell cars with driver assist systems that safety experts say put drivers and pedestrians at risk, and it allows the company to sell a truck made from stainless steel with no rounded edges. Tesla conducted its own crash tests with the Cybertruck in-house, with videos shown during the delivery event in November, but NHTSA has yet to perform its own. The Cybertruck "meets the performance criteria" for standards like lane departure warnings and dynamic brake support, according to the NHTSA website, but there is no five-star safety rating, and the Cybertruck wasn't mentioned in the agency's list of vehicles it will crash test for 2024.
