# AI Copyright And Training Disputes

Universal Music Group's answer to generative AI trained on its catalogue rejects the premise that such training is a neutral technical act, recasting it as a choice of allegiance: "The training of generative AI using our artists' music . . . begs the question as to which side of history all stakeholders in the music ecosystem want to be on: the side of artists, fans and human creative expression, or on the side of deep fakes, fraud and denying artists their due compensation," the label said after a song using AI to imitate Drake and The Weeknd, two of its artists, went viral.

A second objection cuts against the label framing itself. These same conversations and contradictions are manifesting across creative industries, but artists themselves don't always have a seat at the table — so an industry-level statement of grievance is not the same thing as artists speaking for themselves. Independent artists in particular are learning that their voices resonate louder when coming together across disciplines to push back against what Holland describes as an "extraordinary spectrum of exploitation" that leverages their work.

The dispute is not confined to music. In a roundtable hosted by the FTC that same week, the agency brought together figures from across creative industries — from voice acting and science fiction to screenwriting, music, illustration and even fashion — to delve into how generative AI is affecting creatives. TechCrunch reported the exchange on 2023-10-06.
