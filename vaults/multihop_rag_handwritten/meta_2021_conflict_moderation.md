# Meta 2021 Conflict Moderation and Its Aftermath

During the violence following the May 2021 court ruling to evict Palestinian families from Sheikh Jarrah in occupied East Jerusalem, users across Facebook and Instagram accused Meta of removing posts and suspending accounts supporting Palestinians. Over the 13 days leading to the ceasefire, 7amleh documented more than 500 reports of Palestinian digital rights violations.

Meta attributed some incidents to technical issues — one prevented users in Palestine and Colombia from posting Stories — and others to human error, such as blocking the hashtag for Al-Aqsa Mosque after mistaking it for a terrorist organisation. It also blocked journalists in Gaza from WhatsApp without explanation.

At its Oversight Board's recommendation, Meta commissioned a third-party due diligence report, which concluded the company's actions had an "adverse human rights impact" on Palestinian users' rights to freedom of expression and political participation, and found Arabic content flagged as potentially violating at significantly higher rates than Hebrew.

Meta committed to changes including updating keywords associated with dangerous organisations, disclosing government content-removal requests, and launching a hostile speech classifier for Hebrew. Director of human rights Miranda Sissons said Meta was "assessing the feasibility" of reviewing Arabic content by dialect.

Assessments of progress are mixed. Abukhater credits Meta with improved responsiveness to harassment relative to X, and York notes increased contact with regional Palestinian organisations, but both describe slow implementation. As Abukhater put it: "Whenever an escalation like the one we're experiencing now happens, things just go back to zero."
