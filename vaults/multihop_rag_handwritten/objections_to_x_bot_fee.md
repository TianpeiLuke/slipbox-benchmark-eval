# Objections to Charging Users to Stop Bots

Two distinct objections were raised against X's $1 fee as an anti-bot measure.

**It will not deter determined spammers.** WordPress founder Matt Mullenweg argued that spammers already buy domains at costs "usually a lot more than a dollar per year," with millions used for spam or nefarious purposes, and may use stolen credit cards and identities. He concluded that "charging may cause a short-term drop in bots while the bad guys update their scripts, but the value of manipulating X/Twitter is so high I imagine there is already millions of dollars being spent on it." X's Eric Farraro largely agreed, which is why the stated goal is cost imposition rather than elimination.

**It excludes genuine users unevenly.** Aditya Vashistha, an assistant professor of information science at Cornell who studies computing systems in the Global South, noted that "the ability to afford $1 is not the same" in the Philippines and New Zealand, X's two test markets. "Even if the rationale behind creating a paywall is right, such policies would broaden the digital divide."

The precedent is not novel: WhatsApp charged a $1 annual fee until 2016.
