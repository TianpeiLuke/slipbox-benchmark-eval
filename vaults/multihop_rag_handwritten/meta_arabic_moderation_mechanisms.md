# Mechanisms of Moderation Bias Against Arabic Content

Four mechanisms are identified as producing disproportionate restriction of Arabic and Palestine-related content on Meta platforms.

**Linguistic coverage.** Arabic has more than 25 dialects, and Meta has been criticised for not adequately diversifying its language resources. Its moderation is less precise for Arabic than for Hebrew, which is more standardised.

**Policy granularity.** Meta forbids support for terrorist organisations, but black-and-white rules cannot handle nuanced cases such as discussing violence without condoning it. As EFF notes, something as incidental as a Hamas flag in an image background can draw a strike.

**Automation and thin human review.** Jillian York, EFF's director for international freedom of expression, attributes inconsistency to automation and decisions by "minimally trained humans," a pattern that has also affected Libya, Syria and Ukraine. The consequence she identifies is that "these rules can prevent people from sharing documentation of human rights violations, documentation of war crimes, even just news about what's happening on the ground."

**Regulatory gradient.** York observes that moderation of English and European languages is more comprehensive because regulatory pressure is greater there — the EU enforces the [Digital Services Act](digital_services_act.md) — so investment follows enforcement rather than need.

Advocacy groups additionally raise Meta's partnerships with government agencies, such as the Israeli Cyber Unit, as a political influence on policy decisions.
