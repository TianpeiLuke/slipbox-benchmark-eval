# Meta Response to Shadowbanning Claims

Meta rejects the claim of deliberate suppression, attributing the reported effects to global technical faults rather than to content subject matter.

Its newsroom post stated: "Our policies are designed to keep people safe on our apps while giving everyone a voice. We apply these policies equally around the world and there is no truth to the suggestion that we are deliberately suppressing voice … We can make errors and that is why there is an appeals process."

Communications director Andy Stone identified a specific defect: "We identified a bug impacting all Stories that re-shared Reels and Feed posts, meaning they weren't showing up properly in people's Stories tray, leading to significantly reduced reach. This bug affected accounts equally around the globe and had nothing to do with the subject matter of the content — and we fixed it as quickly as possible." A second bug preventing users from going live was described as a global issue fixed within hours.

The response is targeted narrowly. It addresses Story visibility and live access but not the reported flagging of Arabic journalistic content, the Palestinian flag emoji comment filtering, or the al-Ahli Hospital images. Stone declined to comment on the Arabic-flagging allegations, directing TechCrunch to the newsroom post, which did not address them. Whether the bug affected accounts posting content unrelated to Gaza is unestablished.
