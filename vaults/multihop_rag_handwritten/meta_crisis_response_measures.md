# Meta Crisis Response Measures During the Israel-Hamas War

Following the EU's content moderation warning, Meta published an overview of its response to risks stemming from the Israel-Hamas war, confirming several changes.

- **Temporary expansion of the Violence and Incitement policy** to prioritise the safety of Israelis kidnapped by Hamas. Meta removes content that "clearly identifies hostages when we're made aware of it, even if it's being done to condemn or raise awareness of their situation," allowing blurred images of victims; where it is unsure or unable to assess clearly, it prioritises the safety and privacy of kidnapping victims in line with standards established by the Geneva Convention.
- **Prioritised livestream checks** on Facebook and Instagram, above existing Live prioritisation, watching specifically for Hamas attempts to broadcast footage of captured Israelis. Restrictions already apply to Live for users who previously violated certain policies. Meta said it would swiftly remove such content and the accounts behind it, and bank the content in its systems to prevent re-sharing of copies.
- **Reduced amplification** — making systems less likely to recommend potentially violating or borderline content, and reducing the visibility of potentially offensive comments.
- **Hashtag blocking** rendering certain conflict-related terms non-searchable. Meta did not specify which hashtags.
- **A special operations center** staffed with fluent Hebrew and Arabic speakers.
- **Strike-free removals** for some violations, so that content removals during the period do not disable accounts — an explicit mitigation for the raised risk of removing non-violating content in error. Appeals remain available.

Meta reported removing or marking as disturbing more than 795,000 pieces of content in Hebrew and Arabic in the three days following October 7, and removing seven times as much content per day under its Dangerous Organizations and Individuals policy in those two languages as in the preceding two months.

The livestreaming priority follows the 2019 Christchurch attacks in New Zealand, where a shooter livestreamed a killing spree targeting two mosques on Facebook. Israeli media reported that Hamas militants had uploaded a video of a dead woman's body to her own Facebook page using her mobile phone, which her family learned of through the post.
