# Meta Moderation Bias Allegations

The claim is that Meta's moderation practices disproportionately suppress Palestinian voices as a structural property of its processes, not as isolated technical faults — a bias problem rather than a bug.

The grounds are a documented pattern across years and multiple independent sources. A third-party report commissioned by Meta on the May 2021 conflict found Arabic content was flagged as potentially violating at significantly higher rates than Hebrew content and was more likely to be erroneously removed; it attributed this partly to Hebrew being a "more standardized language" and to reviewers lacking competence in less common dialects such as Palestinian Arabic. The Electronic Frontier Foundation described Meta's 2021 actions as "systemic censorship of Palestinian voices," and 7amleh's 2022 report called Meta "still the most restricting company" among social media giants in its moderation of Palestinian digital space. In 2021 a group of Facebook employees filed internal complaints alleging bias against Arab and Muslim users, one attributing it to "years and years of implementing policies that just don't scale globally."

The warrant connecting them is that a pattern reproduced across separate incidents, languages and years is better explained by systematic properties — automation, imprecise enforcement, uneven language investment — than by a sequence of unrelated errors. Meta's own explanations are individually plausible but each is offered as a distinct technical fault.
