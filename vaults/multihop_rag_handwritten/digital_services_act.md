# Digital Services Act

The Digital Services Act (DSA) is the European Union's content moderation rulebook: a pan-EU regulation placing governance obligations on digital services and platforms carrying user-generated content.

It requires platforms to respond to reports of illegal content in a timely, diligent and objective manner, to communicate their terms and conditions clearly to users, and to demonstrate proper enforcement of them. Platforms designated **very large online platforms** (VLOPs) carry additional obligations to identify and mitigate systemic risks such as disinformation, gender-based violence, and negative effects on fundamental rights. A compliance deadline for VLOPs took effect in summer 2023.

The distinction the DSA draws matters for what it can compel: terrorist content is illegal in the EU, whereas disinformation is not illegal per se. For disinformation the obligation is to **mitigate risk**, not to remove content.

Article 36 provides a "crisis response" mechanism enabling the Commission to adopt rapid measures on larger platforms during situations of serious threat, such as war. Article 66 governs the formal opening of proceedings.

Penalties for confirmed breaches reach 6% of global annual turnover, and repeated failure to correct course can result in a service being blocked in the region. Fines may also be imposed for incorrect, incomplete or misleading information supplied in response to a formal request.
